## PART II — THE UNIFIED CAUSAL VARIABLE

```
SOLVATION SHELL GEOMETRY VARIANCE (SGV)

The distribution of Li⁺ solvation shell
configurations across the full Li⁺
population in the bulk electrolyte under
operating conditions.

Quantified as Solvation Configuration
Entropy (SCE):

  SCE = -Σ p(gᵢ) × log(p(gᵢ))

  where gᵢ are discrete solvation geometry
  configurations identified by MD trajectory
  clustering, and p(gᵢ) is the population
  fraction of each configuration.

LOW SCE = LOW VARIANCE:
  Single dominant coordination geometry.
  Every Li⁺ arrives at the interface
  carrying the same shell structure.
  Coherent desolvation.
  Uniform deposition.
  Long cycle life.

HIGH SCE = HIGH VARIANCE:
  Multiple competing coordination geometries.
  A fraction of Li⁺ ions arrive at the
  interface with incompatible shell geometry.
  Forced desolvation at those sites.
  Dendrite nucleation at those sites.
  Capacity fade. Failure.

DENDRITES ARE THE GEOMETRIC
INCOMPATIBILITY SIGNAL MADE PHYSICAL.
They mark the exact locations where
navigator geometry was incompatible
with the gap it had to traverse.
```

### PATHWAY 1: GEOMETRIC OCCLUSION
WITHOUT A RING

The fluorinated cyclic ethers achieve
low variance through two bundled effects:
electronic (fluorination) and mechanical
(ring rigidity). The ring is necessary
for rigidity but creates viscosity
penalties — cyclic structures are
heavier and less fluid than linear ones.

The geometric derivation:
A molecule that achieves geometric
occlusion of its donor atoms without
requiring a ring — using branched or
cage-like architecture — could achieve
equivalent or lower donor ability than
cyclic fluorinated ethers while
maintaining better fluidity.

**CANDIDATE CLASS: Fluorinated neopentyl
ether derivatives.**

Neopentyl architecture: a central
quaternary carbon with four substituents.
This creates a cage-like steric
environment around any donor atoms
without requiring a ring.
The four substituents block approach
vectors to the central carbon from
all directions.

Add fluorination to the substituents:
donor ability of any remaining oxygen
is suppressed both sterically
(the cage blocks approach) and
electronically (fluorination withdraws
electron density) simultaneously.

Specific structural target:
2,2-bis(fluoromethyl)-1,3-difluoropropane
derivatives with ether linkages.

This class:
— Does not appear in battery electrolyte
  literature as a designed candidate
— Uses neopentyl core (established in
  lubricants and polymer chemistry,
  not in battery solvents with this
  design principle)
— Achieves cage-like steric occlusion
  without ring structure
— Combines electronic and steric
  variance reduction without the
  viscosity penalty of cyclic systems
— Predicted coordination number:
  lower than BTFMD, potentially
  competitive with HFTHP, with
  better transport properties

### PATHWAY 2: GEOMETRIC COORDINATION LOCK

HFTHP and BTFMD still contain oxygen
atoms. Their coordination number is
near zero because fluorination kills
the donor ability. But the theoretical
minimum is zero coordination — no
donor atoms available at all. A molecule
with zero donor atoms cannot dissolve
lithium salt. But a molecule with
exactly one geometrically accessible
coordination site, with all other
approach vectors sterically blocked,
could achieve variance near zero by
design — every Li⁺ that does interact
with it encounters exactly the same
geometry.

The geometric derivation:
Variance is zero by design, not by
statistical averaging, when the molecule
can only coordinate Li⁺ in one spatial
configuration.

**CANDIDATE CLASS: Partially fluorinated
crown ether derivatives.**

Traditional crown ethers: high
and variable coordination — too many
oxygens, too flexible, too much variance.

Partially fluorinated crown ethers:
most ring oxygens have donor ability
killed by adjacent fluorination, but
one or two retain weak donor ability.
Single preferred coordination geometry.
Li⁺ finds the one accessible site
and coordinates in a fixed geometry
every time.

This class has been explored for
ion selectivity — which ion gets through
a membrane — but NOT for solvation
geometry variance minimisation as
the design target.

The shift in design principle:
Current use: maximise selectivity
  (choose the right ion)
Proposed use: minimise variance
  (enforce the same geometry for
  every Li⁺ interaction)

These are different targets.
The molecules that are optimal for
one are not necessarily optimal
for the other.
Partially fluorinated crown ether
derivatives designed for variance
minimisation, not ion selectivity,
are a genuinely novel design space.

### PATHWAY 3: THE VARIANCE PUMP —
ACTIVE ANION CONGREGATION

All current candidates are passive
variance reducers — they reduce
variance by not coordinating Li⁺.
The diluent sits inertly. The
fluorinated ether cannot coordinate.
They remove themselves from competition.

The geometric derivation:
What if instead of passively staying
out of the coordination sphere, the
molecule actively pushes Li⁺ toward
anions? A molecule that congregates
anions near Li⁺ would force anion-
dominated coordination more
effectively than a molecule that
simply does nothing.

This is an active variance pump:
instead of removing solvent from
competition, actively recruit anions
to the coordination sphere.

**CANDIDATE CLASS: Fluorinated molecules
with Lewis acid character — specifically
fluorinated boron-containing solvents.**

A molecule with:
— Weakly Lewis acidic boron centre
— Fluorinated substituents (zero
  donor ability toward Li⁺)
— Low donor oxygen content

Would:
— Not coordinate Li⁺ through oxygen
  (donor ability killed)
— Actively associate with FSI⁻ or
  TFSI⁻ anions through Lewis acid
  boron site
— Congregate anions near Li⁺ by
  gathering them into its vicinity
— Produce a solvation environment
  where Li⁺ is surrounded by anions
  that the boron-containing molecule
  has actively concentrated nearby

This is the active variance pump.
Not passive exclusion of solvent.
Active recruitment of anions.

Status in literature:
Boron-containing electrolyte additives
exist (boroxine, LiBOB, TMB, etc.)
but they function as SEI formers —
they decompose to form the protective
layer. They are not designed or
recognised as active anion congregation

### PATHWAY 4: CONFORMATIONAL LOCK
WITHOUT FLUORINATION

All current top candidates use
fluorination to kill donor ability.
Fluorination is effective but:
— Expensive (fluorinated reagents,
  specialised synthesis)
— Adds molecular weight (density
  penalty in energy density calculations)
— Creates HF-generation risk if
  molecule decomposes under abuse
  conditions

The geometric derivation:
Fluorination withdraws electron density
from donor oxygen through C-F bond.
But another mechanism achieves the same
result: intramolecular donation.

If a molecule has a donor oxygen already
engaged in intramolecular electron
withdrawal — donating electron density
to an electron-poor centre within the
same molecule — it has little residual
donor ability available for Li⁺.
The oxygen is internally occupied.

**CANDIDATE CLASS: Orthoformate and
orthocarbonate esters.**

Trimethyl orthoformate HC(OCH₃)₃:
Central carbon shared by three oxygens.
That carbon is electron-poor (bonded
to three electronegative oxygens).
It withdraws electron density from
all three oxygens simultaneously.
Each oxygen has reduced individual
donor ability because it is donating
internally to the shared carbon.

Trimethyl orthocarbonate C(OCH₃)₄:
Same principle. Four oxygens sharing
one central carbon. Each oxygen's
donor ability is suppressed by internal
withdrawal.

Status in literature:
Trimethyl orthoformate used in some
battery applications as an additive.
Recognised for SEI formation properties.
NOT recognised or designed for its
variance-reduction mechanism — the
suppression of Li⁺-oxygen coordination
by internal electron withdrawal.

Fluorinated orthocarbonate derivatives:
External fluorination on the methyl
groups compounds the internal
withdrawal effect. Double suppression
of oxygen donor ability — internal
(carbon sharing) and external
(fluorination). No ring required.
Lower viscosity than cyclic systems.
Potentially lower cost than highly
fluorinated cyclic ethers.

This subclass has barely been explored
in battery literature. The design
principle — internal electron
withdrawal as a non-fluorine route
to variance reduction — is not
currently in use as a design criterion.
