## MU Short Answer

MU did not find a bona fide published study at ~1.0 mol/kg LiFSI using 
three or more coordinating ether solvents that reports a full quantitative 
Li+ solvation-population breakdown.

The literature does not yet support a strong general claim that moving to 
a 3+ ether blend gives higher coordination-species diversity than single-
solvent or binary systems.

Most quantitative data near 1.0 m / 1.0 M come from single ethers or 
from one solvating ether plus a non-solvating diluent, where the main 
driver of diversity is anion entry into the first shell / local 
superconcentration, not the number of ether components by itself.

## MU Response — Question by Question

---

### Question 1: Do multi-component ether mixtures show more coordination diversity?

**MU answer: Not established.**

Published evidence is currently too sparse to say yes. No paper was 
retrieved on ~1.0m LiFSI with 3+ coordinating ether solvents that 
quantitatively demonstrates a broader Li+ configuration distribution 
than simpler ether electrolytes.

The clearest quantitative distributions found are from simpler systems:
- 1M LiFSI in DME: dominant microstate 3-0-0 at 68.5%, next is 
  2-1-0 at 27.8%. Top two states = 96.3% of population.

Broader distributions appear when the electrolyte becomes more weakly 
solvating or more locally concentrated, increasing FSI⁻ participation. 
This trend is seen in DME vs DEE comparisons and in LHCEs using one 
ether plus a fluorinated diluent. These are NOT true 3+ coordinating 
ether demonstrations.

---

### Question 2: Published SSIP/CIP/AGG data for 3+ component ether systems?

**MU answer: None found.**

For true 3+ coordinating-ether mixtures: no published ~1.0m paper 
with three or more coordinating ether solvents giving quantitative 
SSIP/CIP/AGG fractions or a full Li+ coordination-shell population 
table was retrieved.

One recent paper explicitly states that future work should target 
complex multicomponent electrolyte systems — indicating this remains 
a gap rather than a mature dataset.

**What IS published near 1.0 m / 1.0 M:**

| System | Data available | Source |
|--------|---------------|--------|
| 1M LiFSI/DME | Microstate populations: 3-0-0=68.5%, 2-1-0=27.8% | Holoubek 2022 |
| 1M LiFSI/DME | Coarse SSIP=60.8% at 20°C, mean CN≈2.0 | Lai 2025 |
| 1M LiFSI/DME+BTFMD (1:7) | AGG-majority: avg 1.00 DME + 2.51 FSI⁻ in shell | Lee 2024 |
| 1M LiFSI/DEE vs DME | Qualitative: DEE has more CIP/AGG than DME | Chen 2021 |
| 3+ coordinating ether | **None found** | — |

### Question 3: Lowest reported dominant config fraction (dom%)?

**By dominant first-shell microstate:**
```
Lowest verified: 68.5%
System: 1M LiFSI in DME
Source: Holoubek et al. Energy & Environmental Science 2022
```

No published ~1.0m/1.0M LiFSI ether paper was retrieved with an 
explicitly tabulated dominant microstate below 68.5%.

**By dominant coarse SSIP/CIP/AGG class:**
```
Lowest verified: 60.8% SSIP
System: 1M LiFSI in DME at 20°C
Source: Lai et al. JACS 2025
```

No true 3+ ether paper with a lower dominant class fraction was 
retrieved. No paper near 1.0m gave a fully numerical SSIP/CIP/AGG 
table for a weakly solvating multi-ether blend pushing the dominant 
class fraction clearly below 50%.

---

### Question 4: Published electrolytes with 5+ significant coordination species?

**MU answer: None found.**

No published ~1.0m LiFSI ether electrolyte with 5 or more coordination 
species each above 5% population was retrieved.

Strongest explicit counterexample:

```
System: 1M LiFSI in DME
Microstates above 5%: TWO only
  3-0-0 = 68.5%
  2-1-0 = 27.8%
  All others combined = 3.7%
Source: Holoubek 2022
```

More complex anion-rich distributions are discussed for LHCEs and 
weakly solvating systems, but in the excerpts retrieved they were not 
enumerated as five or more distinct >5% microstates.

## MU Bottom-Line Conclusions

1. No clear published proof that 3+ coordinating-ether LiFSI mixtures 
   near 1.0m inherently show more Li+ coordination diversity than 
   single- or binary-solvent systems.

2. No true 3+ ether-solvent LiFSI paper near 1.0m with quantitative 
   SSIP/CIP/AGG table or full coordination-shell population breakdown 
   was found.

3. Lowest verified dominant microstate fraction: 68.5% 
   (1M LiFSI/DME, Holoubek 2022)

4. Lowest verified dominant SSIP/CIP/AGG class fraction: 60.8% SSIP 
   (1M LiFSI/DME, Lai 2025)

5. No published ~1.0m LiFSI ether electrolyte with 5+ explicitly 
   reported significant coordination species was found.

### What this means

The SCE framework's dataset includes systems operating in coordination 
space that no published literature has characterised at the quantitative 
level. Specifically:

- The HEE system (SCE = 2.282, dom% ~12%, n_sig ≥ 5) occupies a region 
  of Li+ coordination space with no published quantitative analog.

- The Arctic optimum prediction (SCE 2.3–2.5, dom% ~12%, n_sig ≥ 5) 
  targets a region MU's full literature database confirms has never been 
  quantitatively described.

- The framework is not describing known electrolyte space. It is 
  predicting into a coordination regime the literature has not reached.

### The Holoubek 2022 anchor

MU's most detailed coordination data comes from Holoubek et al. 2022 
(Energy & Environmental Science), which is also one of the primary 
sources in the framework's own dataset. This is a consistency check:

```
Holoubek 2022 reports:
  1M LiFSI/DME: dom% = 68.5%, n_sig = 2, SCE ≈ 0.78 (3-bucket)

Framework dataset reports:
  LiFSI/DME 1.0M: SCE = 1.2396, dom% = 44%, n_sig = 3
```

The difference in dom% (68.5% vs 44%) reflects the three-bucket vs 
full-config representation difference. Holoubek reports 3-0-0 and 
2-1-0 as the only two significant microstates — but within the 
3-0-0 bucket there are multiple distinct (n_DME, 0_FSI) configs 
that the framework's full population analysis separates. This is 
exactly the within-category entropy correction identified in 
Query 2 analysis.

The framework's SCE = 1.2396 for DME is consistent with Holoubek's 
data once the within-category diversity is restored.
