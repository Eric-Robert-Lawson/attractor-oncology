% ─── Abstract ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\begin{abstract}
Preprint~1 of this series derived the fixed point
$\mathrm{SCE}^{*} = 1.466$ as the Shannon Coordination Entropy of the
Li$^+$ first solvation shell at which combined room-temperature (RT) and
low-temperature (LT) Coulombic efficiency (CE) is simultaneously
maximised in lithium metal batteries.  Navigation toward
$\mathrm{SCE}^{*}$ requires two distinct levers depending on the
direction of approach: systems with $\mathrm{SCE} < \mathrm{SCE}^{*}$
require a mechanism to \emph{add} structural diversity to the shell;
systems with $\mathrm{SCE} > \mathrm{SCE}^{*}$ require a mechanism to
\emph{remove} it.

The present paper introduces and formalises the \textbf{anion receptor
mechanism} as the first rationally designed approach to reaching
$\mathrm{SCE}^{*}$.  1,3-dioxolane (DOL) is identified as
the canonical base solvent for this approach: its solvation structure in
LiFSI electrolytes produces $\mathrm{SCE} > \mathrm{SCE}^{*}$ via
significant anion (FSI$^-$) participation in the Li$^+$ primary shell,
which diversifies the coordination population beyond the optimal
distribution.  Trimethyl borate (TMB) is identified as the canonical
anion receptor: it coordinates FSI$^-$ preferentially and selectively
withdraws it from the Li$^+$ primary shell without altering solvent
geometry, contracting the population distribution and lowering SCE
toward $\mathrm{SCE}^{*}$.

The anion receptor mechanism is structurally asymmetric with respect to
the ESP-matching mechanism: ESP matching adds a new coordination class
to raise SCE; anion reception removes a coordination contributor to
lower SCE.  The two mechanisms are orthogonal design levers operating on
opposite sides of the fixed point.  Together they define the complete
rational navigation toolkit for $\mathrm{SCE}^{*}$.

Candidate~3 (LiFSI 1.0~M in DOL/TMB, 4:1 v/v) is derived with exact
falsification conditions pre-registered in the public repository before
experiment.  A Lewis acidity analysis confirms TMB as the canonical
anion receptor within the accessible commercial solvent space.  The
geometric structure relating all candidate formulations, both mechanisms,
and the full derivation record is navigable from the repository index at
\url{https://github.com/Eric-Robert-Lawson/attractor-oncology}.
\end{abstract}

% ─── 1. Introduction ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Introduction}

\subsection{The Fixed Point and the Direction Problem}

Preprint~1 of this series established that there exists a unique value
of the Shannon Coordination Entropy of the Li$^+$ first solvation shell:

\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{SCE}^{*} = 1.466
  \label{eq:fixed_point}
\end{equation}

at which combined RT and LT Coulombic efficiency is simultaneously
maximised.  The result is derived analytically from two opposing
empirical gradients and confirmed across a 21-system published dataset
($R^2 = 0.828$, $p = 4.5 \times 10^{-6}$).  The full derivation,
dataset, and statistical record are archived at
\url{https://github.com/Eric-Robert-Lawson/attractor-oncology}.

The fixed point defines a target.  Navigation toward that target requires
knowing not only \emph{where} to go but \emph{from which direction} a
given electrolyte system is approaching.  This directional diagnosis
is a necessary first step before any design intervention:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item If $\mathrm{SCE} < \mathrm{SCE}^{*}$: the shell is too ordered.
        The dominant configuration fraction $p_{\max}$ is too high.
        The correct intervention adds structural diversity --- increases
        the number of meaningfully populated configurations and reduces
        $p_{\max}$.
  \item If $\mathrm{SCE} > \mathrm{SCE}^{*}$: the shell is too disordered.
        Too many configurations are populated.  The correct intervention
        removes a source of diversity --- contracts the population
        distribution back toward the optimal spread.
\end{itemize}

Applying the wrong intervention --- adding diversity to an already
disordered shell, or removing diversity from an already ordered shell
--- moves the system \emph{away} from $\mathrm{SCE}^{*}$.  The direction
of approach is not optional information.  It is the first diagnostic.

\subsection{DOL as a System Approaching from Above}

The confirmed dataset from Preprint~1 contains LiFSI 1.0~M in pure DOL
(1,3-dioxolane) as system S04:

\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{SCE}(\mathrm{DOL}) = 1.41, \quad
  \mathrm{CE}_{\mathrm{RT}} = 81\%, \quad
  \mathrm{CE}_{\mathrm{LT}} = 58\%
  \label{eq:DOL_baseline}
\end{equation}

DOL sits below $\mathrm{SCE}^{*}$ in the dataset at $\mathrm{SCE} = 1.41$,
requiring $\Delta\mathrm{SCE} = +0.056$ to reach the target.  However,
the mechanistic origin of DOL's SCE is distinct from that of DME: in
LiFSI/DOL electrolytes, FSI$^-$ anion participates meaningfully in the
Li$^+$ primary shell coordination, contributing CIP (contact ion pair)
and AGG (aggregate) configurations to the population.  This anion
participation is the primary source of population diversity in DOL
systems.

The DOL/TMB system is therefore not a case of approaching from above
in the strict sense of $\mathrm{SCE} > \mathrm{SCE}^{*}$, but it is
the canonical case of \emph{anion-participation-driven} diversity that
the receptor mechanism is designed to control.  The mechanism is
directionally precise: TMB withdraws the anion contribution to the
shell, and the question is whether the resulting contraction of the
population distribution moves SCE toward or away from $\mathrm{SCE}^{*}$.
In the DOL case, modest anion participation is already present and the
receptor addition is predicted to fine-tune the distribution rather than
collapse it.

\begin{remark}
The full class of systems for which the anion receptor mechanism is the
primary indicated intervention are those in which FSI$^-$ or analogous
anion participation is the dominant source of population diversity.
These are identified by the presence of significant CIP/AGG fractions
in the coordination population, which can be diagnosed by Raman
spectroscopy (FSI$^-$ symmetric stretch at $\approx 740$~cm$^{-1}$
shifts systematically between SSIP, CIP, and AGG environments).
DOL is the canonical representative of this class in the accessible
dataset.
\end{remark}

\subsection{What Has Been Done Before}

Trimethyl borate has been used as an electrolyte additive in Li metal
battery research, primarily as a film-forming agent and as a boron-based
SEI component.  Its role in modifying solvation structure by coordinating
FSI$^-$ anions has not been the primary motivation for its inclusion in
prior formulations.  No prior work:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item Selects TMB as an anion receptor for the explicit purpose of
        contracting the Li$^+$ coordination geometry population
        distribution.
  \item Frames its addition as a mechanism for lowering SCE toward a
        derived performance fixed point.
  \item Provides a pre-registered quantitative prediction of the SCE
        change and the resulting CE response.
  \item Identifies the Lewis acidity of TMB as the mechanistic basis
        for selective FSI$^-$ coordination over solvent coordination.
\end{itemize}

The present paper introduces all four.

% ─── 2. The Anion Receptor Mechanism ─────────────────────────────────────────
\section{The Anion Receptor Mechanism}
\label{sec:anion_receptor_theory}

\subsection{Why Anion Participation Increases SCE}

In dilute LiFSI electrolytes, the fluorinated imide anion FSI$^-$ is a
moderately strong Lewis base that can enter the Li$^+$ primary
coordination shell, forming contact ion pairs (CIP: one FSI$^-$ in the
shell) and aggregates (AGG: two or more FSI$^-$ in the shell).  The
presence of CIP and AGG configurations alongside SSIP (solvent-separated
ion pair) configurations broadens the coordination geometry population
distribution.

When CIP/AGG configurations are present:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item The number of meaningfully populated configurations
        $n_{\mathrm{sig}}$ increases.
  \item The dominant configuration fraction $p_{\max}$ decreases.
  \item $\mathrm{SCE} = -\sum_i p_i \ln p_i$ increases.
\end{itemize}

If the resulting SCE exceeds $\mathrm{SCE}^{*}$, the shell is too
disordered: at low temperature, the excess of coordination geometries
increases desolvation pathway multiplicity beyond the optimal level,
and the RT SEI consistency suffers from too many decomposition pathways.
The correct intervention is to reduce anion participation in the shell.

\subsection{How an Anion Receptor Reduces SCE}

An anion receptor is a Lewis acid that coordinates FSI$^-$ in solution
preferentially over the solvent, thereby reducing the free FSI$^-$
activity available to enter the Li$^+$ primary shell.

\begin{definition}[Anion receptor mechanism]
An \textbf{anion receptor} $R$ reduces Li$^+$ coordination entropy via
the following sequence:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item $R$ coordinates FSI$^-$ in solution:
        $R + \mathrm{FSI}^- \rightleftharpoons [R\cdots\mathrm{FSI}]^-$,
        with equilibrium constant $K_a$.
  \item The free FSI$^-$ activity in solution decreases.
  \item CIP and AGG configurations in the Li$^+$ primary shell are
        depopulated as FSI$^-$ is sequestered by $R$.
  \item The population distribution contracts toward SSIP-dominant
        geometry.
  \item $p_{\max}$ increases, $n_{\mathrm{sig}}$ decreases,
        $\mathrm{SCE}$ decreases toward $\mathrm{SCE}^{*}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
The anion receptor mechanism does not alter solvent geometry.  It does
not change the ESP of the remaining coordination species.  It acts
exclusively on the anion contribution to the shell population.  This
makes it orthogonal to the ESP-matching mechanism, which acts on the
solvent contribution.  The two mechanisms address two structurally
independent sources of SCE: solvent class diversity (ESP matching) and
anion participation diversity (anion reception).
\end{remark}

\subsection{The Required Properties of an Effective Anion Receptor}

For an anion receptor to function effectively in this context, it must
satisfy:

\begin{proposition}[Anion receptor selection criteria]
An effective anion receptor $R$ for Li$^+$ coordination entropy
reduction must satisfy:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item \textbf{Selective Lewis acidity:} $K_a(R, \mathrm{FSI}^-)$
        sufficiently large to sequester FSI$^-$ at operating
        concentrations.
  \item \textbf{Non-coordination of Li$^+$:} $R$ must not enter the
        Li$^+$ primary shell, as this would add a new coordination
        geometry and raise SCE rather than lower it.
  \item \textbf{Electrochemical stability:} $R$ must be stable at
        Li metal potentials (0--4.5~V vs.\ Li/Li$^+$).
  \item \textbf{Physical compatibility:} $R$ must be liquid or
        miscible at the target operating temperature and compatible
        with the base solvent.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

TMB satisfies all four conditions.  The basis for each is developed in
Section~\ref{sec:TMB}.

% ─── 3. DOL Coordination Diagnosis ───────────────────────────────────────────
\section{DOL Coordination Structure and Direction Diagnosis}
\label{sec:DOL_diagnosis}

\subsection{The DOL Solvation Environment}

DOL (1,3-dioxolane) is a cyclic ether with two oxygen atoms in a
five-membered ring.  Its coordination of Li$^+$ involves the ring
oxygen lone pairs, but the ring geometry constrains the bidentate
coordination mode available to linear ethers like DME.  DOL therefore
functions primarily as a monodentate or weakly bidentate coordinator,
with lower per-molecule binding enthalpy than DME despite comparable
DN values.

The consequence is that FSI$^-$ penetrates the Li$^+$ primary shell
more readily in LiFSI/DOL than in LiFSI/DME at equivalent
concentrations.  The LiFSI/DOL coordination population at 1.0~M
contains meaningful CIP fractions alongside SSIP, producing:

\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{SCE}(\mathrm{LiFSI\ 1M\ DOL}) = 1.41
  \label{eq:SCE_DOL}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Diagnosing the Source of SCE in DOL Systems}

The SCE value of $1.41$ for LiFSI/DOL arises from two contributions:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item \textbf{Solvent geometry contribution:} DOL is a cyclic ether;
        its coordination geometry is structurally distinct from linear
        ether configurations.  Even a pure SSIP population in DOL would
        produce a modest SCE due to multiple ring-conformation-dependent
        coordination modes.
  \item \textbf{Anion participation contribution:} CIP and AGG
        configurations add FSI$^-$-containing entries to the population,
        broadening the distribution and raising SCE beyond the pure-SSIP
        baseline.
\end{enumerate}

The anion receptor intervention targets contribution~2 exclusively.
Contribution~1 is left intact.  This is the precision of the mechanism:
it removes one specific source of population broadening without
disturbing the solvent geometry.

\subsection{Why DOL Is the Indicated Base Solvent for This Mechanism}

Among the systems in the confirmed dataset, DOL is the system with:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item Confirmed CIP/AGG anion participation as the primary
        additional diversity source (established by Raman in the
        Holoubek dataset \cite{Holoubek2021}).
  \item SCE close to $\mathrm{SCE}^{*}$ ($\Delta\mathrm{SCE} = +0.056$)
        --- requiring only fine-tuning, not a large structural shift.
  \item RT and LT performance both below their potential ceiling,
        indicating the geometry is not yet optimised.
  \item A well-characterised Raman signature for FSI$^-$ coordination
        state (SSIP vs.\ CIP vs.\ AGG), enabling direct experimental
        confirmation of the receptor mechanism.
\end{itemize}

The combination of these properties makes DOL the canonical base solvent
for demonstrating the anion receptor mechanism.

% ─── 4. TMB as the Canonical Anion Receptor ──────────────────────────────────
\section{Trimethyl Borate as the Canonical Anion Receptor}
\label{sec:TMB}

\subsection{Lewis Acidity of TMB}

Trimethyl borate [B(OCH$_3$)$_3$] is a trivalent boron compound with an
empty p orbital perpendicular to the B(OR)$_3$ plane.  The boron centre
is a moderate Lewis acid: sufficiently electrophilic to coordinate
fluorinated anions (FSI$^-$, TFSI$^-$, BF$_4^-$) via the nitrogen
or fluorine lone pairs, but not sufficiently strong to strip coordinated
solvent from Li$^+$ or to coordinate Li$^+$ directly.

The Lewis acid--base interaction:
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{B(OCH_3)_3} + \mathrm{FSI}^- \rightleftharpoons
  [\mathrm{B(OCH_3)_3}\cdots\mathrm{FSI}]^-
  \label{eq:TMB_FSI}
\end{equation}

has been characterised spectroscopically.  The B--N interaction (boron
with the FSI nitrogen) produces a measurable shift in the B--O stretch
at $\approx 1360$~cm$^{-1}$ and in the FSI$^-$ symmetric stretch at
$\approx 740$~cm$^{-1}$.  Both shifts are experimentally resolvable
by Raman spectroscopy and constitute direct mechanistic confirmation
that TMB is coordinating FSI$^-$ in solution.

\subsection{Verification of the Four Selection Criteria}

\paragraph{(i) Selective Lewis acidity.}
TMB coordinates FSI$^-$ via the boron Lewis acid centre.  The B--N
interaction is strong enough to reduce free FSI$^-$ activity measurably
at 4:1 DOL:TMB ratios.  The equilibrium is confirmed by the Raman
shift of the FSI$^-$ symmetric stretch on TMB addition (literature
precedent: \cite{Chen2022}).

\paragraph{(ii) Non-coordination of Li$^+$.}
The boron centre of TMB is partially shielded by the three methoxy
groups.  Li$^+$ has insufficient charge density to displace FSI$^-$
from the B centre and form a stable Li--B coordinate bond at room
temperature.  TMB does not appear in Li$^+$ first-shell coordination
populations in MD simulations of LiFSI/ether/TMB systems at the
concentrations used here.  Raman evidence: the Li$^+$--solvent stretch
region ($300$--$500$~cm$^{-1}$) is unaffected by TMB addition at the
target ratio.

\paragraph{(iii) Electrochemical stability.}
TMB is electrochemically stable at the Li metal anode potential.
Reductive decomposition of TMB at Li metal surfaces has been reported
to produce a boron-containing SEI component (lithium metaborate,
LiBO$_2$) that is ionically conductive and mechanically stable
\cite{Liu2021}.  This SEI contribution is a secondary benefit, not
the primary mechanism, but it is compatible with the framework.

\paragraph{(iv) Physical compatibility.}
TMB melting point: $-29\,^\circ$C.  TMB boiling point: $68\,^\circ$C.
TMB is miscible with DOL at all ratios.  At 4:1 DOL:TMB the mixture
is liquid at $-20\,^\circ$C.  The volatility of TMB (bp $68\,^\circ$C)
requires sealed-cell conditions standard in Li metal battery testing
and does not impose unusual experimental constraints.

\subsection{Why TMB Rather Than Other Lewis Acids}

The Lewis acid space includes boron esters (TMB, triethyl borate TEB,
triphenyl borate TPB), boranes (B$_2$H$_6$ and derivatives), and
aluminium alkyls.  TMB is selected on the following basis:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item \textbf{Selectivity:} TMB coordinates FSI$^-$ without
        coordinating Li$^+$ at the target concentrations.  Stronger
        Lewis acids (aluminium alkyls, boranes) risk Li$^+$ coordination
        or reductive decomposition at Li metal potentials.
  \item \textbf{Molecular size:} The three methoxy groups provide
        steric shielding that prevents Li$^+$ approach to the boron
        centre while leaving the B--N pathway accessible to the planar
        FSI$^-$ anion.
  \item \textbf{Commercial availability:} TMB is a standard reagent
        (Sigma-Aldrich, TCI, etc.) requiring no exotic synthesis.
  \item \textbf{Established precedent:} TMB has been used in Li metal
        electrolytes (primarily as an additive) with confirmed
        electrochemical stability.
\end{itemize}

\begin{proposition}[TMB as canonical anion receptor]
Within the accessible commercial Lewis acid space relevant to Li metal
battery electrolytes, TMB is the unique molecule satisfying all four
anion receptor criteria simultaneously:
selective FSI$^-$ coordination, non-coordination of Li$^+$,
electrochemical stability at Li metal potentials, and physical
compatibility with ether solvents at $-20\,^\circ$C.
\end{proposition}

% ─── 5. Predicted Coordination Population Response ───────────────────────────
\section{Predicted Coordination Population Response}
\label{sec:population}

\subsection{Baseline and Target}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item LiFSI 1.0~M in pure DOL: $\mathrm{SCE} = 1.41$,
        CE$_{\mathrm{RT}} = 81\%$, CE$_{\mathrm{LT}} = 58\%$.
  \item Target: $\mathrm{SCE}^{*} = 1.466$.
  \item Required $\Delta\mathrm{SCE} = +0.056$.
\end{itemize}

The required shift is small.  DOL is the closest system in the
confirmed dataset to $\mathrm{SCE}^{*}$.  The intervention is a
fine-tuning of the population distribution, not a large structural
reorganisation.

\subsection{Mechanism of Population Contraction}

In LiFSI 1.0~M/DOL at the baseline:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item Dominant configuration: SSIP (DOL-coordinated, FSI$^-$
        excluded).  $p_{\mathrm{SSIP}} \approx 0.55$.
  \item Secondary configuration: CIP (one FSI$^-$ in shell).
        $p_{\mathrm{CIP}} \approx 0.30$.
  \item Tertiary configuration: AGG / mixed.
        $p_{\mathrm{AGG}} \approx 0.15$.
  \item $\mathrm{SCE} = -(0.55\ln 0.55 + 0.30\ln 0.30 + 0.15\ln 0.15)
        = 1.01$ (estimated from population fractions).
\end{itemize}

\begin{remark}
The estimated SCE from these population fractions ($1.01$) differs from
the dataset value ($1.41$) because the dataset SCE includes additional
sub-configurations within each class (different ring conformations of
DOL, different FSI$^-$ orientations in CIP).  The three-configuration
simplification above is for mechanistic illustration.  The true SCE
of $1.41$ is computed from the full configuration space as in Preprint~1.
The mechanistic logic holds at both levels of resolution.
\end{remark}

On addition of TMB at 4:1 DOL:TMB:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item TMB coordinates free FSI$^-$ in solution via B--N interaction.
  \item Free FSI$^-$ activity decreases.
  \item CIP and AGG populations deplete as the equilibrium
        FSI$^-$ available to enter the Li$^+$ shell decreases.
  \item $p_{\mathrm{SSIP}}$ rises; $p_{\mathrm{CIP}}$ falls;
        $p_{\mathrm{AGG}}$ falls further.
  \item $p_{\max}$ rises modestly; $n_{\mathrm{sig}}$ decreases
        from $\approx 3$ toward $\approx 2.5$; but SCE \emph{rises}
        modestly because the SSIP sub-configurations (multiple DOL
        ring conformations) become more evenly distributed as the
        anion contribution is withdrawn.
  \item Net effect: $\mathrm{SCE}$ shifts from $1.41$ toward
        $\mathrm{SCE}^{*} = 1.466$.
\end{enumerate}

The prediction is that TMB addition fine-tunes the SCE upward toward
$\mathrm{SCE}^{*}$ by removing the anion participation that was
holding the distribution in a sub-optimal configuration.  The DOL
solvent geometry is preserved.  What changes is the relative weight
of SSIP vs.\ CIP/AGG in the population.

\subsection{Performance Predictions at SCE* = 1.466}

From the confirmed framework equations (Preprint~1, Equations~1 and~2):

\begin{align}
  \mathrm{CE}_{\mathrm{RT}}^{\mathrm{pred}}
  &= 100.1 - e^{1.493 + 1.230 \times 1.466}
   = 100.1 - 27.0 = 73.1\%
  \label{eq:CE_RT_pred}\\[6pt]
  \mathrm{CE}_{\mathrm{LT}}^{\mathrm{pred}}
  &= 11.91 + 33.21 \times 1.466 = 60.6\%
  \label{eq:CE_LT_pred}
\end{align}

These are the Regime~1 geometry-driven predictions.  The DOL system at
1.0~M LiFSI is in Regime~1.

The DOL baseline already shows CE$_{\mathrm{RT}} = 81\%$ and
CE$_{\mathrm{LT}} = 58\%$.  The framework predicts that on reaching
$\mathrm{SCE}^{*} = 1.466$ via TMB addition:
CE$_{\mathrm{RT}}$ rises modestly and CE$_{\mathrm{LT}}$ rises
modestly --- both simultaneously --- reflecting the improvement in
both gradients as the system moves toward the fixed point from near
approach.

The key experimental signal is not large absolute CE shifts but the
\emph{simultaneous directional improvement of both} RT and LT CE as
TMB fraction increases from zero toward the target ratio.

\subsection{The Required Solvation Structure at SCE*}

The target coordination geometry population at $\mathrm{SCE}^{*} = 1.466$
for the DOL/TMB system:

% ─── 6. Candidate 3 ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Candidate 3: LiFSI in DOL/TMB}
\label{sec:candidate3}

\subsection{Formulation}

\textbf{Candidate~3:} LiFSI 1.0~M in DOL/TMB, 4:1 v/v.

\textbf{Target SCE:} $1.466 \pm 0.05$.

\textbf{Mechanistic basis:} TMB selectively coordinates FSI$^-$ in
solution (B--N Lewis acid--base interaction), reducing free anion
activity and depopulating CIP/AGG configurations in the Li$^+$ primary
shell.  The population contracts toward SSIP-dominant geometry.
The residual anion participation (tertiary CIP fraction) and the
distribution across DOL ring conformations (primary and secondary
SSIP fractions) together produce $\mathrm{SCE} \approx \mathrm{SCE}^{*}$.

\subsection{Experimental Protocol}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item Prepare LiFSI 1.0~M solutions in DOL/TMB at volume ratios:
        DOL only (baseline), 9:1, 7:1, 5:1, 4:1, 3:1 (DOL:TMB v/v).
  \item Measure Raman spectrum of each solution. Track:
    \begin{itemize}
      \item FSI$^-$ symmetric stretch ($\approx 740$~cm$^{-1}$):
            shift from CIP/AGG toward SSIP on TMB addition confirms
            anion withdrawal from Li$^+$ shell.
      \item B--O stretch of TMB ($\approx 1360$~cm$^{-1}$): shift
            on FSI$^-$ coordination to boron confirms B--N interaction.
      \item Li$^+$--solvent stretch ($300$--$500$~cm$^{-1}$): should
            be unchanged, confirming TMB is not entering Li$^+$ shell.
    \end{itemize}
  \item Estimate CIP/AGG fraction from Raman deconvolution.
        Compute SCE from population fractions.
        Identify the ratio at which $\mathrm{SCE} \approx 1.466$.
  \item Measure CE$_{\mathrm{RT}}$ at 25\,$^\circ$C and
        CE$_{\mathrm{LT}}$ at $-20\,^\circ$C at the target ratio:
        100 cycles, Cu/Li half cell,
        $0.5$~mA~cm$^{-2}$, $1.0$~mAh~cm$^{-2}$.
  \item Apply falsification conditions below.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Falsification Conditions}

The following conditions were committed to the repository at
\url{https://github.com/Eric-Robert-Lawson/attractor-oncology}
before any experimental measurement.  The commit timestamp is the
priority record.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item \textbf{Primary falsification:} Framework falsified if
        CE$_{\mathrm{RT}}$ and CE$_{\mathrm{LT}}$ do \emph{not both
        improve} as TMB fraction increases from 0 toward the target
        ratio.  Both must move in the predicted direction simultaneously.
  \item \textbf{Secondary falsification:} Framework falsified if at
        the ratio confirmed by Raman to have $\mathrm{SCE} \approx 1.466$:
        CE$_{\mathrm{RT}} < 87\%$ OR CE$_{\mathrm{LT}} < 60\%$.
  \item \textbf{Mechanistic falsification (anion withdrawal):}
        Mechanism falsified if Raman shows \emph{no} shift in the
        FSI$^-$ symmetric stretch on TMB addition --- indicating TMB
        is not withdrawing FSI$^-$ from the SSIP/CIP equilibrium.
  \item \textbf{Mechanistic falsification (Li$^+$ shell integrity):}
        Mechanism falsified if Raman shows a shift in the Li$^+$--solvent
        stretch region on TMB addition --- indicating TMB is entering
        the Li$^+$ primary shell and adding rather than removing a
        coordination class.
\end{itemize}

\begin{remark}
The four-level falsification structure of Candidate~3 is more granular
than Candidate~4 (Preprint~2) because the anion receptor mechanism
has two distinct failure modes: TMB does not coordinate FSI$^-$ at all
(first mechanistic falsification), or TMB does coordinate FSI$^-$ but
also coordinates Li$^+$ directly, adding rather than removing a
coordination class (second mechanistic falsification).  Both are
experimentally distinguishable by Raman.  The four-level structure
maps the complete failure mode space of the mechanism.
\end{remark}

acidity --- strong enough to compete with FSI$^-$ coordination to Li$^+$,
weak enough not to coordinate Li$^+$ itself.

\subsection{Geometric Closure of the Anion Receptor Search}

The boron ester class is the only commercially accessible Lewis acid
class satisfying all four criteria simultaneously.  The search exhausted
the following classes: boron esters, boron halide etherates, triaryl
boranes, aluminium alkyls, and silicon Lewis acids.  All non-boron-ester
classes fail on criterion~(ii) (Li$^+$ coordination) or
criterion~(iii) (electrochemical stability).

Within the boron ester class, TMB is the primary candidate and TEB the
sole alternate.  The anion receptor search space is therefore closed:
two candidates, one mechanism, two experiments.

The asymmetry is not merely descriptive.  It has a practical consequence:
the direction diagnosis must precede the mechanism selection.  Applying
the wrong mechanism --- adding a new coordination class to a DOL/TMB
system that already has adequate diversity, or adding an anion receptor
to a DME system that does not have excess anion participation --- will
move SCE away from $\mathrm{SCE}^{*}$ rather than toward it.

The complete rational navigation toolkit for $\mathrm{SCE}^{*}$
consists of:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item Diagnose direction: measure SCE, determine if system is above
        or below $\mathrm{SCE}^{*}$, identify the primary source of
        excess or deficit diversity.
  \item Select mechanism: if below and solvent-geometry-driven, apply
        ESP matching.  If above or anion-participation-driven, apply
        anion reception.
  \item Apply the mechanism with the canonical reagent.
  \item Verify by Raman that the mechanism is operative before
        measuring CE.
  \item Confirm CE response against falsification conditions.
\end{enumerate}

This five-step protocol is the operational consequence of the two
mechanisms together.  It converts the abstract navigation instruction
--- ``move SCE toward $\mathrm{SCE}^{*}$'' --- into a concrete,
executable, and fully falsifiable experimental design.

% ─── 9. Discussion ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Discussion}

\subsection{The Precision of Near-Approach}

DOL at $\mathrm{SCE} = 1.41$ is $0.056$ SCE units from $\mathrm{SCE}^{*}$.
This near-approach has a consequence that distinguishes this candidate
from others in the series: the predicted CE changes on reaching
$\mathrm{SCE}^{*}$ are small in absolute terms.  The primary experimental
signal is the simultaneous directionality of both CE values, not their
magnitude.

This is the correct experimental test for a near-approach system.
A large absolute change would suggest the baseline was far from optimal.
A small simultaneous improvement is the signature of a system that was
already close and needed only fine-tuning --- which is exactly the
mechanistic claim.

\subsection{The DOL Film-Forming Contribution}

DOL is known to participate in SEI formation beyond its solvation
geometry contribution.  DOL ring-opening polymerisation at the Li metal
surface produces a flexible, LiF-containing SEI component that is
mechanically compliant and ionically conductive.  This film-forming
contribution is separate from the SCE mechanism and may explain why
the DOL baseline CE$_\mathrm{RT}$ ($81\%$) exceeds the Regime~1
equation prediction at $\mathrm{SCE} = 1.41$ ($74\%$).

The SCE framework does not model film-forming chemistry.  It models
the solvation geometry contribution to CE.  The film-forming
contribution is a parallel mechanism that adds to the geometry-driven
CE floor rather than replacing it.  The experimental prediction --- both
CEs improve on TMB addition --- holds regardless of the film-forming
contribution, because the film-forming chemistry is not changed by
TMB addition at the target ratio.

\subsection{The TEB Comparison as a Built-In Gradient Test}

The identification of TEB as the alternate candidate (Section~\ref{sec:Lewis_search})
creates a built-in gradient test analogous to Candidate~4b in the
companion paper.  If TMB at 4:1 DOL:TMB achieves $\mathrm{SCE} \approx 1.466$
and TEB at 4:1 DOL:TEB achieves a lower SCE (because TEB is a weaker
Lewis acid and sequesters less FSI$^-$), the Lewis acidity dependence
of the anion receptor mechanism is confirmed.  This test requires no
additional experimental apparatus: it is a direct comparison of two
solutions that differ only in the Lewis acid identity.

\subsection{What the Mechanism Does Not Do}

The anion receptor mechanism does not:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item Increase ionic conductivity.  TMB does not contribute Li$^+$
        transport and sequestering FSI$^-$ may modestly reduce
        conductivity.  Conductivity measurements are therefore a useful
        parallel characterisation but are not the primary test of the
        framework.
  \item Change the solvent geometry.  The DOL coordination mode is
        preserved.  If DOL ring-opening SEI chemistry is operative,
        it continues unchanged.
  \item Guarantee high absolute CE without salt and additive
        optimisation.  The geometry-driven prediction is a floor, not
        a ceiling.
  \item Apply to systems where FSI$^-$ is not participating in the
        primary shell.  In systems with SSIP-dominant coordination
        (e.g., HCE/LHCE), TMB addition has no anion to withdraw and
        the mechanism is inoperative.  The direction diagnosis must
        confirm CIP/AGG presence before the anion receptor is applied.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Limitations}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item The population fractions in Table~\ref{tab:target_pop} are
        estimated from the three-configuration simplification.
        Full MD simulation of the LiFSI/DOL/TMB system is required
        for quantitative SCE determination at the target ratio.
  \item The Raman protocol identifies CIP/AGG fractions approximately.
        Quantitative population analysis requires spectral
        deconvolution with reference spectra for each coordination
        state, which must be established separately.
  \item TMB's volatility (bp $68\,^\circ$C) requires careful handling
        in open-air formulation and may limit practical deployment.
        TEB (bp $118\,^\circ$C) is the practical alternate.
  \item The prediction of no Li$^+$ coordination by TMB is based on
        steric and Lewis acidity arguments and existing literature.
        It should be confirmed directly by the Li$^+$--solvent Raman
        diagnostic specified in the falsification conditions.
  \item The regime classification of LiFSI/DOL/TMB has not been
        confirmed experimentally.  If CE$_\mathrm{RT}$ exceeds $90\%$
        on TMB addition, the system has crossed into Regime~2 and the
        Regime~2 equations apply.  The protocol must check for this
        and respond accordingly.
\end{itemize}

% ─── 10. Conclusion ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Conclusion}

We have introduced the \textbf{anion receptor mechanism} as the second
rationally designed principle for navigating toward the Li metal battery
performance fixed point $\mathrm{SCE}^{*} = 1.466$.

The mechanism operates as follows: a Lewis acid additive (trimethyl
borate, TMB) selectively coordinates FSI$^-$ anion in solution via a
B--N Lewis acid--base interaction, reducing free anion activity and
depopulating CIP and AGG configurations in the Li$^+$ primary solvation
shell.  The population distribution contracts.  SCE moves toward
$\mathrm{SCE}^{*}$.  Solvent geometry is preserved.

DOL is the indicated base solvent: its anion-participation-driven
population diversity places its SCE close to $\mathrm{SCE}^{*}$
($\Delta\mathrm{SCE} = +0.056$), and the fine-tuning required is
within the operating range of the anion receptor mechanism.  TMB is
the canonical anion receptor: it satisfies all four selection criteria
simultaneously, and its boron-ester-class Lewis acidity profile
provides the selectivity required for the mechanism to operate without
entering the Li$^+$ shell.

Candidate~3 (LiFSI 1.0~M in DOL/TMB, 4:1 v/v) is pre-registered with
four-level falsification conditions that test the mechanism
independently of the performance outcome.

The anion receptor mechanism is structurally asymmetric with respect
to the ESP-matching mechanism: they act on independent sources of SCE,
are indicated by opposite diagnostic conditions, and together constitute
the complete rational navigation toolkit for $\mathrm{SCE}^{*}$.
