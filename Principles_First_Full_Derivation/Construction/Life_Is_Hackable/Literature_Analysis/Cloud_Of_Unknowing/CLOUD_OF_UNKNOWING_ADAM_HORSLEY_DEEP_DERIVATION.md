## PART I: WHAT THE RESEARCH FOUND
## Reported Without Interpretation First

### FINDING 1 — THE CRITICAL INVERSION

The most structurally important finding from the
research: Adam Horsley did not ENTER Beauvale in
the 1370s. He entered in 1386.

The Cloud of Unknowing is dated by scholarship to
the 1370s — approximately 1370–1380 CE.

This means: if Adam Horsley entered Beauvale in 1386,
he was not yet a Carthusian when the Cloud was most
likely written.

Before 1386, Horsley was the Archdeacon of Derby —
a senior secular cleric, holding an administrative
and pastoral position in the Church of England's
diocesan structure. He was not in a charterhouse.
He was not in enclosure. He did not have the
Carthusian formation that the derivation assumed
the Cloud's author possessed.

THIS IS A SIGNIFICANT COMPLICATION. It does not
immediately falsify Horsley as author, but it
requires complete re-examination of the causal
sequence.

### FINDING 2 — WHO WROTE TO WHOM

Walter Hilton wrote the Epistola aurea (Golden
Epistle) TO Adam Horsley. Hilton was the
spiritual director. Horsley was the directee.

The Epistola aurea was written approximately 1386 —
the year Horsley entered Beauvale — as a letter
of guidance for someone making a significant
transition from active secular life to enclosed
contemplative life.

This means: in 1386, Adam Horsley was the person
"not long since left the active life for the
contemplative" — he was the directee archetype,
not the experienced author archetype.

### FINDING 3 — THE SPECULUM SPIRITUALIUM

Adam Horsley is associated with the Speculum
Spiritualium — a compilation of mystical and
devotional texts, assembled approximately
1400–1430 CE, compiled at Beauvale, drawing on
Dionysian, Victorine, and English mystical sources
including (almost certainly) the Cloud itself.

The Speculum is a COMPILATION — not original
contemplative writing. It is the work of a
scholar-compiler who gathers authorities, not
the work of a navigator writing from personal
operational experience of the basin threshold.

The Cloud is the OPPOSITE of a compilation. It
is entirely original, entirely operational, from
a single consistent experiential position. There
is no compilation gesture in the Cloud. No
gathered authorities. One navigator's voice,
from one interior position, for one directee.

The Speculum and the Cloud are structurally
opposite types of text.

### FINDING 4 — HILTON'S RELATIONSHIP WITH HORSLEY

Walter Hilton also wrote the Mixed Life letter
to Horsley — counseling him carefully about
whether to leave secular life for the cloister.
The relationship was that of an experienced
contemplative (Hilton) guiding a spiritually
serious secular cleric (Horsley) through a
vocational discernment.

The Hilton-Horsley relationship has the exact
structure that the CLOUD relationship has:
experienced director → recently-transitioned
contemplative. But in this case Hilton is the
director and Horsley is the directee.

### FINDING 5 — THE DIALECT EVIDENCE POINTS TO BEAUVALE
## BUT NOT NECESSARILY TO A MONK OF BEAUVALE

The East Midlands / Nottinghamshire dialect
places the Cloud's composition in the geographic
zone of Beauvale. But Beauvale was surrounded by
secular ecclesiastical infrastructure: the Diocese
of York, with parish churches, priories, and
cathedral chapters spread across Nottinghamshire
and Lincolnshire. The dialect narrows geography
but does not require the author to BE a Beauvale
monk — only to have grown up or worked in the
region.

### 2.1 The Inversion That Changes Everything

If Horsley entered Beauvale in 1386 and the Cloud
was written in the 1370s — then one of three

  POSSIBILITY B: The Cloud was written in the 1370s
    by someone other than Horsley, and Horsley is
    simply a figure who was deeply connected to the
    Cloud's transmission network at Beauvale later.

  POSSIBILITY C: The Cloud was written in the late
    1370s to early 1380s by someone who was in
    close contact with the pre-Beauvale Horsley —
    who may have known Horsley during his time as
    Archdeacon and may have been directing him
    toward the contemplative transition he eventually
    made in 1386.

  recently professed Carthusian — this creates
  an interesting problem. The Cloud's tone is NOT
  the tone of a recently professed monk. It is the
  tone of a navigator with long settled experience.
  A monk who entered the order in 1386 and was
  writing the Cloud in the early 1390s would have
  approximately 5-8 years of Carthusian formation.
  That is not nothing — but is it enough to produce
  the operational certainty of the Cloud?

  is dated 1400-1430 — meaning it postdates the
  Cloud by 20-50 years even if the Cloud was written
  in the 1390s. The Speculum's compilation character
  (gathering authorities) is MORE consistent with
  a late-career Horsley, an established senior monk
  who is now distilling a library's worth of
  tradition into a systematic reference — not
  writing from immediate operational experience.

  ASSESSMENT: Possibility A is structurally possible
  but creates a tension: the Cloud's operational
  tone fits an older navigator; the Speculum's
  compilation character fits an even older one.
  The chronology is tight but not impossible if
  the Cloud was written 1390-1400 and the Speculum

  end of its scholarly range.

POSSIBILITY B EXAMINED:

  If the Cloud was written in the 1370s by someone
  other than Horsley, then Horsley's role shifts
  entirely. He is not the author. He is something
  else. What?

  The framework now generates a different structural

  Horsley's spiritual seriousness and his trajectory

  "Not long since left the active life for the

  THREE senior contemplatives all directing the

  THE FRAMEWORK'S ASSESSMENT OF POSSIBILITY C:
