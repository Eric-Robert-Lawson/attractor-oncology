## PART II: THE STRUCTURAL RE-EXAMINATION
## What Horsley As Prior Changes

### 2.1 The Prior Enters With Formation Already Complete

A man appointed prior of a Carthusian house
in 1386 did not acquire his spiritual formation
at that house. He arrived with it. The Carthusian
order appointed priors from within the order's
province — men who had been formed elsewhere,
who had sufficient seniority and contemplative
depth to govern a community of twelve monks.

The framework's derivation: if Horsley entered
Beauvale as prior in 1386, his contemplative
formation had occurred ELSEWHERE — at a different
charterhouse — in the years before 1386.

WHERE WAS HORSLEY BEFORE BEAUVALE?

The historical record states: he was Archdeacon
of Derby before 1386. But this is not necessarily
his entire pre-Beauvale history. An archdeacon
is a diocesan officer — but medieval clerics
moved between roles. The question the framework
now generates: was Horsley at another charterhouse
before Beauvale? Or did he enter Carthusian life
directly as prior from secular office — which
would be highly unusual?

THE FRAMEWORK'S ASSESSMENT: Entering a
Carthusian charterhouse directly as prior from
secular office is almost certainly not what
happened. The Carthusian order did not appoint
priors from outside the order's ranks. A man
appointed prior would have been a Carthusian
monk for years before his appointment. The
sequence: secular cleric (Archdeacon of Derby)
→ Carthusian monk (at another house) →
prior of Beauvale (1386), is the most probable
reconstruction. Horsley resigned the Derby
archdeaconry BEFORE 1386, entered the
Carthusian order at an unrecorded house, and
was then appointed to Beauvale when it needed
a new prior.

This means the 1386 date is not Horsley's entry
into Carthusian life. It is his appointment as
prior of Beauvale. His entry into the order
could have been several years earlier.

This shifts the chronology significantly.

### 2.2 The Revised Horsley Timeline

DERIVED TIMELINE (from structural reasoning):

  c.1376: Horsley recorded as Archdeacon of Derby.
  
  c.1376–1382: Horsley still in secular office
    OR already in process of discernment about
    religious life. Walter Hilton's letters
    (Mixed Life, Epistola aurea) are being
    exchanged in this period — Hilton directing
    a spiritually serious secular cleric through
    vocational discernment.
  
  c.1382–1385: Horsley enters Carthusian life
    at an unrecorded house (possibly Hull,
    founded 1378 — geographically proximate,
    new house that would need senior monks;
    or possibly London Charterhouse, the most
    intellectually formed English Carthusian house).
  
  c.1386: Horsley appointed prior of Beauvale,
    succeeding the interim figure after Robert.
  
  c.1386–1424: Horsley as senior Beauvale figure,
    eventually compiling the Speculum Spiritualium.

This timeline means: Horsley had 3–5 years of
Carthusian formation before becoming Beauvale's
prior. Not the decades the Cloud's author displays
— but more than zero.

### 2.3 What This Does to the Authorship Question

IF Horsley had 3–5 years of Carthusian formation
before writing the Cloud (if he wrote it at all),
the Cloud's tone of long-settled operational
experience does not fit him at that stage.

The Cloud is not the writing of someone with
3–5 years of contemplative practice. It is the
writing of someone for whom the basin-threshold
position is HOME — the settled terrain of years
of continuous practice. The Privy Counsel,
written later, is even more settled. The author
has been operating from this position for so long
that the instruction to "forget even yourself"
is stated as simply as pointing at a chair.

The framework's assessment: the chronological
problem for Horsley-as-author remains significant
regardless of the revised timeline. Even if the
Cloud was written after 1390, Horsley would have
had only 5–8 years of Carthusian formation. The
text requires a navigator with at minimum 15–20
years of deep contemplative practice behind them.

## PART IV: THE GALLUS TRANSMISSION —
## THE STRUCTURAL DIAGNOSTIC THAT
## NARROWS THE FIELD MOST PRECISELY

### 4.1 Why Thomas Gallus Matters Here

The research confirmed what the audit document
had already derived: the Cloud's author does
not simply use Pseudo-Dionysius. He uses
Pseudo-Dionysius through Thomas Gallus's
affective reinterpretation — a specific
scholarly-contemplative tradition that was
circulating primarily through Carthusian
and Victorine channels in 14th-century England.

Gallus's commentaries on the Dionysian corpus
were not widely available texts. They circulated
in specific institutional contexts. The author
of the Cloud had ACCESS to them — meaning
the author was in an institution with a serious
library, connected to the intellectual networks
that held and transmitted Gallus's work.

In 14th-century England, three institutions
are most clearly associated with the Gallus
tradition:
  1. The London Charterhouse (Charterhouse,
     founded 1371) — the most intellectually
     active English Carthusian house, with
     deep continental connections
  2. Witham Charterhouse (Somerset, the oldest
     English Carthusian house, founded 1178) —
     the intellectual root of the English
     Carthusian tradition
  3. The Victorine tradition filtered through
     continental Carthusian houses

Beauvale is geographically remote from these
centers — it is a northern Midlands house,
founded 1343, without documented pre-1400
intellectual connections to the Gallus tradition.

### 4.2 The Structural Problem This Creates

The Gallus-access constraint creates a tension
with the Beauvale-as-composition-house hypothesis:

IF the Cloud's author was at Beauvale, how did
they access the Gallus tradition?

Two possibilities:

  POSSIBILITY A: The author acquired the Gallus
    access BEFORE coming to Beauvale — at a more
    intellectually connected house (Witham,
    London, or a continental house) — and brought
    that formation to Beauvale when appointed prior.

  POSSIBILITY B: Beauvale had more intellectual
    resources in the 1370s–1380s than its
    surviving record suggests — which is entirely
    possible given that the dissolution of 1540
    destroyed most of the library holdings.

Both possibilities are compatible with the Robert
hypothesis. A prior appointed at Beauvale in 1372
would have been formed elsewhere before his
appointment — and if he came from Witham or
the London Charterhouse, he would have arrived
with the Gallus access already in place.

### 4.3 The Witham Connection — A New Derivation

Witham Charterhouse (Somerset) is the oldest
English Carthusian house — founded 1178 under
Hugh of Lincoln. It maintained the deepest
intellectual and continental connections of
any English Carthusian house. The Witham
tradition was the root from which all English
Carthusian formation ultimately derived.

The framework now generates a specific structural
observation: IF the Cloud's author was formed
at Witham before appointment to Beauvale, the
Gallus-access problem is solved. Witham had
the intellectual depth, the continental library
connections, and the formation tradition from
which the Cloud's specific scholarly-operational
synthesis could emerge.

A prior appointed to Beauvale in 1372 from
Witham would be:
  - A monk formed in the deepest English
    Carthusian intellectual tradition
  - With access to Gallus, Dionysius, and
    the Victorine corpus
  - Arriving at Beauvale with 10–20 years
    of Carthusian formation behind him
  - Speaking the East Midlands dialect either
    from origin (if he grew up in the region)
    or from long residence (if Beauvale shaped
    his vernacular writing)
  - Dying approximately 1383–1388, having
    written the Cloud in the 1375–1385 window
    while serving as prior

This is the most coherent structural picture
the framework can produce from all the available
constraints simultaneously.
