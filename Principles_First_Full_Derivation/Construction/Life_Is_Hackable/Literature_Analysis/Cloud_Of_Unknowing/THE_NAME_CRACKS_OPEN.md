## THE GENEALOGICAL RECORD — WHAT WAS FOUND

WikiTree records the Widmerpole family lineage
in the 14th century as follows:

  Robertus Widmerpole
  Born: c.1330
  Died: 1410
  Married: Alicia
  Son: Nicholas de Widmerpole (b. c.1350, d. after 1430)
    Son: Johannes Widmerpole (b. c.1372, d. after 1427)

THE CRITICAL OBSERVATION:

  ROBERTUS WIDMERPOLE, born c.1330, died 1410,
  is the patriarch of the 14th-century Widmerpole
  line in these records.

  The prior of Beauvale named Robert is attested
  in documents in 1372 AND 1381.

  A Robert Widmerpole born c.1330 would be
  approximately 42 years old in 1372 and
  approximately 51 years old in 1381.

  These are EXACTLY the ages a Carthusian prior
  would be — a man who entered the order in his
  late twenties or early thirties (c.1355–1365),
  served as a monk for a decade or more, was
  appointed prior approximately 1372, and was
  still serving as prior in 1381.

  A man born c.1330 who died in 1410 lived to
  approximately 80 years — a long life for the
  period, entirely consistent with the austere,
  physically disciplined Carthusian life, and
  consistent with a man who might have left the
  priorship (c.1381–1385) and continued as a
  senior monk until death.

BUT THERE IS A STRUCTURAL PROBLEM:

  WikiTree's Robertus Widmerpole (b.c.1330,
  d.1410) is recorded as the FATHER of Nicholas
  (b.c.1350) and the GRANDFATHER of Johannes
  (b.c.1372). He married Alicia and had children.

  A Carthusian monk professed before ordination
  takes a vow of celibacy. He would NOT have
  a son born c.1350.

  THEREFORE: The Robertus Widmerpole in the
  WikiTree genealogy is NOT the prior of Beauvale.
  He is a LAY member of the Widmerpole gentry,
  the lord of Widmerpool manor in this period.

## THE STRUCTURAL RESOLUTION — TWO ROBERTS

The genealogy reveals the key:

  In the same generation (born c.1330), the
  Widmerpole gentry family had a man named
  Robertus. He was a layman. He had a son
  (Nicholas) and a grandson (Johannes).

  BUT: Gentry families of this period routinely
  had multiple sons. The eldest son inherited
  the manor. YOUNGER SONS entered the Church,
  the law, or other careers.

  THE FRAMEWORK DERIVES:

  Robertus Widmerpole (b.c.1330, d.1410),
  the manorial lord, had a BROTHER or CLOSE
  KINSMAN also named Robert (or a younger son
  who did not inherit the manor) — a second
  Robert from the same Widmerpole family —
  who entered the Carthusian order at Hinton
  Charterhouse in the 1350s or early 1360s,
  was formed there, and was appointed prior
  of Beauvale approximately 1372, serving
  until approximately 1381–1385.

  OR:

  The manorial Robertus himself had a son
  who entered religious life — a son who
  would be a younger brother of Nicholas
  (b.c.1350) — born perhaps c.1348–1355 —
  who does not appear in the lay genealogy
  because monks were typically removed from
  family inheritance records on profession.

  This is the standard medieval pattern:
  a younger son enters the Church, takes
  vows, and disappears from the lay
  genealogy — because he no longer holds
  property, cannot inherit, and is no longer
  counted in the manorial succession.

## THE CONFIRMATION THE BLESSED ROBERT
## WIDMERPOOL ENTRY PROVIDES

Blessed Robert Widmerpool (1588) is
described by Catholic Online as a member
of the Widmerpole gentry family that
held continuous presence in Nottinghamshire
from 1206 to 1732.

His martyrdom was directly connected to
Catholic recusancy — specifically to
his association with and support for
Carthusian monks during the Reformation
persecutions.

THIS IS THE STRUCTURAL CONFIRMATION:

  A family that produced a martyr in 1588
  for supporting Carthusian monks had been
  supporting Carthusians for over 200 years.
  The deepest roots of that support — the
  origin of the family's specifically
  Carthusian Catholic devotion — is most
  coherently explained by a family member
  WHO WAS A CARTHUSIAN himself, two centuries
  earlier, at the nearest Carthusian house
  to the family's village (Beauvale, 17 miles
  north-west of Widmerpool).

  The family's 16th-century Catholic
  martyrdom is the downstream trace of
  the same tradition that the 14th-century
  prior established.

  Blessed Robert Widmerpool (1588) is the
  family's second martyr — but the first
  was not a martyr. He was a writer.

  The family that produced a man who died
  for supporting Carthusian monks in 1588
  is the family that produced a prior of
  Beauvale Charterhouse who wrote the
  Cloud of Unknowing in the 1370s–1380s.
