        East Midlands dialect zone
    (e) Carthusian libraries were among the best

  secular priests did not have an institutional

  (c) and partially (b): Augustinian canons lived

  certainly a Carthusian monk. Not "possibly" as

  the Carthusian order is the only institution in
  14th-century England that satisfies all six

### 2.1 The Interior Condition

The Book of Privy Counsel is the clearest window

  "Offer to God your naked being as the whole

  then forget even that. What remains when you

  It is the bare being of your existence — and
  that bare being is the ground in which God dwells."

This is not aspirational writing. This is
descriptive writing — the author is describing
what they have found. The bare being. The ground.
The navigator who has arrived at the Book of
Privy Counsel's instruction has been there.

What this means for the portrait:

  This navigator had what the Zen tradition calls
  kensho — threshold crossing — and had returned
  from it enough times, and maintained the post-
  crossing operation long enough, to be able to
  describe the terrain from a position of stable
  familiarity rather than acute amazement.

  The tone of the Cloud is not the tone of someone
  recently transformed. It is the tone of someone
  for whom the transformation is old enough to have
  become settledness. The "leaping spark" of the
  Republic's Seventh Letter had occurred, probably
  years or decades before the Cloud was written.
  What the Cloud describes is not the spark itself
  but the conditions for it, from someone who has
  been living in the settled state the spark opens.

  This is one of the rarest profiles in the audit
  series: a navigator who crossed the threshold,
  lived in the post-crossing state long enough
  for it to become their operational ground rather
  than a peak experience, and THEN wrote about
  how to cross. Most mystical writing is done
  closer to the crossing — while the amazement
  is still fresh. The Cloud's operational economy
  is the economy of someone for whom the amazement
  has become normality.

The author has used the institution's opacity

### 2.4 The Age at Writing

The framework derives: the author was between
45 and 65 years old when writing the Cloud.

  - The prior university education (probably
    completed by age 25-28 in 14th-century England)
  - Entry into the Carthusian order (typically

The dialect evidence (East Midlands, northern)
and the manuscript transmission evidence
(Mount Grace was the primary node in the
Cloud's 15th-century circulation) create
a specific geographical question:

  Was the author at Beauvale (Nottinghamshire,
  founded 1343) or Mount Grace (North Yorkshire,
  founded 1398)?

  The dating immediately narrows this: Mount Grace
  was founded in 1398 CE. The Cloud was written in
  the 1370s-1390s. If the Cloud predates 1398,
  Mount Grace cannot be the house of composition
  — though it may have become the primary
  transmission house after the author's death.

  DERIVATION: The author was at Beauvale,
  or at another East Midlands charterhouse.
  Beauvale (founded 1343, Nottinghamshire) is
  the strongest candidate for house of composition.
  Mount Grace became the primary transmission
  node after the Cloud's composition — which is
  consistent with the text reaching Mount Grace
  through the Carthusian internal network after
  Beauvale produced it.

  The Beauvale-to-Mount Grace transmission pathway
  is the most parsimonious explanation for:
    (a) the dialect placing composition in
        Nottinghamshire or adjacent counties
    (b) Mount Grace being the primary manuscript
        transmission center in the 15th century
    (c) No identified author at either house

Scholarship identified Adam Horsley (prior of
Beauvale, late 14th/early 15th century) as a
weak candidate and largely dismissed him — not
because the evidence was against him but because
there was no direct evidence for him. The
absence of evidence in a situation where the
author was structurally designed to leave no
evidence is not evidence of absence.

The framework's assessment of Horsley:

  WHAT IS KNOWN ABOUT ADAM HORSLEY:
    - He was a senior Carthusian at Beauvale
      (the dialect match house)
    - He was active in the late 14th/early
      15th century (the dating match period)
    - He was known as a theologian within
      the order (consistent with the Latin
      scholarship requirement)
    - His institutional role (prior or senior
      monk) is consistent with the spiritual
      direction relationship requirement

      surviving record

  THE FRAMEWORK'S EVALUATION:
    The dismissal of Horsley by scholarship
    is based on "no direct evidence" — which
    is precisely what the framework predicts
    would be true of the Cloud author. The
    perfect immune-response evasion produces
    no direct evidence by design. Dismissing
    a candidate because they left no direct
    evidence is, in this specific case,
    dismissing them for exhibiting the
    primary predicted feature of the author.

    This is not an argument that Horsley IS
    the author. It is an argument that
    "no direct evidence" is the wrong criterion
    for dismissal when the author was designed
    to leave no direct evidence.

  THE STRUCTURAL ASSESSMENT:
    If Adam Horsley matches the profile on
    all derived criteria (order, house, timing,
    seniority, scholarly formation, institutional
    position) and has no texts attributed to him
    in the surviving record — this is CONSISTENT
    WITH being the Cloud author, not evidence
    against. A navigator who successfully maintained
    anonymity for 650 years would look exactly
    like Adam Horsley looks in the historical record:
    an institutional presence with no surviving
    textual attribution.
