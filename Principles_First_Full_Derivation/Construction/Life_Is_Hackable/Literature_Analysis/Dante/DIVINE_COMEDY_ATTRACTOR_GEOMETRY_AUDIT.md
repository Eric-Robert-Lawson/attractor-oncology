## PART V: THE THREE GUIDES

## The Formally Staged Navigator Sequence

### 5.1 The Source Material

**Virgil** (Publius Vergilius Maro, 70–19 BCE):
- Roman poet, author of the Aeneid
- Virgil guides Dante through the entirety of
  Inferno and all of Purgatorio up to the
  Earthly Paradise at the summit
- Virgil himself is in Limbo — virtuous but
  unbaptized, without field access to Heaven
- His guidance terminates at the Earthly
  Paradise: he cannot enter Paradiso
- His final words to Dante: "Await no further
  word or sign from me: your will is free,
  upright, and whole — to act against it
  would be to err. Therefore I crown and
  mitre you lord of yourself."
  (*Purgatorio*, Canto XXVII)

**Beatrice** (Beatrice Portinari, 1266–1290 CE):
- Dante's idealized beloved, who died young
- She descended from Heaven to commission Virgil
  to guide Dante at the poem's opening
- She replaces Virgil at the summit of Purgatory
- She guides Dante through all nine spheres of
  Paradiso, explaining the structure of each
- In Paradiso she becomes progressively more
  radiant — her beauty increases as they ascend
- She is replaced by Bernard at the Empyrean

**St. Bernard of Clairvaux** (1090–1153 CE):
- Cistercian monk, mystic, theologian
- Appears in the final cantos of Paradiso
- He replaces Beatrice for the final approach
  to the beatific vision
- He does not explain or guide discursively —
  he prays, and he points
- His prayer to the Virgin Mary opens the
  final canto and enables the vision
