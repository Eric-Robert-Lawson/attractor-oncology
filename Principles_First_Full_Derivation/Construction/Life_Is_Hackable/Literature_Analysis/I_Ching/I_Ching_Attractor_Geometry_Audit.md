### 2.1 The Source Material

The Great Commentary (Xi Ci Zhuan) provides the
I Ching's philosophical foundation:

- **"一阴一阳之谓道"** — "One yin, one yang;
  this is called the Tao." (Xi Ci I.5)
- **"形而上者谓之道，形而下者谓之器"** —
  "What is above form is called Tao; what is
  below form is called an instrument." (Xi Ci II.11)
- Change (Yi, 易) has three aspects: unchanging
  (the eternal laws), cyclical (the recurring
  patterns), and transformational (the qualitative
  shifts that produce genuinely new states)
- The Tao is not one of the poles. The Tao is the
  dynamic relationship between the poles: the
  alternation itself, not either term of the
  alternation

Yin and Yang are described as:
- Not opposites that exclude each other, but
  complementary poles that require each other
- Each containing the seed of the other at its
  maximum — the famous Taijitu symbol's embedded
  dots
- The source of all differentiation: all phenomena
  arise from the specific ratio and configuration
  of Yin and Yang in their interaction

## PART IV: THE 64 HEXAGRAMS
## The Framework's First Formal State-Space

### 4.1 The Source Material

Each hexagram consists of two trigrams stacked
(inner/lower + outer/upper), producing 8 × 8 = 64
possible configurations. Each hexagram has:

- A name and an image
- A Judgment (Gua Ci) — the general assessment
  of the situation this configuration represents
- Six individual Line Statements (Yao Ci) — one
  for each line, describing the specific situation
  and advice for each position within the hexagram
- A nuclear hexagram (Hu Gua) — formed from lines
  2-3-4 (lower nuclear trigram) and 3-4-5 (upper
  nuclear trigram) — encoding hidden or underlying
  dynamics within the present configuration

The 64 hexagrams are arranged in 32 pairs. In each
pair, the second hexagram is either:
- The inversion of the first (flip it upside down),
  or (for symmetric hexagrams that look the same
  inverted)
- The complement of the first (flip all lines:
  Yin becomes Yang and Yang becomes Yin)

The series begins with Hexagram 1 (Qian, pure
Yang) and Hexagram 2 (Kun, pure Yin) and ends
with Hexagram 63 (Ji Ji, After Completion) and
Hexagram 64 (Wei Ji, Before Completion).
