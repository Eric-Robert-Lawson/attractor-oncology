## PART I: THE CORPUS AND ITS CONTEXT

### 1.1 What Was Discovered at Nag Hammadi

In December 1945, a sealed earthenware jar was
unearthed near Nag Hammadi, Upper Egypt. Inside:
thirteen leather-bound papyrus codices containing
fifty-two tractates, dating to the 3rd–4th century CE
but encoding traditions ranging from the 1st century BCE
to the 2nd century CE or earlier.

The corpus is not a unified tradition. It contains:

- **Sethian Gnostic texts** (the most cosmologically
  developed, closest to the Enochic tradition):
  Apocryphon of John, Trimorphic Protennoia,
  Apocalypse of Adam, Hypostasis of the Archons,
  Gospel of the Egyptians, Three Steles of Seth,
  Zostrianos, Marsanes, Allogenes, Melchizedek

- **Valentinian Gnostic texts** (the most formally
  developed, most sophisticated sacramental encoding):
  Gospel of Philip, Gospel of Truth,
  Tripartite Tractate, Valentinian Exposition

- **Hermetic texts** (direct overlap with the
  Mediterranean attractor geometry parallel tradition):
  Discourse on the Eighth and Ninth,
  Prayer of Thanksgiving, Asclepius fragment

- **Wisdom texts with Gnostic reading**:
  Gospel of Thomas, Authoritative Teaching,
  Exegesis on the Soul, Teachings of Silvanus

- **Apocalyptic and revelatory texts**:
  Apocalypse of Adam, Apocalypse of Peter,
  First and Second Apocalypse of James,
  Apocalypse of Paul

The corpus was hidden — almost certainly deliberately,
almost certainly around 367 CE when the bishop
Athanasius issued his Festal Letter mandating
the destruction of non-canonical books.
