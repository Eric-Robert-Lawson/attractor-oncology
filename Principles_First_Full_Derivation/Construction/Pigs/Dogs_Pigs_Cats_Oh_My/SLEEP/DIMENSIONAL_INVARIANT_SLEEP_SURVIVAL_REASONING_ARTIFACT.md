    occupation)

### 2.2 Why The Lock Outlasts The Navigator

The Lock survives longer than the Navigator
for a precise geometric reason.

The Navigator's survival depends on the
existence of alternative accessible basins
at the time of environmental disruption.
If the disruption is catastrophic enough
to eliminate all accessible basins
simultaneously — as the Permian-Triassic
event did, killing 96% of all marine
species — the Navigator fails. There is
nowhere to navigate to.

The Lock does not navigate. The Lock
cannot be displaced from its basin because
the basin is too deep. The Permian-Triassic
event restructured the entire biosphere
and the horseshoe crab was still there
on the other side, doing exactly what it
was doing before. The event was not
energetically sufficient to push it out
of its basin.

The geometric implication:

    Basin depth is the ultimate survival
    parameter.

    Navigation is a survival strategy
    for moderate perturbations.

    For catastrophic perturbations,
    only the deepest basins survive.

    The oldest surviving lineages are
    therefore the ones in the deepest
    basins — and the deepest basins
    require the least sleep.

    The minimum of the sleep spectrum
    is the maximum of the survival
    spectrum.

This is the dimensional invariant.

The U-shape means that maximum sleep
does not predict minimum survival.

dependent on niche stability. The koala
may survive hundreds of millions of

eucalyptus disappears, the koala has
no alternative basin. The Lock organism
has no such dependence because its
basin does not require a specific niche
to exist. The basin is encoded in its
genome absolutely.

Seven independent phyla. Seven

a deep basin — a deep basin of
deep basins.

### 5.2 The Navigator Converges Independently
### Across All Major Phyla

The Navigator strategy has been found

- Cephalopoda (octopus): ~300Myr
- Primates (human): ~6Myr

Six independent lineages. Six
independent arrivals at the same
geometric strategy. Ecological
generalism is a deep basin in
evolutionary strategy space. The
Navigator strategy is its own
attractor — Basin Ω of ecological
flexibility.

### 5.3 The Unstable Zone Does Not Converge

Mass extinction casualties do not
converge. Each extinction event
eliminates different taxa via different
mechanisms. The unstable zone has no
deep basin — it is by definition the
region of the evolutionary landscape
that is not deep enough to resist
perturbation and not flexible enough
to navigate away from it. The only
convergent feature of the unstable
zone is its instability.

This is confirmed by the palaeontological
record: the survivors of every mass
extinction event are disproportionately
drawn from Lock and Navigator organisms,
not from intermediate specialists.
The K-Pg event killed the large
specialist predators (T. rex) and the
large specialist megaherbivores while
sparing the crocodilians (Navigator)
and the turtles (partial Lock).
The Permian-Triassic event killed 96%
of marine species while sparing the
horseshoe crab (Lock).

## 6. THE HUMAN POSITION AND THE LIMITS
## OF THE FRAMEWORK

### 6.1 Where The Human Falls

The human is a Navigator with
unprecedented Basin Ω characteristics:

- Ecologically generalist: every biome
- Dietarily generalist: complete omnivory
- High behavioural flexibility: tool use,
  cultural transmission, technology
- Sleep: 7-9 hours — Navigator range
- Social complexity: co-constructed
  inter-generational field via language,
  culture, and institution — a Basin B*
  of unprecedented depth

The human sleep signature places it
firmly in the Navigator category.
Its survival trajectory by the framework
should be long but not maximum —
Navigator range of 100-300Myr if the
strategy is maintained.

### 6.2 The Unprecedented Feature

The human has constructed an inter-
generational Basin B* — a co-constructed
social attractor that persists beyond
the individual lifetime via cultural
transmission. No other species has this.

The dog's Basin B* couples with a human
over one lifetime. The human's cultural
Basin B* couples across generations
indefinitely.

This is a qualitatively new basin type.
Not just a deeper Navigator basin.
A new topology: a basin whose depth
increases over time through active
investment in its own maintenance
(cultural accumulation, institutional
memory, scientific knowledge).

The framework does not yet predict the
survival trajectory of a species that
is actively deepening its own basin in
real time. This is the limit of the
current derivation.

### 6.3 The Risk

The same cultural Basin B* that gives
the human its unprecedented adaptive
capacity is also remodelling the
ecological landscape faster than any
previous organism has done. The human
is both Navigator and active landscape
modifier simultaneously.

A Navigator survives by finding alternative
basins when the current one disappears.
But if the Navigator is the agent
eliminating the alternative basins —
for itself and for all other species —
then the Navigator strategy fails even
for the Navigator.

This is a geometric statement, not a
moral one. The framework predicts that
a species that destroys its own landscape
diversity is eliminating the conditions
that made its own strategy viable.

### P-INVARIANT-1: Sleep Duration Negatively
### Correlates With Lineage Age Across
### All Animal Phyla Controlling For
### Body Mass and Metabolic Rate

PREDICTION:
A cross-phyla analysis of sleep duration
(or rest-state duration where bilateral
sleep is undefined) against lineage age
(millions of years since divergence of
the crown group) will show a negative
correlation that survives correction for
body mass and metabolic rate.

The oldest lineages will have the lowest
sleep requirements. The youngest lineages
will have the highest sleep requirements.
This correlation will be independent of
phylogenetic position and will be
detectable across Porifera, Cnidaria,
Mollusca, Arthropoda, and Vertebrata
simultaneously.

DISCRIMINATING VALUE:
This prediction is not predicted by any
existing sleep function theory (energy
conservation, memory consolidation,
immune function, synaptic homeostasis
alone). It requires the dimensional
invariant framework — specifically the
claim that sleep tracks basin navigation
cost and basin depth predicts deep-time
survival — to generate it.

### P-INVARIANT-2: Mass Extinction Survivor
### Species Will Show Lower Mean Sleep
### Duration Than Mass Extinction
### Casualty Sister Taxa

PREDICTION:
For each major mass extinction event,
comparing the sleep duration (or rest
state duration) of survivor lineages
to the estimated sleep duration of
closely related casualty lineages
will show that survivors sleep
significantly less (Lock survivors)
or show significantly higher dietary
breadth (Navigator survivors) than
their extinct relatives.

The prediction has two parts because
both strategies survive:
- Lock survivors: less sleep than
  casualties
- Navigator survivors: more dietary
  breadth than casualties, sleep
  in moderate range

This is testable using the fossil record
for body plan conservatism proxy (Lock),
dietary reconstruction from isotope and
dental analysis (Navigator), and
phylogenetic comparison.

### Boundary Is Predictable From

LOW SLEEP + LOW DIETARY BREADTH

The boundary between survival and

### P-INVARIANT-4: Extreme Specialists
### (Unstable Zone) Show No Sleep
### Benefit — Their High Sleep Is
### Metabolically Trapped

Extreme ecological specialists with

moderate-sleep Navigator organisms,

This distinguishes two high-sleep

- High sleep from Axis 1 (Basin Ω

  capacity

The koala's high sleep pays for
detoxification. The lion's high sleep
pays for Basin Ω navigation. Same sleep
duration. Different function. Different
survival prediction. Different correlation
with flexibility.

Lock organisms — those in the deepest
