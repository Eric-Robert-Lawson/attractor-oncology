# PART II — THE GEOMETRIC FRAMEWORK

---

## THE THREE COST AXES

Sleep discharges costs that accumulate during waking.
Those costs have three primary sources:

**Axis 1 — Switching Cost**
The metabolic and epigenetic cost of navigating
between attractor states: decision-making,
environmental response, social computation,
threat assessment.

High switching cost = organism lives in a shallow,
variable landscape and must continuously reposition.

Low switching cost = organism lives in a deep,
stable landscape and rarely switches states.

**Axis 2 — Metabolic/Oxidative Cost**
ROS accumulation during aerobic metabolism,
particularly in high-turnover tissues:
gut epithelium and neurons.

High metabolic rate = high ROS production rate
= high oxidative cost requiring discharge.

Low metabolic rate = low ROS production rate
= oxidative cost can be cleared passively
without a dedicated discharge window.

The proximate death mechanism from total sleep
deprivation is ROS accumulation in the gut
epithelium (Vaccaro et al., Cell, 2019).
Administering gut-specific antioxidants
rescues sleep-deprived animals without
restoring sleep — confirming that the cost,
not the sleep itself, is what must be addressed.

**Axis 3 — Computational/Synaptic Cost**
The cost of synaptic weight updates that cannot
be consolidated during active navigation.
Memory encoding, learning, pattern recognition —
all require offline replay and synaptic downscaling
that cannot occur while the organism is actively
navigating its environment.

High computational load = complex social landscape,
learning-intensive foraging, large hippocampal
demand = high REM requirement.

Near-zero computational load = simple sensory-motor
loops, no social hierarchy, no memory-intensive
spatial navigation = near-zero REM requirement.

**Total sleep requirement:**

```
S_total = f(C_switching + C_oxidative + C_computational)
```

When all three approach zero, S_total approaches zero.
This is not a theoretical limit.
It is observed in living organisms.

- Occupies a variable, complex niche (recent lineage)

- Has high computational load (social, spatial, learning)
- Therefore has high sleep requirement
- Therefore is more sensitive to sleep disruption
- Therefore is more vulnerable to extinction events

The Lock strategy is the evolutionary expression
of a near-flat cost landscape.
The Explore strategy is the evolutionary expression
of a high-cost, high-return landscape.

to different landscape topologies.
