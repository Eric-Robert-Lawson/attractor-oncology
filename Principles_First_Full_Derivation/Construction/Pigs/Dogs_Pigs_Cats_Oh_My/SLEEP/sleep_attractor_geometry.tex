\section{The Framework}
\label{sec:framework}
% ═══════════════════════════════════════��══════════════════════

\subsection{The Attractor Geometry Framework}

\[
S + N + G \;\rightarrow\; R
\]

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=0.2em]
  \item $S$ = structural substrate (genome
    and encoded build capacity)
  \item $N$ = coherence gradient field
    (external or internal field specifying
    basin occupation)
  \item $G$ = global attractor landscape
    (basin depths, barrier heights,
    accessible states, topology of
    transitions)
  \item $R$ = resulting phenotype or state
\end{itemize}

The framework predicts that an organism's
position in $G$ simultaneously determines:

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=0.2em]
  \item The total cost of occupying that
    position --- what must be discharged.
  \item The survival trajectory --- how
    resistant the basin is to perturbation.
  \item The topology of the discharge
    process --- what architecture of rest
    is geometrically required.
  \item The biochemical substrate of the
    cost --- oxidative, synaptic, or both,
    in proportions determined by the
    metabolic architecture of the organism.
\end{enumerate}

These four are projections of a single
geometric object: the organism's full
position in the landscape $G$.

\subsection{The Core Claim}

The metabolic cost of occupying a position
in an attractor landscape is a function
of the navigational demand imposed by
that position. Navigational demand is
determined by: (1) the number of accessible
basins; (2) barrier heights between basins;
(3) frequency of required transitions;
(4) the depth of Basin~$\Omega$ if one
exists; and (5) the within-basin metabolic
or computational intensity of the occupied
basin.

Rest is the generic process by which
accumulated cost is discharged.
\textbf{The cost is fundamentally oxidative.}
Navigation consumes metabolic substrate
and generates reactive oxygen species
as a byproduct in proportion to the
rate and intensity of neural and somatic
activity. Sleep deprivation kills via
ROS accumulation in peripheral
high-metabolism tissues --- specifically
the intestinal epithelium in
\textit{Drosophila} (Vaccaro et al.,
\textit{Nature}, 2020), which is the
tissue most directly responsible for
substrate acquisition driving the
navigation.

The synaptic and oxidative discharge
processes are not independent. They are
co-extensive: slow-wave sleep performs
synaptic downscaling via the
Tononi--Cirelli mechanism and
simultaneously drives glymphatic
pulsation (CSF flow through perivascular
AQP4 channels) that clears neurotoxic
metabolites. Both processes require
the same sleep state. The sleep topology
must be sufficient to discharge both
costs simultaneously or in rapid
alternation.

\textbf{The architecture of the rest
process --- its topology --- is not a
separate biological variable.} It is a
further projection of the same landscape
geometry, constrained by the metabolic
architecture of the organism and the
environmental conditions that determine
whether any given topology is
energetically and ecologically feasible.

\subsection{Why the Existing Theoretical
Frameworks Are All Correct and Incomplete}

The four dominant sleep function theories
in the current literature are:

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=0.2em]
  \item Synaptic Homeostasis Hypothesis
    (Tononi \& Cirelli): sleep downscales
    synaptic potentiation. Correct.
    Describes the synaptic component of
    Axis~1 and Axis~3 cost discharge.
  \item Energy conservation: sleep reduces
    metabolic demand. Correct. Describes
    the consequence of reduced navigational
    activity during Axis~2 cost recovery.
  \item Glymphatic clearance (Nedergaard):
    sleep drives CSF pulsation clearing
    neurotoxic waste. Correct. Describes
    the physical mechanism by which the
    oxidative cost substrate is cleared
    during Topology~3 discharge.
  \item Memory consolidation: sleep
    stabilises memory traces. Correct.
    Describes a downstream consequence
    of synaptic downscaling --- the net
    effect of selectively strengthening
    versus weakening synapses during
    discharge.
\end{enumerate}

None of these frameworks generates
the dimensional invariant. None predicts
that the oldest surviving lineages sleep
the least. None predicts convergent sleep
topologies across independent phylogenies.
None predicts that sleep topology is a
geometric property of landscape position.
None connects sleep duration to deep-time
survival trajectories.

They are correct descriptions of
mechanisms within Topology~3 (consolidated
bilateral deep sleep in endothermic
organisms). The dimensional invariant
is the geometric structure that contains
all four as special cases.

\section{The Cost Structure}
\label{sec:cost}
% ════════════════���═════════════════════════════════════════════

\subsection{The Three Axes}

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=0.5em]

  \item \textbf{Axis~1 --- Between-basin
    switching cost.}
    The organism navigates between multiple
    accessible basins. Each transition
    requires bilateral synaptic
    reconfiguration. Cost accumulates
    continuously during waking. Produces
    both synaptic cost (hemispheres must
    reconfigure for new basin) and
    oxidative cost (high neural firing
    rates during contextual switching
    generate ROS).
    \textit{Geometric signature:} High
    behavioural flexibility. Multiple
    accessible basins. High sleep.
    \textit{Examples:} Felidae,
    Basin~$\Omega$ organisms.

  \item \textbf{Axis~2 --- Within-basin
    metabolic cost.}
    A single expensive basin consumes all
    available metabolic resources during
    waking. Sleep is required for
    metabolic resource recovery. Primarily
    an oxidative cost rather than synaptic:
    the organism's peripheral tissues
    accumulate ROS and metabolic debt
    during sustained high-cost waking
    activity in a single specialised basin.
    \textit{Geometric signature:} Low
    behavioural flexibility. Single basin.
    High sleep from resource depletion.
    \textit{Example:} Koala (eucalyptus
    detoxification is a continuous
    oxidative cost).

  \item \textbf{Axis~3 --- Within-basin
    computational intensity.}
    Extreme neural computation within a
    single specialised basin. High firing
    rates generate high ROS in neural
    tissue. Synaptic reconfiguration cost
    from processing intensity, not
    switching. Both synaptic and oxidative
    costs are high.
    \textit{Geometric signature:} Low
    behavioural flexibility. Extreme
    within-basin performance. High sleep.
    \textit{Example:} Brown bat
    (echolocation at millisecond
    resolution for 4 hours generates
    neural ROS and synaptic cost requiring
    20 hours of discharge).

\end{enumerate}

The Lock organism operates on none of
these axes. Zero navigational demand.
Zero within-basin metabolic surplus cost.
Zero within-basin computational intensity.
Minimal ROS generation from minimal
activity. The sleep minimum is the
signature of zero cost at every axis
and zero oxidative debt simultaneously.

\subsection{The Oxidative Cost Substrate:
Why This Matters Geometrically}

The identification of ROS accumulation
as the proximate death mechanism in
sleep deprivation (Vaccaro et al., 2020)
is not merely a mechanistic finding.
It is a geometric finding, because it
tells you what the cost of navigation
actually is at the biochemical level.

\textbf{The geometric consequence:}
The feasibility of any sleep topology is
constrained by the rate at which the
organism generates ROS during its active
period and the rate at which the chosen
topology can clear that ROS.

An endothermic Navigator generates ROS
at endothermic metabolic rates during
8--16 hours of active navigation.
The ROS accumulation rate is high.
Distributed rest (Topology~2) cannot
clear it fast enough because glymphatic
pulsation requires sustained NREM
pressure to drive adequate CSF flow.
Concentrated bilateral deep sleep
(Topology~3) is required.

An ectothermic Navigator generates ROS
at ectothermic metabolic rates --- an
order of magnitude lower per unit time.
The ROS accumulation rate is low enough
that extended distributed bilateral rest
(Topology~2) clears it adequately without
requiring concentrated NREM. This is
the geometric explanation for why the
crocodilian uses Topology~2 rather than
Topology~3. It is not a taxonomic
accident. It is the only feasible topology
for an ectotherm with Navigator-level
ecological complexity.

\textbf{The prediction that follows:}
Any ectothermic Navigator will use
Topology~2 or a topology with lower
NREM concentration than endothermic
Navigators. Any organism shifted from
ectothermy to endothermy (e.g., via
experimental manipulation of body
temperature regulation) will show a
shift toward Topology~3 and increased
sleep concentration. This is a novel,
testable, geometrically derived prediction.

\section{The Seven Sleep Topologies}
\label{sec:topologies}
% ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

The framework predicts that different
landscape positions produce different
sleep architectures. These topologies
are geometric predictions, not empirical
categories. Each is the only
architecturally feasible discharge
solution for its corresponding
landscape position given the full
constraint set (navigational cost
structure, ROS generation rate, metabolic
architecture, environmental lethality
of full sleep, developmental requirements,
immune-basin structure).

\textbf{T1: Metabolic quiescence /
biochemical rhythm only}

\textit{Landscape position:} Deepest Lock.
No nervous system or minimal neural
architecture. Zero navigational demand.
Zero synaptic reconfiguration cost.
Minimal ROS generation from minimal
activity.

\textit{Discharge:} No sleep in any
neurological sense. Circadian biochemical
rhythms regulate metabolic processes.
ROS cleared by antioxidant enzyme cycling
on circadian schedule without requiring
behavioural quiescence.

\textit{Duration:} Undefined or
approaching zero in neurological terms.

\textit{Geometric signature:} Maximum
perturbation-invariance. If an organism
shows T1, it is at or near the extreme
Lock extreme of the survival spectrum.

\textit{Confirmed examples:} Sponge
(Porifera, $\sim$600\,Myr). Horseshoe
crab (Chelicerata, $\sim$450\,Myr).
Most jellyfish (Cnidaria, 500--700\,Myr).
\end{mdframed}

\textbf{T2: Distributed bilateral rest ---
ectothermic Navigator solution}

\textit{Landscape position:} Moderate
Navigator with ectothermic metabolic
architecture. Navigator-level navigational
cost (multiple accessible basins, moderate
switching cost) but ROS generation rate
is an order of magnitude lower than
endothermy. Energy budget for full
bilateral wakefulness is limited per day.

\textit{Discharge:} True bilateral EEG
deep sleep for 2--6 hours discharging
synaptic cost. Extended distributed
bilateral rest state (reduced neural
activity, bilateral EEG, behavioural
vigilance via brainstem alerting, not
cortical asymmetry) for 12--16 hours
clearing the low-rate oxidative debt
continuously. Total Navigator-equivalent
discharge distributed across the inactive
period.

\textit{Why bilateral and not
unihemispheric:} Navigator switching cost
is between ecological basins, not between
waking hemispheres. Both hemispheres carry
equivalent cost and discharge simultaneously.
Unihemispheric asymmetry is not required
because the oxidative and synaptic costs
are bilaterally symmetric.

\textit{Why distributed and not
concentrated:} Low ectothermic ROS
generation rate means glymphatic clearance
does not require sustained high-pressure
NREM to achieve adequate flow. Distributed
low-pressure rest clears the oxidative
debt adequately. Concentrated NREM is
metabolically expensive and ecologically
dangerous for an ectotherm in an
open environment.

\textit{Duration:} 2--6h true sleep plus
12--16h Basin~C rest. Total equivalent:
Navigator range.

\textit{Confirmed examples:} Crocodilian
(Archosauria, $\sim$200\,Myr). Kelly \&
Lesku (2015, 2019): bilateral EEG
confirmed throughout rest periods.
One-eye-open vigilance is behavioural
(brainstem-level alerting), not
unihemispheric in EEG sense.

\textit{Predicted additional examples:}
Monitor lizards and large ectothermic
generalist reptiles. Large ectothermic
generalist fish. Cockroach (Blattodea,
$\sim$300\,Myr) --- ectothermic Navigator,
12h distributed rest.
\end{mdframed}

\textbf{T3: Consolidated bilateral deep
sleep --- endothermic solution}

\textit{Landscape position:} Endothermic
organism at any cost level. High ROS
generation rate during waking (endothermic
metabolic rates) requires concentrated
high-efficiency glymphatic clearance
during sustained NREM. Bilateral synaptic
reconfiguration cost discharged during
same concentrated window.

\textit{Discharge:} Concentrated NREM
slow-wave sleep driving high-pressure
glymphatic pulsation (CSF flow through
AQP4 perivascular channels) to clear
neurotoxic metabolites and amyloid-beta.
Simultaneous synaptic downscaling via
slow waves. REM phase discharges
computational replay cost (Axis~3) and
consolidates navigational memory.

\textit{Duration:} 2--22 hours depending
on cost axis. The full range from minimal
Lock (2--5h) to extreme specialist (20h)
is expressed within this topology. Duration
within T3 is a valid direct proxy for
total cost because the metabolic
architecture is held constant (endothermy).

\textit{Confirmed examples:} All
endothermic organisms: rodent ($\sim$13h),
human (7--9h), koala ($\sim$20h), brown
bat ($\sim$20h), lion ($\sim$16h),
elephant ($\sim$2h), giraffe ($\sim$30min
when not suppressed).

\textit{Note on cross-topology comparison:}
Duration is a valid proxy for cost
comparison \textit{within} T3 only. A
crocodilian (T2, 2--6h true sleep) and an
elephant (T3, 2h) have similar true sleep
durations but completely different cost
structures. The topology must be identified
before duration is interpreted.

\textbf{T4: Permanent Basin~$\Omega$ ---
dissolved waking/sleep barrier}

\textit{Landscape position:} High
navigational cost in an environment where
full bilateral sleep is lethal (open ocean
mandatory ventilation, multi-directional
predation in continuous motion). The cost
of entering the sleep basin's exit barrier
exceeds the accumulated synaptic and
oxidative cost of remaining partially
awake indefinitely.

\textit{Discharge:} The barrier between
waking and sleep is dissolved. One
hemisphere sleeps (synaptic downscaling,
partial glymphatic pulsation) while the
other remains active (navigation, motor
control, sensory processing), in continuous
alternation. Each hemisphere discharges
in rotation. Oxidative cost clearance
is continuous and distributed at the
hemispheric level.

\textit{Why unihemispheric:} The
navigational cost and oxidative cost are
bilaterally symmetric (same as T2), but
the lethal constraint on full bilateral
sleep forces the discharge to be
asymmetric. T4 is T2 with an additional
lethal constraint that forces the
distributed rest to be hemispheric rather

waking occupation cost. Sleep cannot
be postponed without developmental

\textit{Geometric structure:} Entering

basin-occupation cost that must be
discharged to allow recovery. The

by which the immune-basin cost is
discharged: reduced navigational demand
frees metabolic resources for immune

\textit{The geometric framing:} Sickness

occupation cost of the immune response
basin. Cytokines are the basin-entry
signal --- they are $N$ in the

which basin the organism must occupy.

occupation cost falls to zero. T7 sleep
cannot be prevented without impairing
immune function --- this is the definitive
distinction from all other topologies.

Confirmed across all major animal phyla
where tested.

\section{The Population-Level Basin
Portfolio}
\label{sec:portfolio}
% ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

This section presents a finding derived
from exploratory research in v3.0 that
extends the dimensional invariant below
the organism level.

\subsection{Bacterial Persister Cells:
The Dimensional Invariant at Population
Level}

Bacterial persister cells (reviewed in
Niu et al., \textit{Signal Transduction
and Targeted Therapy}, 2024) are
phenotypic variants within a clonal
population that enter deep metabolic
quiescence under stress. They are not
genetically resistant to antibiotics.
They are transiently in a state of
near-zero metabolic activity that renders
them immune to antibiotics that require
active metabolic processes as targets.
Upon stressor removal, persisters
resuscitate and repopulate.

\textbf{The geometric reading:}

A clonal bacterial population under
antibiotic challenge simultaneously
maintains two distinct basin positions:

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=0.2em]
  \item \textbf{Active majority
    (Navigator-equivalent):} Moderate
    metabolic activity. Flexible metabolic
    response. High ROS generation.
    Subject to antibiotic killing. Short
    individual survival horizon.

  \item \textbf{Persister minority
    (Lock-equivalent):} Near-zero
    metabolic activity. Deep metabolic
    quiescence. Near-zero ROS generation.
    Perturbation-invariant to the
    antibiotic challenge. Maximum
    individual survival horizon for
    the duration of the challenge.
\end{enumerate}

The population holds a \textbf{portfolio
of basin depths} rather than a single
basin position. This is the dimensional
invariant operating as an evolutionary
portfolio strategy at the sub-organismal
population level.

\textbf{The geometric prediction this
generates:}

The proportion of persisters in a
population will scale with the historical
frequency and intensity of perturbation
events in the organism's ecological
history. A population with a history of
frequent antibiotic-equivalent challenges
(high-perturbation environment) will
maintain a higher persister fraction
than a naive population. This is the
population-level analogue of the
Lock/Navigator trade-off at the
individual level: populations in
volatile environments evolve toward
higher Lock fractions; populations in
stable environments evolve toward lower
Lock fractions and higher active fractions.

\textbf{The further implication:}

The same portfolio logic applies at the
organism level in a different form.
Basin~$\Omega$ organisms (cats, dolphins,
corvids) maintain the \textit{capacity}
for both basins simultaneously but
express one at a time. The persister
cell population maintains both basin
positions simultaneously as distinct
cellular phenotypes within the same
clonal population. These are two
implementations of the same geometric
strategy: maintaining access to both
deep (Lock-equivalent) and flexible
(Navigator-equivalent) attractor positions
simultaneously, with the allocation
between them determined by the current
perturbation environment.

\section{Local Sleep: Circuit-Level
Application of the Dimensional Invariant}
\label{sec:local}
% ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Recent work (Nir \& Krueger, 2024;
Tononi \& Cirelli, 2023) has demonstrated
that sleep is not a global brain state
but a collection of local events:
individual cortical columns can cycle
between sleep-like and wake-like states
on a second timescale, independently
of global sleep state. Brain regions
more active during waking show higher
slow-wave activity during subsequent
sleep.

\textbf{The geometric reading:}

The dimensional invariant applies at
circuit level. Individual cortical
circuits each have their own
basin-occupation cost that accumulates
independently during waking. Circuits
used more heavily during the day
accumulate higher synaptic and oxidative
cost. They discharge that cost locally
during sleep, independently of other
circuits.

The global slow wave is not a global
reset. It is the statistically concurrent
discharge of many local circuits that
were used during the same waking period,
appearing global because waking activity
uses most circuits simultaneously. When
waking activity is asymmetric (one
hemisphere used more, one cortical
region used more), the sleep response
is correspondingly asymmetric.

\textbf{This is a novel extension of
the framework:} The three cost axes
apply at circuit level, not just organism
level. A hand motor cortex region running
Axis~3 (computational intensity) during
a skilled motor task discharges that
specific Axis~3 cost locally during
subsequent sleep. The visual cortex
discharges its own cost. The prefrontal
cortex its own. All simultaneously but
independently.

\textbf{The prediction this generates:}

The local sleep finding predicts that
surgical removal or lesioning of a
cortical region will reduce the
corresponding component of whole-brain
slow-wave activity during subsequent
sleep, proportional to the navigational
cost that region was carrying. A lesion
to a high-Axis-3 region should reduce
total NREM slow-wave power more than a
lesion to a low-activity region. This
is testable and has not been explicitly
framed this way in the current literature.

\subsection{The Migratory Sparrow:
Basin Restructuring, Not Debt Suppression}

White-crowned sparrows (\textit{Zonotrichia
leucophrys}) reduce sleep to $\sim$30\%
of normal during migration and show no
sleep rebound after migration terminates.
P-INVARIANT-5 predicts that minimal sleep
under pressure is associated with T5
(rebound on pressure removal). The sparrow
shows no rebound. Is this a falsification?

\textbf{Geometric resolution:}

The migratory state is not the same
attractor as the non-migratory state.
Migration recruits the organism into a
distinct basin in $G$ --- the migratory
attractor --- with a different navigational
cost structure than the residential
ecological basin. In the migratory basin:

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=0.2em]
  \item Axis~1 switching cost is reduced:
    the organism is in a single focused
    basin (directional flight and fuelling)
    rather than navigating between multiple
    ecological basins.
  \item Axis~3 cost is redirected: neural
    computation is dominated by
    magnetoreceptive navigation rather
    than social and ecological
    decision-making.
  \item The sleep requirement in the
    migratory basin is genuinely lower
    than in the residential basin, not
    suppressed.
\end{itemize}

The no-rebound finding confirms this:
rebound would only occur if debt had
accumulated. No rebound means no debt.
The sparrow was paying its actual
(lower) migratory-basin cost through
unihemispheric microsleeps during flight
(T4-adjacent) and brief rest bouts at
stopovers.

\textbf{Refinement to P-INVARIANT-5:}
Sleep rebound identifies T5 (external
suppression of unchanged navigational
demand). It will \textit{not} be observed
when sleep reduction accompanies genuine
basin restructuring that lowers actual
navigational demand. The two cases are
distinguishable by measuring whether
performance within the current basin is
maintained during the low-sleep period.
T5: performance declines (debt
accumulating). Basin restructuring:
performance maintained within the new
basin's demands.

\subsection{Ecological Generalism as
Consequence: Bidirectional Reinforcement}

A 2024 Nature study reported that
ecological generalism may be more a
consequence of widespread success than
a direct cause of survival. This appears
to challenge the Navigator framework.

\textbf{Geometric resolution:}

The dimensional invariant is not a
cross-sectional claim about currently
living species. It is a deep-time
trajectory claim. The Nature 2024
finding describes a bidirectional
reinforcement mechanism that the
framework predicts and that makes
the Navigator basin deeper over time:

Navigational success (survival of
perturbations) $\rightarrow$ wider
geographic range $\rightarrow$ more
ecological contexts encountered
$\rightarrow$ Navigator basin deepened
through experience and population-level
exploration $\rightarrow$ greater
navigational success.

This is a positive feedback loop that
deepens Basin~$\Omega$ over evolutionary
time. The cockroach has been running this
loop for 300\,Myr. Its current generalism
is simultaneously the cause and consequence
of 300\,Myr of Navigator survival. The
Nature 2024 finding is correct about the
consequence direction. The dimensional
invariant describes the full loop and
its deep-time trajectory.
