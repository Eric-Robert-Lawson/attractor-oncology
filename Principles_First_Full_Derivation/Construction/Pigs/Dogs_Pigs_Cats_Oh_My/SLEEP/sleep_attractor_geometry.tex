\subsection{The Attractor Geometry Framework}

The attractor geometry framework:
\[
S + N + G \;\rightarrow\; R
\]
where:
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=0.2em]
  \item $S$ = structural substrate (genome
    and encoded build capacity)
  \item $N$ = coherence gradient field
    (external or internal field specifying
    basin occupation)
  \item $G$ = developmental and evolutionary
    geometry (global attractor landscape ---
    basin depths, barrier heights, accessible
    states)
  \item $R$ = resulting phenotype or state
\end{itemize}

Previous documents in this series established:

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=0.2em]
  \item Sleep duration tracks the total
    synaptic and metabolic cost of an
    organism's landscape position,
    decomposable into three axes: Axis~1
    (between-basin switching cost), Axis~2
    (within-basin metabolic cost), and
    Axis~3 (within-basin computational
    intensity).

  \item The meta-attractor Basin~$\Omega$ ---
    the stable state of being an organism
    that navigates between two basins
    simultaneously --- has its own depth and
    its own metabolic maintenance cost.

  \item Deep basins are survival hacks:
    evolution converges on them because they
    are the attractor of the evolutionary
    process itself in stable environments.
\end{enumerate}

This document extends the framework to its
full temporal scope:

\begin{quote}
\itshape
The attractor geometry of an organism's basin
position predicts not just its current sleep
requirement but its survival trajectory across
deep time. Sleep duration is a temporal sonar.
\end{quote}

\subsection{The Core Claim}

The metabolic cost of occupying a position in
an attractor landscape is a function of the
navigational demand imposed by that position.
Navigational demand is determined by: (1) the
number of accessible basins from the current
position; (2) the height of the barriers
between those basins; (3) the frequency with
which the system must assess and execute basin
transitions; and (4) the depth of Basin~$\Omega$
if one exists.

Rest is the metabolic process by which the
synaptic cost of navigation is paid. Sleep
duration is a measurement of the navigational
demand of the organism's landscape position.

\textbf{The dimensional invariant:} Basin depth
is the same geometric property at every scale
--- molecular, cellular, developmental,
organism, evolutionary. Sleep tracks the cost
of occupying a position in the landscape.
Deep-time survival is a direct function of
basin depth. Therefore sleep duration is a
proxy for survival trajectory.

\subsection{Why The Lock Outlasts The Navigator}

The Lock survives longer than the Navigator
for a precise geometric reason. The Navigator's
survival depends on the existence of accessible
alternative basins at the time of environmental
disruption. If the disruption is catastrophic
enough to eliminate all accessible basins
simultaneously --- as the Permian--Triassic
event did, killing 96\% of all marine species
--- the Navigator fails. There is nowhere to
navigate to.

The Lock does not navigate. The Lock cannot
be displaced from its basin because the basin
is too deep. The Permian--Triassic event
restructured the entire biosphere and the
horseshoe crab was still there on the other
side, doing exactly what it was doing before.

\begin{quote}
\itshape
Basin depth is the ultimate survival parameter.
Navigation is a survival strategy for moderate
perturbations. For catastrophic perturbations,
only the deepest basins survive. The minimum of
the sleep spectrum is the maximum of the
survival spectrum.
\end{quote}

\subsection{The U-Shaped Survival--Sleep
Relationship}

The survival--sleep relationship is not linear.
It is U-shaped with a flat minimum. The minimum
of the sleep spectrum corresponds to the maximum
of the survival spectrum (Lock strategy). The
moderate range is the Navigator survival zone.
The high-sleep range is the uncertain zone ---
survival depends on niche stability or
Basin~$\Omega$ depth.

The critical insight is that high sleep does
not predict low survival. It predicts
\textit{conditional} survival. The koala may
survive hundreds of millions of years if
eucalyptus persists. But if eucalyptus
disappears, the koala has no alternative basin.
The Lock organism has no such dependence because
its basin is encoded absolutely in its genome.

\subsection{The Three Sleep Axes Revisited}

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=0.3em]
  \item \textbf{Axis~1 (Between-basin switching
    cost):} The organism navigates between
    multiple accessible basins under its own
    motivational field. High behavioural
    flexibility. High sleep. Examples: Felidae,
    Basin~$\Omega$ organisms.

  \item \textbf{Axis~2 (Within-basin metabolic
    cost):} A single highly expensive basin
    consumes all available metabolic resources.
    Sleep conserves energy for basin maintenance.
    Low behavioural flexibility. High sleep.
    Example: koala.

  \item \textbf{Axis~3 (Within-basin
    computational intensity):} Extreme neural
    computation within a single specialised
    basin during waking. Synaptic reconfiguration
    cost from intensity, not switching. Low
    flexibility. High sleep. Example: brown bat.
\end{enumerate}

The Lock organism operates on none of these
axes. It has minimal within-basin cost and no
between-basin switching. The sleep minimum is
the signature of zero navigational demand at
any axis.

Mass extinction casualties do not converge.

the survivors of every mass extinction event are
disproportionately drawn from Lock and Navigator
organisms. The K--Pg event killed large specialist
predators and specialist megaherbivores while
sparing crocodilians (Navigator) and turtles
(partial Lock). The Permian--Triassic event
killed 96\% of marine species while sparing the
horseshoe crab (Lock).

\label{sec:human}
% ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

\subsection{Geometric Classification}

The human is a Navigator with unprecedented
Basin~$\Omega$ characteristics:

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=0.2em]
  \item Ecologically generalist: every biome
  \item Dietarily generalist: complete omnivory
  \item High behavioural flexibility: tool use,
    cultural transmission, technology
  \item Sleep: 7--9 hours --- Navigator range
  \item Social complexity: co-constructed
    inter-generational field via language,
    culture, and institution --- a Basin~B*
    of unprecedented depth
\end{itemize}

The human sleep signature places it firmly in
the Navigator category. Its survival trajectory
by the framework should be long but not maximum
--- Navigator range of 100--300\,Myr if the
strategy is maintained.

\subsection{The Unprecedented Feature}

The human has constructed an inter-generational
Basin~B* --- a co-constructed social attractor
that persists beyond the individual lifetime via
cultural transmission. No other species has this.
The dog's Basin~B* couples with a human over one
lifetime. The human's cultural Basin~B* couples
across generations indefinitely, with depth
increasing over time through active investment in
its own maintenance (cultural accumulation,
institutional memory, scientific knowledge).

\subsection{The Risk}

The framework predicts that a species that
destroys its own landscape diversity is
eliminating the conditions that made its own
strategy viable. A Navigator survives by finding
alternative basins when the current one
disappears. If the Navigator is simultaneously
the agent eliminating the alternative basins
--- for itself and for all other species --- then
the Navigator strategy fails even for the
Navigator.

This is a geometric statement, not a moral one.
The framework does not predict the long-term
trajectory of a species that is actively
deepening its own basin in real time while
simultaneously remodelling the landscape that
other species' basins depend on. This is the
honest limit of the current derivation.

\section{Literature Confirmations}
\label{sec:confirmations}
% ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

\subsection{Oldest Surviving Lineages Sleep
the Least}
\textit{Derived before literature:} Minimum
sleep corresponds to maximum survival horizon.

\textit{Confirmed:} Horseshoe crab (450\,Myr,
minimal defined sleep --- tidal rest state only).
Nautilus (500\,Myr, no true sleep). Jellyfish
(500--700\,Myr, undefined sleep, no bilateral
nervous system). Shark (400\,Myr, distributed
rest periods only). All Lock organisms confirmed
as minimal sleepers.

\textit{Source:} Comparative sleep literature;
living fossil biology; horseshoe crab ecology
(Shuster et al., 2003).

\subsection{Mass Extinction Survivors Are
Disproportionately Lock or Navigator}
\textit{Derived:} The unstable zone is where
mass extinction concentrates its casualties.

\textit{Confirmed:} K--Pg survivors include
crocodilians (Navigator), turtles (partial
Lock), birds (Navigator), and small mammals
(Navigator). K--Pg casualties concentrated
in large specialist predators and specialist
megaherbivores. Permian--Triassic survivors
include horseshoe crab (Lock), nautilus (Lock).

\textit{Source:} Jablonski (1986); Barnosky
et al. (2011); K--Pg extinction survivor
literature.

\subsection{Both Strategies Are Convergent
Across Independent Phyla}
\textit{Derived:} Lock and Navigator are deep
basins in evolutionary strategy space,
therefore convergent.

\textit{Confirmed:} Lock morphology confirmed
independently in Porifera, Cnidaria, Mollusca,
Chelicerata, Chondrichthyes, Sarcopterygii,
Lepidosauria. Navigator ecology confirmed
independently in Blattodea, Archosauria,
Rodentia, Aves, Cephalopoda, Primates.

\textit{Source:} Conway Morris (2003,
\textit{Life's Solution}); living fossil
convergence literature; mass extinction
survival trait literature.

\subsection{Slow Metabolism Correlates With
Deep-Time Survival}
\textit{Derived:} The Lock strategy requires
minimal metabolic expenditure.

\textit{Confirmed:} Tuatara (slowest reptile
metabolism, 200\,Myr). Coelacanth (deep cold
water, minimal metabolism, 400\,Myr). Horseshoe
crab (ectothermic, months without food,
450\,Myr). Slow metabolism is confirmed as a
convergent trait of Lock organisms across
independent lineages.

\textit{Source:} Comparative metabolism
literature; tuatara biology (Cree, 2014).

\subsection{Generalist Diet and Ecological
Flexibility Predict Extinction Survival}
\textit{Derived:} The Navigator's survival
mechanism is dietary/ecological generalism.

\textit{Confirmed:} Capellini et al.\ (2008)
showed diet breadth is a significant predictor
of mammalian sleep duration. Barnosky et al.\
(2011) confirmed generalist diet as a predictor
of survival through mass extinction events.
Crocodilian K--Pg survival attributed
specifically to dietary generalism.

\textit{Source:} Capellini et al.\ (2008,
\textit{Proc Royal Soc B}); Barnosky et al.\
(2011).

Negatively Correlates With Lineage Age

\subsection{P-INVARIANT-3: The Unstable Zone
Boundary Is Predictable From Sleep Duration and
Dietary Breadth Alone}

A two-dimensional plot of sleep duration (y-axis)
against dietary breadth (x-axis) will show a
survival boundary:

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=0.2em]
  \item Low sleep + any dietary breadth = Lock
    strategy = high survival probability
  \item High sleep + high dietary breadth =
    Navigator strategy = high survival probability
  \item High sleep + low dietary breadth =
    Unstable zone = low survival probability
  \item Low sleep + low dietary breadth = Lock
    strategy = maximum survival probability
\end{itemize}

The boundary between survival and extinction risk
is predictable from these two measurements alone.

\subsection{P-INVARIANT-4: Extreme Specialists
Show No Sleep--Flexibility Correlation}

Extreme ecological specialists with high sleep
requirements (Axis~2 or 3 cost) will show no
correlation between sleep duration and cognitive
flexibility or learning rate --- unlike moderate-
sleep Navigator organisms, where sleep duration
tracks flexibility. The koala's high sleep pays
for detoxification. The lion's high sleep pays
for Basin~$\Omega$ navigation. Same sleep
duration. Different function. Distinguishable by
flexibility measurement.

\subsection{P-INVARIANT-5: Lock Organisms Are
Undomesticable}

Lock organisms --- those in the deepest single
basins with minimal sleep requirements --- will
show the greatest resistance to domestication of
any animal group. No Lock organism has ever been
domesticated. This is predicted by the dimensional
invariant: the same basin depth that allows
survival of mass extinctions renders the basin
immovable by any domestication field that does
not also destroy the organism.

\subsection{P-INVARIANT-6: The Dimensional
Invariant Is Detectable in Single-Celled
Organisms via Metabolic Rest State Duration}

Single-celled organisms show metabolic rest
states whose duration tracks their niche breadth
in the same direction as the invariant predicts
for multicellular organisms. Specialist bacteria
(obligate extremophiles) will show longer
continuous activity periods with minimal rest
relative to generalist bacteria (\textit{E.~coli},
\textit{B.~subtilis}). This extends the
dimensional invariant below the level of nervous
system entirely, confirming it as a geometric
property of basin topology, not of neural
architecture.
