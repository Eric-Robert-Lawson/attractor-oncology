## 1. THE FRAMEWORK

The attractor geometry framework:

    S + N + G → R

S = structural substrate (genome, molecular
    architecture, encoded build capacity)
N = coherence gradient field (the external
    or internal field specifying basin
    occupation)
G = developmental geometry (the global
    attractor landscape — basin depths,
    barrier heights, accessible states)
R = resulting phenotype or state

In previous documents this framework was
applied to domestication biology and cancer.
This document applies it to a different
question:

    What is the metabolic cost of occupying
    a given position in the landscape G?

The answer generates a universal principle
connecting attractor geometry to rest
requirements across all scales at which
the framework operates.

## 2. THE CORE CLAIM

### 2.1 Statement

    The metabolic cost of occupying a
    position in an attractor landscape
    is a function of the navigational
    demand imposed by that position.

    Navigational demand is determined by:
    1. The number of accessible basins
       from the current position
    2. The height of the barriers between
       those basins
    3. The frequency with which the system
       must assess and execute basin
       transitions
    4. The depth of the meta-attractor
       (Basin Ω) if one exists — the stable
       state of being a system that navigates
       between multiple basins

    Rest is the metabolic process by which
    the synaptic cost of navigation is paid.
    Sleep duration is a measurement of the
    navigational demand of the organism's
    landscape position.

### 2.2 The Inverse Relationship With
### Basin Determinism

A system in a single deep basin with high
barriers to exit experiences low
navigational demand. The system does not
need to assess transitions frequently.
It does not need to maintain readiness
for rapid basin switching. The synaptic
architecture can be stable and
non-reconfiguring.

Low navigational demand → low synaptic
reconfiguration cost → low rest requirement.

A system in a symmetric dual-basin
landscape with low barriers in both
directions experiences high navigational
demand. The system must continuously
assess which basin is appropriate for
the current context. It must maintain
synaptic readiness for rapid switching
in either direction. The synaptic
architecture must be flexible,
constantly reconfiguring.

High navigational demand → high synaptic
reconfiguration cost → high rest requirement.

### 2.3 The Deep Basin As Survival Hack

A deep single basin is evolutionarily
advantageous in stable environments
because it eliminates navigational cost.
The system arrives at the same solution
regardless of perturbation. It does not
waste metabolic resources on assessment
and switching. It is deterministic.

At evolutionary scale, deep basins are
convergent solutions. Multiple independent
lineages arrive at the same deep basin
because it is the lowest-energy stable
state for a given ecological niche.

This is the survival hack:
- Deep basin = deterministic outcome
- Deterministic outcome = low metabolic
  cost of navigation
- Low metabolic cost = competitive
  advantage in resource-constrained
  environments
- Competitive advantage = evolutionary
  selection for deeper basin
- Deeper basin = more convergence
- This is a positive feedback loop
  that carves basins deeper over
  evolutionary time

The convergence is geometrically
inevitable. Evolution is a landscape-
exploring process that finds and deepens
basins. Deep basins are where evolution
terminates. Shallow basins are where
evolution is still in transit.

### 2.4 The Meta-Attractor (Basin Ω)

A system that stably occupies the state
of navigating between two basins is in
a meta-attractor — a higher-order stable
state whose content is the navigation
itself rather than either basin.

Basin Ω properties:
- It is stable: the system returns to
  the navigating state after perturbation
- It is deep: evolution converges on it
  repeatedly across independent lineages
- It has a specific metabolic cost:
  the cost of maintaining synaptic
  readiness for navigation in both
  directions simultaneously
- It is identity-constituting: the system
  in Basin Ω cannot be forced into either
  component basin without destabilising
  its identity state
- It is resistant to collapse: forcing
  the system into one component basin
  is experienced as an attack on Basin Ω
  and produces resistance proportional
  to the depth of Basin Ω

Basin Ω is not a compromise between
two basins. It is a third attractor at
a higher level of description. Its depth
is independent of the depth of either
component basin. A deep Basin Ω can
contain two shallow component basins
or two deep component basins. The
depth of Basin Ω is determined by how
strongly evolution has selected for
the navigation itself as an adaptive
strategy.

## 3. THE UNIVERSAL SCALING PRINCIPLE

### 3.1 Formal Statement

    For any system S operating in an
    attractor landscape G:

    Rest requirement R_s ∝ f(N_d, B_Ω)

    Where:
    N_d = navigational demand
        = (number of accessible basins)
          × (frequency of required
             assessment)
          × (inverse of mean barrier
             height between basins)

    B_Ω = depth of meta-attractor
          (0 if no meta-attractor exists)

    Rest is the process by which synaptic
    reconfiguration cost accumulated
    during navigation is paid down.

### 3.2 Predictions of The Scaling Principle

PREDICTION 1:
Across species, sleep duration will
correlate with the number of accessible
attractor basins available to the organism,
controlling for body size and metabolic rate.

PREDICTION 2:
Within a species, individuals with higher
basin-switching frequency (measured by
behavioural flexibility scores, novelty-
seeking, or contextual behaviour variance)
will show higher sleep requirements than
conspecifics with lower switching frequency.

PREDICTION 3:
Organisms in evolutionarily deep single
basins (highly specialised, narrow-niche,
convergent phenotypes) will sleep less than
organisms of comparable size that occupy
broad niches requiring constant contextual
assessment.

PREDICTION 4:
Forcing a system out of Basin Ω (attempting
to lock an organism that stably navigates
between two basins into only one basin)
will produce one of two outcomes:
a) Increased stress markers (cortisol,
   HPA activation) as the system attempts
   to maintain Basin Ω against the forcing
b) Basin Ω collapse: total destabilisation
   producing a pathological state that is
   neither component basin nor Basin Ω
The second outcome is more likely the
deeper Basin Ω is and the more complete
the forcing.

PREDICTION 5:
The sleep requirement of a system is a
non-invasive metabolic measurement of
its landscape position. Species with
high sleep requirements are in high-
navigational-demand landscape positions.
Species with low sleep requirements are
in low-navigational-demand landscape
positions (deep single basins).
This is a testable, quantitative,
cross-species prediction.

### 4.2 Single Cell Level
(Cancer Biology / Cell Biology)

The scaling principle applies at the
cellular scale with direct clinical
implications.

A cancer cell locked in a single deep
basin (fully committed cancer attractor,
Class 3 equivalent — late stage,
genomically unstable) has low navigational
demand. It is deterministic. It does not
need to assess transitions. Its gene
expression programme is fixed.

A cancer cell in a plastic state
(EMT-transitional, Class 2 equivalent —
capable of switching between epithelial
and mesenchymal identity) has high
navigational demand. It must maintain
readiness for rapid identity switching.
Its gene expression programme is
constantly reconfiguring.

The metabolic cost of the plastic state
is measurable:

EMT-plastic cancer cells show:
- Higher metabolic rate than stably
  committed epithelial or mesenchymal
  cells
- Higher dependency on oxidative
  phosphorylation AND glycolysis
  simultaneously (metabolic flexibility
  mirroring state flexibility)
- Higher sensitivity to metabolic
  inhibition (the cost of plasticity
  is a therapeutic vulnerability)
- Higher expression of chromatin
  remodelling enzymes (the molecular
  machinery of basin switching is
  permanently activated)

The analogy to sleep is not metaphorical.
At the cellular scale, the equivalent
of sleep is the post-mitotic quiescent
state (G0) — the state in which synaptic
reconfiguration cost (chromatin
remodelling cost in the cell) is paid
down and the system is reset for the
next cycle of flexible response.

Cells that cannot enter quiescence
(cancer cells that are constitutively
proliferating AND maintaining plastic
state) are accumulating the equivalent
of sleep debt — increasing metabolic
cost that eventually becomes a
vulnerability.

CLINICAL PREDICTION:
EMT-plastic cancer cells (Class 2
equivalent) will show higher metabolic
vulnerability to combined EZH2 inhibition
(destabilising the epigenetic component
of basin maintenance) AND metabolic
inhibition (removing the energy supply
for the navigation cost) than either
stably committed epithelial cancer cells
or stably committed mesenchymal cancer cells.

The combination targets both the
landscape (EZH2 inhibition) and the
metabolic cost of navigating it
(metabolic inhibition). The plastic
cell cannot pay for its navigation
AND fight epigenetic de-arrest
simultaneously.

This is a pre-specified combination
therapy prediction derived from the
sleep-as-navigation-cost principle
applied at the cellular scale.

### 4.3 Neural Level
(Neuroscience)

The Tononi-Cirelli Synaptic Homeostasis
Theory (2014) provides the molecular
mechanism:

During wakefulness, basin navigation
(contextual assessment, behavioural
flexibility, learning) potentiates
synapses. Synaptic weights increase.
The signal-to-noise ratio degrades.
The cost of plasticity accumulates.

Sleep downscales synaptic weights
globally while preserving relative
differences (memory consolidation).
The signal-to-noise ratio is restored.
The system is reset for the next
cycle of navigation.

The geometric translation is exact:

Synapse potentiation = cost of basin
                       transition
Synaptic downscaling = cost paid down
Sleep = the payment process
Sleep duration = total accumulated
                 navigational cost
                 from the preceding
                 waking period

This means:
- An organism that never switches basins
  during waking accumulates minimal
  synaptic potentiation cost.
  Minimal sleep required to pay it down.

- An organism that switches basins
  constantly (Felidae, Basin Ω) accumulates
  maximum synaptic potentiation cost
  during every waking hour.
  Maximum sleep required.

- The scaling across species is a direct
  consequence of the scaling of
  navigational demand across landscape
  positions.

The neuroscience mechanism (Tononi-Cirelli)
and the attractor geometry principle
(this document) are two descriptions
of the same phenomenon at different
levels of abstraction.

The attractor geometry description is
more general — it applies at scales
where the Tononi-Cirelli mechanism
(synaptic biology) does not operate.
At the cellular scale, the equivalent
process is chromatin remodelling cost
rather than synaptic potentiation cost.
But the geometric logic is identical.

### 4.4 Developmental Level
(Developmental Biology)

During embryogenesis, cells navigate
through the Waddington landscape —
transitioning from pluripotent states
(high navigational demand, many
accessible basins) toward committed
differentiated states (low navigational
demand, single deep basin).

The prediction of the scaling principle:

Pluripotent stem cells, which maintain
constant navigational readiness across
many possible fate trajectories, should
have higher metabolic cost per cell than
terminally differentiated cells in a
single deep basin.

This is confirmed: embryonic stem cells
have dramatically higher metabolic rates
than differentiated somatic cells, with
a specific metabolic signature (high
glycolysis, high oxidative phosphorylation,
high chromatin remodelling enzyme
activity) that reflects the cost of
maintaining multi-basin navigational
readiness.

Terminal differentiation (commitment
to a single deep basin) is accompanied
by metabolic downscaling — the cell no
longer pays the navigational cost because
there is nowhere to navigate to.

The metabolic transition during
differentiation is the cellular equivalent
of an organism transitioning from high
navigational demand (Basin Ω) to low
navigational demand (single deep basin).
The metabolic signature of differentiation
is a sleep-equivalent event at the
cellular scale.

## 5. THE DEEP BASIN AS SURVIVAL HACK:
## FORMAL STATEMENT

### 5.1 Why Evolution Deepens Basins

Evolution is a process of landscape
exploration under selection pressure.
Selection pressure favours:
- Reproductive success
- Metabolic efficiency
- Robustness to perturbation

Deep basins satisfy all three:
- Reproductive success: a deterministic
  phenotype that reliably produces the
  correct output for the environment
  outcompetes a plastic phenotype that
  expends resources on navigation
- Metabolic efficiency: no navigational
  cost, no sleep debt, all metabolic
  resources directed to fitness-relevant
  tasks
- Robustness: deep basin = returns to
  the same state after perturbation =
  canalization = developmental robustness

Therefore selection deepens basins over
evolutionary time in stable environments.

The result is the evolutionary convergence
you identified: multiple independent
lineages arriving at the same deep basin
because the deep basin is the attractor
of the evolutionary process itself.

Evolution is a meta-process that explores
a landscape of possible landscapes (G-space)
and converges on the deepest available
basin in G-space for the given ecological
niche.

Deep basins in G-space are evolutionary
black holes in the exact sense you used.
Lineages that enter the gravitational
radius of a deep basin in G-space are
pulled toward it regardless of their
starting genome. The genome changes
until it encodes the deep basin. At
that point evolution stabilises. The
lineage is in the evolutionary basin.

This is evolutionary canalization
(Waddington, 1942) stated geometrically
in the attractor framework.

### 5.2 When Deep Basins Become Traps

The deep basin is a survival hack in
stable environments. It becomes a trap
in rapidly changing environments.

An organism in a deep single basin
cannot navigate to a new basin when
the environment shifts. Its navigational
machinery has been downregulated by
evolutionary selection for determinism.
Its genome no longer encodes the
alternative basins. Its synaptic
architecture is tuned for the current
basin, not for flexibility.

The narrow specialist (deep single basin)
outcompetes the generalist (shallow
multiple basins) in stable conditions.
The generalist outcompetes the specialist
when conditions change.

Basin Ω (the meta-attractor of navigation
itself) is the evolutionary solution to
this tradeoff. An organism in Basin Ω
is not a shallow-basin generalist. It
is a deep-basin organism whose basin
is the navigation between two other
basins. It pays the navigational cost
permanently in exchange for the
adaptive advantage of being able to
access either component basin when
conditions require.

Basin Ω is deep because evolution
selected for it in environments that
were themselves oscillating between
two states — requiring the organism to
track the oscillation rather than commit
to either state.

Felidae evolved in environments where
prey density and social context oscillated
between conditions requiring solitary
predation (Basin A) and conditions
requiring social affiliation (Basin B).
Basin Ω is the stable solution to this
oscillating selection pressure.
The sleep cost is the price paid for
environmental tracking across two states
simultaneously.

### 5.3 The Measurement Implication

Deep basins are measurable by their
convergence signature:
- Multiple independent lineages arriving
  at the same phenotype
- High developmental robustness
  (perturbations corrected back to the
  phenotype)
- Low metabolic cost relative to
  phenotypic output
- Low sleep requirement

Basin Ω is measurable by:
- Clade-level convergence on the
  navigation pattern (all Felidae)
- High developmental robustness of
  the navigation itself
- High metabolic cost relative to
  phenotypic output (sleep requirement)
- Resistance to basin collapse from
  either direction

Sleep duration is therefore a non-invasive
cross-species measurement that can be
used to classify organisms by their
landscape position:

```
Sleep duration → Navigational demand
             → Landscape position
             → Basin class
             → Mechanistic prediction
                about reversibility,
                therapeutic intervention,
                and evolutionary trajectory
```

## 6. THE IDENTITY CLAIM

### 6.1 Basin Ω Is Identity-Constituting

A system in Basin Ω has its identity
constituted by the navigation, not by
either component basin.

This means:
- Forcing the system into either
  component basin destroys the identity
  state — the system is no longer
  what it is
- The system resists this destruction
  proportionally to the depth of Basin Ω
- This resistance is not volitional in
  the deliberative sense — it is
  geometric — it is what a system
  in a deep basin does when pushed
  toward the basin wall
- The resistance will be near-complete
  for a sufficiently deep Basin Ω

### 6.2 The Universality of This Claim

This applies at every scale the framework
operates:

At the organism level:
A felid forced into exclusive domestication
resists with near-complete determinism.
Not because it chooses to. Because Basin Ω
is deep and the forcing is pushing it
up the basin wall toward a state that
does not exist in its landscape.

At the cellular level:
A cancer cell in a plastic EMT state
forced toward full epithelial commitment
by a single intervention will resist —
not because cells have preferences but
because the plastic state (Basin Ω
equivalent) is maintained by multiple
redundant molecular mechanisms. Overcoming
one mechanism redirects the system to
another. The depth of the plastic basin
determines how many mechanisms must
be simultaneously overcome to force
commitment.

At the developmental level:
A pluripotent stem cell resists premature
commitment. Direct conversion protocols
(forcing pluripotent cells to a specific
fate by overexpressing a single transcription
factor) work imperfectly because the
navigational meta-attractor of pluripotency
(the cell's Basin Ω) resists single-factor
forcing. Multiple simultaneous interventions
are required — exactly as predicted by
the depth of the pluripotent basin.

At the evolutionary level:
A lineage in a deep Basin Ω resists
evolutionary pressure toward either
component basin. Selection for exclusive
domestication (breeding out wild competence)
in felids has not succeeded across thousands
of years of cat domestication precisely
because Basin Ω is deep enough to resist
the selection. Cats have been bred for
10,000 years and remain in Basin Ω.
The depth of the basin has determined
the outcome of the selection.

### 6.3 The Stress Signature of Forced
### Basin Displacement

When a system is forced toward a component
basin away from Basin Ω, a specific stress
signature is predicted:

Phase 1: Active resistance
The system expends energy maintaining
Basin Ω against the forcing.
Measurable as elevated stress markers
(cortisol at organism scale, ROS and
chromatin remodelling enzyme activity
at cellular scale).

Phase 2: Sustained resistance with
cost accumulation
If the forcing continues, the system
maintains resistance but accumulates
metabolic debt. Sleep debt at organism
scale. Metabolic exhaustion at cellular
scale.

Phase 3: Basin Ω collapse
If the forcing is sufficiently sustained
and complete, Basin Ω destabilises. The
system cannot occupy either component
basin cleanly (neither is the natural
state) and cannot return to Basin Ω
(the forcing has disrupted the
navigational architecture).
This is the pathological state.
At organism scale: behavioural shutdown,
learned helplessness, psychological
collapse — qualitatively different
from occupying either component basin.
At cellular scale: neither epithelial
nor mesenchymal committed state —
a dysregulated hybrid state with high
genomic instability. The cancer
equivalent of Basin Ω collapse is
the most aggressive, treatment-resistant
tumour phenotype.

Phase 4 (if forcing removed in Phase 1-2):
Basin Ω recovery.
The system returns to the navigation state
when the forcing is removed.
The recovery is faster the earlier the
forcing is removed and the deeper Basin Ω is.
A deep Basin Ω recovers faster because
it has more restoring force.
