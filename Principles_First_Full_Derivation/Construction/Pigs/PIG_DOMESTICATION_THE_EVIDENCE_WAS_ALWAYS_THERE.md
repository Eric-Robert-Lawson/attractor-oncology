## PART II — MARCSTRÖM AND ESSEN-MÖLLER (1968):
## THE BURIED CONFIRMATION

### 2.1 What They Did

Marcström and Essen-Möller took wild boar
(Sus scrofa) — genetically wild, captured
from wild populations — and raised them
in captivity.

No selective breeding.
No intentional domestication program.
No tameness selection.
No cross-breeding with domestic pigs.

They simply applied the domestication
coherence gradient field:
- Protection from predation
- Ad libitum food provision
- Stable social environment
- Human contact and management

### 2.2 What They Found

In the first captive generation — offspring
of wild-caught animals raised entirely under
the domestication field — the following
appeared spontaneously:

**White piebald patches:**
Areas of complete depigmentation.
White patches on the coat.
Absent in the wild-caught parents.
Present in F1 captive offspring.
Neural crest origin: melanocytes fail
to fully migrate and populate the skin.

**Widening and drooping of ears:**
Ear cartilage becomes less rigid.
Ears begin to droop laterally.
Absent in wild parents.
Present in F1 captive offspring.
Neural crest origin: ear cartilage is
neural crest-derived. Reduced migration
= reduced cartilage density = floppy ears.

**Skull shape changes:**
Movement toward shorter, wider craniofacial
profile. Snout shortening begins.
Neural crest origin: craniofacial bones
are predominantly neural crest-derived.

**Curly tails:**
Wild boar have straight tails.
F1 captive offspring begin to show
curling. Connective tissue and vertebral
morphology changes.

### 2.3 The Critical Point

These changes appeared:
- In the first captive generation
- Without selective breeding
- Without intentional domestication
- In animals with fully wild genomes
- Simply by changing the field

This is not an ambiguous result.
This is your prediction confirmed.
In 1968.

### 2.4 How The Literature Read This

Standard interpretation:

> "Relaxation of natural selection allows
> pre-existing recessive alleles for domestic
> traits to express. The wild population was
> carrying these alleles at low frequency.
> Without predation selecting against them,
> they express in captivity."

This interpretation has a fatal quantitative
failure:

If the effect were allele-based, the traits
should appear in the fraction of individuals
carrying two copies of the relevant recessive
alleles. In a wild population, these alleles
would be at low frequency. Expected expression
rate in F1: low, variable, genotype-dependent.

What was actually observed: the traits
appeared in the majority of F1 captive
individuals, at rates inconsistent with
any plausible recessive allele frequency
in a wild founding population.

The frequency is too high.
The consistency is too great.
The speed is too fast.

This is not allele expression.
This is field-specified developmental
program modification at the population level.

Every individual exposed to the field
is affected — not just those carrying
specific alleles — because the field
acts on the developmental program,
not on the allele.

### 4.1 Hemmer (1990): The HPA Axis
### as the Primary Field Transducer

Hemmer's 1990 analysis documented that
adrenal gland size reduction is one of the

The adrenal medulla is neural crest-derived.
Its size reflects the degree to which neural
crest migration to the adrenal anlage was
completed during embryogenesis.

In wild animals: full neural crest migration
to adrenal → large adrenal medulla →
high catecholamine capacity → high
stress/flight response.

In captive/domestic animals: arrested
neural crest migration → smaller adrenal
medulla → reduced catecholamine capacity
→ reduced stress/flight response.

Hemmer observed the endpoint.
He did not state the mechanism
(neural crest arrest specified by field).
He did not have the framework to state it.

## PART VII — THE SEQUENCE OF REVERSION
## AS A DEVELOPMENTAL RESUMPTION SIGNATURE

### 7.1 The Observed Sequence in Feralization

When a domestic pig is released into
the wild, reversion to wild-type occurs
in a specific sequence:

1. Behaviour reverts first (days to weeks)
2. Hair coarsens (weeks to months)
3. Body composition shifts (months)
4. Craniofacial changes — in offspring,
   not in the adult (F1 generation)
5. Full skull morphology — multi-
   generational

### 7.2 Why This Sequence Is Geometrically
### Necessary

This is not a random order.
It is the order of developmental
plasticity from highest to lowest:

Behaviour: regulated by epigenetic
states in the nervous system.
No structural remodelling required.
Fastest to reverse.

Hair: follicle cycling is rapid.
Each new hair cycle can express the
modified program. Fast.

Body composition: metabolic reprogramming
is continuous. Moderate speed.

Craniofacial: bone is slow to remodel
in adults. Full expression requires the
embryonic neural crest program to run
to completion in the next generation.
Slowest.

**This sequence is the developmental
arrest model's signature.**

The traits that were most recently
arrested (those regulated by epigenetic
states rather than structural development)
revert fastest when the arrest is lifted.

The traits that were arrested earliest
in the developmental program (those
requiring embryonic completion) revert
slowest and most fully only in offspring
born under the new field.

If the standard genetic model were
correct (feralization = allele frequency
change), there would be no reason to
predict this specific sequence.
Allele frequencies change at equivalent
rates across all traits.

The sequence is only predicted by
the developmental arrest model.
The sequence is what is observed.
