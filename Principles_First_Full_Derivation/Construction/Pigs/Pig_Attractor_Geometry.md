## PART II — THE NEURAL CREST MECHANISM

### 2.1 What Wilkins, Wrangham and Fitch (2014) Found

The 2014 paper identified a single developmental
perturbation that accounts for every trait of
the domestication syndrome across every
domesticated species:

**Mild reduction in neural crest cell migration.**

Neural crest cells are multipotent embryonic cells
that migrate from the dorsal neural tube during
early embryogenesis and differentiate into:

- Ear cartilage → floppy ears when migration reduced
- Craniofacial structures → shorter snout, smaller
  jaw, smaller teeth when migration reduced
- Pigment cells (melanocytes) → depigmentation,
  white patches when migration reduced
- Adrenal medulla (chromaffin cells) → smaller
  adrenal gland, reduced stress response when
  migration reduced
- Peripheral nervous system components → reduced
  fear/aggression circuitry when migration reduced

When neural crest migration is mildly reduced,
all traits of the domestication syndrome appear
simultaneously. Not separately. Not independently.
Together. Because they all derive from the same
developmental program running to a lesser degree
of completion.

### 2.2 The Geometric Reading of This Finding

The Wilkins et al. paper identified the mechanism
without having the framework to state what the
mechanism is.

In attractor geometry terms:

**Neural crest cell migration IS the developmental
trajectory from Basin B (juvenile-proximate) toward
Basin A (adult wild-type).**

The migration program has a starting state
(neural crest cells at dorsal neural tube),
a trajectory (directed migration through
embryonic tissue), and an endpoint (full
differentiation into all neural crest-derived
tissues).

Full migration to completion = Basin A = wild boar.

Arrested migration at early stage = Basin B
= domestic pig.

Domestication is not the addition of new genes
for domestic traits. It is the field-specified
arrest of the neural crest migration trajectory
at the juvenile-proximate stage.

The domestication syndrome is not a collection
of separately selected traits. It is the
phenotypic signature of neural crest migration
arrested at one specific developmental stage.

Every trait appears together because they all
derive from the same arrested trajectory.

### 2.3 The Field Dependence of Neural Crest Migration

Neural crest cell migration is not genetically
autonomous. It is guided by coherence gradients:

- BMP gradient fields

## PART III — THE FERALIZATION MECHANISM

### 3.1 Feralization Is Developmental Resumption

The standard reading of feralization:

> Domestic pigs that escape into the wild
> revert to wild-type phenotype as natural
> selection favours wild-type alleles.

This reading requires allele frequency change.
Allele frequency change in a single individual
across months is impossible.

Morphological changes in feral pigs occur in
months in single individuals. The reading is
wrong for the individual timescale.

The geometric reading:

Feralization is not genetic reversion.
It is developmental resumption.

When the domestication field is removed:
- HPA axis suppression is removed
- Cortisol and stress hormone levels rise
  (predation pressure, food scarcity,
  temperature stress)

  in the next generation
- Neural crest migration is no longer arrested
  at the juvenile-proximate stage
- The developmental trajectory resumes toward
  Basin A
- Wild-type traits emerge as the trajectory
  completes

In the individual adult pig already born:
The morphological changes (hair coarsening,
tusk development, body composition shift)

mechanisms that specified the developmental
arrest in the first place. The arrest was
maintained by continuous field-dependent
epigenetic states. Removing the field destabilises
those states. The wild-type developmental program
de-represses and partially resumes even in the
adult organism.

### 3.2 The Sequence of Reversion

The sequence of morphological reversion in
feral pigs follows a specific order:

1. Behaviour reverts first (days to weeks)
   → fastest because: no structural remodelling
   required. Epigenetic changes in nervous system
   are sufficient. The HPA axis responds
   immediately to predation pressure.

2. Hair coarsens (weeks to months)
   → faster because: hair follicle cycling
   is rapid. Each new hair cycle can express
   the modified program.

3. Body composition shifts (months)
   → moderate speed: metabolic reprogramming
   is continuous but structural.

4. Craniofacial elongation (months to F1)
   → slower because: bone remodelling is slow
   in adults. Full expression requires
   embryonic-stage neural crest program
   running to completion in the next
   generation.

5. Tusk development (months to years)
   → dependent on sustained hormonal change
   driving continuous tissue modification.

**This sequence is a direct prediction of the
developmental arrest model.** The traits that
were most recently arrested (behavioural,
epigenetically maintained) revert fastest.
The traits that were arrested earliest
(craniofacial, requiring embryonic completion)
revert slowest and most fully only in offspring
born under the feral field.

**The juvenile striping is not a selected trait.
