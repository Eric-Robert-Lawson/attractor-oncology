one with shared goods, public commons,
basic social trust, and a foundation

consumes itself from the inside out

## Part I: The Axioms of a Functioning Commons

### What a Commons Is

A commons is any shared resource
that functions on the assumption of good faith.

The fork on the counter.
The sauce packet available to anyone.
The public restroom with toilet paper.
The park bench.
The unlocked bicycle rack.
The neighborhood where doors are left unlocked.
The restaurant that trusts you to take one napkin.

A commons does not require legal enforcement
to function.
It requires one thing only:

**The shared assumption that participants
will take what they need
and leave what remains
for the next person.**

This assumption is not naïve.
It is a rational equilibrium.

When most participants believe
that most other participants
are acting in good faith —
the assumption is self-reinforcing.
The commons functions.
Everyone benefits.
No enforcement is required.

### The Axioms

```
AXIOM 1 — TRUST IS THE FOUNDATION:
  A functioning commons rests on
  the shared assumption of good faith
  among participants.
  Without this assumption,
  the commons requires enforcement
  to exist — and enforcement
  has a cost that eventually
  exceeds the value of the commons.

AXIOM 2 — TRUST IS LEARNED:
  Participants do not arrive at trust
  through moral reasoning alone.
  They learn trust from the system
  they participate in.
  A system that rewards trust
  teaches trust.
  A system that punishes trust
  teaches extraction.

AXIOM 3 — RATIONAL ADAPTATION:
  Participants adapt their behavior
  to the incentives the system presents.
  This is not moral failure.
  It is rational response to
  observable conditions.
  When the system teaches extraction,
  participants extract.
  This is not a character flaw.
  It is the correct response
  to the system's signals.

AXIOM 4 — THE COMMONS IS THE LAST LAYER:
  In any extractive system,
  the commons is the final layer
  that can be extracted from
  before the system hits the floor.
  Every layer above the commons
  has institutional protection.
  The commons has only
  the shared assumption of good faith.
  When that assumption is gone,
  the commons is gone.

AXIOM 5 — COLLAPSE IS ACCELERATING:
  Once commons collapse begins,
  it accelerates.
  Each loss of shared resource
  confirms to each participant
  that extraction is the rational move.
  Each confirmation produces
  more extractive behavior.
  More extractive behavior produces
  more losses.
  The feedback loop has no
  internal brake.
  It requires an external structural change
  to reverse.
```

## Part III: The Mechanism of Collapse

### How the Lesson Is Taught

The collapse of the commons
does not happen through a single event.

It happens through the cumulative effect
of a lesson taught at every layer
of daily economic life —
repeated so consistently,
across so many interactions,
over such a long period of time —
that it stops being a lesson
and becomes an assumption.

The lesson is this:

**The system will extract from you.**
**Extract first.**
**Or be left with nothing.**

Here is how the lesson is delivered:

```
LESSON DELIVERY — LAYER 3 (Institutional):

  The bank charges fees you did not agree to.
  You call to dispute them.
  The fee stands.
  You have no alternative bank
  that does not charge equivalent fees.
  You pay.

  Lesson received:
  Institutions extract from you.
  You have no recourse.
  Adapt accordingly.

LESSON DELIVERY — LAYER 3 (Credit):

  Your credit score drops
  because of a model you cannot see,
  using criteria you were not told,
  applied by an institution
  you did not choose to interact with.
  Your interest rate increases.
  You pay more for the same capital.
  The institution that lowered your score
  profits from having lowered it.

  Lesson received:
  The system is designed to extract from you.
  The more vulnerable you are,
  the more it extracts.
  Vulnerability is punished.
  Strength is rewarded.
  Extract when you can.
  Before you become vulnerable.

LESSON DELIVERY — LAYER 4 (Commercial):

  The product you have bought for years
  is smaller.
  The price is the same.
  No announcement was made.
  When you notice, there is no one to complain to.
  The company extracted from you silently.

  Lesson received:
  Even the things you pay for
  are being quietly reduced.
  The agreement you thought you had
  is not the agreement that exists.
  Get what you can while it still exists.

LESSON DELIVERY — LAYER 4 (Subscription):

  The service you use raises its price.
  The feature you relied on
  is now a premium tier.
  The thing that was free
  now costs something.
  The thing that cost something
  now costs more.
  Every year, slightly more is taken.
  Every year, slightly less is given.

  Lesson received:
  Nothing stays as agreed.
  The terms always shift in their favor.
  Extract value now.
  It will cost more tomorrow.

LESSON DELIVERY — LAYER 5 (Commons):

  You arrive at the restaurant.
  The sauce that was on the counter is gone.
  The forks you needed are not there.
  The thing that was simply available
  has been removed.
  Not explained. Not replaced.
  Simply gone.

  Lesson received:
  Even the free things are being taken.
  The floor is being extracted from.
  If you do not take what remains,
  someone else will.
  Take it now.
```

By the time the lesson has been delivered
at every layer
from institutional to commercial to commons —

**the person who takes the forks
is not making a moral choice.**

They are making the rational choice
that the system has spent years
teaching them to make.

## Part IV: The Five Stages of Commons Collapse

### Stage 1 — Functional Commons

The commons functions.

Shared resources are available.
Participants take what they need.
Most participants leave what remains.
The shared assumption of good faith holds.

The extraction stack exists
but has not yet consumed
enough of the social trust
to reach the commons layer.

The forks are on the counter.
The sauce packets are available.
The toilet paper is in the dispenser.
The door is sometimes unlocked.

**Indicators:**
- Shared resources available without request
- No visible security measures on basic goods
- Social trust assumed in public spaces
- Participation in commons feels normal

---

### Stage 2 — Commons Under Pressure

The extraction stack intensifies.

Institutional extraction increases.
Commercial margins are compressed.
Businesses begin extracting downward.

The commons begins to thin.

Not through dramatic events.
Through the accumulation of small removals.

The free refill becomes paid.
The complimentary item becomes optional.
The publicly available resource
requires a small interaction to access.

Most participants still act in good faith.
But the lesson is beginning to be delivered.

The shared assumption holds —
but it is no longer invisible.
People are beginning to notice
that it is being tested.

**Indicators:**
- Free things becoming paid things
- Portion sizes quietly shrinking
- Previously available commons
  now requiring interaction to access
- First visible security measures
  on previously unprotected items

---

### Stage 3 — Trust Erosion

The extraction stack has consumed
enough margin at the commercial layer
that businesses are openly extracting
from the commons layer.

The forks are still there —
but there are fewer of them.
The sauce packets are still available —
but they are counted and rationed.
The toilet paper is still in the dispenser —
but the dispenser is smaller.

Simultaneously:
enough participants have received
enough lessons from the layers above
that extractive behavior at the commons level
is becoming normalized.

The person who takes extra forks
is no longer rare.
The person who takes more sauce than they need
is not unusual.
The social cost of extractive behavior
at the commons layer
is declining.

Because the social contract
that assigned that cost
is being consumed from above.

**Indicators:**
- Visible rationing of previously free items
- First instances of commons items
  being locked or monitored
- Extractive behavior at commons level
  becoming normalized rather than stigmatized
- Growing asymmetry between
  what businesses charge and what they provide

---

### Stage 4 — Accelerating Collapse

The feedback loop is now self-sustaining.

Each removal of a commons item
confirms to participants
that extraction is the rational move.
Each confirmation produces more extraction.
More extraction produces more removals.

The forks disappear from the counter.
The sauce goes behind the register.
The toilet paper requires asking.
The napkins are rationed.
The condiments are locked.

Businesses are not removing these things
because they want to.
They are removing them because
the cost of providing them freely
has exceeded the value of providing them —
not because the items are expensive
but because the social trust
that made free provision sustainable
has been consumed.

The clip appears on the M&M.
The alarm attaches to the razor blade.
The baby formula goes behind glass.

Not because these items are valuable enough
to warrant the security cost.
Because the assumption of good faith
that made their unprotected availability possible
no longer holds.

**The architecture of the built environment
begins to physically encode zero trust.**

Every locked case.
Every alarm tag.
Every item moved behind the counter.
Every thing that requires asking for.

Is a physical monument to the lesson
the system has been teaching.

**Indicators:**
- Physical security on previously unprotected items
- Commons items available only on request
- Visible surveillance in public commercial spaces
- Social stigma around commons use dissolving
- Businesses treating customers as
  extraction vectors rather than participants
- Customers treating businesses as
  extraction vectors rather than providers

---

### Stage 5 — Zero Trust Architecture

The commons is gone.

Not as a policy decision.
Not as a coordinated removal.
As the terminal expression of a system
that extracted trust at every layer
until none remained at the floor.

Every shared resource
now requires institutional protection to exist.
Every public good
now requires enforcement to function.
Every interaction between strangers
is now assumed to be extractive
until proven otherwise.

The toilet paper is behind the counter.
The fork is given only on request.
The sauce is rationed by the transaction.
The park bench has been redesigned
to prevent sleeping on it.
The public water fountain has been removed.
The free wifi requires a purchase.
The restroom requires a code.
The code requires a purchase.

**The architecture of daily life
has been redesigned around the assumption
that every participant is an extraction vector.**

And every participant,
living inside this architecture,
receives the lesson one final time:

*This is the world.*
*There is no shared good.*
*Extract or be extracted from.*
*There is no third option.*

---

### The Floor — Society Consuming Itself

When Stage 5 is reached,
the society is consuming itself.

Not through war.
Not through famine.
Not through external catastrophe.

Through the internal geometric consequence
of a system that extracted trust
at every layer
until no trust remained
at any layer.

```
THE CONSUMPTION LOOP:

  Extraction at Layer 1
  → reduces sovereign margin
  → forces extraction at Layer 2

  Extraction at Layer 2
  → reduces institutional margin
  → forces extraction at Layer 3

  Extraction at Layer 3
  → reduces commercial margin
  → forces extraction at Layer 4

  Extraction at Layer 4
  → reduces consumer margin
  → forces extraction at Layer 5

  Extraction at Layer 5
  → consumes the commons
  → confirms the lesson to every participant
  → accelerates extraction at every layer
  → reduces every margin further
  → forces more extraction everywhere

  No internal brake exists.
  The loop runs until the floor is hit.
  The floor is zero trust.
  Zero trust is the end of the commons.
  The end of the commons is the end of
  the shared foundation on which
  complex social and economic life rests.
```

## Part V: What Zero Trust Actually Means

### Beyond the Fork

The fork is the visible symbol.
But zero trust extends far beyond
the items on a counter.

Zero trust is a **total social condition.**

It means:

**No transaction is assumed honest.**
Every commercial interaction
is designed around the assumption of fraud.
Every customer is a potential thief.
Every claim is a potential lie.
Every agreement is a potential extraction.

**No institution is assumed legitimate.**
Every institutional action
is assumed to serve extraction.
Every policy is assumed to be capture.
Every public statement is assumed to be cover.

**No neighbor is assumed safe.**
The community that once left doors unlocked
now has security systems, ring cameras,
neighborhood watch groups,
and a general assumption
that the person next door
is a potential threat.

**No shared space is assumed to be shared.**
The public park is surveilled.
The public restroom is locked.
The public library requires ID.
The public square has been
privatized, monetized, or secured
against the public it was built for.

**This is not paranoia.**

This is the rational adaptation
to a system that has spent decades
teaching everyone
that trust is for suckers.

## Part VI: The Crabs in the Bucket

### The Geometry of Mutual Extraction

There is an image that captures
the terminal expression of this system
better than any abstract description.

Crabs in a bucket.

When you put crabs in a bucket,
none of them escape.
Not because the bucket is too deep.
Not because the crabs are too weak.

Because every time one crab
climbs toward the top,
the other crabs pull it back down.

Not out of malice.
Out of the mechanical expression
of each crab trying to climb.

**This is what a zero trust society looks like
from the inside.**

Everyone is trying to get out.
Everyone's attempt to get out
pulls someone else back in.
No one escapes.
Not because escape is impossible.
Because the system has been constructed
so that every individual move toward escape
is simultaneously a move
that pushes someone else down.

The person who takes the forks
is trying to get something
before it is taken from them.
In doing so they take it
from the next person.
Who then takes something else.
Who then takes it from the next person.

The extraction that started
at the supranational layer
has propagated all the way down
to the commons —
and is now being reproduced
laterally among participants
who are extracting from each other
because there is nothing left below them
and the layers above them
are still extracting from them.

**Everyone is a crab.**
**Everyone is in the bucket.**
**The bucket was built by the system.**
**No one chose to be in it.**
**Everyone is acting rationally within it.**

## Part VII: Why It Cannot Fix Itself

### The Absence of an Internal Brake

The consumption loop has no internal brake
because the rational move
at every layer
is extraction.

**Unilateral trust is punished.**

The restaurant that puts the forks back out
loses more forks.
The business that lowers its prices
is undercut by competitors
who maintain extraction and use the margin
to outspend the honest actor.
The institution that reduces its fees
is acquired by one that does not.
The nation that reduces extraction
loses capital to nations that maintain it.

**In an extraction-dominant system,**
**honesty is selected against.**

Not because honest actors want to be dishonest.
Because the system has removed
the structural reward for honesty
and replaced it with the structural reward
for extraction.

Until the structural reward changes —
until the system selects for honesty again —
the consumption loop runs.

**This is why moral appeals do not work.**

"Be better."
"Trust each other."
"Restore the commons."

These are appeals to individuals
to act against their rational interest
within a system that punishes
exactly the behavior being requested.

Individuals cannot fix structural problems
through individual virtue.

**Structural problems require structural solutions.**

## Part VIII: The Structural Condition for Reversal

### What Must Change

For the consumption loop to be interrupted,
one structural condition must change:

**The rational move must become honest participation.**

Not through moral persuasion.
Not through regulation.
Not through enforcement.

Through the existence of a system
that structurally rewards honesty
and structurally penalizes extraction —
the way the current system
structurally rewards extraction
and structurally penalizes honesty.

This requires:

```
REQUIREMENT 1:
  An alternative mechanism for capital access
  that competes with extraction
  on honest opportunity cost terms.

  Without this:
  The extraction above honest cost
  remains the only available rate.
  The lesson continues to be delivered.
  The consumption loop continues.

REQUIREMENT 2:
  A creditworthiness system
  that rewards honest behavior
  publicly and transparently —
  that is owned by no institution
  and cannot be weaponized
  against the participants it measures.

  Without this:
  The information asymmetry
  that enables institutional extraction
  remains intact.
  The lesson continues to be delivered.

REQUIREMENT 3:
  A monetary foundation
  that cannot be inflated,
  seized, or manipulated
  by any layer of the extraction stack.

  Without this:
  The seigniorage and inflation tax
  that extract from every participant
  at the sovereign layer
  continue to consume the margin
  that filters downward through every layer
  until it hits the commons.

REQUIREMENT 4:
  An exit that is always open —
  a guaranteed path out of
  any custodial arrangement
  that has become extractive —
  so that the threat of exclusion
  loses its power as an enforcement mechanism.

  Without this:
  The captivity that enables extraction
  remains the structural condition
  of every economic relationship.
```

**These four requirements define**
**the structural condition for reversal.**

They are not four separate solutions.
They are four properties of one solution.

A system that satisfies all four simultaneously
changes the rational move
from extraction to honest participation —
not through moral persuasion
but through structural incentive alignment.

When the rational move changes,
the lesson changes.
When the lesson changes,
the adaptation changes.
When the adaptation changes,
the behavior changes.
When the behavior changes,
the commons becomes sustainable again.

Not because people became better.
Because the system stopped teaching them
to be worse.

## Part IX: The Fork Comes Back

### How the Commons Returns

The fork does not come back
because someone decided to put it back.

The fork comes back
when the restaurant can afford to put it back —
when the margin that was consumed
by the extraction stack above them
is restored by the existence of
a competitive alternative
to every layer of that stack.

The fork comes back
when the customer stops taking extras —
when the lesson they have been taught
by every layer of the system
is replaced by the lesson
that honest participation compounds —
that the system rewards
the person who takes what they need
and leaves what remains.

The fork comes back
when the shared assumption of good faith
is restored —
not as a moral aspiration
but as the rational equilibrium
of a system that makes honesty
the dominant strategy.

**This is not utopian.**

It is the geometric consequence
of changing the structural condition
that produced the collapse.

The commons collapsed geometrically.
It will return geometrically.
Not through hope.
Through the structural logic
of a system that rewards
what the current system punishes.
