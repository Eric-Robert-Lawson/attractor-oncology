## 1. THE SYSTEM DEFINED

Bitcoin is a **closed system with a supply ceiling and a constant leakage rate.**

The ceiling is absolute: 21,000,000 BTC total. No authority can change this.  
The leakage is structural: coins enter permanent illiquidity through loss, mandated custody, and sovereign lock.  
The supply entering circulation is decaying: the halving mechanism cuts new issuance by 50% every ~4 years.

**The operative question is not whether scarcity exists.**  
It already exists at the protocol level.

**The operative question is: at what rate is the demand floor permanently absorbing the available float,**  
and what does the arithmetic of that absorption produce as a price consequence?

The answer to that question is the subject of this document.

### System Boundaries

```
TOTAL SUPPLY CAP:          21,000,000 BTC (immutable)
TOTAL MINED (May 2026):   ~19,720,000 BTC
REMAINING TO MINE:          ~1,280,000 BTC (over ~120 years)
DAILY NEW ISSUANCE:               450 BTC/day
  (3.125 BTC/block × 144 blocks/day post-April 2024 halving)
NEXT HALVING:              ~April 2028 → 225 BTC/day
```

**The supply ceiling is not static. It is shrinking.**  
Every halving compresses new issuance by 50%.  
Every coin that enters permanent custody exits the tradeable float forever.  
The system leaks supply at a rate that is structurally accelerating.

## 5. THE STRUCTURAL PRICE DYNAMIC

### 5.1 The Fundamental Mechanism

Bitcoin's pricing is not driven by sentiment in a supply-constrained demand environment.  
It is driven by one arithmetic: **the price required to incentivize existing holders to sell.**

When demand permanently exceeds supply:
- Buyers must bid higher to find sellers
- Sellers require higher prices to abandon mandated positions
- The price discovery mechanism shifts from exchange speculation to **bilateral OTC negotiation between large holders**

This is not a bull market. It is a **structural repricing** of an asset where the available float is being systematically eliminated by entities that have stated publicly they will never sell.

### 6.4 The Stablecoin Yield Ban — The Structural Moat It Creates

**GENIUS Act (now law)** bans yield on payment stablecoins. [45][46]

This is structurally significant for the demand floor in a non-obvious way:

The banking lobby banned stablecoin yield to protect deposit bases. In doing so, they have:
1. **Eliminated the primary competitor to Bitcoin custodial delegation** — you cannot earn yield on USD-pegged instruments outside the banking system
2. **Created regulatory space** for Bitcoin-denominated custodial benefit exchange (services for delegation), which is definitionally NOT yield
3. **Forced yield-seeking behavior** toward Bitcoin appreciation as the only non-regulated return mechanism available

The White House's own economists found the yield ban creates a **net welfare loss of $800 million** and has minimal benefit to bank lending (only $2.1B increase). [46]

**The banking lobby succeeded in banning the mechanism that competes with Bitcoin.**  
The structural irony: by eliminating stablecoin yield, they accelerated the gravitational pull of the demand floor toward Bitcoin as the only uncapped, sovereign, yield-equivalent asset available outside the regulated banking system.
