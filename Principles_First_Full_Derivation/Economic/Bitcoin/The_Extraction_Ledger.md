**The compound lifetime calculation — shown in full:**

```
INPUT: $10,000/year contributed for 35 years
Gross market return: 7%/year (conservative, inflation-adjusted)

FORMULA:
  Future Value = Annual_Contribution × [(1 + net_rate)^years - 1]
                                        ─────────────────────────
                                                net_rate

  Net return: 7% - 2% = 5%
  FV = $10,000 × [(1.05^35 - 1) / 0.05]
  FV = $10,000 × 90.32
  FV = $903,200

SCENARIO B: Index fund, self-directed (0.05% fees)
  Net return: 7% - 0.05% = 6.95%
  FV = $10,000 × [(1.0695^35 - 1) / 0.0695]
  FV = $10,000 × 140.2
  FV = $1,402,000

DIFFERENCE: $1,402,000 - $903,200 = $498,800

$498,800 is the lifetime cost of a 2% annual fee
on a $10,000/year retirement saver
over 35 years.

This is certain.
It is arithmetic.
It requires no assumptions about markets
except that they continue to exist.
The fee drag is mechanical.
```
