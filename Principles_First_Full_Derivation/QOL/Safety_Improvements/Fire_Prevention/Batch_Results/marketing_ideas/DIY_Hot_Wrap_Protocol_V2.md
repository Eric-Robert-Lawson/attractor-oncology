## THE CORE PRINCIPLE

```
P1S gel is a thermal delivery medium.
Any flexible material that can:
  (a) hold a quantity of gel
  (b) be placed in contact with skin
  (c) be heated in a microwave or hot water bath

  The heated gel conforms to the skin surface.
  The SAP reservoir delivers sustained moisture
  through the heat-opened stratum corneum.

PAPER TOWEL → flat surface application

Every one of these is the same product.
The clothing is the delivery architecture.
```

# PART I: SAFETY — READ BEFORE ANY HEATING

## Temperature Limits

```
BELOW 38°C:    Sub-therapeutic. Moisture and mineral
               delivery active but no vasodilation.
               Acceptable for sensitive skin or first use.

38–42°C:       GENTLE THERAPEUTIC RANGE.
               Mild vasodilation. Good for face, neck,
               sensitive skin, first-time users.

42–48°C:       OPTIMAL THERAPEUTIC RANGE.
               Full vasodilation. Stratum corneum opens.
               Muscle relaxation. Correct for body,
               back, shoulders, joints, feet.

48–55°C:       CAUTION ZONE.
               Acceptable ONLY via fabric carrier —
               never apply gel directly to skin above 48°C.
               Fabric provides a 2–4°C buffer.
               Temperature drops rapidly on skin contact.
               Do NOT use above 48°C direct on face,
               neck, or any sensitive area under any format.

ABOVE 55°C:    DO NOT APPLY TO SKIN in any format.
               Risk of thermal injury. Remove immediately
               if accidental contact occurs. Cool with water.
               Allow gel to cool to safe range before using.
               NOTE: SAP begins degrading above 60°C.
               Gel at 60°C+ will thin and lose water
               retention. Allow to cool — it recovers.

ABOVE 80°C:    DISCARD BATCH.
               SAP crosslink network permanently degraded.
               Bentonite thixotropy disrupted.
               This temperature requires extreme microwave
               overheating and is not a normal risk.
```

## The Wrist Test — Universal Temperature Check

```
Required before every application, every session.
No exceptions regardless of experience level.

METHOD:
  Hold the loaded carrier (towel, sock, cloth) against
  the inside of your wrist for 3 seconds.
  
  The inside wrist is thinner-skinned and more
  temperature-sensitive than most body surfaces —
  if it is comfortable on the wrist, it is safe
  on any other surface.

FEEL GUIDE:
  TOO COOL:   Barely distinguishable from body temperature.
              Reheat.
  
  CORRECT:    "Pleasantly warm. Would feel good on a sore
               muscle. I would be happy to leave this on
               my wrist for 15 minutes."
  
  TOO HOT:    Instinct to pull away within 2 seconds.
              You do not hold it voluntarily.
              Cool down before applying.
              
  DANGEROUS:  Immediate pain or burning on contact.
              Remove immediately. Do not apply to skin.
              Allow significant cooling.
```

## Skin Sensitivity Check — First Use

```
BEFORE FIRST APPLICATION (any format):
  Apply a small amount of gel at target temperature
  to inner forearm. Leave 3 minutes.
  
  PASS:    Comfortable warmth, mild clay sensation,
           no unusual redness beyond normal flush.
           Proceed.
  
  CAUTION: Pronounced redness, tightness, or mild
           burning. Reduce temperature by 4–5°C.
           Retest at lower temperature.
  
  STOP:    Any pain, blistering, or allergic response.
           Do not proceed. Investigate component
           sensitivity before further applications.

POPULATIONS — ALWAYS USE GENTLE RANGE (38–42°C MAX):
  □ Diabetic individuals (reduced heat sensitivity)
  □ Elderly skin (thinner, more reactive)
  □ Broken, irritated, or sunburned skin
  □ Any area with reduced or abnormal sensation
  □ Pregnant individuals — consult first
  □ Anyone using their first batch or new formula
```

# PART II: HEATING METHODS

## The Three Heating Methods Available Without Specialized Equipment

---

### Method 1 — Microwave (Primary Method, Recommended)

The microwave is the fastest and most controllable method.
P1S gel is ~95% water — microwaves heat it evenly and
predictably. The SAP network distributes heat throughout
the gel mass quickly.

```
UNIVERSAL MICROWAVE RULES:
  Always use microwave-safe vessel (glass, ceramic,
  microwave-safe plastic — NO metal, no standard zip-lock
  in microwave).
  
  For LOADED CARRIERS (sock, cloth, towel):
  The carrier goes INTO the microwave. See format-specific
  instructions in Part III for each format's microwave
  procedure. The gel inside the carrier heats with the
  carrier — do not remove and re-load after heating.
  
  For GEL DIRECTLY IN A BOWL:
  Standard procedure below.
```

**Direct Gel in Bowl — Microwave Procedure:**

```
STEP 1 — PORTION:
  Transfer the required gel volume to a microwave-safe bowl.
  Fill bowl no more than 2/3 — gel may bubble at surface
  when hot.
  
  Volume guide:
    Face / neck:            100–150mL
    One joint (knee, elbow): 150–200mL
    Shoulder / one arm:      200–300mL
    Back (one zone):         300–400mL
    Full back:               400–600mL
    One foot:                400–600mL

STEP 2 — FIRST CYCLE:
  Microwave on HIGH for 20 seconds.
  Remove. Stir for 15 seconds with wooden or silicone spoon.
  
  WHY STIR EVERY CYCLE:
  Microwave creates temperature gradients — the container
  edge heats faster than the center. Stirring distributes
  heat, eliminates hot spots, and re-homogenizes the
  thixotropic gel (which firms up between stirring).

STEP 3 — TEMPERATURE CHECK:
  Wrist test. If below target: continue.
  If at target: apply immediately.
  If above 55°C: set uncovered on counter. Check every
  2 minutes until in range.

STEP 4 — ADDITIONAL CYCLES AS NEEDED:
  15 seconds → stir → wrist test → repeat.
  Use 15-second increments only — never 60 seconds.
  The gel heats faster than expected. Overshoot is easy.
  
  Typical total time from refrigerator temperature:
  4–6 cycles (approximately 90 seconds total heat time).
  From room temperature: 2–4 cycles (45–75 seconds total).

STEP 5 — FINAL STIR:
  When target temperature reached: stir vigorously
  20 seconds before application.
  This:
  1. Confirms no remaining hot spots
  2. Returns gel to fluid, spreadable consistency
  3. Re-distributes settled bentonite clay
  
  Apply within 3–5 minutes of final stir.
```

---

### Method 2 — Hot Water Bath (No Microwave Needed)

Use for sealed bags, zip-lock formats, or when microwave
is unavailable.

```
PROCEDURE:
  Boil water. Pour into large bowl.
  Allow to cool 2–3 minutes (target bath: 65–75°C).
  
  Place sealed bag or sealed container of gel into bath.
  Submerge as fully as possible.
  Leave 8–12 minutes, turning once at 5 minutes.
  
  Wrist test through the bag or container before applying.
  Too cool: return to bath 3–5 more minutes.
  Too hot: set on counter 2–3 minutes, retest.

ADVANTAGE: Cannot overshoot as easily as microwave.
           Sealed bag heats without opening.
DISADVANTAGE: Slower. Requires managing hot water bowl.
```

---

### Method 3 — Stovetop (Large Volume or Repeat Sessions)

For full batches (400mL+) or when preparing gel for
multiple applications in one session.

```
PROCEDURE:
  Place gel in small saucepan.
  Heat on LOW to MEDIUM-LOW only.
  Stir continuously — never leave unattended.
  
  The gel will thin noticeably as it warms — this is
  correct. Thixotropy decreases with heat. The warm
  gel is more fluid for a reason: easier to load into
  carriers and spread on skin. It re-gels on contact
  with the cooler carrier material and skin.
  
  Remove from heat at 45–50°C.
  Stir 1–2 minutes off heat (residual pan heat continues
  warming briefly after removal).
  Transfer to insulated bowl or thermos to hold temperature
  during the session.
  
  NEVER leave unattended — water evaporates from the
  gel at sustained heat, concentrating SAP and bentonite,
  changing consistency and reducing batch volume.
```

## Format 1 — Paper Towel (Flat Surface, Any Body Area)

**Best for:** Face, neck, individual joints, spot treatment,
testing new temperatures, any area requiring precision.

**Field confirmed:** Yes — tested 2026-05-13.

```
LOAD METHOD:
  Stack 2 sheets of paper towel flat on clean surface.
  Spoon heated gel onto center.
  Spread to within 2cm of edges.
  The towel should be saturated — heavy, gel visible
  through paper, not dripping.

VOLUME PER TOWEL (2-sheet stack):
  Face:            30–45mL   (2–3 tablespoons)
  Neck:            30–45mL
  Knee:            60–90mL   (4–6 tablespoons)
  Shoulder:        90–120mL  (6–8 tablespoons)
  Upper back zone: 150–200mL (10–14 tablespoons)

MICROWAVE (pre-loaded towel):
  Load towel, fold in half over gel, place on microwave-safe
  plate. Microwave 15 seconds. Wrist test through towel.
  15-second increments if needed.
  
  OR: Heat gel in bowl first, then load towel.
  Both methods work. Pre-loading then microwaving is
  faster for repeat sessions.

APPLICATION:
  Place gel-side down against skin.
  Press firmly 5 seconds to establish full contact.
  For back: lie directly on the loaded towel —
  body weight holds it without any securing required.
  For shoulder: hold in place or loosely bind with
  dry cloth or elastic bandage.
  
DURATION: 15–25 minutes.

REMOVAL:
  Peel away gently.
  Leave gel residue on skin 3–5 minutes before rinsing.
  Rinse warm then cool. Pat dry.

HEAT RETENTION: 10–15 minutes at therapeutic temperature.
                Continues delivering moisture and minerals
                as it cools.
```

## Format 2 — Cloth Towel or Washcloth (Extended Coverage)

**Best for:** Back, abdomen, large body zones, treatments
where paper towel is too small or too fragile.

```
SELECTION:
  Any cotton or terry cloth towel.
  Thicker terry cloth retains heat longer.
  A thin cotton washcloth is more conformable to
  curved surfaces (shoulder, knee, neck).
  
  For back: use a hand towel or small bath towel
  folded to match the treatment zone.

LOAD METHOD:
  Lay towel flat. Ladle or pour gel over one half.
  Fold towel over itself — gel is now sandwiched inside.
  Press gently to distribute gel through the towel fabric.
  The gel should saturate the fabric, not pool in one spot.

VOLUME:
  Washcloth (face/neck):          100–150mL
  Hand towel (back zone):         300–500mL
  Half bath towel (full back):    600–900mL

MICROWAVE:
  Loaded and folded towel goes into microwave directly
  on a microwave-safe plate.
  Start with 30 seconds for a washcloth, 45 seconds
  for a hand towel (more mass = more time needed).
  Squeeze gently through towel to feel interior temperature.
  Wrist test on unloaded edge or through fabric before
  applying to treatment zone.
  Continue in 20-second increments if needed.
  
  NOTE: Terry cloth retains heat well and can feel cooler
  on the outside than the gel inside. Always test through
  the loaded zone, not the dry edge.

APPLICATION:
  Unfold loaded section against skin.
  Cover with a dry towel for insulation — this extends
  therapeutic temperature window significantly.
  A dry towel layer over the wet towel traps heat.
  
  For full back: lie on the loaded towel. Place a dry
  towel on top of your body over the same area.
  You are insulated above and below.

DURATION: 20–35 minutes.
           Terry cloth retains heat longer than paper towel.
           The dry insulating layer adds another 5–10 minutes
           of therapeutic temperature.

REMOVAL:
  Peel loaded towel off. Residue on skin — leave 3–5 min.
  Rinse warm then cool. Pat dry.
  
LAUNDERING:
  Rinse the towel under running water immediately after use.
  All P1S components are water-soluble.
  No staining. No residue. Machine washable normally.
```

## Format 3 — Sock (Foot, Ankle, and Hand Wrap)

**Best for:** Foot and ankle treatment, hand treatment,
wrist and lower arm treatment, toe joint treatment.
**Field proposed:** 2026-05-13.

```
THE SOCK AS A WRAP ARCHITECTURE:
  A sock is a pre-shaped, foot-fitted, wearable bag.
  When filled with P1S gel and worn on the foot,
  it delivers gel contact to:
  - The entire plantar surface (sole)
  - The heel
  - The arch
  - All toe surfaces including interdigital spaces
  - The ankle and lower leg to sock height
  
  No other format delivers this level of full-foot
  surface coverage simultaneously.
  
  A standard sock worn on a hand delivers:
  - Full palm coverage
  - All finger surfaces to the knuckle
  - Wrist coverage
  
  The sock is the most ergonomically complete wearable
  wrap format for extremities.

SOCK SELECTION:
  Thick cotton athletic socks: best. Terry-lined interior
  holds more gel and retains more heat. Conform to foot
  without restriction.
  
  Knee-high sock: allows ankle and lower calf coverage.
  Use when ankle or Achilles tendon is the treatment target.
  
  Thin nylon or compression socks: not recommended —
  too little fabric to hold adequate gel volume.
  
  Wool socks: excellent heat retention. Harder to launder
  gel out of, but fully washable with water.
  
  TWO-SOCK METHOD (recommended):
  Inner sock: thin cotton sock worn on bare foot.
  Outer sock: thick sock filled with gel, pulled over inner sock.
  The inner sock provides:
  1. A comfort buffer between bare skin and gel
  2. Slight compression to hold gel against foot contours
  3. Easy removal — the inner sock can be peeled off
     cleanly with gel mostly on the outer sock
  Use this method for first-time foot applications
  or for sensitive skin.
  
  SINGLE SOCK METHOD:
  Bare foot directly into gel-loaded sock.
  More direct mineral and moisture contact.
  Slightly more gel residue on skin at removal.
  Preferred once you know how the gel feels on your feet.

LOADING THE SOCK:

  METHOD A — BOWL LOADING:
    Prepare gel in bowl (heat to target temperature first).
    Hold sock open over the bowl.
    Spoon gel into the sock, filling to ankle level.
    Volume: 150–250mL for a standard foot.
    Distribute gel into toe area by squeezing gently
    through the outside of the sock.
    
  METHOD B — IMMERSION LOADING:
    Place sock in a bowl of heated gel.
    Press sock into the gel, forcing gel into the fabric.
    Remove from bowl. Squeeze out excess so gel stays
    IN the fabric, not dripping out of it.
    This produces a more uniformly saturated sock
    than spoon-loading.
    
  METHOD C — DRY SOCK MICROWAVE (fastest):
    Spoon room-temperature gel into the sock.
    Place loaded sock on a microwave-safe plate.
    Microwave 20 seconds. Squeeze through sock to assess
    temperature. Continue in 15-second increments.
    Wrist test along the bottom of the sock before wearing.
    
    THIS IS THE FASTEST ROUTE FROM STORAGE TO WEARING:
    Scoop gel into sock → microwave → wrist test → put on foot.
    Total time: 3–5 minutes from gel storage.

MICROWAVE PARAMETERS FOR LOADED SOCK:
  150–250mL of gel in a cotton sock:
  Start: 25 seconds. Squeeze and wrist test.
  Add: 15-second increments until correct temperature.
  Typical: 3–5 cycles from refrigerator temperature.
  
  The sock exterior will feel cooler than the gel inside
  because cotton insulates. Always test by squeezing
  the sock toe area against your wrist — the loaded
  zone, not the dry sock cuff.

WEARING:
  SINGLE SOCK METHOD:
  Pull loaded sock onto bare foot.
  The gel will compress and flow to all foot surfaces
  as you put weight on it.
  Do NOT stand and walk — sit or recline with feet up.
  Walking spreads the gel unevenly and presses it
  out of the sock at the ankle opening.
  
  TWO-SOCK METHOD:
  Put thin inner sock on bare foot first.
  Pull loaded outer sock over the inner sock.
  The gel distributes between the two sock layers
  AND through the inner sock to the foot surface.

  FOOT ELEVATION:
  Elevate feet slightly (ottoman, pillow, sofa arm).
  This prevents gel from migrating toward the ankle
  opening by gravity and maximizes toe coverage.

DURATION: 20–30 minutes.

REMOVAL:
  Peel sock off slowly over a bowl or sink —
  excess gel will drip. Collect it (the gel is
  reusable — return to storage container).
  
  Leave thin gel residue on foot surface.
  Wait 3–5 minutes.
  IMMEDIATELY use pumice on callus areas if treating feet —
  this is the moment of maximum callus softness.
  Rinse warm then cool. Pat dry.
  Put clean dry socks on to maintain residual moisture.

LAUNDERING THE SOCK:
  Rinse under running water immediately.
  Squeeze out gel under running water — the gel
  releases from cotton fibers easily.
  Machine wash normally. No staining. No residue.
  
HAND APPLICATION WITH SOCK:
  Same loading and microwave procedure.
  Insert hand into loaded sock (wear a thin glove
  liner underneath for easy removal if preferred).
  Rest hand palm-up on a flat surface with wrist
  elevated slightly.
  Leave 15–20 minutes.
  Remove. Residue on skin 3–5 min. Rinse. Pat dry.
```

**Best for:** Hand treatment, arthritis relief, knuckle
treatment, full finger and palm coverage.

```
THE MITTEN AS A HAND WRAP:
  A mitten provides:
  - Full palm and back-of-hand coverage
  - All finger surfaces simultaneously
  - Wrist coverage to mitten cuff height
  
  A winter mitten with a thick insulating lining is
  the ideal format — the insulation is already built in,

MITTEN SELECTION:
  Thick winter mitten (fleece, wool, or sherpa lining):
  EXCELLENT — built-in insulation, large interior volume,

  allow moisture or mineral contact through the material.
  
  Thin knit glove: functional but less heat retention

  inner thin glove as liner).
  
  Oven mitt: too rigid and too large for skin contact.

LOADING THE MITTEN:
  Same methods as sock loading.
  Volume: 100–200mL for a standard adult hand.
  Distribute into finger spaces by squeezing from

MICROWAVE:
  Same parameters as sock.
  20-second start, 15-second increments.
  Test temperature by pressing the palm area of the

WEARING:
  Slide hand into loaded mitten.
  Let the gel distribute to all hand surfaces.
  Rest hand in lap or on flat surface — do not use

  For arthritis or joint treatment:
  Gently open and close hand twice after inserting —
  this distributes gel into all joint spaces and

BOTH HANDS SIMULTANEOUSLY:
  Load two mittens. Microwave one at a time (same plate,

  This is the P1S equivalent of a paraffin hand treatment —
  both hands treated simultaneously at therapeutic
```

## Format 5 — T-Shirt (Torso, Back, Chest, Shoulder)

**Best for:** Full back treatment, chest treatment,
bilateral shoulder treatment, upper body coverage,
treating large surface areas in one application.

```
THE T-SHIRT AS A BODY WRAP:
  A t-shirt loaded with P1S gel and worn against the
  skin creates a wearable body wrap covering:
  - Entire back surface
  - Full chest
  - Both shoulders
  - Upper abdomen

  This is the format that replaces a professional
  spa body wrap for upper body treatment —
  covering more surface area simultaneously than
  any towel-based format.

T-SHIRT SELECTION:
  Old/dedicated cotton t-shirt — one you do not mind
  getting gel on (it washes out completely, but treat
  it as your P1S application shirt).
  
  Tighter fit: more gel-to-skin contact, better for
  moisture and mineral delivery.
  Looser fit: easier to put on and take off when loaded.
  
  RECOMMENDATION: Use a fitted t-shirt one size smaller
  than your normal size. It holds more gel against the
  skin and prevents the gel from pooling downward.
  A loose shirt allows the gel to migrate to the shirt
  hem before contacting the treatment area.
  
  Long-sleeve t-shirt: extends coverage to arms.
  Use when arms and elbows are treatment targets.

LOADING METHOD — INSIDE-OUT BUCKET SOAK:

  This is the most effective loading method for
  a full t-shirt application.
  
  STEP 1: Turn the t-shirt inside-out.
  STEP 2: Prepare 600–900mL of heated gel in a wide
          bowl or bucket (gel at target temperature,
          already stirred and wrist-tested).
  STEP 3: Submerge the inside-out shirt in the gel
          bowl or ladle gel over the shirt interior
          while holding it over the bowl.
          Focus on the back panel and both shoulder areas.
          The front panel receives less gel if treating
          primarily the back.
  STEP 4: Gently squeeze the shirt to press gel into
          the fabric fibers.
  STEP 5: Do not wring — you want the shirt saturated
          and heavy, not wrung dry.

LOADING METHOD — TARGETED LADLE (LESS GEL, SPECIFIC ZONES):
  Lay shirt flat, inside-out, on a waterproof surface.
  Ladle gel onto specific zones only:
    - Back panel only: 300–500mL
    - Both shoulder zones: 150–200mL each
    - Chest panel: 200–300mL
  Press gel into fabric with your palm.

MICROWAVE (LOADED SHIRT):
  A fully loaded t-shirt is too large for most
  standard microwaves — use the stovetop or hot water
  bath method to heat the gel BEFORE loading the shirt.
  
  FOR TARGETED ZONES only (not full shirt):
  Fold the shirt so only the loaded zone is layered.
  Microwave on a large plate, 30 seconds.
  Wrist test the loaded fabric before wearing.
  
  PRACTICAL SEQUENCE FOR FULL SHIRT:
  1. Heat full gel volume to target temperature
     using stovetop or microwave-in-portions method.
  2. Load shirt immediately with hot gel.
  3. Wear immediately — the shirt arrives at skin
     at correct temperature if loaded and worn quickly
     (within 2–3 minutes of loading).

WEARING:
  STEP 1: Turn the loaded shirt right-side-out while
          holding over the sink or a bowl — some gel
          will drip. This is expected.
  STEP 2: Put shirt on as normal.
  STEP 3: Immediately lie down on a waterproof surface
          (plastic sheet on bed or floor).
          The shirt will drip and transfer gel to
          whatever surface you lie on.
  STEP 4: Cover with a dry blanket or towel for
          insulation — this extends therapeutic
          temperature significantly.
  STEP 5: Rest completely still.

DURATION: 20–35 minutes.

  Do not stand and walk in a loaded shirt —
  the gel migrates downward with movement.
  This is a lying-down treatment.

REMOVAL:
  Carefully pull shirt off over the sink.
  Gel will release from skin as shirt is removed.
  Leave gel residue on skin 3–5 minutes.
  Shower with warm water first (30 seconds full rinse),
  then cool water (15 seconds).
  Pat dry. Do not rub.
  
  Stand up slowly after removing the shirt —
  full back vasodilation reduces blood pressure
  briefly. Take 30 seconds before standing fully.

LAUNDERING:
  Rinse shirt under running water until gel clears.
  Machine wash cold normally.
  No staining. No residue. No special treatment needed.
```

## Format 6 — Pants Leg (Thigh, Knee, Calf, Full Leg Treatment)

**Best for:** Knee treatment, quadriceps and hamstring
muscle recovery, calf treatment, full lower leg coverage.

```
THE PANTS LEG AS A LEG WRAP:
  A single pants leg (sweatpants leg, compression pants leg,
  yoga pants leg) creates a tube-shaped wrap around the
  entire limb cross-section — contacting:
  - Anterior (front) of leg simultaneously with
  - Posterior (back) of leg
  - All lateral surfaces
  
  No flat carrier (towel, paper towel) achieves this
  simultaneous circumferential coverage.

PANTS SELECTION:
  Sweatpants leg (cotton or fleece): EXCELLENT.
  Large interior, thick fabric holds gel, built-in
  cuff at ankle prevents gel from migrating down.
  
  Yoga pants leg (tight, stretchy): excellent for
  knee treatment — the stretch holds the gel firmly
  against the knee surface. More contact pressure
  than a loose sweatpants leg.
  
  Jeans: NOT recommended — denim does not absorb gel
  well and is difficult to launder if gel sets in fabric.
  
  Compression pants leg: very effective for muscle
  recovery applications — the compression holds gel
  against muscle surfaces with consistent pressure.

LOADING:
  Turn pants leg inside-out.
  Ladle gel into the interior while holding it over a bowl.
  Volume: 200–400mL for a standard adult leg,
  depending on limb size and target area.
  
  For knee specifically: concentrate gel at the
  knee zone (middle of the pants leg).
  For full leg: distribute evenly from cuff to thigh.

MICROWAVE:
  For targeted knee zone: fold loaded pants leg so
  knee zone is doubled. Microwave 25 seconds.
  Wrist test on the loaded zone. 15-second increments.
  
  For full leg load: heat gel before loading
  (same approach as full t-shirt — too much mass
  for efficient microwave loading).

WEARING:
  Turn pants leg right-side-out.
  Step into the pants leg or pull it up over the leg
  like a sleeve.
  Sit with leg elevated (foot on ottoman or pillow).
  Do not stand and walk — gel migrates down with movement.
  
  For bilateral treatment (both legs):
  Use both pants legs from a pair of sweatpants.
  Load both, wear both simultaneously.

DURATION: 20–30 minutes.
```

## Format 7 — Zip-Lock Bag Immersion (Foot and Hand Submersion)

**Best for:** Complete foot submersion, complete hand
submersion, maximum surface contact, callus treatment.

**Field confirmed:** Paper towel immersion variant
tested 2026-05-13.

```
THE BAG AS A SUBMERSION VESSEL:
  Unlike every other format where the gel is in
  a carrier pressed against the skin, the bag format
  SUBMERGES the foot or hand in gel — every surface
  is in gel contact simultaneously with no air gaps.
  
  This is the highest-intensity surface contact format.
  The foot (or hand) is surrounded on all sides.
  The gel fills interdigital spaces completely.
  The arch and heel are in full contact without any
  bridging or missed zones.

EQUIPMENT:
  Gallon zip-lock FREEZER bag (not sandwich bag —
  freezer bags are thicker and hold the gel weight
  without splitting).
  
  Use a new bag each session or rinse and reuse —
  the gel does not damage the plastic.

PROCEDURE:

  STEP 1 — HEAT GEL:
  Heat 400–600mL gel to 44–48°C for feet,
  42–46°C for hands.
```

# PART VI: POST-TREATMENT PROTOCOL

**The same for every format.**

```
IMMEDIATELY AFTER REMOVAL:
  Leave gel residue on skin exactly where it is.
  Do not wipe. Do not rinse yet.
  The residue continues delivering moisture and
  minerals to the warmed, open skin for several
  minutes after the carrier is removed.
  This is part of the treatment — not a cleanup problem.
  
  Wait 3–5 minutes with residue on skin.
  Use this time: gentle massage of treated area
  if accessible. The residual gel is a massage medium.
  
FOR FEET ONLY:
  Do not wait — go directly to pumice while feet are
  still warm and callus is maximally softened.
  Pumice first, residue time second, then rinse.

RINSING:
  Warm water first — not hot. 20–30 seconds.
  The warm water removes the bulk of the gel.
  Then cool or cold water — 15 seconds.
  The cool water closes the pores that were opened
  by the heat and locks in the moisture that was
  delivered during treatment.
  
  NEVER rinse with hot water post-treatment:
  Hot water continues driving moisture OUT of the
  already-treated skin, reversing the hydration effect.
  The goal of the cool rinse is to seal, not strip.

DRYING:
  Pat dry. Never rub.
  The skin is softened and more vulnerable to
  friction immediately after heat treatment.
  Gentle patting preserves the surface integrity.

POST-TREATMENT FOLLOW-UP:
  Optional but effective:
  Apply a small amount of room-temperature P1S gel
  to the treated areas after drying.
  The cool gel on post-treatment skin:
  1. Closes the treatment with a final mineral
     and moisture contact
  2. The cool temperature after heat contrast
     is pleasant and mildly stimulating
  3. Extends the total mineral delivery time
     beyond the hot treatment session

FOR FULL BODY OR LARGE AREA TREATMENTS:
  Sit or lie down for 5–10 minutes before resuming
  normal activity.
  
  WHY: Vasodilation causes a temporary reduction in
  blood pressure. Standing up suddenly after a
  full-body treatment can cause lightheadedness.
  
  This is normal and expected. It means the treatment
  worked — your circulation responded. Take your time.
```
