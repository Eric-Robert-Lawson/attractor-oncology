## WHY HEAT CHANGES THE P1S APPLICATION

```
AT ROOM TEMPERATURE (20–22°C):
  The gel delivers moisture and minerals to skin via
  sustained contact. The skin is the passive recipient.
  Mechanisms active: moisture delivery, mineral contact,
  occlusion. All present, all working.

AT THERAPEUTIC TEMPERATURE (42–48°C):
  THREE ADDITIONAL EFFECTS ENGAGE:

  EFFECT 1 — VASODILATION:
    Heat causes superficial blood vessels to dilate.
    Increased blood flow to the skin surface.
    Nutrient delivery to skin cells increases.
    Waste product removal from skin tissue increases.
    This is why heat therapy feels deeply relieving —
    it is increasing local circulation, not just warming.

  EFFECT 2 — STRATUM CORNEUM OPENING:
    The outermost skin layer (stratum corneum) is a
    tightly packed barrier of flattened dead cells.
    At elevated temperature, the lipid matrix between
    these cells softens and expands slightly.
    The barrier becomes more permeable — water moves
    through it more easily in both directions.
    
    P1S effect: the SAP water reservoir pushes moisture
    INTO the skin through this opened barrier more
    effectively than at room temperature.
    The bentonite minerals also penetrate more readily
    into the softened barrier layer.

  EFFECT 3 — MUSCLE AND CONNECTIVE TISSUE RELAXATION:
    Heat penetrates ~1–2cm into superficial tissue.
    Muscle fibers relax. Connective tissue becomes
    more pliable. Nerve endings carrying pain signals
    are partially suppressed by thermoreceptor activation.
    
    Applied to: neck, shoulders, lower back, joints —
    the hot wrap provides genuine muscular relief,
    not just skin benefit.

COMBINED EFFECT:
  Hot P1S gel = thermotherapy + sustained moisture delivery
  + mineral contact + occlusion — all simultaneously.
  No existing single product delivers this combination.
  Each element is amplified by the others:
  heat opens skin → minerals penetrate more → moisture
  delivers more deeply → occlusion retains all of it.
```

# PART I: SAFETY BOUNDARIES

## Temperature Limits — Non-Negotiable

```
BELOW 38°C:    Comfortable but sub-therapeutic.
               Feels warm, no vasodilation, limited
               stratum corneum effect. Acceptable for
               sensitive skin or first-time application.

38–42°C:       GENTLE THERAPEUTIC RANGE.
               Mild vasodilation. Comfortable for extended
               contact (30+ minutes). Recommended for:
               face, neck, sensitive areas.

42–48°C:       OPTIMAL THERAPEUTIC RANGE.
               Strong vasodilation. Full stratum corneum
               effect. Muscle relaxation. Recommended for:
               body, back, shoulders, joints, feet.
               Maximum recommended for sustained skin contact.

48–55°C:       CAUTION ZONE.
               Tolerable for brief initial contact only.
               Will feel hot within 30–60 seconds.
               Acceptable only if applied via paper towel
               carrier (which provides ~2–3°C buffer).
               Do NOT apply gel at this temperature directly
               to face, neck, or sensitive skin.
               Temperature will drop to safe range quickly
               once applied to skin.

ABOVE 55°C:    DO NOT APPLY TO SKIN.
               Risk of thermal injury at sustained contact.
               Also: SAP polymer begins losing water
               retention capacity above ~60°C — the gel
               becomes thinner and less effective.
               If gel reaches 60°C+: allow to cool before use.
               The gel is not damaged permanently —
               it recovers on cooling.

ABOVE 80°C:    DISCARD BATCH.
               Prolonged high temperature degrades the
               SAP crosslink network. Bentonite may begin
               to lose thixotropic structure. The batch
               will not perform correctly after cooling.
               This temperature is only reachable by
               boiling or extreme microwave overheating —
               not a normal risk with correct protocol.
```

## Skin Sensitivity Check

```
BEFORE FIRST HOT APPLICATION:
  Apply a small amount of gel at target temperature
  to inner wrist for 60 seconds.
  
  PASS: No unusual heat sensation beyond expected warmth.
        No redness developing beyond slight flush.
        Proceed with full application.
  
  CAUTION: Significant redness developing at wrist.
            Reduce temperature by 3–4°C and retest.
            
  STOP: Pain, burning sensation, blistering.
        This should not occur at correct temperatures.
        If it does: the gel is too hot.
        Cool immediately. Do not proceed until temperature
        is confirmed in the safe range.

POPULATIONS REQUIRING REDUCED TEMPERATURE (38–42°C MAX):
  □ Diabetic individuals (reduced skin temperature sensitivity)
  □ Elderly skin (thinner, more heat-sensitive)
  □ Broken, irritated, or sunburned skin
  □ Any area with reduced sensation
  □ First-time application — always start cooler
```

# PART II: HEATING METHODS

## Method A — Microwave Heating (Recommended)

The microwave is the most controllable heating method for
small to medium gel volumes. P1S gel heats evenly because
it is ~95% water — microwaves heat water directly and
the SAP network distributes the heat through the gel mass.

### Equipment

```
  Microwave-safe bowl or container
  (glass, ceramic, or microwave-safe plastic)
  Kitchen thermometer (instant-read preferred)
    OR wrist temperature test method (see below)
  Wooden or silicone spoon for stirring
  Paper towels (2–4 sheets for application)
  Timer
```

### Procedure

**Step 1 — Portion the gel.**

```
Remove the required amount of gel from storage:
  Face / neck application:   100–150mL
  One body zone (back, shoulder, knee): 200–300mL
  Full back or large area:   400–600mL
  Foot treatment:            400–600mL (per foot)

Place in microwave-safe container.
The container should be no more than 2/3 full —
the gel may bubble slightly at the surface when hot.
```

**Step 2 — First heat cycle.**

```
Microwave on HIGH for 20 seconds.
Remove. Stir thoroughly with spoon for 15 seconds.

WHY STIR BETWEEN INCREMENTS:
  The microwave heats water molecules throughout the
  gel volume but creates temperature gradients —
  areas near the container edge heat faster than
  the center. Stirring distributes heat uniformly
  and prevents hot spots that could exceed safe
  temperature locally while the bulk gel is still cool.
  
  Stirring also re-homogenizes the thixotropic gel —
  the gel will have partially firmed up in storage.
  Warming + stirring returns it to a workable
  spreading consistency.
```

**Step 3 — Temperature check.**

```
THERMOMETER METHOD (most accurate):
  Insert instant-read thermometer into the center
  of the gel mass (not touching the container wall).
  Read temperature.
  
  Target: 42–48°C for body applications.
  Target: 38–44°C for face/neck applications.
  
  If below target: continue to Step 4.
  If at target: proceed to application.
  If above 55°C: allow to cool uncovered, check every
  2 minutes until within range.

WRIST METHOD (no thermometer required):
  Dip the inside of your wrist into the gel
  (the same test used for baby bottle temperature).
  
  CORRECT TEMPERATURE FEEL:
  "Pleasantly warm — noticeably warmer than body
   temperature, would feel good on a sore muscle,
   not uncomfortable to hold the wrist there."
  
  TOO COOL: Barely distinguishable from body temperature.
  TOO HOT:  Instinct to pull away within 2 seconds.
            Must pull back. Do not use at this temperature
            for direct skin application.
```

**Step 4 — Additional heat cycles (if needed).**

```
If gel has not reached target temperature:
  Microwave 15 seconds. Stir 15 seconds. Check temperature.
  Repeat in 15-second increments only.
  
  DO NOT use 60-second or longer increments —
  the gel heats faster than expected at therapeutic
  temperatures and overshoot is easy.
  
  15 seconds → stir → check → repeat.
  This is the correct rhythm. It takes 2–4 cycles
  total from refrigerator temperature.
  From room temperature: 1–3 cycles typically.
```

**Step 5 — Final stir before application.**

```
Once target temperature is reached:
  Stir vigorously for 20 seconds.
  This final stir:
  1. Confirms uniform temperature (no hot spots)
  2. Restores gel to fluid, spreadable consistency
     (thixotropic gel firms under stillness — stirring
     returns it to optimal spreading viscosity)
  3. Redistributes any settled bentonite clay
     (clay settles during storage — stirring brings
     mineral contact back to full distribution)
     
Proceed to application within 3–5 minutes.
The gel will cool once removed from the microwave.
The paper towel carrier method retains heat for
10–15 minutes on the skin. Direct application retains
heat for 5–10 minutes before dropping below therapeutic
range.
```

## Method B — Hot Water Bath (No Microwave Required)

Use when a microwave is unavailable or when heating
a sealed bag of gel (gel in zip-lock cannot go directly
in the microwave without the bag melting).

```
PROCEDURE:
  Boil water in a kettle or pot.
  Pour boiling water into a large bowl.
  Allow to cool for 2–3 minutes (boiling water is 100°C —
  too hot. Target bath temperature: 65–75°C).
  
  Place the sealed gel bag or gel container into the
  hot water bath.
  Leave 8–12 minutes, turning the bag once at 5 minutes.
  
  Remove. Squeeze bag to assess gel temperature (wrist test).
  If not yet warm enough: return to bath for 3–5 more minutes.
  If too hot: set on counter 2–3 minutes, retest.
  
  Once at target temperature: proceed to application.
  For sealed bag application: cut corner and squeeze
  onto paper towel or directly onto skin.

ADVANTAGE OF HOT WATER BATH:
  Gentle, even heating. Cannot overshoot as easily
  as microwave. Sealed bag heats without opening.
  
DISADVANTAGE:
  Slower (8–15 minutes vs. 1–3 minutes for microwave).
  Requires managing a bowl of hot water.
```

## Method C — Stovetop (Large Batch / Repeat Sessions)

For preparing a larger quantity (400mL+) to be used
across multiple applications or for a shared session.

```
PROCEDURE:
  Place gel in a small saucepan.
  Heat on LOW to MEDIUM-LOW heat.
  Stir continuously — do not leave unattended.
  
  The gel will thin significantly as it warms
  (thixotropy reduces with heat — the gel becomes
  more fluid at therapeutic temperatures).
  This is expected and correct — the warm gel is
  more fluid for a reason: it spreads more easily.
  It re-gels on the skin as it cools.
  
  Monitor temperature with thermometer.
  Remove from heat at 45–50°C.
  Allow 1–2 minutes of stirring off heat before application —
  the residual heat in the pan will continue to warm
  the gel briefly after removal.
  
  Transfer to an insulated bowl or thermos to maintain
  temperature during the session.
  
IMPORTANT: Never leave P1S gel on a hot burner unattended.
  The water in the gel will evaporate if held at high
  temperature for extended periods, concentrating the
  SAP and bentonite — changing the consistency and
  reducing total batch volume.
```

# PART III: APPLICATION METHODS

## Application A — Paper Towel Hot Wrap (Recommended)

This is the method confirmed in field testing (2026-05-13).
It is the most practical, most versatile, and most
self-applicable format for every body surface.

```
MATERIALS:
  Heated P1S gel (at target temperature)
  2–3 sheets of paper towel (standard household)
  Optional: plastic wrap or dry towel to cover
  Optional: elastic bandage or cloth to hold in place

PROCEDURE:

STEP 1 — PREPARE THE CARRIER:
  Lay 2 sheets of paper towel flat on a clean surface.
  Stack them (doubled thickness retains more gel
  and stays warmer longer than a single sheet).

STEP 2 — LOAD THE CARRIER:
  Spoon or pour heated gel onto the center of the
  paper towel stack. Amount:
    Face/neck:     2–3 tablespoons (30–45mL)
    Shoulder/knee: 4–6 tablespoons (60–90mL)
    Full back:     10–14 tablespoons (150–210mL)
  
  Spread gel to within 2cm of the towel edges.
  The gel should saturate the towel — visible through
  the paper, heavy, uniform coverage.

STEP 3 — WRING ASSESSMENT:
  The towel should be saturated but not dripping.
  If gel is running off the edge: too much gel or
  too thin (add more paper towel layers).
  If towel feels barely damp: too little gel — add more.

STEP 4 — APPLY TO SKIN:
  Lay the loaded towel against the target skin surface,
  gel side down.
  Press firmly for 5 seconds to establish full contact.
  
  The gel will begin transferring from towel to skin
  immediately — this is correct. The towel acts as
  a reservoir, maintaining a continuous supply of
  gel against the skin surface throughout the treatment.

STEP 5 — SECURE (OPTIONAL):
  For body applications where the towel cannot lie flat
  by gravity (shoulder, back, knee):
  Wrap loosely with a dry cloth or elastic bandage
  to hold the wet towel against the skin.
  Do not compress tightly — light contact pressure only.
  
  For back application: lie on the loaded towel.
  Body weight holds it in place without any securing.
  This is the most effective self-application method
  for the back — simply lie on it.

STEP 6 — TREATMENT DURATION:
  Face / neck:    10–15 minutes
  Body zones:     15–25 minutes
  Back (full):    20–30 minutes
  Joints:         15–20 minutes
  Feet:           20–30 minutes
  
  The towel will cool toward room temperature over
  this period. This is acceptable — the mineral and
  moisture delivery mechanisms continue even as the
  thermal effect reduces. If you want sustained heat:
  reheat the towel in the microwave for 10 seconds
  at the halfway point, or prepare a second loaded
  towel in advance.

STEP 7 — REMOVAL AND RINSE:
  Peel towel away from skin gently.
  The gel will leave a thin residue on the skin surface.
  This residue continues to work for several minutes
  after the towel is removed — do not rinse immediately.
  Leave residue on skin for 3–5 additional minutes.
  Rinse with warm water first, then cool.
  Pat dry. Do not rub — the skin is softened and
  more receptive to friction damage immediately after
  a hot treatment.
```

## Application B — Direct Gel Application (No Carrier)

For treatments where the gel is applied directly to skin
and covered to retain heat. More intensive than the
paper towel method — more total gel in contact with skin.

```
MATERIALS:
  Heated P1S gel
  Spoon or spatula for spreading
  Plastic wrap (cling film) for covering
  Dry towel to place over plastic wrap

PROCEDURE:

STEP 1 — SKIN PREPARATION:
  Clean the skin surface. Damp skin (post-shower or
  light misting with water) is slightly better than
  dry skin — the gel adheres more uniformly to
  damp skin and the starting moisture level is higher.

STEP 2 — APPLICATION:
  Spoon gel onto target skin area.
  Spread to 3–5mm even thickness using spoon back
  or fingers (gloves recommended for hygiene if
  gel will be returned to storage after session).
  
  3mm thickness: standard — good heat retention,
                 good mineral contact, rinses easily.
  5mm thickness: extended heat retention, more gel
                 for deep moisture delivery, takes
                 slightly longer to rinse.

STEP 3 — COVER:
  Lay plastic wrap directly on top of the gel layer.
  The plastic wrap:
  1. Prevents evaporation (extends heat retention
     and maintains the moisture at the skin surface)
  2. Prevents gel from sliding off curved surfaces
  3. Allows the treatment to be done clothed above
     the wrapped zone without gel contact on fabric
  
  Optional: place a dry warm towel over the plastic wrap.
  The towel insulates the treatment zone and extends
  the time the gel remains at therapeutic temperature.
  A towel that has been briefly warmed in the dryer
  or with hot water (wrung out) is ideal.

STEP 4 — TREATMENT DURATION:
  Direct application with covering: 20–30 minutes.
  The covering significantly extends the therapeutic
  temperature window vs. uncovered application.

STEP 5 — REMOVAL AND RINSE:
  Peel back plastic wrap. The gel will be partially
  absorbed into the skin — less gel than you applied
  will be visible. This is correct — the moisture
  has moved into the skin.
  Rinse with warm then cool water.
  Pat dry gently.
```

## Application C — Hot Foot Bootie (Zip-Lock Bag Method)

```
MATERIALS:
  Heated P1S gel (400–600mL per foot)
  1 gallon zip-lock freezer bag per foot
  Chair with foot rest or sofa to recline

PROCEDURE:

STEP 1 — HEAT GEL:
  Heat 400–600mL P1S gel to 42–48°C using microwave
  method (see Part II). For feet specifically:
  the 42–48°C range is correct — plantar skin is
  thicker and less heat-sensitive than facial skin.
  Stir thoroughly before pouring.

STEP 2 — FILL BAG:
  Pour heated gel into gallon zip-lock bag.
  Do not seal yet.

STEP 3 — INSERT FOOT:
  Slide bare foot into bag slowly.
  The gel will displace around the foot, filling
  all contours — arch, heel, between toes.
  The thixotropic gel conforms to the foot geometry
  and holds position — it does not pool at the bottom
  of the bag like water would.

STEP 4 — SEAL:
  Press air out of the bag and seal around the ankle.
  You can fold the top of the bag over and hold it,
  use a rubber band, or simply rest the sealed bag
  on your lap. The weight of your foot holds the
  bag in position.

STEP 5 — TREATMENT:
  Rest comfortably with foot inside bag for 20–30 minutes.
  The gel maintains therapeutic temperature for
  approximately 15 minutes before cooling to comfortable
  warmth. Beyond 15 minutes: the treatment continues
  at moisture and mineral level even as thermal effect
  reduces.
  
  Optional: re-warm mid-treatment.
  Remove foot, reseal bag, microwave for 15 seconds,
  stir through bag, re-insert foot.

STEP 6 — REMOVAL AND AFTERCARE:
  Remove foot from bag.
  IMMEDIATELY use pumice stone on any callus areas.
  The callus is at maximum softness immediately after
  a hot mineral soak — this is the optimal moment
  for physical callus removal.
  
  Do not let callus areas cool before pumice work.
  Rinse foot with warm then cool water.
  Pat dry. Apply a small amount of cool P1S gel to
  the treated areas if desired — the skin is maximally
  receptive to the room-temperature gel in the hours
  following a hot treatment.
```

# PART IV: WHAT TO EXPECT — SENSATION AND RESULT GUIDE

## During Application

```
EXPECTED SENSATIONS (correct):
  □ Pleasant, spreading warmth at skin contact
  □ Gentle drawing or tightening sensation from clay
    as minerals contact skin (bentonite surface charge
    interaction with skin — normal and correct)
  □ Mild earthy scent from bentonite — fades after
    first few minutes of application
  □ Skin feeling "full" or "plumped" under the gel layer
  □ Muscle relaxation in treated area within 5–10 minutes
    (thermotherapy effect)

UNEXPECTED SENSATIONS (assess and respond):
  □ Burning or stinging immediately on application:
    Gel is too hot. Remove immediately. Cool with water.
    Allow gel to cool before reapplying.
  □ Itching under gel layer:
    This may be the bentonite clay mineral interaction.
    Remove and rinse. Perform patch test at lower
    concentration before re-attempting.
  □ No warmth felt at all:
    Gel cooled before application or is below
    therapeutic range. Reheat and reapply.
```

## After Removal

```
EXPECTED RESULTS (correct):
  □ Skin visibly more hydrated — surface sheen, reduced
    appearance of fine lines and dry texture
  □ Skin feels softer to touch than pre-treatment
  □ Slight redness/flush from vasodilation — normal,
    resolves within 20–30 minutes
  □ Muscle area feels relaxed, less tense (body application)
  □ Pores appear temporarily smaller (silica effect if
    full P1S used — or clay adsorption of surface oils)
  □ Mild mineral smell on skin that rinses off completely

RESULTS THAT DEVELOP OVER TIME (1–6 hours post-treatment):
  □ Continued skin softness as moisture redistributes
    through skin layers from the hydrated stratum corneum
  □ Reduction in tightness or pain in treated muscle zones
  □ Dryness takes longer to return than without treatment
    (the SAP moisture depot in the skin takes time to deplete)

WHAT DOES NOT HAPPEN:
  □ The skin does not feel greasy (no oils in formula)
  □ Residue does not remain on skin after rinsing
    (all components water-soluble — rinses completely clean)
  □ Clothing is not stained if gel contacts fabric
    (rinses out with water)
```

# PART V: FULL SESSION PROTOCOLS

## Session Type 1 — Face and Neck (20 Minutes Total)

```
FORMULA: No-silica variant (SAP + bentonite + water)
         Eye area: skip or use reduced-clay formulation
TEMPERATURE: 38–44°C
MATERIALS: 100–150mL gel, 2 paper towels, timer

T+00:00  Heat gel to 38–44°C. Stir. Wrist-test temperature.
T+02:00  Cleanse face with water. Pat to damp (not dry).
T+03:00  Load paper towel with gel. Apply to face.
         Avoid eye area, eyebrows, hairline if desired.
         Press firmly for 5 seconds to establish contact.
T+03:00  Set timer 12–15 minutes. Rest comfortably.
T+15:00  Remove towel. Leave thin gel residue on skin.
         Rest 3–5 minutes with residue on skin.
T+18:00  Rinse with warm water. Then cool water.
         Pat dry gently.
T+20:00  Observe skin condition. Record results.

EXPECTED: Noticeably smoother, more hydrated skin surface.
           Slight flush resolving over 20 minutes.
           No dryness visible where applied.
```

## Session Type 2 — Back and Shoulder (35 Minutes Total)

```
FORMULA: Standard P1S or no-silica variant
TEMPERATURE: 42–48°C
MATERIALS: 300–500mL gel, 4 paper towels (2 per zone),
           elastic bandage or dry towel, timer

T+00:00  Heat gel to 42–48°C. Stir thoroughly.
T+03:00  Prepare 2 paper towel wraps (shoulder + back).
         Load each with 150–200mL gel.
T+05:00  Apply to shoulder: press loaded towel to shoulder,
         secure with dry cloth or elastic bandage.
T+06:00  Apply to back: lie down on loaded towel.
         Body weight holds towel in place.
T+06:00  Set timer for 25 minutes. Rest completely still.
T+31:00  Remove towel from shoulder. Remove back towel
         by rolling to one side.
T+31:00  Leave thin residue on skin. Rest 3–5 minutes.
T+35:00  Shower: warm water first to remove gel (30 seconds).
         Then cool water to close pores (15 seconds).
         Pat dry.

EXPECTED: Noticeable muscle relaxation in shoulder and back.
           Sustained soft skin texture.
           Warmth in treated areas persisting 20–40 minutes
           post-removal as vasodilation slowly resolves.
```

## Session Type 3 — Foot Hot Bootie Treatment (45 Minutes Total)

```
FORMULA: No-silica or standard P1S, higher bentonite preferred
         (5g SAP + 20g bentonite + 500mL water per foot)
TEMPERATURE: 44–48°C (feet tolerate higher temperature)
MATERIALS: 400–600mL gel per foot, 2 gallon zip-lock bags,
           pumice stone, foot towel, chair

T+00:00  Heat gel to 44–48°C. Stir thoroughly.
T+03:00  Pour 400–500mL gel into each zip-lock bag.
T+04:00  Wrist-test temperature of gel in bag.
T+04:00  Insert bare foot into each bag. Seal around ankle.
T+04:00  Sit comfortably with feet resting flat.
         Set timer 25–30 minutes.
T+30:00  Remove feet from bags.
T+30:00  IMMEDIATELY apply pumice to callus areas while
         skin is maximally softened (heel, ball of foot,
         toe edges). Work briskly before skin cools.
T+35:00  Rinse feet with warm then cool water.
T+37:00  Pat dry. Apply small amount of room-temperature
         P1S gel to heels and dry areas as follow-up
         moisture treatment.
T+40:00  Put on clean dry socks to retain residual moisture.
T+45:00  Record results.

EXPECTED: Significant callus softening — pumice removes
           callus material more easily than without treatment.
           Heel skin noticeably softer and more supple.
           Foot odor reduced (bentonite adsorption).
           Sustained soft skin texture for 12–24 hours
           depending on daily moisture retention habits.
```

## Session Type 4 — Full Body Hot Wrap (60 Minutes Total)

```
FORMULA: Standard P1S or no-silica, 1 full batch (1750mL)
TEMPERATURE: 42–48°C
MATERIALS: Full batch heated gel, multiple paper towels or
           a large thin cotton sheet/muslin, plastic wrap,
           dry towels, warm space to lie down

T+00:00  Heat full batch to 42–48°C (microwave in portions
         if vessel too large — heat 500mL at a time,
         combine and stir).
T+08:00  Prepare your space: lay dry towel on floor or bed.
         Have plastic wrap ready.
T+10:00  Apply heated gel to all accessible skin surfaces
         using hands or paper towel applicators.
         Start from feet upward — gel at feet will cool
         first, so applying there first means it is still
         warm when you finish the upper body.
T+15:00  Wrap applied areas in plastic wrap loosely.
         Alternatively: lie on a large muslin sheet soaked
         in the gel, then wrap the sheet around the body.
T+17:00  Cover with dry towels for insulation.
         Lie still for 25–30 minutes. Rest. No movement.
T+45:00  Shower immediately: warm water 60 seconds,
         then cool water 30 seconds.
T+50:00  Pat dry gently. Do not rub.
T+55:00  Rest 10 minutes before returning to activity —
         vasodilation means slightly lower blood pressure
         immediately post-treatment. Stand up slowly.
T+60:00  Record results.

EXPECTED: Full-body skin softness and hydration.
           Visible improvement in skin texture across
           all treated areas. Significant relaxation
           of muscle tension throughout body.
           The treatment depletes a full batch — this
           is the maximum-yield single application.
```
