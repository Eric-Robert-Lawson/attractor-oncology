# USE CASE 1 — FIRE-RESISTANT CLOTHING TREATMENT
## Soaking Fabric in P1S Gel as a Fire Protection Layer

---

## 1.1 — The Structural Derivation

The paper towel fire test (2026-05-13) confirmed that a fabric
substrate soaked in P1S gel does not ignite under direct flame.
It chars without burning. It survives re-exposure.

```
WHAT THE FABRIC SOAKING DOES STRUCTURALLY:

  The gel penetrates the fiber matrix of the fabric.
  Every fiber junction and void in the fabric becomes
  a micro-reservoir of gel.
  
  On flame contact:
  MECHANISM 1: Each fiber-junction reservoir releases water
               via evaporation — the fabric cannot exceed
               ~100°C while water is present.
               
  MECHANISM 2: The gel coating on each fiber excludes oxygen
               from the fiber surface. Cellulose combustion
               requires oxygen at the fiber surface.
               No oxygen contact = no ignition regardless
               of temperature.
               
  MECHANISM 3: As gel dehydrates under sustained heat,
               bentonite mineral residue deposits on each
               fiber surface — a conformal mineral coating
               that continues to exclude oxygen after all
               water is gone.
               
  The fabric does not burn because EACH INDIVIDUAL FIBER
  is coated and protected — not just the surface.
  This is geometrically different from a surface spray
  treatment. The gel is in the fabric, not on it.
```

## 1.2 — Application Method

```
SOAKING PROTOCOL:
  Submerge garment in P1S gel or diluted P1S (50% water)
  for 5–10 minutes. Wring out excess. Allow to partially
  dry before wearing or testing.
  
  The gel in the fabric will begin to dry on the outer
  surface first — creating a slightly stiff outer layer.
  The interior fiber gel remains hydrated longer.
  
  DILUTION FOR WEARABILITY:
  Full-strength P1S gel on clothing = stiff, uncomfortable.
  P1S at 20–30% concentration in water = flexible, wearable,
  still provides meaningful protection while gel is present.
  
  The protection is not permanent — it depletes with time
  and heat exposure. This is not a permanent treatment.
  It is an active protection layer that lasts as long as
  the gel in the fabric remains hydrated.
  
  DURATION OF PROTECTION ESTIMATE:
  In ambient conditions (no heat): gel in fabric hydrated
  for several hours before evaporation depletes it.
  Under active flame: gel depletes in seconds to minutes
  depending on fabric thickness and gel concentration.
  
  REAPPLICATION:
  Redip or re-spray with P1S to restore protection.
  The mineral residue from the previous application
  remains on the fibers and adds to each subsequent layer.
```

# USE CASE 2 — P1S THERMAL SUIT
## Gel Layer + Insulation Shell = Lightweight Personal Cooling/Protection System

---

## 2.1 — The Structural Derivation

This is the most architecturally ambitious of the six use cases.
It derives from two confirmed P1S properties combined:

```
PROPERTY A — THERMAL CEILING:
  P1S gel maintains a ~100°C ceiling at the gel-surface
  interface via evaporative cooling. The surface under
  the gel cannot exceed this temperature while water
  is present, regardless of external heat flux.

PROPERTY B — CONTROLLED EVAPORATION RATE:
  The SAP network holds water at reduced vapor pressure.
  This slows the evaporation rate relative to free water —
  the gel releases water more slowly than a wet cloth.
  This extends the duration of the thermal ceiling.

COMBINED ARCHITECTURE:
  A thin layer of P1S gel applied directly to skin or
  a thin inner fabric layer provides the thermal ceiling.
  An outer insulating shell (neoprene, foam, reflective
  mylar) slows the heat flux reaching the gel layer —
  reducing the evaporation rate and extending duration.
  
  The insulating shell is not doing the cooling.
  The gel is doing the cooling.
  The shell is extending the cooling duration by
  reducing the heat load the gel must manage.
  
  THE GEOMETRY:
  
  External heat source
        ↓ (reduced by outer insulating shell)
  Outer shell (reflective/insulating)
        ↓ (reduced heat flux)
  P1S gel layer (thermal ceiling at ~100°C, cooling actively)
        ↓ (skin temperature maintained)
  Skin surface
```

## 2.2 — The Cooling Suit Application

```
TWO DISTINCT VERSIONS OF THIS SYSTEM:

VERSION A — HEAT PROTECTION SUIT (external heat):
  For: proximity to fire, industrial heat, emergency response
  The gel layer faces outward (between insulation and heat source).
  The insulation is between the gel and the skin.
  
  Architecture:
    Skin → thin inner layer → P1S gel in fabric layer
    → outer insulating/reflective shell
  
  The skin never contacts the gel directly.
  The gel manages the thermal ceiling at the outer surface.
  The insulation prevents the warm gel surface from
  transferring heat to the wearer.

VERSION B — BODY COOLING SUIT (body heat management):
  For: heat stress, athletic recovery, medical cooling,
       hot environment work
  The gel layer is against the skin or thin inner layer.
  Evaporative cooling from the gel draws heat from the body.
  
  Architecture:
    Skin → P1S gel layer (direct or thin fabric separator)
    → outer breathable shell (allows water vapor to escape)
  
  The gel acts as a controlled-evaporation cooling medium.
  Heat moves from skin → gel → evaporation to atmosphere.
  The SAP slows the evaporation rate relative to plain water
  — extending cooling duration per gel charge.
  
  RECHARGING:
  When gel dries out, rewet with water.
  The SAP rehydrates immediately on water contact.
  The gel can be recharged in the field with any water source.
  (Quality degrades with non-distilled water but function
  is maintained — emergency recharge with tap or river water
  is viable.)
```

# USE CASE 3 — P1S FLEXIBLE REUSABLE COOLING PACK
## Non-Rigid Ice Pack That Recharges in the Refrigerator

---

## 3.1 — The Structural Derivation

```
PROPERTY BEING USED:
  SAP gel has a very high specific heat capacity —
  it is ~95% water by mass. Water has the highest
  specific heat of any common substance (4,186 J/kg·K).
  
  Frozen or refrigerated P1S gel:
  - Absorbs heat from the contact surface at 4,186 J/kg·K
    (the same as water — because it is mostly water)
  - The SAP network holds the cold water in contact
    with the surface — the gel conforms to the body
    surface and maintains cold contact rather than
    pooling or running away like ice melt water.
  - The bentonite clay remains stable at cold temperatures.
    No degradation of the thixotropic network at 2–5°C.
  - The gel is non-rigid when refrigerated (unlike
    standard gel packs which freeze solid).
    
  WHAT HAPPENS IN THE FREEZER:
  At 0°C or below, the SAP gel partially freezes.
  The SAP network prevents full rigid crystallization —
  the gel becomes semi-solid, flexible, and pliable
  rather than a rigid frozen block.
  This is the same property that makes SAP-based cold
  packs conformable to body geometry.
  
  WHAT HAPPENS IN THE FRIDGE:
  At 2–5°C, the gel remains fully gel-state — cold,
  conformable, flexible. This is the optimal state for
  cold compress application — cold enough to reduce
  inflammation, flexible enough to conform to any surface.
  
  RECHARGE:
  Used pack warms to room temperature.
  Return to fridge: gel cools again to 2–5°C.
  Full cold capacity restored.
  Indefinitely reusable — the SAP does not degrade
  through freeze/thaw cycling (within normal household
  temperature ranges).
```

## 3.2 — Product Architecture

```
SIMPLEST FORM:
  P1S gel sealed in a zip-lock freezer bag.
  Refrigerate. Apply. Return to fridge. Repeat.
  This works right now with existing batch material.
  Cost: essentially zero beyond the gel itself.

COMMERCIAL FORM:
  P1S gel sealed in a heat-sealed flexible pouch

    Small (hand/wrist/eye): 100mL pouch
    Medium (knee/shoulder/back): 300–500mL pouch
    Large (full back/body zone): 1L pouch
  
  OPTIONAL ADDITION — OUTER FABRIC SLEEVE:
  A fabric sleeve around the gel pouch:
  - Prevents direct skin contact with cold plastic
  - Provides light compression
  - Can be washed separately
  
  This is standard ice pack construction.
  The differentiation is the gel contents — P1S gel
  vs. standard polyacrylamide/glycol gel in competitors.

WHAT P1S ADDS OVER STANDARD GEL PACKS:
  1. No ethylene glycol or propylene glycol — food-safe,
     non-toxic if pouch leaks
  2. Bentonite mineral contact through pouch fabric
     (ions migrate through fabric on contact — the mineral

     the skin surface rather than separating under gravity
  4. Biodegradable and water-disposable if discarded
     (unlike glycol-based gels which require disposal care)
```

# USE CASE 4 — ENCAPSULATED HAIR TREATMENT SYSTEM
## Full Hair Submersion in P1S Gel — Scalp Cap and Hair Bubble Format

---

## 4.1 — The Structural Derivation

```
THE CORE INSIGHT:
  Current deep conditioning masks are applied to hair
  and then the hair is wrapped in a towel or plastic cap.
  The cap traps body heat, which opens the hair cuticle,
  allowing the conditioning agents to penetrate.
  
  The P1S system inverts this:
  Instead of applying mask and then covering:
  → the mask IS the encapsulation.
  
  Hair submerged in P1S gel is:
  - Surrounded on every strand surface by a sustained
    moisture delivery medium (SAP water reservoir)
  - In continuous contact with clay minerals
    (bentonite calcium, magnesium, potassium at
    every strand surface simultaneously)
  - Physically protected from air contact (oxygen
    exclusion = no oxidative damage during treatment)
  - In a microenvironment of controlled humidity
    (the gel maintains near-100% relative humidity
    at every hair strand surface — maximum cuticle
    hydration state possible without heat)
    
  THE HAIR BUBBLE GEOMETRY:
  Hair gathered and pushed into a bag or cap filled
  with P1S gel. The gel fills all voids between strands.
  Every strand is in continuous gel contact.
  Standard conditioning mask: product on outer strand surfaces.
  P1S hair bubble: product on every strand surface including
  the interior of the hair mass simultaneously.
  
  The scrunching motion suggested distributes the gel
  into the hair mass interior before the bag is sealed —
  ensuring full penetration before the treatment begins.
```

## 4.2 — Product Architecture

```
FORMAT A — PRE-FILLED GEL CAP:

  hair simultaneously. Leave 30+ minutes. Remove and rinse.

  - Bentonite clay adsorption of excess sebum and buildup

  - Clay minerals to scalp surface
  This is simultaneously a scalp treatment and hair treatment.

FORMAT B — ACTIVATION BAG WITH ADD-WATER SACHET:
  A sealed bag with dry blend inside.
  User adds their wet hair to the bag.
  Adds a small amount of warm water to activate the dry blend.
  Closes the bag around their head (hair inside, bag sealed
  at the scalp line with an elastic or drawstring).
  Scrunches hair to distribute activating gel throughout
  the hair mass. Leaves 30+ minutes. Removes bag. Rinses.
  
  ACTIVATION WATER TEMPERATURE:
  Warm water (37–42°C) for conditioning application:
  slightly warm water opens the cuticle, improving
  mineral and moisture penetration.
  
  The dry blend format means:
  - Long shelf life (18–24 months without preservative)

  Under scrunching pressure: gel flows into hair voids.
```

# USE CASE 5 — P1S HOT WRAP
## Heated Gel on Paper Towel as Self-Applied Hot Compress / Home Spa Treatment

---

## 5.1 — The Structural Derivation

```
THE THERMAL CEILING PROPERTY — USED IN REVERSE:
  All prior fire protection uses exploit the ~100°C ceiling
  as a maximum temperature limit to protect from heat.
  
  The hot wrap inverts this:
  The ~100°C ceiling is now a SAFETY CEILING for a heated
  therapeutic application — the gel can be heated to
  therapeutic temperature (40–50°C) and applied to skin,
  with the thermal ceiling ensuring it cannot exceed
  the gel's evaporation temperature at the skin interface.
  
  A hot compress at 42–45°C provides:
  - Vasodilation (increased local blood flow)
  - Muscle relaxation (heat penetrates superficial tissue)
  - Enhanced absorption of topical actives (heat opens
    skin pores and increases diffusion rate)
  - Pain relief via thermoreceptor stimulation
  
  P1S GEL AS HOT COMPRESS MEDIUM:
  Heat P1S gel to 42–50°C (hot enough for therapeutic
  effect, well below the evaporation ceiling).
  Apply gel directly to skin or via paper towel/cloth carrier.
  The SAP network holds the heat against the skin surface
  as the gel conforms to body geometry.
  The bentonite clay delivers minerals to warmed skin
  (heat increases skin permeability — mineral delivery
  is enhanced vs. room temperature application).
  The SAP simultaneously delivers sustained moisture
  to the heat-opened skin surface.
  
  THREE MECHANISMS ACTIVE SIMULTANEOUSLY:
  1. Thermotherapy (heat from gel)
  2. Mineral delivery (bentonite, enhanced by heat)
  3. Sustained moisture delivery (SAP)
  
  No existing single product delivers all three.
```

## 5.2 — Application Method

```
PREPARATION:
  Heat P1S gel in microwave-safe container:
  30-second increments, stir between, until gel
  reaches 42–50°C (test with thermometer or wrist test —
  should feel pleasantly warm, not hot or painful).
  
  DO NOT EXCEED 60°C in preparation:
  Above 60°C the SAP polymer begins to lose water
  retention capacity. The gel becomes thinner and
  less effective. Stay below this threshold.
  
  TARGET TEMPERATURE: 42–48°C
  This is the therapeutic hot compress range —
  above body temperature (37°C), below pain threshold
  (~48°C for brief contact, ~43°C for sustained contact).
  
  PAPER TOWEL APPLICATION METHOD:
  Soak paper towel in heated gel.
  Wring slightly so towel is saturated but not dripping.
  Apply to skin surface. Leave 10–20 minutes.
  Re-warm if desired for extended treatment.
  
  DIRECT APPLICATION METHOD:
  Spoon or pour heated gel directly onto skin.
  Spread to 3–5mm layer.
  Cover with a dry cloth to retain heat.
  Leave 10–20 minutes.

FORMULA NOTE:
  The 5g SAP + 15g bentonite + 1000mL water formula
  is particularly well suited to the hot wrap application:
  - Lower viscosity when warm (easier to apply)
  - High bentonite content for maximum mineral delivery
    during the heat-enhanced absorption window
  - Sufficient SAP for sustained moisture during treatment
  - Rinses cleanly from skin after treatment
  
  The no-silica formula is correct for this application.
  Fumed silica adds no benefit to a thermal application
  and is not required.
```
