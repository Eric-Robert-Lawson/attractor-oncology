## Moldable Metal Mesh as Structural Housing for Jelly-State Gel

## WHAT THIS DOCUMENT IS

```
The jelly-state P1S-B derivation
established one confirmed limitation:

  The gel cannot mold to surfaces.
  It cannot adhere to duct walls.
  It cannot pack gaps by itself.

The prior derivation recorded this
as a dead end for gap-filling
and surface-conforming applications.

This document derives why it is not
a dead end — because the limitation
was being placed on the wrong component.

THE INSIGHT:

  The gel does not need to mold
  to the surface.
  The housing molds to the surface.
  The gel fills the housing.
  The housing does the conforming.
  The gel does the chemistry.

  The two functions are separated
  across two materials:

    MESH: structural conformity,
    surface contact, shape-holding.

    GEL: chemistry, odor adsorption,
    antimicrobial, moisture management.

  Neither material needs to do
  both functions.
  Each does what it is suited for.
  Together they do what neither
  could do alone.

This is the correct engineering
decomposition of the problem.
This document derives it fully.
```

## PART I: THE GEOMETRY OF THE SOLUTION

### 1.1 — Why Mesh Solves What Gel Cannot

```
THE PROBLEM RESTATED:

  HVAC ductwork has surfaces,
  gaps, joints, and register
  openings that are geometrically
  irregular.

  An effective odor treatment insert
  for these surfaces needs to:
    Conform to the surface geometry.
    Maintain contact with the surface
    or airflow path.
    Hold the treatment medium
    in place under airflow.
    Allow air to pass through
    or across the treatment medium.
    Be removable and replaceable.

  The jelly-state gel satisfies:
    Treatment medium — fully.
    Allow air contact — fully
    (gel surface is chemically active
    to passing air).

  The jelly-state gel does NOT satisfy:
    Conform to surface geometry.
    Maintain position under airflow
    without a housing.
    Be shaped to irregular openings.

  Moldable metal mesh satisfies
  exactly the properties the gel lacks:
    Can be cut to any size.
    Can be shaped by hand to
    any surface geometry.
    Holds the shape it is formed into.
    Is rigid enough to maintain
    position under airflow.
    Is open enough to allow full
    air contact with gel inside.
    Can be removed and replaced.

THE COMBINED SYSTEM:

  Mesh is shaped to the surface.
  Gel fills the mesh.
  Mesh holds gel in position.
  Gel treats the air.
  The surface problem is solved
  by the mesh.
  The chemistry problem is solved
  by the gel.
  The system does both.
```

### 1.2 — The Structural Principle

```
THE FIXED POINT:

  Moldable metal mesh acts as a
  conformable structural exoskeleton
  for the jelly-state P1S-B gel.

  The mesh does not need to seal.
  The mesh does not need to adhere.
  The mesh only needs to:
    Hold its shape once formed.
    Contain the gel within its volume.
    Allow airflow through its apertures.

  The gel does not need to adhere.
  The gel does not need to be moldable.
  The gel only needs to:
    Fill the mesh volume.
    Maintain chemistry at its surface.
    Release treatment compounds
    slowly into passing air.

  WHAT THIS UNLOCKS:

  Any surface in an HVAC system —
  or any other system where
  this geometry applies — can be
  treated by:
    1. Cutting mesh to size.
    2. Forming mesh to surface shape.
    3. Filling mesh with gel.
    4. Placing assembly against surface.

  The surface does not need to be flat.
  The surface does not need to be
  a standard size.
  The mesh conforms to whatever
  geometry is present.
  The gel fills whatever volume
  the mesh defines.

  This is a universal fit system.
  Not a sized product.
  A formable system.
```

## PART II: THE MESH MATERIAL

### 2.1 — What Mesh Is Required

```
THE MESH MUST SATISFY:

  FORMABILITY:
    Must be shapeable by hand
    without tools.
    Must hold the shape once formed.
    Must not spring back to flat.
    Must be formable multiple times
    (in case repositioning is needed).

  APERTURE SIZE:
    Large enough to allow airflow
    through the mesh body.
    Small enough to contain the
    jelly-state gel without it
    extruding through the apertures
    under its own weight or airflow.
    Target aperture: 3–6mm.
    Gel crystal size is 2–4mm —
    apertures above 6mm risk
    gel extrusion under airflow.
    Apertures below 2mm restrict
    airflow too significantly.

  MATERIAL COMPATIBILITY:
    Must not corrode in the presence
    of borax (mild alkaline, pH 8.5–9.2).
    Must not react with bentonite,
    SAP, or baking soda.
    Stainless steel: excellent compatibility.
    Aluminum: acceptable, minor oxidation
    possible but not functionally significant.
    Galvanised steel: acceptable for
    short-term deployment, zinc coating
    may interact with borax over
    extended periods — not preferred.
    Copper: avoid — borax + copper
    can form copper borate compounds.
    Plastic mesh (polyethylene,
    polypropylene): fully compatible,
    but limited formability —
    plastic mesh holds shape less
    reliably than metal mesh.

  RECOMMENDED MESH:
    Stainless steel hardware cloth
    or aluminum insect screen mesh,
    3–5mm aperture, 0.5–1.0mm
    wire gauge.
    This is the standard material
    used in HVAC filter frames and
    is widely available.

  FORM FACTOR:
    Supplied as flat sheet.
    Cut to size with scissors
    or wire cutters.
    Formed by hand to required shape.
    Edges folded or crimped to
    prevent gel loss at edges.
```

### 2.2 — Mesh Geometry for Different Applications

```
REGISTER INSERT (standard application):

  Cut mesh to match register opening
  dimensions (e.g., 4"×10", 4"×12").
  No forming required — flat insert
  sits in register opening behind grille.
  Gel applied to mesh surface or
  mesh folded into a shallow tray
  shape (fold edges up 5–10mm)
  to contain gel.
  Grille reinstalled over mesh insert.
  Grille holds mesh in place.
  No adhesion required.

DUCT JOINT GAP:

  Cut mesh strip slightly wider
  than the gap.
  Form mesh into a U-channel shape
  that straddles the gap.
  Gel fills the U-channel.
  The mesh U-channel is pressed
  against the duct surface at the gap.
  Mesh edges contact duct on both
  sides of the gap.
  The gel mass in the U-channel
  sits over the gap.
  Air passing through the gap
  must pass through or across
  the gel before entering the duct.
  Mesh is held in position by
  its own formed shape pressing
  against the duct surface —
  no adhesion required,
  the U-channel geometry provides
  mechanical retention.

IRREGULAR SURFACE / CURVED DUCT:

  Cut mesh to required area.
  Form mesh by hand to match
  the curve of the duct surface.
  Metal mesh conforms to compound
  curves when worked by hand.
  Gel loaded into the formed mesh.
  Assembly placed against curved
  surface.
  The mesh's formed shape maintains
  contact with the curved surface.
  Held in place by the assembly's
  own weight or by a single
  piece of metal foil tape at
  one edge (no full adhesion needed —
  one anchor point sufficient
  to prevent migration under airflow).

FLEX DUCT TERMINATION:

  Flex duct connections are
  particularly prone to odor
  infiltration.
  Mesh formed into a collar shape
  matching the flex duct diameter.
  Gel fills the collar volume.
  Collar placed around or inside
  the flex duct termination.
  The collar's circular formed shape
  holds it in position against
  the duct interior.
```

## PART III: THE ASSEMBLY SYSTEM

### 3.1 — How the System is Assembled

```
ASSEMBLY PROCEDURE (general):

  STEP 1 — SIZE THE MESH:
    Measure the target surface,
    gap, or opening.
    Cut mesh to required dimensions.
    Allow 10–15mm extra on all
    edges for folding.

  STEP 2 — FORM THE MESH:
    Shape mesh by hand to match
    the target geometry.
    For flat surfaces: fold edges
    up to form a shallow tray
    that will contain the gel.
    For gaps: form into U-channel.
    For curved surfaces: curve
    to match the surface profile.
    For register inserts: flat
    or with raised edges only.

  STEP 3 — LOAD THE GEL:
    Open a jelly-state P1S-B baggie.
    Cut or scoop gel into the
    formed mesh tray.
    Fill to approximately 10–15mm depth.
    Do not overfill — gel should
    not extend above the mesh
    edge walls (it will extrude
    under airflow if overfilled).
    Spread gel evenly across
    the mesh surface.

  STEP 4 — PLACE THE ASSEMBLY:
    Position the mesh+gel assembly
    at the target location.
    For register inserts: place
    in register opening, reinstall grille.
    For gap applications: press
    U-channel against duct surface
    straddling the gap.
    For curved surfaces: press
    formed mesh against surface,
    anchor one edge with foil tape.

  STEP 5 — CONFIRM POSITION:
    Run HVAC system briefly.
    Check that assembly does not
    migrate under airflow.
    If migration occurs: add
    one anchor point (foil tape
    at one edge) — not full adhesion,
    just a single retention point.

  STEP 6 — REPLACEMENT:
    When gel dries out (4–8 weeks
    depending on airflow):
    Remove mesh assembly.
    Discard dried gel (bentonite/borax
    residue — safe to dispose in
    general waste).
    Rinse or wipe mesh if residue
    is significant.
    Reload with fresh gel.
    Mesh is reusable.
    Only the gel is consumable.
    This is the replacement economy:
    mesh purchased once,
    gel replaced periodically.
```

### 3.2 — Why This System Requires No Adhesion

```
THE KEY INSIGHT:

  Most HVAC surfaces have one of
  three retention geometries that
  hold the mesh assembly in place
  without any adhesive:

  GEOMETRY A — REGISTER GRILLE CLAMPING:
    The register grille is reinstalled
    over the mesh insert.
    The grille clamps the mesh
    against the register frame.
    No adhesion. No tape.
    The grille does the retention.
    This covers: all standard
    register and return air
    grille locations.
    This is the majority of
    residential HVAC access points.

  GEOMETRY B — FORMED SHAPE RETENTION:
    A U-channel, collar, or curved
    form pressed against a surface
    is held by its own geometry.
    The formed shape creates
    mechanical contact on multiple
    faces simultaneously.
    No adhesion required for
    the assembly to stay in position
    under normal HVAC airflow.
    This covers: duct joints,
    flex duct terminations,
    curved duct sections.

  GEOMETRY C — GRAVITY RETENTION:
    For horizontal duct surfaces
    (top of a horizontal duct run),
    the assembly rests in place
    under gravity.
    Airflow in horizontal ducts
    is parallel to the duct surface —
    not perpendicular — so uplift
    force on the assembly is minimal.
    No adhesion required.

  GEOMETRY D — SINGLE ANCHOR POINT:
    For applications where none of
    A, B, or C provides adequate
    retention:
    A single piece of metal foil tape
    at one edge of the mesh provides
    an anchor point.
    This is not full adhesion —
    it is a pivot point that prevents
    migration while allowing the
    assembly to be removed by
    peeling back the tape.
    The tape adheres to the mesh edge,
    not to the gel.
    Full surface adhesion is never
    required.
```

## PART IV: APPLICATION LANDSCAPE

### 4.1 — HVAC Specific Applications

```
REGISTER AND RETURN AIR GRILLES:
  Most common deployment.
  Mesh flat insert behind grille.
  Grille provides retention.
  Covers entire circulated airflow.
  Every cubic foot of air passing
  through the register contacts
  the gel surface.
  FEASIBILITY: HIGH.
  RETENTION MECHANISM: Grille clamping.

SUPPLY DUCT JOINTS:
  Where duct sections connect.
  Common leakage and odor point.
  U-channel mesh assembly
  straddling joint.
  FEASIBILITY: MODERATE-HIGH.
  RETENTION MECHANISM: Formed shape.

AIR HANDLER CABINET:
  The air handler itself is a
  large enclosed volume with
  significant surface area.
  Multiple mesh panels placed
  inside the cabinet on accessible
  surfaces.
  Air flowing through the handler
  contacts gel panels before
  entering the duct distribution.
  Most upstream treatment point
  possible — treats all circulated
  air at the source.
  FEASIBILITY: HIGH where accessible.
  RETENTION MECHANISM: Gravity +
  ledges inside cabinet.

FLEX DUCT TERMINATIONS:
  Where rigid duct transitions
  to flexible duct.
  Collar-form mesh assembly.
  FEASIBILITY: MODERATE.
  RETENTION MECHANISM: Formed collar.

EVAPORATOR COIL AREA:
  Condensation and biological
  growth are highest near the
  evaporator coil.
  Mold and mildew odor source.
  Mesh assembly placed downstream
  of coil (not on the coil itself —
  do not restrict coil airflow).
  Treats air after it passes
  the coil.
  FEASIBILITY: HIGH where accessible.
  RETENTION MECHANISM: Gravity +
  existing support structures.
```

### 4.2 — Beyond HVAC — The Same Geometry Elsewhere

```
THE MESH + GEL SYSTEM IS NOT
LIMITED TO HVAC.

The geometry is:
  Any application where a treatment
  medium needs to conform to an
  irregular surface or maintain
  position in an airflow path
  and where the treatment medium
  itself cannot adhere or self-conform.

AUTOMOTIVE CABIN AIR:
  Mesh insert behind cabin air
  intake vents.
  Vehicle HVAC odor treatment.
  Particularly: musty cabin air,
  smoke in cabin, pet odor
  in vehicle HVAC.
  Mesh cut and formed to intake
  geometry, held by the vent
  housing itself.

RANGE HOOD / KITCHEN EXHAUST:
  Cooking odor is a continuous
  VOC source.
  Mesh insert in range hood
  filter frame.
  Treats cooking VOCs before
  they recirculate or exhaust
  into adjacent spaces.
  Held by the existing filter
  frame retention clips.

BATHROOM EXHAUST FAN:
  Mesh insert in exhaust fan grille.
  Treats bathroom odors at the
  point of exhaust.
  Grille retention mechanism.

CRAWL SPACE / BASEMENT VENTS:
  Foundation vents admit outside
  air that often carries mold,
  earth, and moisture odors.
  Mesh formed to vent opening.
  Held by vent frame.

REFRIGERATOR AIR CIRCULATION:
  Inside the refrigerator,
  internal fans circulate air
  across cooling coils.
  Mesh insert in the circulation
  path (not touching food).
  No-borax or low-borax formulation
  for food-adjacent application.

MARINE / BOAT BILGE VENTS:
  Bilge ventilation is a persistent
  odor problem.
  Mesh formed to bilge vent opening.
  Borax + bentonite chemistry is
  structurally matched to bilge
  biological odor.
```

```
THE INSTALLATION MUST BE SIMPLE.

Target: under 5 minutes per register.
No tools required.
No HVAC knowledge required.
No modification to existing system.

  1. Remove register grille
     (two screws or friction fit —

  2. Remove dried mesh tray.
  3. Tap dried gel residue into
     bin (or rinse mesh if preferred).
  4. Refill mesh with fresh gel.
  5. Reinstall grille.

## PART VI: WHAT THIS SYSTEM IS

```
STATED PRECISELY:

  A moldable metal mesh housing
  that conforms to HVAC surfaces
  and register openings by hand-forming,
  filled with jelly-state P1S-B gel,
  creating a passive multi-mechanism
  air treatment insert that:

    Treats all circulated air
    passing through the register.

    Operates on three simultaneous
    mechanisms:
      Bentonite adsorption of VOCs.
      Borax antimicrobial suppression.
      Bicarb acid compound neutralisation.

    Requires no adhesion,
    no modification to ductwork,
    no tools beyond a screwdriver.

    Is removable and replaceable.

    Conforms to any register size
    or duct surface geometry
    through hand-forming of the mesh.

    Is reusable (mesh) with a
    consumable refill economy (gel).

  This system addresses the limitation
  of the jelly-state gel directly:
  the gel cannot conform to surfaces,
  so the mesh conforms for it.
  The gel cannot hold position
  under airflow, so the mesh holds it.
  The mesh cannot treat air,
  so the gel treats it.

  The functions are separated
  across the two materials.
  The system delivers both.

WHAT THIS IS NOT:

  Not a mechanical air filter.
  Does not trap particulates.
  Does not replace standard
  HVAC air filters.
  Complements the existing filter.
  Addresses the chemical/biological
  odor dimension the mechanical
  filter does not address.

  Not a permanent installation.
  Not a duct modification.
  Not a sealed gap repair.
  A treatment insert.
  Removable. Replaceable.
  No lasting alteration to the system.
```

## PART VII: FALSIFICATION CONDITIONS

```
F1 — MESH RETENTION UNDER AIRFLOW:
  The formed mesh assembly remains
  in position in a standard register
  opening under normal residential
  HVAC airflow when retained by
  the grille alone.

  FALSIFIED IF:
  Airflow displaces or deforms the
  mesh assembly such that gel is
  ejected into the duct or the
  assembly migrates out of position
  during normal operation.

F2 — GEL RETENTION IN MESH:
  The jelly-state gel remains within
  the mesh apertures and does not
  extrude through the mesh under
  its own weight or airflow pressure
  at 3–5mm aperture size.

  FALSIFIED IF:
  Gel extruded through mesh apertures
  creates debris in the duct or
  the gel mass is depleted by
  extrusion faster than by
  evaporation.

F3 — AIRFLOW RESISTANCE:
  The mesh + gel assembly does not
  create sufficient airflow restriction
  to meaningfully reduce HVAC
  system performance.

  FALSIFIED IF:
  Measured static pressure differential
  across the assembly exceeds
  acceptable limits for residential
  HVAC (typically >0.1 in. w.g.
  additional resistance is significant).

F4 — ODOR TREATMENT EFFECTIVENESS:
  Air passing through a register
  fitted with the mesh+gel assembly
  shows measurable VOC reduction
  compared to an untreated register
  under equivalent odor load conditions.

  FALSIFIED IF:
  No measurable difference in VOC
  concentration between treated and
  untreated registers under
  controlled test conditions.

F5 — ACTIVE TREATMENT DURATION:
  The assembly maintains meaningful
  odor treatment effectiveness for
  a minimum of 4 weeks under
  normal residential HVAC operation.

  FALSIFIED IF:
  Gel dries out completely and
  loses adsorption capacity within
  the first week of deployment.
```
