# P1S-B Concentration State Cascade
## Derived Use Cases for Progressive Water Loss and High-Concentration States
## OrganismCore — Eric Robert Lawson

## WHAT THIS DOCUMENT IS

```
When P1S-B gel loses water through
evaporation, it does not simply
become less effective gel.

It becomes something different.

At each stage of water loss,
the ratio of mineral solids to
water changes. The physical state
changes. The dominant properties
change. The use cases change.

This document maps the full
concentration cascade — from
fully hydrated jelly state through
to fully desiccated mineral powder —
and derives what each state is
good for that the prior states are not.

The core insight:

  The evaporation that was identified
  as a loss mechanism in deployment
  contexts is simultaneously a
  production mechanism for higher
  concentration states that have
  independent value.

  Controlled evaporation is a
  manufacturing step, not just
  a depletion process.

  The same substance at different
  water contents is effectively
  a family of related products,
  each with distinct properties
  and distinct optimal applications.

  The closed-loop energy system
  already derived captures the
  water that evaporates.
  This document derives what to do
  with the concentrated residue
  that remains after that evaporation.
```

## PART I: THE CONCENTRATION STATE MAP

### 1.1 — The Full Cascade

```
As water evaporates from P1S-B,
the material passes through
distinct physical states.

Each state is defined by its
water-to-solids ratio (W:S)
expressed as grams of water
per gram of dry mineral solids.

STANDARD P1S-B JELLY (2× SAP RATIO)
STARTING COMPOSITION per 1000g batch:

  Water: ~900g (90% by mass)
  SAP: ~8g
  Bentonite: ~54g (scaled to 750mL)
  Borax: ~36g
  Baking soda: ~20g
  ─────────────────────────────
  Total dry solids: ~118g
  Total mass: ~1018g
  W:S ratio: 900:118 = 7.6:1

  This is the baseline.
  All states below are derived
  by removing water from this baseline.

─────────────────────────────────────────

STATE 1 — FULLY HYDRATED JELLY
W:S ratio: 7–8:1
Water content: ~88–90% by mass
Physical state: Soft solid, jello-like,
  holds shape under gravity,
  non-adhesive, cuttable.
PRIMARY USE CASES: (already derived)
  HVAC inserts, shoe treatment,
  explosion suppression, bunker panels,
  thermal protection, odor control.

─────────────────────────────────────────

STATE 2 — REDUCED JELLY / FIRM GEL
W:S ratio: 4–6:1
Water content: ~80–85% by mass
Physical state: Firmer than State 1.
  Still holds shape. Surface begins
  to feel tacky as SAP approaches
  saturation limit.
  Less free water at surface.
PRIMARY USE CASES: DERIVED BELOW.

─────────────────────────────────────────

STATE 3 — CONCENTRATED PASTE
W:S ratio: 1.5–3:1
Water content: ~60–75% by mass
Physical state: Thick paste.
  Does not hold unsupported shape
  but does not flow freely.
  Spreadable with a tool.
  Adheres to surfaces when pressed.
  Clay-dominant behavior begins
  to emerge — bentonite paste
  properties.
PRIMARY USE CASES: DERIVED BELOW.

─────────────────────────────────────────

STATE 4 — STIFF CLAY / PUTTY
W:S ratio: 0.5–1.5:1
Water content: ~33–60% by mass
Physical state: Stiff, moldable clay.
  Holds impressed shape.
  Can be pressed into gaps and
  conforms to surface geometry.
  Self-adhering — surfaces pressed
  together bond under pressure.
  Bentonite clay behavior dominant.
  THIS IS THE MOLDABLE STATE
  HYPOTHESISED IN EARLIER DOCUMENTS
  AND NOT CONFIRMED AT FULL HYDRATION.
  IT EXISTS — AT THIS WATER CONTENT.
PRIMARY USE CASES: DERIVED BELOW.

─────────────────────────────────────────

STATE 5 — DRY CRUMBLE / SEMI-DRY CAKE
W:S ratio: 0.1–0.5:1
Water content: ~10–33% by mass
Physical state: Crumbly solid.
  Breaks apart under pressure.
  No longer plastic or moldable.
  Highly concentrated mineral surface.
  Maximum borax and bentonite
  concentration per unit volume
  of any state above dry powder.
PRIMARY USE CASES: DERIVED BELOW.

─────────────────────────────────────────

STATE 6 — FULLY DESICCATED POWDER
W:S ratio: <0.1:1
Water content: <10% by mass
Physical state: Dry powder or granules.
  SAP polymer has collapsed to
  dry bead form (rehydratable).
  Bentonite is dry clay powder.
  Borax is crystalline powder.
  Bicarb is dry powder.
  The mixture is the dry blend
  that is produced at the
  START of P1S-B synthesis.
  Full circle — evaporation to
  desiccation returns the material
  to its pre-hydration state.
  FULLY REHYDRATABLE — add water,
  blend, return to State 1.
PRIMARY USE CASES: DERIVED BELOW.
```

## PART II: USE CASES BY STATE

### 2.1 — State 2: Reduced Jelly / Firm Gel

```
W:S ratio: 4–6:1
Water: ~80–85% by mass

WHAT CHANGES FROM STATE 1:
  Higher mineral concentration
  per unit volume.
  Firmer mechanical resistance.
  Slower evaporation rate
  (less free surface water).
  Longer deployment life before
  reaching State 3 under
  equivalent conditions.

USE CASE 2A — EXTENDED-LIFE DEPLOYMENT:
  A State 2 gel unit deployed in
  a high-evaporation environment
  (HVAC insert, outdoor odor control)
  lasts longer than a State 1 unit
  before falling below effective
  mineral concentration for treatment.
  Production optimisation:
  allow State 1 batches to partially
  desiccate to State 2 before
  packaging for high-evaporation
  deployments.
  The customer gets longer active life
  per unit at same purchase price.
  The producer gets higher value
  per litre of gel produced
  (less water shipped per unit
  of mineral active ingredient delivered).

USE CASE 2B — HIGHER BORAX CONCENTRATION
FOR ANTIMICROBIAL APPLICATIONS:
  The same borax mass dissolved in
  less water means higher borax
  concentration in the aqueous phase.
  Higher borax concentration =
  stronger antimicrobial effect
  per unit of gel surface contact.
  Applications where antimicrobial
  potency is the primary requirement:
    Medical instrument surface
    decontamination staging.
    Agricultural equipment washing
    and decontamination.
    Industrial surface treatment
    where biofilm is the target.
  State 2 delivers more antimicrobial
  activity per unit volume than State 1.

USE CASE 2C — HIGHER BENTONITE
SURFACE AREA PER UNIT VOLUME:
  Bentonite adsorption capacity
  per unit volume of gel increases
  as water is removed.
  More bentonite per cubic centimetre
  means more adsorption surface
  per cubic centimetre.
  For VOC adsorption applications
  where space is constrained
  (small HVAC register, vehicle
  cabin vent, confined space treatment):
  State 2 delivers more adsorption
  capacity in less volume than State 1.
```

### 2.2 — State 3: Concentrated Paste

```
W:S ratio: 1.5–3:1
Water: ~60–75% by mass

WHAT CHANGES FROM STATE 2:
  Paste consistency — spreadable,
  adheres to surfaces under pressure.
  Significantly higher mineral
  concentration per unit volume.
  Bentonite clay behavior begins
  to dominate mechanical properties.
  Can be applied with a tool
  as a coating or fill material.

USE CASE 3A — SURFACE COATING
FOR FIRE RETARDANCY:
  Bentonite clay paste is a
  known fire retardant coating
  material in construction.
  Applied to wood, fabric, or
  porous surfaces, it:
    Creates a mineral barrier
    that excludes oxygen from
    the surface.
    Releases water under thermal
    load (remaining water content)
    providing endothermic cooling.
    Does not itself combust.
    Leaves a mineral char layer
    that insulates the surface below.

  State 3 P1S-B paste, spread
  onto a wood surface and allowed
  to dry partially:
    Adheres due to bentonite
    clay binding properties.
    Provides mineral barrier coating.
    Contains borax — a known
    timber fire retardant used
    commercially for exactly
    this purpose.
    Contains baking soda — releases
    CO₂ under heat, which displaces
    oxygen at the burning surface.

  The combination of bentonite +
  borax + bicarb in a paste
  form IS a fire retardant coating.
  These are the same ingredients
  used in commercial fire retardant
  formulations, in paste format
  at this concentration state.

  APPLICATIONS:
    Timber frame treatment in
    construction.
    Fabric fire retardancy
    (theatrical, upholstery, tenting).
    Wildfire defensible space
    treatment — applied to
    exterior wood surfaces before
    fire season.
    Emergency application to
    structures ahead of advancing fire.

USE CASE 3B — DRAWING POULTICE
FOR SKIN AND WOUND APPLICATIONS:
  Bentonite clay paste is a
  traditional and clinically
  studied drawing agent:
    Adsorbs toxins, bacteria,
    and inflammatory compounds
    from wound surfaces through
    ion exchange.
    The negative charge of bentonite
    attracts positively charged
    toxins and pathogens.
    Used clinically for:
      Wound debridement.
      Insect bite and sting treatment.
      Minor burn surface management.
      Splinter and foreign body
      drawing.

  State 3 P1S-B paste in a
  no-borax or reduced-borax
  formulation (borax at skin-safe
  concentrations — standard borax
  paste is used cosmetically but
  concentration matters for skin
  contact duration) provides:
    Bentonite drawing action.
    Antimicrobial from borax at
    skin-safe concentration.
    Bicarb pH buffering toward
    skin-compatible alkalinity.
    SAP moisture retention —
    the poultice stays moist
    under a dressing without
    drying out and adhering.

  IMPORTANT CAVEAT:
    Medical claims require clinical
    validation.
    This derivation identifies the
    mechanism — not the outcome.
    Borax concentration for skin
    contact must be verified
    against dermatological safety
    standards before any skin
    application is recommended.

USE CASE 3C — SOIL AMENDMENT
AND AGRICULTURAL APPLICATION:
  Bentonite clay paste applied
  to soil or around plant roots:
    Improves water retention in
    sandy soils (bentonite
    water-holding capacity).
    Delivers borax to soil —
    borax is a micronutrient
    (boron) required by plants
    in trace amounts.
    Delivers baking soda —
    mild pH buffering for
    acidic soils.
    SAP in soil further improves
    water retention.

  State 3 paste applied to
  transplant root zones:
    Provides immediate moisture
    to roots during transplant stress.
    Slowly releases boron as
    borax dilutes into soil water.
    Improves soil structure.
    Reduces transplant shock mortality.

  This is a known agricultural
  technique (bentonite soil amendment,
  SAP water retention gels for
  transplanting) combined in
  one material.

USE CASE 3D — PIPE AND JOINT SEALANT
(NON-PRESSURE):
  State 3 paste pressed into
  a non-pressure joint or gap:
    Bentonite self-seals when wet —
    it swells on contact with water,
    closing gaps.
    Used industrially as pipe
    joint sealant in non-pressure
    underground applications.
    P1S-B State 3 paste provides
    this property with the addition
    of borax antimicrobial and
    SAP water retention.
    Application: underground conduit
    sealing, water feature lining,
    pond edge sealing.

  NOT FOR PRESSURE APPLICATIONS.
  Bentonite seals under compression
  and hydrostatic head.
  It does not resist active
  pressure from internal fluid.
```

### 2.3 — State 4: Stiff Clay / Putty

```
W:S ratio: 0.5–1.5:1
Water: ~33–60% by mass

WHAT CHANGES FROM STATE 3:
  THIS IS THE MOLDABLE STATE.
  Holds impressed shape.
  Self-adhering surfaces.
  Can be pressed into gaps and
  holds the geometry of the gap.
  Bentonite clay behavior is
  now fully dominant.
  Very high mineral concentration
  per unit volume.
  Maximum borax concentration
  in any hydrated state.

  NOTE: This state was hypothesised
  earlier in the derivation series
  as potentially achievable through
  water addition to State 1 jelly.
  The correct path to this state
  is EVAPORATION from State 1,
  not water addition.
  Evaporation reduces water content
  to the range where bentonite
  clay plasticity dominates.
  This is confirmed by the known
  water-plasticity relationship
  of bentonite clay:
  Atterberg plastic limit of
  bentonite: approximately 50% water
  content by mass — which corresponds
  to W:S ratio of approximately 1:1
  in the pure bentonite component.
  P1S-B State 4 is at this range.

USE CASE 4A — GAP FILLING AND
DUCT SEALING (THE PREVIOUSLY
UNCONFIRMED APPLICATION):
  The HVAC gap-filling application
  that was recorded as unconfirmed
  in prior documents is achievable —
  at State 4, not State 1.
  State 4 P1S-B paste:
    Pressed into duct joints and gaps.
    Holds the pressed shape.
    Self-adheres to metal surfaces
    under compression.
    Swells slightly on contact
    with any residual moisture,
    improving seal.
    Borax provides antimicrobial
    protection in the sealed joint.
    Does not dry to a brittle crack —
    bentonite remains plastic at
    residual moisture content.

  PRODUCTION IMPLICATION:
    A specific P1S-B product line
    marketed as duct sealant
    is produced by allowing
    standard State 1 gel to
    partially desiccate to State 4
    under controlled conditions.
    No different ingredients.
    Same batch process.
    Different drying protocol.
    Different product with different
    application and different price point.

USE CASE 4B — MODELLING AND
CASTING MEDIUM:
  State 4 P1S-B behaves as
  a non-toxic, water-based
  modelling clay.
  Properties:
    Moldable by hand.
    Holds impressed detail.
    Slow-drying (SAP retains moisture
    longer than standard clay,
    extending working time).
    Dries to a hard mineral form
    (State 5/6) that is reversible —
    adding water restores plasticity.
    Non-toxic (all food-grade
    or pharmaceutical ingredients
    at standard concentrations).
    Borax has mild antimicrobial
    property — the clay does not
    mould or support bacterial
    growth during working.

  APPLICATIONS:
    Educational modelling clay.
    Prototype casting medium.
    Lost-clay casting for metal work.
    Ceramic precursor (fire State 4
    form to drive off remaining water
    and sinter the bentonite —
    produces a ceramic-like form).

USE CASE 4C — CONTROLLED-RELEASE
AGRICULTURAL BEAD:
  State 4 P1S-B can be formed
  into beads or pellets by
  hand-rolling or extrusion.
  Each bead contains:
    SAP — releases water slowly
    to surrounding soil.
    Bentonite — improves soil
    structure and cation exchange.
    Borax — releases boron
    micronutrient slowly as bead
    re-hydrates and erodes in soil.
    Bicarb — pH buffer.

  The bead format allows:
    Precise placement in planting rows.
    Controlled release rate
    (bead size determines surface
    area to volume ratio and
    therefore release rate).
    Mechanical application with
    standard seeding equipment.

  This is an agrochemical delivery
  format using the same material
  as the consumer gel.
  Agrochemical markets pay
  significant premiums for
  controlled-release formats.

USE CASE 4D — RADIATION SHIELDING
SUPPLEMENTARY MATERIAL:
  Bentonite is used in nuclear
  waste containment for its
  radiation interaction properties.
  At State 4 concentration, the
  bentonite mass per unit volume
  is at its highest hydrated level.
  Hydrogen in water also contributes
  to neutron moderation.
  P1S-B State 4 putty as a
  gap-fill and surface treatment
  around radiation sources
  (not as primary shielding —
  as supplementary gap-fill
  to eliminate streaming paths
  through joints and penetrations
  in primary shielding structures).

  IMPORTANT: This is a derived
  potential, not a validated
  capability. Radiation shielding
  applications require extensive
  testing against specific
  source geometries and energies.
  Stated as a direction, not a claim.
```

### 2.4 — State 5: Dry Crumble / Semi-Dry Cake

```
W:S ratio: 0.1–0.5:1
Water: ~10–33% by mass

WHAT CHANGES FROM STATE 4:
  No longer plastic — crumbles
  under pressure.
  Highest mineral concentration
  of any hydrated state.
  Maximum antimicrobial potency
  per unit mass.
  Maximum adsorption capacity
  per unit mass.
  Lightweight — water has largely
  departed.
  Easily ground to coarse powder.

USE CASE 5A — CONCENTRATED
ANTIMICROBIAL POWDER:
  Ground State 5 material is a
  dry powder with borax distributed
  throughout a bentonite matrix.
  Sprinkled onto surfaces:
    Borax deposits for persistent
    antimicrobial action.
    Bentonite absorbs surface
    moisture and odor compounds.

      Compost bins (odor control

      Animal bedding (absorbs

      Crawl space floors (moisture
      absorption + mold suppression).
      Trash bins (odor and bacterial
      suppression between washes).

  This is effectively a premium
  borax/bentonite powder with
  SAP humectant matrix,
  packaged from the desiccated
  residue of State 1 deployment.

USE CASE 5B — INSECT DESICCANT:
  Borax is a registered insecticide
  (specifically for ants, cockroaches,
  silverfish) through desiccation
  and ingestion mechanisms.
  Diatomaceous earth works similarly
  through desiccation of insect
  exoskeletons.
  State 5 P1S-B ground to powder:
    Borax component provides
    the insecticidal action.
    Bentonite component provides
    the desiccant action.
    SAP is hygroscopic but at
    State 5 the free SAP capacity
    is largely depleted — the
    material is not competing
    with itself.
  Applied along ant trails,
  in wall voids, under appliances:
  functional as a multi-mechanism
  insect control powder.

  IMPORTANT: Regulatory status
  of borax as an insecticide
  varies by jurisdiction.
  Application claims require
  confirmation against local
  pesticide registration requirements.

USE CASE 5C — ODOR ABSORPTION
GRANULES FOR SOLID WASTE:
  Kitty litter works by:
    Absorbing liquid (clay/SAP function).
    Adsorbing odor compounds (clay function).
    Suppressing bacterial growth
    (antimicrobial additive function).
  State 5 P1S-B ground to granules
  performs all three functions:
    SAP absorbs liquid.
    Bentonite adsorbs odor.
    Borax suppresses bacteria.
  This is a functional substitute
  for premium clumping cat litter
  with the addition of antimicrobial
  properties not present in
  standard bentonite litter.
  The material was always these
  ingredients — at State 5,
  the granular form makes
  this application accessible.

USE CASE 5D — SOIL DESICCANT ADDITIVE
FOR OVERWATERED OR ANAEROBIC SOILS:
  Waterlogged soils kill plants
  through oxygen exclusion and
  root rot pathogen proliferation.
  State 5 granules worked into
  waterlogged soil:
    SAP absorbs excess water rapidly.
    Bentonite improves soil structure.
    Borax suppresses root rot
    pathogens (fungal and bacterial).
    As the soil dries to optimal
    moisture, SAP begins to
    release retained water,
    buffering against subsequent
    drought.
  This is a two-phase water
  management function — absorb
  excess, then buffer against deficit.

### 2.5 — State 6: Fully Desiccated Powder

```
W:S ratio: <0.1:1
Water: <10% by mass

WHAT THIS STATE IS:
  The dry mineral blend that
  is produced at the START of
  P1S-B synthesis.
  State 6 is the circle completing:
  dry blend → hydration → deployment
  → evaporation → dry residue
  = dry blend.

  THE CRITICAL PROPERTY:
  FULLY REHYDRATABLE.
  Add water and blend:
  State 6 returns to State 1.
  The material is not degraded
  by the drying cycle.
  SAP polymer is reversibly
  dehydratable — it hydrates
  and desiccates repeatedly
  without loss of function.
  Bentonite is similarly reversible.

  THIS MEANS:

  The closed-loop system already
  derived for thermodynamic
  energy extraction has an
  additional output:
    Evaporate State 1 to extract
    energy and recover water.
    The residue is State 5/6.
    State 5/6 is shipped as a
    concentrated dry product.
    The customer adds local water
    and reconstitutes to State 1.

  THE SHIPPING GEOMETRY IMPLICATION:
    State 1 P1S-B is 90% water by mass.
    Shipping 1,000 kg of State 1 gel
    means shipping 900 kg of water.
    Shipping 1,000 kg of State 6 powder
    means shipping 1,000 kg of
    mineral active ingredient —
    enough to produce ~10,000 kg
    of State 1 gel at the destination.
    10× concentration factor in shipping.
    10× reduction in shipping cost
    per unit of active ingredient delivered.

  AT INDUSTRIAL SHIPPING SCALE,
  THIS IS A SIGNIFICANT COST REDUCTION.

USE CASE 6A — CONCENTRATED DRY
PRODUCT FORMAT FOR DISTRIBUTION:
  Sell State 6 powder in sealed bags.
  Customer adds water at point of use.
  Dramatically reduces shipping cost.
  Reduces packaging volume and weight.
  Extends shelf life (dry product
  stores indefinitely without
  refrigeration or borax preservation
  concerns — it's already dry).
  The reconstitution instruction
  is simple: add water and blend.

USE CASE 6B — EMERGENCY PREPAREDNESS
CACHE FORMAT:
  For bunker and emergency preparedness
  applications, storing State 6
  powder with a water supply
  is more practical than storing
  pre-made gel:
    Less weight to store.
    Less volume to store.
    Indefinite shelf life.
    Deployed on demand by
    adding available water.
    In an emergency scenario,
    the water supply is likely
    already stored separately —
    the P1S-B powder converts
    stored water into protection
    medium on demand.
  The emergency cache is:
    Sealed bags of State 6 powder.
    A water supply.
    An immersion blender or
    manual mixing equipment.
  From these inputs, full
  State 1 gel deployment is
  achievable within minutes of decision.

USE CASE 6C — INTERNATIONAL EXPORT
AND DEVELOPING MARKET FORMAT:
  In markets where:
    Shipping costs are high
    relative to product value.
    Water is locally available
    but chemical inputs are not.
    Cold chain is unavailable
    (dry powder needs none).
    Local production capacity
    is limited.
  State 6 powder is the correct
  product format.
  The customer imports the
  mineral active ingredients
  in concentrated dry form.
  Water — the dominant mass
  component — is sourced locally.
  Local reconstitution requires
  only water and a blender.
  The import cost is 10× lower
  per unit of deployed gel
  than importing pre-made State 1.
  This opens markets where State 1
  gel would be uneconomic to import.

USE CASE 6D — QUALITY CONTROL
REFERENCE STANDARD:
  State 6 powder, produced by
  controlled desiccation of
  a validated State 1 batch,
  is the reference material
  for all batch quality control.
  A weighed sample of State 6
  rehydrated under standard
  conditions should produce
  State 1 gel with known properties.
  If it does not, the batch
  is out of specification.
  State 6 provides a stable,
  measurable, shippable reference
  material for quality assurance
  across distributed production
  facilities.
```

## PART III: THE PRODUCTION GEOMETRY
## REVISED BY THE CASCADE

### 3.1 — What the Cascade Changes About Production

```
THE ORIGINAL PRODUCTION MODEL:
  Produce State 1 gel.
  Package State 1 gel.
  Ship State 1 gel.
  Customer deploys State 1 gel.
  Gel evaporates.
  Customer reorders State 1 gel.

THE CASCADE-AWARE PRODUCTION MODEL:
  Produce State 1 gel (unchanged).

  DECISION POINT — STATE TARGET:
    Route 1: Package at State 1
    for direct deployment applications
    (HVAC inserts, shoe treatment,
    odor control, explosion suppression).
    Route 2: Controlled desiccation
    to State 3/4 for paste and
    putty product lines
    (fire retardant coating,
    duct sealant, agricultural paste).
    Route 3: Full desiccation to
    State 6 for concentrated powder
    product line (export, emergency
    cache, reconstitutable format).

  THE SINGLE PRODUCTION PROCESS
  PRODUCES MULTIPLE DISTINCT PRODUCTS
  BY ROUTING THROUGH DIFFERENT
  DESICCATION STAGES.

  No different ingredients.
  No different blending process.
  Different post-blend handling only.

THE PRODUCT FAMILY:

  TIER 1 — HYDRATED PRODUCTS:
    State 1: Standard gel (all
    current applications).
    State 2: Extended-life gel
    (high-evaporation deployments).

  TIER 2 — PASTE PRODUCTS:
    State 3: Concentrated paste
    (fire retardant coating,
    drawing poultice, soil amendment,
    joint sealant).
    State 4: Stiff putty
    (duct sealant, gap fill,
    modelling clay, agricultural bead,
    casting medium).

  TIER 3 — DRY PRODUCTS:
    State 5: Dry crumble/granule
    (insect control, odor absorption,
    antimicrobial powder, kitty litter,
    soil amendment).
    State 6: Reconstitutable powder
    (export format, emergency cache,
    long-term storage, QC reference).

  Six distinct products.
  One production process.
  One set of ingredients.
  Six price points.
  Six market segments.

THE MARGIN IMPLICATION:

  State 1 gel value per litre:
  $5–7 wholesale (as previously derived).

  State 3 paste value per litre:
  Comparable to commercial fire
  retardant paste: $8–15/L.
  Comparable to bentonite
  drawing clay: $5–12/L.

  State 4 putty value per unit:
  Comparable to duct sealant
  mastic: $10–20/L.
  Comparable to premium modelling
  clay: $15–30/kg.

  State 5 granules value per kg:
  Comparable to premium cat litter: $3–6/kg.
  Comparable to borax-based insect
  control products: $8–15/kg.
  Comparable to agricultural
  controlled-release granules: $5–20/kg.

  State 6 powder value per kg:
  Equivalent to 10kg of State 1 gel.
  At $5/kg State 1 equivalent:
  $50/kg of State 6 powder
  on a per-active-ingredient basis.
  Even at a discount for dry format:
  $20–35/kg is defensible.

  THE VALUE PER KG INCREASES
  AS WATER CONTENT DECREASES —
  BECAUSE THE ACTIVE MINERAL
  INGREDIENTS ARE MORE CONCENTRATED.

  The water that evaporates does
  not decrease the value.
  It concentrates the value into
  less mass.
  And the water is recovered
  by the closed-loop system
  already derived.

  THE COMBINED GEOMETRY:
    Water evaporates → recovered
    as distilled water (value) +
    energy extracted (value).
    Mineral concentrate remains →
    higher-value product at
    lower mass (value).

  Evaporation creates value at

WHAT THIS DOCUMENT IS
When P1S-B gel loses water through evaporation, it does not simply become less effective gel. It becomes something different.
At each stage of water loss, the ratio of mineral solids to water changes. The physical state changes. The dominant properties change. The use cases change.
This document maps the full concentration cascade — from fully hydrated jelly state through to fully desiccated mineral powder — and derives what each state is good for that the prior states are not.
The core insight:
The evaporation that was identified as a loss mechanism in deployment contexts is simultaneously a production mechanism for higher concentration states that have independent value. Controlled evaporation is a manufacturing step, not just a depletion process.

3.2 — The Controlled Desiccation Process
Completing from draft — State 5 → State 6:
Final desiccation at 40–50°C in drying chamber or extended ambient air-drying.

SAP will begin to rehydrate from atmospheric moisture within minutes of exposure to ambient air at normal humidity. This is not a slow process — dry SAP is aggressively hygroscopic. The window between achieving State 6 and packaging must be short.
Critical packaging requirement for State 6:
	•	Moisture-proof laminate or foil bag, heat-sealed
	•	If long-term storage: add a small silica gel desiccant packet inside the outer bag as a secondary moisture barrier
	•	Do not use cardboard, paper, or unsealed plastic
	•	Label with: batch date, dry weight, water addition ratio for reconstitution
Temperature ceiling for all desiccation: 50°C.
Above 50°C, SAP polymer chains begin to degrade. The cross-linked polyacrylate network that gives SAP its extreme water absorption capacity is heat-sensitive above this threshold. Degraded SAP produces a gel with reduced water absorption, weaker structure, and increased residue risk.
Domestic ovens at their lowest setting often exceed 60–70°C. Do not use a kitchen oven for desiccation. Use:
	•	A food dehydrator with temperature control (most models: 35–70°C range)
	•	A drying chamber with a fan and heat lamp with thermometer monitoring
	•	Extended ambient air-drying at room temperature (slower but safe)

PART V: REHYDRATION FIDELITY
5.1 — Does State 6 Reconstitute to State 1?
Based on component material properties:
SAP polymer (agricultural grade) is designed for repeated hydration and desiccation cycles across growing seasons. The polyacrylate network is reversible by design. Standard SAP specifications include cycle testing.
Bentonite is inorganic and fully reversible — hydration and desiccation behavior is cyclic by definition. Clay minerals do not degrade through drying cycles at temperatures below 200°C (partial dehydration of interlayer water) or 500°C+ (structural alteration).
Borax crystallizes on desiccation and redissolves on hydration. No degradation across this temperature and hydration range.
Baking soda is stable when dry below 50°C. No degradation.
Expected outcome: State 6 rehydrated with the original water mass and blended using standard protocol produces gel functionally equivalent to State 1. Minor viscosity differences may occur due to SAP bead contraction during desiccation (contracted beads hydrate slightly more slowly than never-dried SAP), but final gel properties should fall within acceptable range after the standard 60-minute rest.
5.2 — Rehydration Fidelity Test Protocol
REHYDRATION FIDELITY TEST

Materials:
  - 50g State 6 powder (weighed after desiccation)
  - 450g distilled water (9× powder mass)
  - Immersion blender, tall vessel

Calculation basis:
  50g powder = ~9% of original batch (dry solids fraction)
  Original water = 90% of total batch mass
  Water to add = 50 × (90/9) × (90/100) ≈ 450g

Protocol:
  1. Measure 450g distilled water into tall narrow vessel
  2. Start blender at high speed, create vortex
  3. Add 50g State 6 powder slowly at liquid surface edge
     over 60–90 seconds
  4. Blend 10 minutes continuous at high speed
  5. Cover, rest 60 minutes
  6. Run standard quality assessment:
     - Lift test: target 3–5 seconds hold on spoon
     - Channel test: target 2–4 seconds to close
     - Thixotropy: firms within 2 minutes of stirring

Pass: all three quality checks within Variant A specification range
Fail: any check outside specification — record deviation for batch record

Note: If rehydrated gel is slightly thinner than reference State 1,
add 0.5g additional SAP, blend 2 minutes, rest 20 minutes, reassess.

a slightly higher SAP ratio on reconstitution.

PART VII: THE THREE-VALUE EVAPORATION EVENT
Every kilogram of water that evaporates from a P1S-B deployment simultaneously creates value at three points:
Value 1 — Concentrated residue (always accessible): Higher active ingredient density in the remaining material. Higher margin per kilogram at any state above State 1.
Value 2 — Recovered water (requires condenser infrastructure): Distilled water recovered from vapor. Negligible at small scale. Meaningful in water-scarce contexts. Offsets production input cost at industrial scale.
Value 3 — Energy (requires condenser and converter): Latent heat extractable at condenser: 2,260 J/g water evaporated. At 10% net efficiency: 0.063 Wh per gram. Meaningful only at industrial array scale (10,000m²+).

SUMMARY
The Core Manufacturing Insight
P1S-B is not one product. It is a family of six products differentiated by water content alone, all produced from the same four-ingredient batch, all using the same blending protocol, differentiated only by how far the post-blend drying is taken.
The drying step:
	•	Costs ~$0.03–0.06/kg in energy
	•	Produces higher mineral concentration per kilogram
	•	Increases shelf life from weeks to years
	•	Reduces shipping weight by up to 10×
	•	Removes cold chain requirement for States 4–6
	•	Increases price per kilogram
The manufacturing decision is not what to make. It is how far to dry it.
First Steps
	1	Make a standard State 1 batch using the V3 immersion blend protocol
	2	Set aside 200g for State 6 rehydration test
	3	Dry the 200g sample to State 6 using gravimetric control
	4	Package immediately in sealed moisture-proof bag
	5	48 hours later: rehydrate using the fidelity test protocol
	6	Compare to a fresh State 1 reference batch
	7	Record results

The dry blend was always the product. The water was always borrowed. Evaporation returns the loan. What remains is what was always there.
