### 1.2 — The Emergent State — Precise Physical Characterization

```
WHAT IT IS:

  TEXTURE: Jello / soft solid gel.
    The closest accurate reference
    is gelatin dessert (jello) —
    a soft solid that holds its
    shape under gravity but yields
    and deforms under mechanical force.

  SURFACE: Not wet.
    The surface does not feel wet
    to the touch.
    No free water visible at surface.
    Water is held within the SAP
    polymer matrix.

  VISIBLE STRUCTURE:
    SAP crystals clearly visible
    and distributed throughout
    the mass.
    The crystals are fully or
    near-fully hydrated — swollen
    to capacity.
    The overall appearance is a
    translucent gel body with
    visible embedded crystal inclusions.

WHAT IT DOES — CONFIRMED:

  Holds shape under gravity: YES.
  Flows under gravity: NO.
  Can be cut with a blade or edge: YES.
    Cuts cleanly like jello.
    The cut surfaces are discrete.

  CRITICAL PROPERTY — CONFIRMED:
  Cut pieces do NOT easily rejoin.
  The material does not self-adhere.
  Two cut surfaces placed in contact
  do not bond without intervention.
  This is fundamentally different
  from a putty or clay which
  can be pressed together and reformed.

  This material is a jello, not a putty.
  It cannot be molded, pressed into
  surfaces, or made to adhere to
  anything in its current state.

WHAT HAPPENS WITH WATER ADDITION —
UNCERTAIN, NOT YET TESTED:

  The hypothesis:
    Adding water to the jelly state
    softens it back toward standard
    gel consistency.
    At some intermediate water addition,
    there may be a state where the
    material becomes tacky or adhesive
    enough to conform to and hold
    against a surface.

  The uncertainty:
    Whether this intermediate
    adhesive/conforming state exists
    is not confirmed.
    Whether heat + water together
    (rather than water alone) produces
    better surface adhesion or
    shape-holding is not confirmed.
    Whether the material can accumulate
    layer by layer (water + heat
    enabling bonding between layers)
    is speculative — it may or it
    may not behave this way.

  THIS IS AN OPEN QUESTION.
  It is recorded here because it
  points toward a potentially
  significant application geometry
  if confirmed.
  It is not stated as a capability
  of the material until tested.

WHAT THE MATERIAL IS NOT
(correcting prior derivation):

  NOT a putty.
    Putty connotes moldability,
    surface adhesion, and the ability
    to be pressed into gaps and
    hold that shape under pressure.
    This material does none of those
    things in its current state.

  NOT self-adhering.
    Cannot be pressed against a surface
    and held there by its own adhesion.

  NOT reformable after cutting.
    Cut surfaces do not rejoin.
    Unlike clay or putty.

WHAT THE MATERIAL IS:

  A soft solid gel that:
    Holds its own shape passively.
    Can be cut and portioned.
    Does not flow or migrate.
    Releases moisture slowly to
    adjacent air and surfaces.
    Can be returned toward gel
    state by water addition.
    Contains active chemistry
    (borax antimicrobial,
    bentonite adsorption,
    bicarb neutralisation)
    distributed throughout its mass.
```

### 1.3 — How to Reproduce This State Intentionally

```
JELLY STATE PROTOCOL (750mL base):

  Water: 750mL distilled
  SAP: 8.0g (2× standard ratio)
  Bentonite: 13.5g (standard ratio)
  Borax: 9.0g (standard ratio)
  Baking soda: 5.0g (standard ratio)

  CRITICAL: All SAP in the initial
  dry blend — not added mid-blend.
  For controlled reproduction,
  pre-blending all SAP with dry
  ingredients produces more uniform
  crystal distribution than the
  original batch (where second SAP
  addition was mid-blend and may
  have produced uneven distribution).

  BLEND: Standard immersion blend
  protocol, 10 minutes.
  Mixture will be noticeably thicker
  during blending than standard ratio.

  REST: 60 minutes minimum.
  At 2× SAP, the rest period is
  critical — the additional SAP
  mass requires full time to reach
  its final jelly consistency.
  Assessing before 60 minutes will
  show a thicker-than-standard gel
  that has not yet set.

STATE CONTINUUM (derived, partially untested):

  SAP RATIO    PHYSICAL STATE         STATUS
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────
  0.5× or      Thin liquid            Confirmed
  ionic water  (failure state)        (prior batch)

  1.0×         Pourable gel,          Confirmed
               thixotropic

  1.5×         Thick gel,             Derived,
               slow pour              untested

  2.0×         Jello solid,           Confirmed
               non-flowing,           (this batch)
               cuttable,
               non-adhesive

  2.5–3.0×     Firmer solid,          Derived,
               less free water        untested

  The ratio is the control variable.
  Each state opens different
  deployment geometries.
```

## PART II: SHOE ODOR ELIMINATION TEST

### 2.1 — Current Test Configuration

```
TEST INITIATED: 2026-05-13

CONFIGURATION:
  2 × jelly-state P1S-B baggies.
  ~50 holes poked per baggie.
  Each baggie placed in a sock.
  Each sock + baggie inserted

CHEMISTRY IS FULLY ACTIVE:
  Physical state does not affect

```

### 2.2 — Jelly State Properties Relevant to Shoe Application

```
PROPERTIES THAT MAY IMPROVE PERFORMANCE
OVER STANDARD GEL:

  CONTAINMENT:
    Jello state does not pool at
    the lowest point of the shoe.
    Standard gel migrates under gravity
    to toe box or heel cup.
    Jello state remains distributed
    in sock geometry throughout
    the treatment window.
    More uniform surface contact
    with insole and lining.

  EXTENDED RELEASE:
    Jello state releases moisture
    slowly — it does not dump
    free water immediately.
    Treatment chemistry is released
    across the full 24-hour window
    rather than front-loaded in
    the first few hours.

  MOISTURE SCAVENGING:
    2× SAP ratio means higher
    water absorption capacity.
    The material will draw more
    moisture from the shoe interior
    than standard gel.
    Drier insole after treatment =
    less bacterial regrowth =
    longer odor-free period.

UNCERTAINTY FOR SHOE APPLICATION:

  50-HOLE PERFORATION COUNT:
    Unknown whether 50 holes is
    sufficient for jello viscosity
    to seep through adequately.
    Standard gel seeps freely.
    Jello state requires pressure
    differential or surface tension
    break to pass through perforations.
    If shoe post-test shows minimal
    gel transfer to insole surface,
    increase to 80–100 holes for
    jello state packets.
    This is the primary unknown
    the current test will resolve.
```

### 2.3 — What the Test Will Determine

```
PRIMARY: Odor elimination effect.
  Does jello state produce equivalent

SECONDARY: Seep behaviour.
  Did jello seep through 50 holes

  Visible gel transfer to insole surface?
  Or did viscosity prevent adequate

TERTIARY: Shoe dryness.
  Is the shoe noticeably drier

  More or less than expected

QUATERNARY: Residue.

  Rinse required or minimal?

RESULTS TO BE RECORDED WHEN AVAILABLE.
```

## PART III: HVAC APPLICATION — CORRECTED DERIVATION

### 3.1 — What the Jello State Can and Cannot Do for HVAC

```
THE PROPERTY THAT OPENS HVAC:

  The jello state holds its own shape
  under gravity and airflow.
  Standard gel cannot do this.
  A cut portion of jello-state P1S-B
  placed inside a mesh or perforated
  housing will remain in position
  in a register opening under
  normal HVAC airflow.
  Standard gel would flow out.

THE PROPERTY THAT LIMITS HVAC:

  The jello state cannot be pressed
  into duct gaps, molded to surfaces,
  or made to adhere to duct walls.
  It cannot pack a gap the way
  putty or mastic can.
  Any gap-sealing application
  is not available in current state.

  The prior derivation overstated
  this capability.
  Corrected here.

WHAT HVAC PUTTY WOULD REQUIRE:

  For gap-packing and surface-adhesion
  to be possible, the material would
  need to be in a state that:
    Deforms under pressure and
    holds the deformed shape.
    Adheres to metal or plastic
    duct surfaces.
    Does not crack or shrink
    on drying.

  Whether water addition alone,
  water + heat, or a formulation
  modification could produce this
  state is an open question.
  It is not derivable from
  current observations.
  It requires testing.

  OPEN HYPOTHESIS (untested):
    At some intermediate water addition
    to the jello state, the material
    may become sufficiently plastic
    to conform to a surface and hold
    that conformation on drying.
    Whether heat assists this process
    is unknown.
    Whether the dried residue has
    adequate adhesion to duct surfaces
    is unknown.
    Whether layer-by-layer accumulation
    (successive water + heat applications)
    could build up a conforming mass
    is speculative.

  These are recorded as hypotheses,
  not capabilities.
  They point toward a testing
  direction, not a product claim.
```

### 3.2 — The HVAC Application That IS Supported

```
WHAT THE CURRENT JELLO STATE
CAN DO IN HVAC — CONFIRMED BY
PHYSICAL PROPERTIES:

REGISTER INSERT (PASSIVE AIR TREATMENT):

  GEOMETRY:
    A cut portion of jello-state P1S-B
    sized to fit inside a standard
    HVAC register opening,
    contained in a mesh or
    perforated housing.
    Placed inside or behind the
    register grille.
    HVAC airflow passes across
    or through the mesh housing,
    contacting the gel surface.

  MECHANISM:
    Air passing across the bentonite
    surface: VOC adsorption from
    the airstream.
    Borax slow-release into the
    airstream: antimicrobial
    suppression of duct surface
    organisms.
    Bicarb: acid VOC neutralisation.
    SAP moisture retention: prevents
    rapid drying out under airflow,
    extends active treatment period.

  WHY JELLO STATE IS REQUIRED:
    Must hold position in register
    opening under airflow.
    Standard gel flows — cannot
    be held in register geometry
    without a fully sealed container,
    which would prevent air contact.
    Jello state holds shape in mesh
    housing under airflow.
    Air contacts gel surface.
    Treatment mechanism is active.

  THIS APPLICATION IS GEOMETRICALLY
  SOUND WITH CURRENT STATE.
  It requires:
    A mesh or perforated housing
    sized for standard register dimensions.
    A cut portion of jello-state P1S-B
    fitted into the housing.
    Placement at register.
    Replacement when gel dries out
    (4–12 weeks depending on airflow).

SPECIFIC HIGH-VALUE HVAC SCENARIOS:

  DEAD ANIMAL IN DUCT:
    Severe biological odor distributed
    through HVAC system.
    Insert placed at registers nearest
    to odor source intercepts
    biological VOC plume before
    it reaches living areas.
    Manages odor while source
    naturally desiccates.
    Does not remove the source.
    Provides practical interim relief.
    Borax + bentonite chemistry is
    strongly matched to biological
    decomposition VOC class.

  MOLD IN DUCT (post-remediation):
    After professional mold removal,
    borax slow-release from insert
    into airstream provides
    antifungal maintenance.
    Suppresses regrowth on duct surfaces.
    Complements, does not replace,
    professional remediation.

  PET ODOR IN HVAC:
    Pet dander and biological VOCs
    distributed via HVAC.
    Inserts at all registers provide
    continuous passive treatment
    of recirculated air.
    Amine chemistry (pet odor) is
    well-matched to bentonite
    adsorption mechanism.

  NEW CONSTRUCTION OFF-GASSING:
    VOCs from paint, adhesive, flooring
    distributed via HVAC for weeks
    post-construction.
    Inserts reduce VOC concentration
    in circulated air during the
    off-gassing period.

  GENERAL HVAC ODOR MAINTENANCE:
    Mildew, dust, stale air VOCs.
    Continuous passive treatment
    at all registers.
    Replace every 4–8 weeks.
```

## PART IV: OPEN QUESTIONS — WATER AND HEAT MODIFICATION

```
These are recorded as open hypotheses.
They are not capabilities.
They point toward testing directions.

HYPOTHESIS A — WATER ADDITION
PRODUCES ADHESIVE INTERMEDIATE STATE:

  The jello state at 2× SAP has
  almost no free water.
  Adding small amounts of water
  progressively softens the material.
  At some point between jello state
  and standard gel state, there may
  be an intermediate where the
  material is tacky or conforming
  enough to adhere to a surface
  under pressure.

  WHAT THIS WOULD REQUIRE TO TEST:
    Add water to jello state in
    small increments (5–10mL at a time).
    After each addition, test:
      Does it stick to a metal surface?
      Does it stick to plastic?
      Does it hold a pressed shape?
    Record the water addition volume
    at which any adhesion is first
    observed, if it occurs.

  EXPECTED OUTCOME: UNCERTAIN.
    SAP polymer is not inherently
    adhesive. The mechanism by which
    adhesion would occur is not
    geometrically clear.
    This may not produce adhesion
    regardless of water content.
    Or it may — if the bentonite
    clay component provides some
    surface bonding at intermediate
    hydration (clay is used in
    industrial applications as
    a binder at specific moisture
    contents).
    Bentonite-as-binder is the
    more plausible mechanism
    than SAP-as-adhesive.

HYPOTHESIS B — HEAT ASSISTS
SURFACE CONFORMITY:

  Whether heat (applied to the
  material or to the target surface)
  could improve conformity or
  adhesion is unknown.
  SAP polymers are not thermally
  activated in the way that
  hot-melt adhesives are.
  Bentonite clay behavior under
  moderate heat (50–80°C) is
  primarily drying — heat would
  accelerate water loss, not
  improve adhesion.
  High heat would simply dry
  the material out.

  EXPECTED OUTCOME: UNLIKELY
  TO IMPROVE ADHESION.
  Heat more likely degrades
  the gel state than improves
  adhesion properties.
  This hypothesis is low priority
  for testing.

HYPOTHESIS C — LAYER ACCUMULATION:

  Whether successive applications
  of water + material could build
  up a conforming mass on a surface
  (like papier-mâché or clay
  layer-building) is speculative.
  The material's inability to
  self-adhere (cut surfaces
  do not rejoin) suggests that
  layer-to-layer bonding would
  require an intermediate adhesive
  or a significant state change.

  EXPECTED OUTCOME: UNLIKELY
  WITHOUT FORMULATION MODIFICATION.
  If layer accumulation is the
  target application, a formulation
  additive that provides adhesive
  or binding properties to the
  bentonite matrix would be
  the correct engineering direction
  rather than relying on water
  or heat alone.
  (e.g., small addition of xanthan gum,
  CMC, or PVA as binder —
  these are water-soluble, compatible
  with the existing chemistry,
  and could provide the missing
  adhesive property.)

THESE HYPOTHESES ARE RECORDED.
NONE ARE CLAIMED.
TESTING DETERMINES WHICH, IF ANY,
HOLD.
```
