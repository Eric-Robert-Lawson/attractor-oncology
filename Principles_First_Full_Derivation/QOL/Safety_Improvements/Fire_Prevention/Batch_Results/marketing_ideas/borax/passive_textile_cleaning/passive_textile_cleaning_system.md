# 0. PURPOSE OF THIS DOCUMENT

This document derives the feasibility and structural behavior of using P1S-B as:

# a passive low-agitation textile cleaning system.

The core question is NOT:

> “Can P1S-B clean clothing?”

The deeper question is:

# Can P1S-B alter the geometry of textile cleaning itself?

Specifically:
- reducing or eliminating mechanical agitation
- reducing water consumption
- reducing detergent dependence
- reducing energy consumption
- improving cleaning of delicate materials
- improving prolonged-contact contaminant extraction
- simplifying the cleaning process

The proposed geometry is:

## soak → persistent interaction → rinse → dry

rather than:

## tumble → mechanical agitation → rinse → spin → dry

This is a fundamentally different cleaning architecture.

---

# 1. THE PRIMARY REALIZATION

The strongest emerging insight appears to be:

# clothing contamination is often adhesion-dominated rather than mechanically locked.

Meaning:
many stains and contaminants persist because:
- oils bind fibers
- particulates remain trapped
- residues dry into textile geometry
- detergents lack sufficient contact time

Mechanical agitation compensates for:
- insufficient chemical persistence
- insufficient hydration duration
- insufficient contaminant destabilization

P1S-B changes this equation by:
- prolonging hydration
- prolonging alkalinity
- prolonging adsorption interaction
- suspending contaminants gradually

Thus:
## time may partially substitute for agitation.

That is the core structural hypothesis.

# 5.4 — Protein Stains

Potentially weaker.

P1S-B alone may not excel here.

# 5.5 — Heavy Grease

Likely mixed.

However:
- prolonged exposure could still loosen residues substantially.

More testing required.

# 11.2 — Over-Hydration of Fabrics

Long soaking may:
- weaken some natural fibers

# 11.3 — Cleaning Speed
