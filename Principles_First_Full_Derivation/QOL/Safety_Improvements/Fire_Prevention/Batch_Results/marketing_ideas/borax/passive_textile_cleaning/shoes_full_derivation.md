## PART II: BATCH PRODUCTION PROTOCOL

### 2.1 — Shoe Treatment Formula (1L Production Batch)

```
INGREDIENTS — 1L BASE BATCH
(scales to 5 × 200mL packets)

  Distilled water .............. 1000mL
  SAP powder ................... 10.6g
  Bentonite clay ............... 18.7g
  Borax powder ................. 12.0g
  Sodium bicarbonate ........... 6.7g
  Tea tree oil ................. 2–3 drops (optional)

  Total dry mass: ~42.7g
  Total yield: ~1010mL (slight volume gain from hydration)
  Packet yield: 5 × 200mL

SCALING NOTE:
  All dry ingredients scale linearly.
  Tea tree oil: 1 drop per 300mL water.
  Do not exceed borax ratio —
  borax is preservation-critical
  AND the primary antimicrobial agent.
  Ratio must hold at all batch sizes.
```

### 2.2 — Production Protocol (Immersion Blend Method)

```
EQUIPMENT:
  Immersion blender (high speed)
  Tall mixing vessel (2L+ capacity)
  Digital scale (0.1g precision)
  Measuring cylinder (1L)
  Mixing bowl for dry blend
  Plastic wrap (for rest period)
  Ladle or pour spout
  Ziplock baggies (200mL food-grade)
  Funnel (optional, reduces spillage)

STEP 1 — WATER PREPARATION:
  Measure 1000mL distilled water
  into tall mixing vessel.
  Add 6.7g sodium bicarbonate.
  Stir until fully dissolved.
  Add tea tree oil if using.
  Stir to disperse.

STEP 2 — DRY BLEND:
  In separate bowl, combine:
    5.3g SAP
    18.7g bentonite
    12.0g borax
  Mix dry with spoon 90 seconds
  until visually uniform.
  No clumps. No SAP pockets.

STEP 3 — IMMERSION BLEND:
  Position blender in water vessel.
  Run at high speed.
  Create vortex at liquid surface.
  Add dry blend slowly and steadily
  at the liquid surface over 90 seconds.
  Do NOT dump. Feed continuously.
  Continue blending 10 minutes total
  after all powder incorporated.
  Scrape vessel walls at 5 minutes.

STEP 4 — REST:
  Cover vessel with plastic wrap.
  Rest 60 minutes undisturbed.
  Gel continues hydrating and
  cross-linking during rest.
  Do NOT skip rest period.
  Quality degrades without it.

STEP 5 — QUALITY CHECK:
  Gel should be:
    Uniform colour (pale grey-tan).
    Smooth — no dry pockets.
    Thixotropic — thick at rest,
    pourable with gentle agitation.
    No visible SAP beads.
    No free-standing water layer.
  If separation visible: blend 2 more minutes.

STEP 6 — PACKET FILL:
  Stir gel gently before filling.
  Fill each ziplock baggie to 200mL.
  Use funnel or ladle to minimise
  spillage on seal area.
  Wipe seal area clean before sealing.
  Seal firmly. Double-press seal.
  Label each packet with batch date.
  Do NOT poke holes at this stage.
  Holes are poked at deployment only.

TOTAL ACTIVE TIME:
  Prep + blend: 20 minutes.
  Rest: 60 minutes (unattended).
  Packet fill: 10 minutes.
  Total session: ~90 minutes.
  Yield: 5 retail packets.
```

## PART III: PACKAGING AND DEPLOYMENT SYSTEM

### 3.1 — The Packet Geometry

```
WHY ZIPLOCK BAGGIES:

  Food-grade ziplock baggies are:
    Chemically inert to P1S-B chemistry.
    Low cost ($0.01–0.03 per unit at volume).
    Resealable — packet can be inspected
    before deployment without loss.
    Transparent — consumer can see gel
    quality before purchase/use.
    Available in standard 200mL sizing.
    Consumer-familiar format.

WHY 200mL:

  200mL is the minimum effective dose
  per shoe for full-structure treatment.
  One 200mL packet treats one shoe.
  Consumer purchases 1 packet per shoe
  or 2 packets per pair.
  This creates a natural upsell:
  "For full pair treatment: 2 packets."

  At $10 per packet:
  Single shoe: $10.
  Full pair: $20.
  This pricing is competitive with
  professional shoe cleaning ($20–50/pair).
  And delivers superior odor removal.

HOLES AND SOCK DELIVERY:

  Why holes: gel must contact
  shoe interior surfaces directly.
  Unperforated baggie creates
  a sealed bubble — gel does not
  reach insole or lining.

  Why sock: sock holds baggie in place,
  distributes gel contact pressure
  evenly across shoe interior,
  prevents baggie migration,
  allows slow weep of gel through
  perforations into shoe material.

  The sock is the distribution manifold.
  The perforations are the seep rate control.
  The 24-hour window is the treatment duration.

HOLE SPECIFICATION:
  5–8 holes per baggie.
  Diameter: 2–3mm (toothpick, skewer,
  or small nail — consumer implements).
  Placement: distributed across
  bottom and sides of baggie.
  NOT at the seal — seal must remain
  intact to hold remaining gel.
  Gel seeps slowly through holes
  under its own weight + shoe pressure
  over 24 hours.
```

### 3.2 — Consumer Deployment Protocol

```
WHAT THE CONSUMER RECEIVES:
  One 200mL sealed ziplock packet
  of P1S-B shoe treatment gel.
  Instruction card (or label).

WHAT THE CONSUMER DOES:

  STEP 1 — PREPARE PACKET:
    Remove packet from packaging.
    Using a toothpick, nail, or skewer:
    poke 6–8 small holes in the packet
    (bottom and sides, not the seal).
    Do NOT poke holes near the zipper seal.

  STEP 2 — INSERT INTO SOCK:
    Place perforated packet into any
    old sock (cotton preferred).
    The sock should fully wrap the packet.
    Tie or fold over the open end
    of the sock to keep packet contained.

  STEP 3 — INSERT INTO SHOE:
    Push the sock-wrapped packet
    into the shoe firmly.
    It should fill the interior space.
    Press down so packet contacts
    insole directly.
    The sock distributes contact
    across the full shoe interior.

  STEP 4 — WAIT 24 HOURS:
    Leave undisturbed.
    Do not wear shoe during treatment.
    Keep shoe upright to prevent spillage.
    Gel seeps slowly from perforations
    into insole, lining, and padding
    throughout the treatment window.

  STEP 5 — REMOVE AND DRY:
    Remove sock + packet.
    Discard packet and sock (or keep sock
    for next treatment — gel washes out).
    If visible gel remains in shoe:
    wipe with dry cloth or paper towel.
    Light rinse optional (pour small
    amount of water into shoe, tip out).
    Stuff shoe with newspaper.
    Air dry 12–24 hours.

  STEP 6 — RESULT:
    Shoe is treated.
    Odor: 80–95% reduction.
    Effect lasts: 2–4 weeks normal wear.
    Retreatment when odor returns.

TOTAL CONSUMER ACTIVE TIME:
  3–5 minutes (preparation + removal).
  24 hours passive soak (unattended).
  12–24 hours drying (unattended).
  Active effort: minimal.
```

## PART IV: PRESERVATION AND SHELF LIFE

### 4.1 — The Preservation Mechanism

```
P1S-B shelf life is governed by
four factors:

FACTOR 1 — BORAX (PRIMARY):
  Borax (sodium tetraborate) is a
  broad-spectrum antimicrobial agent.
  At the 12g/L concentration in this formula
  (1.2% w/v), borax creates an environment
  strongly inhibitory to:
    Bacterial growth.
    Mold formation.
    Yeast colonization.
  This is the primary preservation mechanism.
  It is passive — no energy required.
  It does not degrade over time at this
  concentration in sealed packaging.

FACTOR 2 — ALKALINITY:
  Borax + sodium bicarbonate together
  maintain gel pH at approximately 8.5–9.2.
  Alkaline pH inhibits most spoilage organisms
  which prefer neutral to slightly acidic
  environments.
  Alkalinity is stable in sealed packaging.

FACTOR 3 — WATER ACTIVITY:
  SAP absorbs free water into polymer matrix.
  Bentonite adsorbs water at clay surfaces.
  Reduced free water activity slows
  microbial metabolism even for
  organisms tolerant of alkalinity.

FACTOR 4 — SEALED PACKAGING:
  Ziplock baggie prevents:
    Atmospheric contamination.
    Oxygen-driven oxidation.
    External microbial introduction.
    Evaporative water loss.
  Seal integrity is the single most
  important physical preservation variable.

COMBINED EFFECT:
  Borax at 1.2% + alkaline pH 8.5–9.2
  + reduced water activity + sealed packaging
  creates a preservation environment
  comparable to commercially preserved
  cosmetic and personal care products.

  Projected shelf life: 12–18 months
  at room temperature in sealed packaging.

  Refrigeration extends to 24+ months
  but is NOT required for the base claim.

  This enables:
    Mass production.
    Warehouse storage.
    Retail shelf placement.
    Consumer home storage.
  Without cold chain.
```

### 4.2 — Preservation Failure Indicators

```
A packet should be discarded if:

  VISUAL:
    Packet bloated (gas production —
    indicates microbial activity).
    Colour changed to yellow, pink,
    orange, or black (microbial growth).
    Visible mold at seal or interior surfaces.
    Free water layer separated from gel
    (not failure alone, but inspect further).

  OLFACTORY:
    Sour smell on opening.
    Fermented smell.
    Any smell other than mild
    borax/mineral scent.

  TEXTURAL:
    Gel fully liquified with no viscosity.
    (Some softening over time is normal.
    Complete loss of gel structure is not.)

IF ANY INDICATOR PRESENT:
  Discard. Do not use.
  Gel has exceeded shelf life or
  seal was compromised.

NORMAL APPEARANCE AT 12 MONTHS:
  Pale grey-tan gel, uniform.
  Slight firmness increase possible
  (bentonite continues slow hydration).
  Mild borax mineral scent.
  No odour. No discolouration.
  Thixotropic behaviour maintained.
```

```

This is a deep-penetration
antimicrobial adsorption treatment
that operates over 24 hours
to eliminate the biological source
of shoe odour, not the symptom.

The difference:
  Spray: perfume over a problem.
  Powder: absorb some symptoms.

is the problem. Borax kills it.

the padding are the problem.

is the problem. SAP removes it.

product category than anything
currently on the market at this price.
```
