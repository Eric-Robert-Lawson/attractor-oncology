## PART I: DRY MIX SUPPLY REQUIREMENTS

```
FILL CYCLES NEEDED:

  Per week:   7 events/day × 7 days = 49 events
              49 / 15 = 3.27 fills → 3.3 fills/week

  Per month:  7 × 30 = 210 events
              210 / 15 = 14 fills/month

  Per year:   7 × 365 = 2,555 events
              2,555 / 15 = 170.3 → 170 fills/year

DRY MIX REQUIRED BY WEIGHT:

  Per week:   3.3 × 400g =  1,320g  =  1.32kg
  Per month:  14  × 400g =  5,600g  =  5.60kg
  Per year:   170 × 400g = 68,000g  = 68.00kg

  One person consumes 68kg of dry mix per year
  at full (100%) displacement.
  At 50% displacement (home use only):
    34kg/year. 85 fill cycles.
```

## PART IV: REVENUE AND PROFIT

### Per Urination Event

```
HARVEST PER EVENT:
  531g / 15 events = 35.4g = 0.0354kg per event.

REVENUE PER EVENT:
  At $10/kg:  0.0354 × $10  = $0.354
  At $14/kg:  0.0354 × $14  = $0.496
  At $18/kg:  0.0354 × $18  = $0.637

NET PROFIT PER EVENT
(revenue minus industrial dry mix cost):
  At $10/kg:  $0.354 - $0.010 = $0.344
  At $14/kg:  $0.496 - $0.010 = $0.486
  At $18/kg:  $0.637 - $0.010 = $0.627

EVERY TIME A PERSON URINATES
INTO THE BUCKET:
  They generate $0.34–$0.63 of net value.
  The input cost is one cent.
  The return is 34–63× the input cost.
```

  revenue at current harvest pricing.

  IMPLICATION:
  The economics only close positively
  for the end user if they receive the
  dry mix at or near industrial cost —
  meaning they are part of the supply
  chain (community co-op, bulk buyer,

  The retail bag price is priced for
  operators who sell the harvest,
  not for individuals buying retail.
  Operators make money on both the
  bag sale and the harvest buyback.
  Individual users make money only
  if they access near-industrial pricing.
