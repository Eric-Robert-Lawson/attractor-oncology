```

─────────────────────────────────

Under Illinois Uniform Trade Secrets Act (UTSA):
- You own the formula as intellectual property
- It's protected indefinitely (as long as kept secret)
- Misappropriation is illegal
- You can sue for damages if someone steals it

3. Document when/why decisions made (shows independent creation)
4. Don't publish or present publicly
5. Include confidentiality in any employee contracts

Cost to maintain: $0 (just discipline and documentation)

- Potential investor
- Potential acquirer

✓ Agreement that formula is trade secret
✓ Agreement to keep confidential
✓ Agreement not to use for own benefit
✓ Agreement to return/destroy upon termination
✓ Consequences for violation (damages clause)

Cost: $200-500 (legal template or attorney)
Enforceability: VERY HIGH (NDAs are standard, upheld in courts)

Example NDA clause:
"Employee acknowledges that the OrganismCore fertilizer formula
is proprietary and confidential. Employee agrees to maintain this
confidentiality during and after employment, and not to disclose,
use, or reproduce this formula for any purpose without written
consent. Violation will result in liability for damages."


Layer 3: DOCUMENTATION OF INDEPENDENT CREATION
───────────────────────────────────────────────

Create a file showing:
- When you developed the formula
- Why you chose each ingredient
- What alternatives you tested
- How you arrived at final ratios
- Problems you solved with this formula
- Results from initial testing

This documentation:
✓ Proves you independently created formula
✓ Shows it's your original work (not copied)
✓ Makes trade secret claim stronger in court
✓ Useful if you ever need to defend in litigation

Time to create: 2-3 hours
Cost: $0
Benefit: Strengthens legal position significantly


Layer 4: MANUFACTURING PROCESS PATENTS (Strategic Option)

- Your specific use of flash dryer for this application

But they last 17 years and legally block competitors from copying exact process.

Trade-off:
- GAIN: 17 years of legal exclusivity on process
- LOSE: Formula becomes public knowledge (anyone can see it)
- NET: Could be worth it if you want process protection + can live with formula being public

Cost: $3K-8K for patent attorney
Timeline: 18-24 months to get patent

Strategic decision: 
- DON'T patent the formula (keep it secret)
- OPTIONALLY patent the process (if you want legal blockade)
- Most likely: Don't patent, keep both formula AND process secret
- Reason: Trade secret is better than patent (indefinite vs. 17 years)

Year 3: Formula + process + improvements + local sourcing network = Stronger moat
```
