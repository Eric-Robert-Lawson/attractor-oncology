# Grass Seed Germination Test — Formula Validation Experiment

## Experiment Overview

Testing a proprietary soil amendment formula against a normal soil control using a split-container germination bioassay with real outdoor soil and coated grass seed mix.

---

## Formula Composition

### Batch 1 (Pot)
- **750ml** human urine
- **75g** potassium polyacrylate
- **22.5g** bentonite clay
- **30g** citric acid

### Batch 2 (Baking Pan)
- **580g total** — same ratio as Batch 1
- Larger surface area — expected to dry faster

### Ratio Reference
`1000:100:30:40` — urine : potassium polyacrylate : bentonite clay : citric acid

---

## Drying Protocol

- **Oven temperature**: 200–205°F (93–96°C)
- **Door**: Slightly propped open for airflow/humidity escape
- **Target**: No jiggling, no rubbery texture, crystals hard and brownish-yellow to translucent
- **Use whichever batch dries first**

### Drying Success Criteria
| Indicator | Pass | Fail |
|---|---|---|
| Hydrogel crystals | Hard, small, brownish-yellow to translucent (due to urine capture) | Still jiggling or rubbery |
| Texture | Crumbles easily when pressed | Clumps or feels damp |
| Weight | Significantly lighter than wet batch | Still heavy |
| Smell | Mild earthy | Strong ammonia (incomplete dry) |

---

## Soil Preparation

- **Source**: Real outdoor soil, yard collected
- **Processing**: Strained through strainer to remove rocks, roots, debris
- **Inventory**:
  - 4 x 1kg ziplock bags (strained)
  - 1 x ~350g ziplock bag (strained)

---

## Dilution Protocol

- **Ratio**: 20:1 soil to dry powder by weight
- **Method**:
  1. Weigh dry powder output
  2. Weigh out exactly 20x that amount of soil into a single ziplock bag
  3. Add dry powder to bag
  4. Seal and mush/knead until uniformly distributed
  5. No clumps of powder visible

### Dilution Success Criteria
| Indicator | Pass | Fail |
|---|---|---|
| Visual uniformity | Even color throughout | Visible powder clumps or streaks |
| Texture | Consistent throughout bag | Gritty or uneven patches |
| Smell | Mild earthy | Strong ammonia |

---

## Test Container Setup

- **Container**: Food takeout dish
- **Drainage**: 9 holes cut in bottom
- **Drainage collection**: Plate underneath to capture runoff
- **Divison**:
  - Left half — normal outdoor soil (control)
  - Right half — 1:20 diluted formula soil (enriched)
- **Seed**: Coated green pellet grass mix, scattered evenly across both halves
- **Seed placement**: Surface scattered, lightly pressed down, not buried
- **Location**: Sliding door windowsill for natural light

---

## Watering Schedule

| Phase | Frequency | Amount | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Day 1–7 | Once daily | Few tablespoons, gentle | Keep surface moist, not soaked |
| Day 7–14 | Every 1–2 days | Finger test first | Water if top 1cm feels dry |
| Day 14+ | Every 2–3 days | Slightly more | Allow slight dry between waterings |

### Watering Rules
- Always water from the **control side** and let it wick naturally
- **Empty the plate** beneath after each watering — no standing water
- **Finger test**: 1cm into soil — dry/crumbly = water now, cool/damp = check tomorrow, wet/soggy = skip

---

## Documentation Protocol

- **Photo**: Every day, same angle, same distance
- Include something for scale in frame
- **Written notes**: Date, watering amount, observations per side
- Record any anomalies (mold, unusual smell, non-grass sprouts)

## Expected Timeline & Milestones

| Day | Expected Observation | Success | Failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1–2** | Pellet coating begins swelling on both sides | Coating visibly softening and swelling | Pellets unchanged, surface dry |
| **3–5** | First white root tips emerging | Enriched side may show 1–3 days earlier emergence | No root tips on either side by day 5 |
| **5–7** | First green shoots breaking surface | Both sides showing shoots, enriched side denser | No shoots by day 7 |
| **7–10** | Color differentiation | Enriched side noticeably richer/deeper green | Enriched side yellowing or browning |
| **10–14** | Density comparison | More shoots per square inch on enriched side | Sparse or patchy on enriched side |
| **14–21** | Height and vigor | Enriched side taller, thicker, more uniform | Enriched side stunted or dying off |
| **Day 21** | Root mass comparison (pull apart gently) | Deeper, denser roots on enriched side | Shallow or rotted roots on enriched side |

---

## What Success Looks Like

### Strong Success
- Enriched side germinates **1–3 days ahead** of control
- Richer deeper green color visible by day 10
- Noticeably denser shoot coverage by day 14
- Root mass at day 21 is visibly deeper and denser than control
- No die-off or stress signs at any stage

### Acceptable Success
- Germination rate roughly equal but enriched side greener by day 10
- Slightly better root mass at day 21
- No stress or die-off on enriched side
- Formula demonstrably not harming growth

---

## What Failure Looks Like & Likely Causes

| Symptom | Likely Cause | What to Adjust |
|---|---|---|
| Yellowing leaves on enriched side | pH too low from citric acid OR nitrogen burn | Increase dilution ratio to 25:1 or 30:1 next test |
| Seeds not germinating on enriched side | EC too high — salt stress from formula | Increase dilution ratio |
| Sprouting then dying at soil line | Overwatering — hydrogel retaining too much moisture | Water less frequently on enriched side |
| Patchy/uneven germination | Uneven mixing at 1:20 stage | More thorough kneading in bag next batch |
| Strong ammonia smell from enriched side | Incomplete drying — urea still volatile | Ensure full drying before next test |
| Mold on surface | Overwatering combined with poor airflow | Reduce watering, increase light/airflow |

## Notes

- Outdoor soil carries real microbes, natural pH, and possibly weed seeds — note any non-grass sprouts but do not count them in metrics
- Potassium polyacrylate will retain significantly more moisture on enriched side — always err toward less water on that side
- Rotate dish 180° every 3–4 days to ensure equal light exposure on both sides
- This test uses real-world soil conditions making results more meaningful than sterile potting mix
