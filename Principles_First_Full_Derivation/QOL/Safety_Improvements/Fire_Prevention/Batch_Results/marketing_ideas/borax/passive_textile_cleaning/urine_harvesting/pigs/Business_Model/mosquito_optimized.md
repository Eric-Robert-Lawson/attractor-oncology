────────────────────────────────────────

SAP POLYMER DEGRADATION & REHYDRATION
CHEMISTRY:

Standard understanding (wrong):
"SAP traps water, released slowly"

Your discovery (correct):
"SAP polymer chains degrade over time,
releasing trapped ammonia. When
re-hydrated, ammonia re-volatilizes
and releases again."

SAP (Sodium Polyacrylate) structure:
- Long polymer chains
- Cross-linked network
- High water-holding capacity
- Reversible hydration/dehydration
- Degradation over time releases
  trapped compounds

Hydration-Release-Rehydration process:

1. Fresh SAP + water (Initial state)
   - Polymer chains intact
   - Water absorbed into polymer
   - Ammonia trapped in water

2. Partial drying (Week 1-2)
   - Excess water (beyond SAP capacity)

   - Ammonia volatilizes with excess
     water evaporation
   - Strong smell
   - Flies attracted

3. Continued drying (Week 3-4)
   - More water leaves SAP
   - Polymer network starts breaking
     down (hydrolysis)
   - Ammonia trapped in polymer
     chains begins releasing
   - Smell continues (polymer
     degradation releasing trapped
     ammonia)

4. Heavy drying (Week 5-8)
   - Minimal water in polymer
   - Polymer chains significantly
     degraded
   - Ammonia continues releasing
     (chemical degradation, not
     evaporation)
   - Smell decreases but persists

5. REHYDRATION (Optional, adds new
   cycle)
   - Water added to mixture
   - Water re-hydrates dried SAP
     polymer
   - SAP polymer re-absorbs water
   - Trapped ammonia in polymer
     chains becomes mobile again
   - Water seeping through polymer
     displaces trapped ammonia
   - Ammonia volatilizes as new water
     evaporates from rehydrated polymer
   - New release cycle begins (Week 1-2
     equivalent performance)
   - Can repeat 2-3 times total

6. Complete drying (after multiple
   recharges, Week 15+)
   - Powder appears "used up"
   - But polymer still holds some
     ammonia
   - Very slow release (entropy)
   - Smell: FAINT (<15%)
   - Recommend replacement

This explains why:
- Yellow spot appears (ammonia
  compound in oversaturated state)
- Smell continues even after drying
  (polymer degradation)
- Rehydration reactivates release
  (re-hydration allows ammonia to
  volatilize again as water moves
  through polymer)

OVERSATURATION MECHANISM:

SAP water absorption capacity:
- Standard: 300-400× its weight

Beyond maximum:
- Add more water than capacity allows
- SAP can't absorb it all
- Excess water sits on top of
  saturated polymer
- This excess water contains high

- This is OVERSATURATION

Why oversaturation is CRITICAL:

Undersaturated SAP:
- All water absorbed into polymer
- Ammonia spread throughout gel
- Low concentration
- Weak smell
- Insufficient attraction
- No visible yellow spot

Oversaturated SAP:
- Excess water on top of gel
- Ammonia highly concentrated in

- Strong smell
- Yellow compound visible (ammonia
  precipitate)
- High attraction
- Clear visual indicator
