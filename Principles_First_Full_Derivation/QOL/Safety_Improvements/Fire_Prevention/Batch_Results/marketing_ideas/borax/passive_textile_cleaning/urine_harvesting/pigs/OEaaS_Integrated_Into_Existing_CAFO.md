## PART I: CURRENT CAFO URINE MANAGEMENT SYSTEMS

### 1.1 — The Standard Systems Already Deployed

```
CAFO URINE DISPOSAL BASELINE:

All large CAFOs (1,000+ pigs) have
some form of urine management system.

They MUST have it (EPA requirement
for waste containment).

The urine cannot just flow into
ground (environmental liability).

It must be contained and managed.

There are THREE primary systems
in use:

────────────────────────────────────────

SYSTEM 1: SLURRY PIT / LAGOON

(Most common in US, ~60% of CAFOs)

Design:
- Earthen or lined pond/pit.
- Size: 50,000-500,000 gallons
  (20-200 cubic meters).
- Located near barn or downslope
  from barn.

Urine flow:
- Barn gutters/channels drain to pit.
- Gravity-fed (no pumping usually).
- Can be open or covered.

Annual capacity:
- 5,000-pig CAFO: 500,000-1,000,000
  gallons/year urine.
- Slurry pit: 50,000-200,000 gallons
  active capacity.
- Pit empties 2-4 times per year
  (contractor comes to pump out).

Current emptying process:
- Vacuum truck arrives.
- Pump sucks liquid from pit.
- Hauls to:
  * Farmer's own crop land (spread
    on fields as fertiliser).
  * Cooperative lagoon (shared
    with other farms).
  * Contractor's disposal facility
    (spread on contract land or
     landfill).

Cost:
- Pit construction: $10,000-30,000
  (one-time, usually already built).
- Annual emptying: $2,000-5,000
  per year (contractor).
- Chemicals for treatment: $500-1,000
  /year (if used).
- Total annual: $2,500-6,000.

Advantages:
- Already exists at nearly all CAFOs.
- Gravity-fed (no electricity needed).
- Large capacity (can handle 3-6
  months of urine).
- Contractor infrastructure exists
  (pumping services available).

Disadvantages:
- Open pit (odor release).
- Uncontrolled decomposition
  (ammonia loss).
- Seasonal capacity issues (overfull
  in winter, underfull in summer).

Retrofit potential: EXCELLENT.

────────────────────────────────────────

SYSTEM 2: SOLIDS SEPARATOR / SCREW
PRESS

(Growing adoption, ~20% of new CAFOs)

Design:
- Mechanical system that separates
  solid manure from liquid urine.
- Liquid flows to storage (tank or
  lagoon).
- Solids go to compost or storage.

Urine flow:
- Barn channels drain to separator.
- Separator separates solids
  (>90% efficiency).
- Liquid urine goes to holding tank
  or lagoon.
- Capacity: 50,000-200,000 gallons
  in holding tank.

Annual capacity:
- Same as slurry pit (500,000-1,000,000
  gallons/year).

Current emptying process:
- Same as slurry pit (contractor pumps
  to fields or disposal).

Cost:
- Separator equipment: $20,000-50,000
  (one-time, often already installed
   as part of environmental upgrade).
- Annual operation: $2,000-5,000
  (electricity, maintenance, contractor
  pumping).

Advantages:
- Better odor control than open pit
  (covers solids).
- Slightly better nutrient retention
  (less volatilisation).
- Already exists at many progressive
  CAFOs.

Disadvantages:
- Urine still goes to open/uncovered
  tank (odor).
- Uncontrolled decomposition still
  occurs.

Retrofit potential: EXCELLENT.

────────────────────────────────────────

SYSTEM 3: COVERED LAGOON / ANAEROBIC
DIGESTER

(Least common, ~10% of CAFOs,
primarily in EU, less common in US)

Design:
- Lagoon with synthetic cover
  (tarp/geomembrane).
- Sometimes heated and managed for
  biogas production.

Urine flow:
- Barn channels drain to covered lagoon.
- Cover traps odors and methane.
- Anaerobic bacteria decompose waste.
- Biogas is captured or flared.

Annual capacity:
- Same as slurry pit, but with
  biological processing (reduces
  volume over time).

Current emptying process:
- Less frequent emptying (every 2-3
  years if biogas is harvested).
- Contractor removes solids that
  accumulate.

Cost:
- Covered lagoon construction:
  $50,000-150,000 (one-time).
- Annual operation: $5,000-15,000
  (maintenance, biogas capture system).

Advantages:
- Best odor control (covered system).
- Biogas production (can offset
  electricity cost or generate revenue).
- Most sophisticated/modern system.

Disadvantages:
- Most expensive upfront.
- Requires more maintenance.
- Biogas capture adds complexity.

Retrofit potential: GOOD (but less
critical since system already controls
odor somewhat).

────────────────────────────────────────

DISTRIBUTION:

System 1 (Slurry pit/lagoon):
- Prevalence: ~60% of CAFOs (most
  common).
- Retrofit difficulty: EASIEST.
- Cost to retrofit: $0-500/CAFO.

System 2 (Solids separator):
- Prevalence: ~20% of new CAFOs.
- Retrofit difficulty: EASY.
- Cost to retrofit: $500-2,000/CAFO.

System 3 (Covered lagoon):
- Prevalence: ~10% of CAFOs (rare in
  US).
- Retrofit difficulty: MODERATE.
- Cost to retrofit: $1,000-5,000/CAFO.

Other/unclassified:
- ~10% of CAFOs (very small,
  alternative systems).

────────────────────────────────────────

KEY INSIGHT:

80% of CAFOs already have systems
that can be retrofitted for <$2,000.

60% of CAFOs already have slurry
pits/lagoons that can be retrofitted
for $0-500.

You don't need to build capture
infrastructure.

It ALREADY EXISTS.

incorporate your P1S-B system.
```
