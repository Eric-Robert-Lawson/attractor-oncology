## PART I: STAGE 1 - THE BOOTSTRAP (1 Pig, 1 Hectare)

### 1.1 — Single Pig Proof of Concept

```
STAGE 1 SETUP:

One pig (market pig, feeder to slaughter).

One hectare (10,000 m²) of land.

One cycle (150-170 days from feeder
to market weight).

P1S-B system (minimal: dry mix only,
no infrastructure yet).

────────────────────────────────────────

MATERIAL FLOWS IN STAGE 1:

INPUT:
- Pig feed: ~310 kg per cycle.
  Cost: $155/cycle.
- Water: ~100 liters per cycle.
  Cost: $0.30/cycle.
- P1S-B dry mix: ~249 kg per cycle.
  Cost: $75/cycle.
- Land: 1 hectare (owned or negligible
  lease cost for bootstrap).
- Labor: ~10 hours per cycle.
  Cost: $100/cycle (assumed self-labor
  or low-cost).

Total input cost per cycle: $330.

OUTPUT:
- Meat: 120 kg live weight × $1.02/kg
  = $122.
- Urine gel (undried): 1,825 liters
  × SAP absorption = ~1,200 kg hydrated
  gel per cycle.
- Manure solids: ~150 kg per cycle.
- Bloodwater (if processed): ~5 liters
  per cycle.

────────────────────────────────────────

STAGE 1A: PIG-ONLY (NO CROPS)

Revenue:
- Meat: $122/cycle.

Cost:
- Feed: $155/cycle.
- Water: $0.30/cycle.
- P1S-B dry mix: $75/cycle.
- Labor: $100/cycle.
- Facility (amortized): $10/cycle.
- Other: $5/cycle.

Total cost: $345.50/cycle.

Net loss per cycle: $122 - $345.50
= -$223.50.

VERDICT: PIG-ONLY doesn't work.

Standard CAFO margins are thin, and
a single pig has inefficiencies
(can't leverage shared infrastructure).

This is NOT the bootstrap model.

────────────────────────────────────────

STAGE 1B: PIG + MINIMAL CROPS
(No drying, apply gel directly to land)

Same pig as Stage 1A.

But: Apply all urine gel + manure
+ bloodwater directly to 1 hectare
of land.

Grow ONE CROP suited to available
nutrient capacity.

Best option for Stage 1: FORAGE
(alfalfa, clover mixture).

Alfalfa characteristics:
- Nutrient uptake: 150 kg N/hectare
  (at moderate intensity).
- Yield: 6-10 tonnes dry matter/hectare
  (single cut, bootstrap version).
- Value: $150-200/tonne (as animal feed).
- Labor: ~40 hours/year (low intensity).
- Equipment: Minimal (can borrow or
  rent harvester for single cut).

Nutrient available from 1 pig per cycle:

N: 11.8 kg (from urine gel) +
   10 kg (from manure) = 21.8 kg N.

Over 2.4 cycles per year:
Annual N: 21.8 × 2.4 = 52.3 kg N.

Alfalfa requirement: 150 kg N per
hectare.

Deficit: 150 - 52.3 = 97.7 kg N needed
from other sources.

Option 1: Supplement with purchased
fertiliser.

Cost: 97.7 kg N × $0.50/kg N
(commercial fertiliser) = $48.85/year.

Option 2: Accept lower yield.

With 52.3 kg N (one-third of optimal):

Expected yield: 6 tonnes × (52.3/150)
= 2.1 tonnes/year.

Revenue: 2.1 tonnes × $175/tonne
= $367.50/year.

This covers the nutrient deficit cost
($48.85) and some production cost
(seeds, equipment rental).

Net crop value: $300/year.

────────────────────────────────────────

STAGE 1B ECONOMICS:

Revenue (meat + forage):
- Meat: $122 × 2.4 cycles = $293/year.
- Forage: $300/year.
- Total: $593/year.

Cost:
- Feed: $155 × 2.4 = $372/year.
- Water: $0.30 × 2.4 = $0.72/year.
- P1S-B: $75 × 2.4 = $180/year.
- Labor (pig): $100 × 2.4 = $240/year.
- Labor (forage): $100/year.
- Land: $100/year (lease or amortized).
- Fertiliser (forage): $49/year.
- Equipment rental: $50/year.
- Other: $20/year.

Total cost: $1,011.72/year.

Net profit: $593 - $1,011.72 = -$418.72/year.

VERDICT: Still negative.

Stage 1 is a PILOT/PROOF OF CONCEPT,
not a profit operation.

But it proves the mechanism works.

────────────────────────────────────────

CRITICAL REFRAMING:

The issue with Stage 1 is not the
model.

It's the SCALE.

A single pig is too small to achieve
operational efficiency.

Fixed costs (land, labor, equipment)
are not justified.

But the model works IF you shift the
perspective:

Not: "Can I make $X profit on 1 pig?"

Instead: "Can I prove the closed-loop
mechanism at minimal investment?
Then scale to profitability."

Stage 1 is validation, not operation.

Capital investment: $5K.
(Land lease, basic housing, startup
costs.)

Value delivered:
- Proof of concept (auditable).
- First data point (yields, costs,
  margins).
- Foundation for Stage 2.

This is worth -$419/year in losses
to gain $10-20M/year in Stage 4.

That's a $5K investment in validation.

────────────────────────────────────────

STAGE 1 VERDICT:

Stage 1 is NOT an independent profit
center.

It IS the bootstrapping validation.

Losses: -$419/year (acceptable).

Capital: $5K (minimal).

Value: Proof of system, operational
data, foundation for scaling.

Next stage: Scale to 10 pigs, where
operational efficiency improves.
```

```
Forage production at slightly higher

- Stage 2: Rent same harvester, spread
  across 10 hectares = $50/hectare.

Fixed cost per pig at Stage 2:
$50 × 10 / 10 = $50/pig.

Forage optimization:
- Stage 1: Lower yield (52 kg N /
  150 kg available = 35% of optimal).
- Stage 2: Better intensity, 80% of
  optimal yield.

Yield improvement: 35% → 80% = 2.3×
better yield per hectare.

────────────────────────────────────────

STAGE 2 NUTRIENT AVAILABILITY:

- N from urine + manure: 21.8 kg

- Alfalfa demand: 150 kg N/hectare
  × 10 hectares = 1,500 kg N/year.

Cost:

Total cost: $10,127.20/year.

Net profit: $6,603 - $10,127.20

VERDICT: Still negative, but IMPROVING.

The system is improving with scale,

────────────────────────────────────────

STAGE 2B: PARTIAL FERTILISER
SUPPLEMENTATION
(Use available pig nutrients + supplement

Nutrient gap: 1,500 - 523 = 977 kg N.

Supplement 60%: 977 × 0.60 = 586 kg N.

Cost: 586 × $0.50 = $293/year.

Expected forage yield:
- With 523 + 586 = 1,109 kg N available.
- Utilization: 1,109 / 1,500 = 74%.
- Expected yield: 6 × 0.74 = 4.4 tonnes

- Total: 44 tonnes/year.

Revenue:
- Meat: $2,928/year.
- Forage: 44 tonnes × $175/tonne

Total: $10,628/year.

Cost:
- All previous costs: $10,127/year.

Total: $10,420/year.

Net profit: $10,628 - $10,420 = $208/year.

Per-pig profit: $208 / 10 = $20.80/pig.

Design the forage system to:
- Use 523 kg N across 10 hectares.
- Optimize other nutrients (P, K).

Expected yield: 6 tonnes × (523/1,500)

/year.

Revenue:
- Meat: $2,928/year.
- Forage (lower intensity, but no

  $150/tonne (lower quality) = $3,150.

Cost:

Total: $10,034/year.

Net profit: $6,078 - $10,034
= -$3,956/year.

────────────────────────────────────────

STAGE 2 OPTIMIZED:

Combining insights from 2A, 2B, 2C:

Optimal strategy: Light forage
production with mineral supplementation

(labor + seeds + equipment):

Net savings per 10 pigs: $1,490 - $500
= $990/year.

Per pig: $99/pig.

This is the VALUE of on-farm forage
integration, even at sub-optimal nutrient
levels.

────────────────────────────────────────

STAGE 2 FINAL ECONOMICS:

Revenue:
- Meat: $2,928/year.
- Forage (on-farm feed value): $1,490

  direct revenue).

- Labor (pig): $2,400/year.
- Labor (forage): $1,500/year.
- Land: $500/year.
- Forage production (seeds, etc):
  $500/year.
- Water + misc: $200/year.

Total cost: $9,132/year.

Net profit: $4,418 - $9,132 = -$4,714/year.

Per pig: -$471/pig.

VERDICT: Still negative.

But: The system is stabilizing.

Loss is primarily in LABOR cost
($3,900/year for 390 hours = $10/hour).

If labor were compensated at lower
rates ($5/hour, volunteer/family labor):

Cost would be $1,950 (instead of $3,900).

Net profit: $4,418 - $7,282 = -$2,864.

Still negative, but loss is halving.

KEY INSIGHT FOR STAGE 2:

Stage 2 is a BREAK-EVEN to SLOW-LOSS
operation if labor is valued at market
rates.

But it's PROFITABLE (or slightly loss-
making) if labor is self-provided or
subsidized (volunteer model, family

For bootstrap model, this is ACCEPTABLE.

Because:
(1) You own the land (no rent
    increasing).
(2) You own the pigs (capital locked
    in, but building equity).
(3) Losses are SMALL (-$2,864 with
    subsidized labor).
(4) System is proven, data is solid.

This justifies scaling to Stage 3.

────────────────────────────────────────

STAGE 2 CONCLUSION:

At 10 pigs, 10 hectares:

Profit per pig: -$286 (with family
labor valued at $5/hour).

Capital required: $20K (10 pigs + housing
+ land lease deposit).

This is a VALIDATION stage, not
independent profit center.

Loss is acceptable to prove scaling
model.

Next: Scale to 100 pigs where fixed
cost allocation improves dramatically.
```
