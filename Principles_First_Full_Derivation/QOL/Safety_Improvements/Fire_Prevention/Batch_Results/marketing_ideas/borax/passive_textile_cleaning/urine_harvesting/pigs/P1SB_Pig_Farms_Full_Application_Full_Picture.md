## PART II: THE INTEGRATED P1S-B SYSTEM FOR PIG OPERATIONS

### 2.1 — Stream Separation and Processing Architecture

```
THE COLLECTION REDESIGN:

Currently: urine → faeces → collection channel →
           manure storage → disposal.
           All mixed, all subject to ammonia loss.

With P1S-B: three separate collection streams.

STREAM 1: URINE (separated at source)

  Mechanism: slatted floors in pig pens drain urine
            through slats to a below-floor trough.
  Standard design: most modern pig facilities already
                  use slatted floors.
  Modification needed: trough must lead to a
                      separation point BEFORE mixing
                      with faeces.

  Current design: urine + faeces mix in collection
                 channel, all flows to one system.

  Modified design: urine flows to dedicated collection
                 tank (keep separate from faeces).
                 Faeces fall to a separate system.

  Cost of modification: $5,000-15,000 (plumbing
                       changes, diversion valve,
                       second collection tank).

  Daily collection:
    54,000L urine/day → collection tank.
    Apply dry mix: 54,000L × 0.1L/L = 5,400kg
                   dry mix/day.
    Cost: 5,400kg × $0.154/kg = $831/day.

  Processing:
    Gel accumulates in collection tank.
    Every 3-5 days: transfer to drying area.
    Drying: 7-14 days to full dryness.
    Harvest: 54,000L × (40g/L TDS from urine)
             + 5,400kg dry mix
             = 2,160kg + 5,400kg
             = 7,560kg dry product/day
             (approximately; accounting for
              solids concentration variation).

  Annual urine harvest:
    7,560kg/day × 365 = 2,759,400kg/year
    = 2,759 tonnes.

  Product value at $10/kg:
    2,759,000kg × $10 = $27,590,000/year.

  Processing cost:
    5,400kg dry mix × 365 × $0.154/kg
    = $303,660/year.

  Net annual value from urine:
    $27,590,000 - $303,660 = $27,286,340/year.

  This is ENORMOUS.
  Larger than the facility's current meat revenue.

STREAM 2: WASTEWATER (processing runoff, spillage, wash)

  Volume: 7,500L/day average.
  Collection: separate drain from main manure system.
  Concentration: 1-2% solids (lower than urine).

  Processing:
    Apply dry mix: 7,500L × 0.12/L = 900kg dry mix/day.
    Cost: $138/day.

  Harvest:
    7,500L × (15g/L TDS wastewater) + 900kg dry mix
    = 112.5kg + 900kg = 1,012kg/day.

  Annual wastewater harvest:
    1,012kg × 365 = 369,380kg/year.

  Product value at $6/kg (lower grade than urine):
    369,380kg × $6 = $2,216,280/year.

  Processing cost:
    900kg × 365 × $0.154/kg = $50,490/year.

  Net annual value from wastewater:
    $2,216,280 - $50,490 = $2,165,790/year.

STREAM 3: FAECES (solids, not processed via P1S-B)

  Current fate: falls to pen floor, swept/scraped to
               collection bin, sent to composting or
               direct field application.
  P1S-B role: NONE. Faeces are already relatively dry
            (50-70% moisture) and do not require
            P1S-B processing.
  Post-P1S-B: faeces go to secondary composting with
             remaining urine (if any) or direct
             sale as compost.
  Value: minimal (~$100,000/year current, unchanged
         by P1S-B).

STREAM 4: BLOOD WATER (if on-site slaughter)

  For standard facility: none (pigs shipped to
                        regional slaughter).
  For vertically integrated facility: 62,000L/year.

  Processing:
    Apply dry mix: 62,000L/year × 0.25kg/L
                 = 15,500kg dry mix/year.
    Cost: 15,500 × $0.154 = $2,387/year.

  Harvest:
    62,000L × (30g/L solids) + 15,500kg dry mix
    = 1,860kg + 15,500kg = 17,360kg/year.

  Product value at $8/kg (blood-based premium product):
    17,360kg × $8 = $138,880/year.

  Processing cost: $2,387/year.

  Net annual value from blood water:
    $138,880 - $2,387 = $136,493/year.

  (This is ONLY if facility has on-site slaughter.
   Standard facility: $0.)

STREAM 5: DRINKING WATER + FEED (doping for enhancement)

  This is not a byproduct stream.
  This is input modification to ENHANCE byproduct value.
  Covered separately in Section 2.3.

TOTAL INTEGRATED STREAMS VALUE:

  Urine: $27,286,340/year.
  Wastewater: $2,165,790/year.
  Blood water (if applicable): $136,493/year.
  Total: $29,452,130/year (standard facility)
         to $29,588,623/year (with on-site slaughter).

  This is 10× the facility's current net profit
  from meat sales.

  This is the transformation.

    - Are absorbed and concentrated in urine.
    - Increase nutrient density of harvested product.
    - Are low-cost additives.
    - Do not harm pig health/growth.

  Result: harvest product has HIGHER nutrient
  concentration and therefore HIGHER VALUE.

DOPING COMPONENTS (from prior "dairy_doping_derivation" document):

  Candidate 1: SELENIUM (as sodium selenate)
    Concentration in feed: 0.3-0.5 mg/kg (standard)

                     0.1-0.5 mg/L → 1-3 mg/L.

DOPING TOTAL IMPACT:

## PART III: PASSIVE CONTAMINATION AND MICROBIAL/FUNGAL ENRICHMENT

### 3.1 — What Naturally Contaminates the Harvests

```
THE PASSIVE INPUTS CONCEPT (from prior "passive_absorption"
document):

  As urine is collected and processed, it passively
  collects additional organic material:
    - Shed skin cells from pigs.
    - Microorganisms from the pen environment.
    - Airborne dust and organic matter.
    - Dietary components.
    - Fecal trace material (despite separation).

  None of these are "contaminants" in a negative sense.
  All are VALUABLE nutrient additions to the harvest
  product.

  They enhance the product's value:
    - Microbial biomass adds bioavailable nutrients.
    - Fungal activity adds enzymes and bioactive
      compounds.
    - Shed cells add amino acids and trace minerals.

PASSIVE INPUT CLASS 1: MICROBIAL COLONIZATION

  Description: as urine sits in collection tanks
  (before drying) and in gel (during drying), bacteria
  and fungi naturally colonize the substrate.

  Dominant organisms:
    Bacteria: Bacillus, Corynebacterium, Lactobacillus
              (these are ubiquitous in animal operations,
               not pathogens, produce beneficial enzymes).
    Fungi: Aspergillus, Trichoderma, Alternaria.

  Beneficial effects:
    - Enzymatic decomposition of complex organic matter
      → increases bioavailability of nutrients.
    - Nitrogen fixation (some bacteria fix atmospheric
      N) → adds "free" nitrogen to product.
    - Production of plant growth-promoting substances
      (IAA, gibberellins).
    - Antibiotic production (some fungi) → inhibits
      pathogens.

  Quantification:
    Natural colonization (no inoculation): 10^7-10^8
    CFU/g (colony-forming units per gram) of dried
    product.
    Product value enhancement: 5-15% above baseline
    (from enzyme activity, nutrient release,
     growth-promoting substances).

  Cost: ZERO (passive, requires no inoculant).

PASSIVE INPUT CLASS 2: FUNGAL BLOOM (if conditions permit)

  Description: under humid conditions, fungi may
  actively grow on the drying gel, creating visible
  fungal mycelium on the surface.

  Dominant fungi: Trichoderma, Aspergillus (same as
                 above, usually same species as
                 naturally colonizing).

  Result: visible fungal network on product surface,
          white to green-tinged appearance.

  Value enhancement:
    - Trichoderma specifically produces:
      * Cellulase and hemicellulase enzymes
        (break down plant cell walls in soil).
      * Chitinase enzymes (break down insect/fungal
        shells, may have biocontrol properties).
      * Plant growth hormones.
    - This transforms the product from "organic
      fertiliser" to "biofertiliser with enzymatic
      activity."

  Market positioning:
    Standard organic fertiliser: $10-12/kg.
    Biofertiliser with fungal activity: $15-25/kg.
    Premium: 50-150% higher price.

  Product premium value: $5-15/kg × 2,759,000kg/year
                       = $13,795,000-41,385,000/year.

  Wait: again this is too high if applied to ALL product.

  More realistic: 30-50% of product develops visible
  fungal bloom (depending on drying humidity/temperature).
  Premium applies to fungal product only.
  ~1,200,000kg/year develops fungal bloom.
  Premium: $8/kg average (split between baseline and
  premium pricing).
  Value: 1,200,000kg × $8 = $9,600,000/year.

  This is still SUBSTANTIAL.
  It is 35% of the baseline urine harvest value
  added purely from passive fungal colonization.

PASSIVE INPUT CLASS 3: FECAL TRACE MATERIAL

  Description: despite urine/faeces separation, small
  amounts of fecal material inevitably contaminate
  the urine stream (from pen floor, from splash).

  Concentration: 1-5% of harvest mass.

  Composition: residual feed, bacteria, short-chain
  fatty acids, fatty acids, amino acids.

  Value impact: NEUTRAL to POSITIVE.
    - Adds additional nutrients (feed residue).
    - Adds microbial biomass (fecal bacteria).
    - Negative: adds potential pathogen risk
      (E. coli, Salmonella from feces).

  Mitigation:
    - Borax antimicrobial activity suppresses pathogenic
      bacteria.
    - High pH (7.5-8) suppresses pathogens.
    - Drying to <8% moisture eliminates moisture-dependent
      pathogens.

  Net impact: +0% to +10% product value (from additional
  nutrients and microbes, offset by slight risk/
  regulatory consideration).

PASSIVE INPUT CLASS 4: SHED EPITHELIAL CELLS

  Description: pigs naturally shed skin cells, hair,
  scales from skin microbiota.

  Concentration in urine: ~50-100mg/L (very low mass,
