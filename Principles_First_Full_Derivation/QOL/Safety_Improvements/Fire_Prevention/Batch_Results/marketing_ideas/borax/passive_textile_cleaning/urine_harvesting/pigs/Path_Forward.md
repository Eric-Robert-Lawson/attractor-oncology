## PART III: THE IP MOAT STRATEGY

### 3.1 — What To Patent vs. What To Keep Secret

```
PATENT STRATEGY:

Patents are PUBLIC.

Once you file a patent, the entire
world knows your invention.

When patent expires (20 years), anyone
can copy.

BUT: During patent term, you have
exclusive rights.

You can license it (force competitors
to pay royalties).

You can enforce it (sue copycats).

What should you patent?

STRATEGY: Patent only what
COMPETITORS WOULD INDEPENDENTLY
DISCOVER anyway.

This is called "defensive patenting."

Purpose: Prevent competitors from
patenting what they discover, force
them to license from you.

Example:

Formulation for cosmetics incorporating
your substrate:

If you DON'T patent: Unilever develops
formula, patents it themselves, you
have no claim.

If you DO patent: Unilever must license
your patent, pay you royalties, cannot
patent their own variation.

PATENTS TO FILE:

Product formulations:
- "Cosmetic formulation containing
  bentonite, SAP, and nitrogen-rich
  nutrient substrate."
- "Feed supplement comprising mineral-
  enriched substrate and microbial
  inoculants."
- "Medical hydrogel wound dressing
  containing water-absorbent polymer
  and antimicrobial boron compound."

These are broad enough to cover
competitor formulations, specific
enough to be defensible.

Application methods:
- "Method of enhancing aquaculture
  feed efficiency by adding substrate
  at 3-8% concentration."
- "Method of improving wound healing
  time using hydrogel dressing derived
  from substrate."

These are process patents, protect
HOW the product is used.

Manufacturing processes:
- "Method of producing nutrient-rich
  substrate through animal urine
  immobilisation using SAP and borax."
- "Method of optimising nutrient
  concentration in substrate through
  feed doping."

These protect HOW the product is made.

Patent cost: $100K-500K per patent,
globally.

Patent portfolio size: 30-50 patents
across all applications.

Total cost: $3-25M over 10 years.

ROI on patents: Extremely high.

One successful lawsuit against
copying competitor: $100M-1B settlement
(patent infringement damages).

PATENTS ARE INSURANCE AGAINST
COMPETITIVE COPYING.

────────────────────────────────────────

TRADE SECRETS STRATEGY:

Trade secrets are PRIVATE.

You never file them.

They are never published.

They are protected by confidentiality
agreements with employees, partners.

Trade secrets protect FOREVER
(no expiration).

BUT: If someone independently
discovers the secret, they can use it
(trade secret protection only works
against people you told).

What should you keep secret?

STRATEGY: Keep secret everything that
COMPETITORS CANNOT INDEPENDENTLY
DISCOVER.

This includes:

Cost reduction techniques:
- Exact pig feed formulas that
  maximise nutrient output.
- Farming layouts that optimise
  labour efficiency.
- SAP and bentonite sourcing tactics
  (where to buy cheapest, how to
  negotiate volume discounts).
- Processing techniques (drying,
  grinding, quality control methods)
  that achieve $25/kg cost while
  competitors must spend $40/kg.

These are UNIQUE to your operation.

Competitors cannot discover them
without looking at your internal
books.

Advantage: Indefinite cost advantage
(competitors cannot match your
$25/kg cost without copying your
methods).

────────────────────────────────────────

Nutrient enhancement formulas:
- Exact mineral doping recipes.
- Specific microbial cultures used
  to enhance nutrient conversion.
- Feed additives and timing of
  administration.
- Animal genetics selection criteria.

These are PROPRIETARY PROCESSES
that you develop over years of
experimentation.

Competitors cannot discover them
without access to your farm data.

Advantage: Superior product quality.

Your substrate performs better than
commodity alternatives.

Partners prefer your substrate because
results are measurably better.

────────────────────────────────────────

Strategic information:
- Which partners you're negotiating
  with.
- Which partners are close to signing
  exclusive deals.
- Which markets you're targeting next.
- Which product lines are under
  development.
- Which patents you plan to file.

This information is valuable for
COMPETITIVE ANTICIPATION.

If competitors know you're planning
to enter cosmetics, they can pre-emptively
partner with suppliers.

If competitors know you're negotiating
with Cargill, they can try to block
the deal.

Keeping this secret prevents
competitive blocking.

────────────────────────────────────────

PROTECTION OF TRADE SECRETS:

Employee agreements:
- All employees sign non-disclosure
  agreements (NDAs).
- Non-compete clauses (cannot work
  for competitor for 2-5 years).
- Invention assignment (anything
  developed belongs to company).

Partner agreements:
- All partners sign confidentiality
  clauses.
- They cannot disclose your
  formulations, processes, or supply
  prices to competitors.
- Breach results in contract
  termination and legal action.

Data security:
- Proprietary recipes stored in
  encrypted databases.
- Limited employee access (need-to-know
  basis only).
- No written documentation of key
  processes (memorized by limited
  staff).
- Regular audits to prevent leaks.

────────────────────────────────────────

COMPARISON:

Patent protection:
- PUBLIC (everyone knows your
  invention).
- TIME-LIMITED (20 years).
- DEFENSIBLE (can sue infringers).
- EXPENSIVE (filing, prosecution,
  litigation).

Trade secret protection:
- PRIVATE (only known to you).
- INDEFINITE (forever, as long as
  you keep it secret).
- DEFENSIBLE (can sue for theft,
  breach of contract).
- CHEAP (just confidentiality costs).

OPTIMAL STRATEGY:

Patent obvious formulations that
competitors would discover anyway
(defensive patenting).

Keep secret the cost-reduction and
optimization techniques that are
unique to you (indefinite advantage).

Result: Competitors cannot copy your
cost structure (trade secret), cannot
patent similar formulations (your
patents block them), and cannot
recruit your staff (NDAs enforce

Your competitive moat is essentially
UNBREAKABLE.

## PART VI: COMPETITIVE BLOCKING STRATEGY

```

File patents so broadly that competitor

Competitor decides to enter aquaculture

You sue again (your patents cover
formulation variations).

They try different approach (algae-

You counter with provisional patents
you filed 2-3 years ago (when you

Eventually, competitor gives up

Alternatively, competitor licenses
your patents (pays you royalties).

RESULT: Competitors cannot compete
without paying you.

────────────────────────────────────────

DEFENSIVE STRATEGY 2: SUPPLY
EXCLUSIVITY

Lock in partners with exclusive
supply agreements.

If partner is bound to buy from you
exclusively, they cannot buy from
competitor.

customer base before they even start
production.

Cargill cannot buy from your

Unilever cannot buy from your

3M cannot buy from your competitor

RESULT: Customer lock-in is
