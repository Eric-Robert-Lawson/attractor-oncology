```

that emerges from the P1S-B system: the conversion of
EXISTING INDUSTRIAL LIQUID BYPRODUCTS to dry, saleable
products using the same dry ingredient mechanism.

Unlike urine harvesting, which requires a new collection
and processing infrastructure, this model targets byproducts
that ALREADY EXIST in operating facilities, are already
being managed as waste or disposal costs, and can be
processed ON-SITE using the P1S-B dry mix.

The critical constraint is TRANSPORT ECONOMICS:
  Liquid byproducts are 85-99% water.

  Evaporation or concentration on-site is REQUIRED.

evaporate the liquid, capture the dissolved solids, and
deliver a dry, saleable product.

## PART I: THE BYPRODUCT OPPORTUNITIES

### 1.1 — Slaughterhouse Blood Water Runoff

```
THE BYPRODUCT:

  Source: Slaughterhouse processing, kill floor.
  Nature: Water mixed with blood, meat particles,
          fat, processing water.
  Disposal challenge: Extremely high nutrient load
                     (BOD 100,000+ mg/L in raw blood,
                      COD 150,000-400,000 mg/L).
  Current disposal cost: $0.02-0.10/L to treat and
                        discharge. For a 500-animal/day
                        slaughterhouse producing 50,000L
                        blood water daily: $1,000-5,000/day
                        = $365,000-$1,825,000/year.

NUTRIENT COMPOSITION:

  Total Nitrogen (N):      800-2,000 mg/L.
  - Form: proteins, amino acids, urea.
  - In raw blood: up to 15-18% protein.
  - Therefore N concentration in raw blood
    can reach 2,400-2,800 mg/L.

  Phosphorus (P):          150-300 mg/L.
  - From heme iron, nucleic acids.

  Potassium (K):           200-400 mg/L.
  - From muscle tissue, cell contents.

  Organic matter (BOD):    4,000-25,000 mg/L in
                          diluted runoff
                          (100,000+ in raw blood).

THE PROFIT OPPORTUNITY:

  Current cost to facility: $0.05/L disposal cost (average).
  Daily blood water: 50,000L/day (typical 500-animal facility).
  Annual disposal cost: 50,000L × 365 × $0.05
                      = $912,500/year.

  IF the byproduct can be processed on-site to a dry product:
    Processing cost: negligible (dry ingredients $0.15/L).
    Facility pays $0.02/L net (or even gets paid if byproduct
    is taken off-site).
    Profit to processor: $0.05 - $0.02 = $0.03/L saved.
    Plus harvest value: ~$0.10-0.20/L (nutrient value).
    Net value: $0.13-0.23/L.

  At 50,000L/day:
    Daily value capture: 50,000 × $0.18 (average) = $9,000.
    Annual value: $3,285,000.

  DRY PRODUCT SPECIFICATION:

    Water content: 95-99% (blood water runoff).
    Dissolved solids: 1-5%.
    Primary nutrients: N (mostly protein), P, K.

    Applying SAP + bentonite + borax + baking soda:
      SAP absorbs water: 50,000L × 0.30-0.40 = 15,000-20,000kg
                        SAP needed.
      Borax inhibits urease in protein: 1,500kg.
      Bentonite captures mineral ions: 7,000kg.
      Baking soda buffers pH: 5,000kg.
      Total dry mix per day: ~28,500kg.

    Harvest mass: 50,000L × 2% TDS + 28,500kg dry mix
                = 1,000kg solids + 28,500kg = 29,500kg.

    This is a MASSIVE volume of output per day
    (29,500kg = ~295 tonnes per month per facility).
    But it is all dry solid that can be stored,
    bagged, and sold.

  CHARACTERISTICS:

    The product is a blood-based nitrogen concentrate.
    NPK profile: ~8-12% N, 2-4% P, 1-2% K
    (actual varies with dilution ratio).
    Market: high-end organic fertilizer, horticulture,
            turf management.
    Price: $12-20/kg retail, $5-10/kg wholesale.
    Comparable product: blood meal, $10-12/kg.

  FACILITY SPECIFICS:

    Slaughterhouses kill 200-1,000+ animals/day.
    Blood production: 10-15L per animal (average).
    = 2,000-15,000L fresh blood per day.
    Additional wash water (kill floor cleaning): 30,000-50,000L.
    Total blood water runoff: 40,000-60,000L/day.

    Not all slaughterhouses have dedicated blood collection.
    But ~70% of US slaughterhouses (USDA federally inspected)
    are likely candidates for this system.
    = ~1,500 slaughterhouses × 50,000L/day average
    = 75 million L/day addressable market.

  THE KILLER INSIGHT:

    The slaughterhouse is ALREADY disposing of this liquid.
    The disposal cost is a sunk cost.
    The facility would PAY to have it removed if necessary.
    Processing it on-site to a dry product ELIMINATES
    a cost (disposal) and creates a REVENUE STREAM (product sale).
    The facility saves money AND gets a product to sell.
    This is a perfect economic alignment.

  RISK FACTORS:

    1. Blood water contains pathogens (bovine TB, E. coli).
       Mitigation: borax antimicrobial activity suppresses
       pathogens at pH 7.5-8. High-temperature drying
       (if used) further reduces pathogen load. Product
       would require testing before sale.

    2. Blood product is odorous during collection/processing.
       Mitigation: SAP gel immobilizes liquid immediately,
       reducing odor. Drying further eliminates smell.

    3. Product is saturated protein - may have shelf stability
       issues (oxidation).
       Mitigation: antioxidant (vitamin E) could be added
       to dry mix if needed.

    4. Regulatory: finished product from blood processing
       may require pathogen testing, regulatory approval.
       Mitigation: standard biosolids testing protocols apply.

  IMPLEMENTATION TIMELINE:

    Day 1: Install dry ingredient hopper above collection
           trough. Mix dry ingredients into blood water
           as it is pumped from kill floor.
    Day 1-7: Allow gel to dry (passive, no equipment).
    Week 1: Harvest dry product, bag, label.
    Week 2: First product sale.
    Payback period: < 1 month (at $3.3M/year value).
```

### 1.2 — Cheese/Dairy Whey Serum

```
THE BYPRODUCT:

  Source: Cheese making (dairy processing).
  Nature: Liquid after cheese curds are removed.
  Composition: ~6-7% solids (lactose, minerals,
              residual protein, fat).
  Disposal challenge: High BOD/COD, expensive to discharge
                     to sewer or environment.
  Current disposal cost: $0.01-0.05/L treatment + disposal.

FACILITY SCALE:

  Typical cheese facility: 10,000-100,000L milk processed/day.
  Whey production: ~9L whey per 1L milk processed
  (milk to cheese is ~10% yield, whey is ~90%).
  = 90,000-900,000L whey/day at a large facility.

  Disposal cost for large facility:
    500,000L whey × $0.03/L = $15,000/day.
    Annual: $5,475,000.

NUTRIENT COMPOSITION:

  Total Nitrogen (N):      0.3-0.8 g/L.
  - Form: residual whey protein, amino acids.
  - Whey contains ~0.6% protein typically.

  Lactose:                 4-5 g/L (NOT NUTRIENTS FOR CROPS,
                          but energy source for microbes).

  Phosphorus (P):          0.5-1.2 g/L.
  - From milk minerals.

  Potassium (K):           1.2-1.8 g/L.
  - From milk.

  Calcium:                 0.4-0.6 g/L.
  - From milk.

  Organic matter (BOD):    3,000-5,000 mg/L.

THE PROFIT OPPORTUNITY:

  Current cost to facility: $0.03/L disposal (average).
  Daily whey: 500,000L/day (large facility).
  Annual disposal cost: 500,000L × 365 × $0.03
                      = $5,475,000/year.

  Processing on-site with P1S-B:
    Dry mix cost: 500,000L × $0.10/L = $50,000/day.
    (Note: this is HIGHER than slaughterhouse blood
     because whey has lower density - needs more
     SAP per L to absorb the moisture and lactose.)

    Daily value captured: $15,000 disposal cost avoided
                         + $20,000 product value
                         = $35,000/day net.
    Less dry mix cost: $50,000.
    Net: −$15,000/day? 

  WAIT: This doesn't work. Whey is too dilute.

  RECONSIDER:

    The issue is that whey is 93% water, 7% solids.
    SAP absorption requires significant loading.
    BUT: whey is already being handled by the facility
    in some form. Most large facilities have whey
    processing equipment (evaporators, reverse osmosis).

    The question is not "is P1S-B the most efficient
    drying method?" but "is P1S-B the LOWEST-COST
    alternative if the facility wants to capture
    the nutrient value?"

    Answer: probably not optimal for whey alone.
    BUT: whey + other byproducts (blood from same facility
    if it's a integrated facility, or combined with
    brewery waste) could be processed as a mixed stream.

  REVISED OPPORTUNITY:

    For cheese facilities WITH slaughterhouse operations
    (integrated meat + dairy production):
      Blood water (more concentrated): 30,000L/day.
      Whey (more dilute): 200,000L/day.
      Combined: 230,000L/day.
      Mixed nitrogen content: higher than whey alone.
      P1S-B efficiency: viable.

    For standalone cheese facilities:
      Whey alone: P1S-B is MARGINAL or NEGATIVE ROI
      unless whey is concentrated first (via existing
      evaporators) and then P1S-B is applied.

    Ideal scenario: multi-stream byproduct facility
    (dairy + meat) where whey is co-processed with
    higher-nutrient blood water.

  FACILITY SPECIFICS:

    US has ~1,200 cheese-making facilities.
    Average daily whey: ~200,000L.
    Large facilities (>1M lbs milk/day): 500,000L+ whey.
    = addressable market is large but economically
      challenging for whey alone.

  VERDICT:

    Whey is a secondary opportunity, best paired with
    higher-nutrient byproducts (blood, fish) in
    co-processing scenarios.

    Standalone whey is MARGINAL and not a primary target
    (existing whey concentration systems are more efficient
    for whey alone).
```

### 1.3 — Fish Processing Hydrolysate Liquor

```
THE BYPRODUCT:

  Source: Fish processing plants (fish meal, fish oil
          production, fish hydrolysate operations).
  Nature: Liquid remaining after protein extraction,
          enzymatic digestion, or fermentation of fish
          waste (heads, frames, offal, processing water).
  Composition: ~2-6% solids (proteins, amino acids,
              minerals, fats, organic matter).
  Disposal challenge: Extremely high nutrient and
                     organic load (BOD 2,000-5,000 mg/L,
                     high N, high P).
  Current disposal cost: $0.02-0.08/L treatment.

FACILITY SCALE:

  Typical fish processing facility: 50-300 tonnes/day
  raw fish processed.
  Hydrolysate liquor generation: 20-40% of input weight
  in liquid byproduct.
  = 10,000-120,000L/day hydrolysate liquor.

NUTRIENT COMPOSITION (exceptional - highest of all byproducts):

  Total Nitrogen (N):      500-2,000 mg/L.
  - Form: amino acids, soluble proteins, urea.
  - This is 2-5× higher than dairy whey.
  - This is comparable to URINE CONCENTRATION.

  Phosphorus (P):          50-200 mg/L.
  - From fish bone, nucleic acids.

  Potassium (K):           100-300 mg/L.
  - From fish muscle tissue.

  Organic matter (BOD):    2,000-5,000 mg/L.

  TRACE ELEMENTS: Iron, zinc, copper, selenium
  (from fish tissue) in bioavailable forms.

THE PROFIT OPPORTUNITY:

  Current cost to facility: $0.04/L disposal (average).
  Daily hydrolysate: 50,000L/day (medium facility).
  Annual disposal cost: 50,000L × 365 × $0.04
                      = $730,000/year.

  Processing on-site with P1S-B:
    Dry mix cost: 50,000L × $0.12/L = $6,000/day.
    Dry mix cost/year: $2,190,000.

  WAIT: This is even more expensive than whey.
  The issue is the same: low solids concentration means
  high SAP/bentonite requirement.

  RECONCEPTUALIZE THE APPROACH:

    The problem is applying dry mix to 50,000L/day liquid
    requires 6,000kg/day dry mix.
    This is expensive and produces massive volumes of
    wet gel initially.

    ALTERNATIVE: pre-concentration.

    Most fish processing facilities ALREADY have
    evaporation equipment (for fish meal, oil concentration).
    They can evaporate the hydrolysate liquor from
    50,000L → 10,000L (5:1 concentration ratio,
    standard practice).

    Now 10,000L of CONCENTRATED hydrolysate:
      Solids: ~30-50% instead of 2-6%.
      N concentration: 2,500-10,000 mg/L instead of
                      500-2,000 mg/L.

    Apply P1S-B to the CONCENTRATED product:
      Dry mix needed: 10,000L × $0.03/L = $300/day
      (much lower, because solids are already concentrated).

    Daily revenue from disposal cost avoided:
      10,000L concentrated × $0.04 disposal cost
      = $400/day disposal cost avoided.
      Plus product value (concentrated, high N):
      10,000L × $0.30/L nutrient value = $3,000/day.
      Total daily value: $3,400.

    Less dry mix cost: $300.
    Net: $3,100/day = $1,131,500/year.

  THE KEY INSIGHT:

    For dilute byproducts (whey, brewery waste, etc.):
    Pre-concentrate using existing facility equipment,
    THEN apply P1S-B.

    For concentrated byproducts (blood water):
    Apply P1S-B directly.

  REVISED OPPORTUNITY:

    Fish processing with integrated evaporators:
      Pre-concentrate hydrolysate 5:1 via evaporator
      (facility may already do this).
      Apply P1S-B to concentrate.
      Harvest dry product (ultra-high N, high value).
      Sell as premium fish hydrolysate fertilizer.
      Annual facility value: $1,000,000+.

  FACILITY SPECIFICS:

    US has ~200-300 fish processing facilities of
    significant scale.
    Many already have evaporators for byproduct handling.
    Addressable market: ~100-150 facilities.
    Average value per facility: $800,000-$1,200,000/year.

  PRODUCT CHARACTERISTICS:

    Concentrated fish hydrolysate + P1S-B gel dried:
      NPK: ~3-5% N, 1-2% P, 0.5-1% K.
      Plus: amino acids, trace minerals, bioactive compounds.
      Market: premium organic fertilizer, specialty horticulture,
              aquaculture (fish farmers buy fish hydrolysate
              for water-based nutrient supplementation).
      Price: $15-25/kg retail (premium positioning).
      Comparable: commercial fish hydrolysates trade at
                  $10-15/kg; this product with P1S-B
                  advantages (slow-release, moisture-stable,
                  biosolids benefits) can command premium.

  VERDICT:

    Fish hydrolysate is a HIGH-VALUE opportunity IF
    the facility has existing pre-concentration equipment.
    If not, the ROI is marginal.
    This is a SECONDARY target for facilities that
    already have evaporators.
```

### 1.4 — Brewery Grain Steep Water

```
THE BYPRODUCT:

  Source: Brewery operations (beer production).
  Nature: Water run-off from grain mashing and lautering,
          or spent grain wash water.
  Composition: ~1-3% solids (residual sugars, proteins,
              amino acids, vitamins, minerals).
  Disposal challenge: High BOD (organic matter from
                     grain extraction residues).
  Current management: Often used as animal feed additive
                     or disposed to wastewater treatment.
  Disposal cost: $0.01-0.03/L if treatment required.

FACILITY SCALE:

  Typical brewery: 1,000-50,000 barrels/year.
  = 120,000-6,000,000 liters beer/year.
  Grain steep water production: ~0.1-0.3L per L beer.
  = 12,000-1,800,000L grain water/year.
  = 30-5,000L/day average brewery.

NUTRIENT COMPOSITION:

  Total Nitrogen (N):      0.2-0.6 g/L.
  - Form: amino acids, soluble proteins.

  Phosphorus (P):          50-200 mg/L.
  - From grain.

  Potassium (K):           150-400 mg/L.
  - From grain.

  Sugars:                  0.5-2 g/L.
  - Residual fermentable sugars.

  Organic matter (BOD):    500-2,000 mg/L.

  B-vitamins: Trace amounts.

THE PROFIT OPPORTUNITY:

  Current cost to facility: $0.01/L disposal (if managed
                           as waste) or $0/L if used
                           as animal feed feedstock.
  Daily grain water: 200L/day (medium brewery).

  Scenario A: brewery currently pays to dispose.
    Annual disposal cost: 200L × 365 × $0.02
                        = $1,460/year.
    Tiny compared to brewery operations.

  Scenario B: brewery currently gives away to local farms
              as animal feed additive.
    Annual value "given away": 200L/day × $0.05/L
                              (feed value) = $3,650/year.
    Loss of opportunity.

  Processing on-site with P1S-B:
    Dry mix cost: 200L × $0.08/L = $16/day.
    Annual: $5,840.

    Product value: 200L grain water produces ~2-4kg
    dry product (depending on concentration).
    At $5/kg (commodity organic fertilizer):
    3kg × $5 = $15/day.
    Annual: $5,475.

    Net economics: −$365/year (barely break-even).

  VERDICT:

    Brewery grain water is LOW OPPORTUNITY.
    Volume is small (even large breweries produce
    <5,000L/day).
    Solids concentration is low.
    Product value is commodity (low price).
    Economics are break-even or marginal.

    This is NOT a primary target.
```

### 1.5 — Sugar Processing Wastewater

```
THE BYPRODUCT:

  Source: Sugar beet or sugarcane processing.
  Nature: Defecation liquor (from juice clarification)
          or general process wastewater.
  Composition: ~3-7% solids (residual sugars, organic
              matter, minerals, nutrients).
  Disposal challenge: High organic load, nitrogen,
                     potential discharge restrictions.
  Current disposal cost: $0.02-0.05/L treatment.

FACILITY SCALE:

  Typical sugar beet factory: 1,500-2,500 tonnes beets/day.
  Wastewater volume: 30,000-100,000L/day.
  Seasonal operation: 120-150 days/year (harvest season).

NUTRIENT COMPOSITION:

  Total Nitrogen (N):      0.3-0.8 g/L.
  - Form: amino acids, soluble proteins from
          crop residue.

  Phosphorus (P):          0.1-0.4 g/L.
  - Low relative to N.

  Potassium (K):           0.5-1.5 g/L.
  - From beet tissue.

  Organic matter (BOD):    2,000-5,000 mg/L.

THE PROFIT OPPORTUNITY:

  Current cost to facility: $0.03/L disposal (average).
  Daily wastewater: 50,000L/day (medium facility).
  Seasonal operation: 150 days/year.
  Annual disposal cost: 50,000L × 150 × $0.03
                      = $225,000/year.

  Processing on-site with P1S-B:
    Dry mix cost: 50,000L × $0.08/L = $4,000/day.
    Annual (150 days): $600,000.

    Product value: 50,000L produces ~2,500kg dry product
    (5% solids → 2,500kg harvest).
    At $5/kg: $12,500/day.
    Annual (150 days): $1,875,000.

    Less dry mix cost: $600,000.
    Net: $1,275,000/year.

  EXCELLENT OPPORTUNITY.

  FACILITY SPECIFICS:

    US sugar beet processing: ~11 facilities.
    US sugarcane processing: ~18 facilities (Louisiana).
    = ~29 major sugar facilities.

    BUT: each facility processes 1,500-2,500 tonnes/day
    for 120-150 days/year.
    Annual value per facility: $1-1.5M net.
    Total addressable market: ~$25-40M/year.

  PRODUCT CHARACTERISTICS:

    Sugar processing wastewater → P1S-B harvest:
      NPK: ~2-4% N, 0.2-0.5% P, 1-2% K.
      Plus: residual sugars, minerals.
      Market: organic fertilizer for field crops,
              horticulture.
      Price: $5-8/kg wholesale.
      Comparable: standard compost, $3-5/kg.

  VERDICT:

    Sugar processing is a HIGH-OPPORTUNITY byproduct.
    Large volume, seasonal concentration, existing
    infrastructure, strong disposal cost, high product value.

    However: SEASONAL OPERATION limits annual cash flow.
    Facility operates 150 days/year → $1.3M/year is
    concentrated into 150-day season.
    This requires upfront capital for drying/storage
    infrastructure and working capital for inventory.
```

NUTRIENT COMPOSITION:

          GI tract content).

  Phosphorus (P):          0.1-0.5 g/L.
  - From blood, bone fragments.

  Potassium (K):           0.2-0.6 g/L.
  - From tissue.

  Organic matter (BOD):    2,000-8,000 mg/L.
  - High due to fat, blood, protein content.

  Fats/Lipids:             0.5-2% (from skin, viscera).

THE PROFIT OPPORTUNITY:

  Current cost to facility: $0.03/L disposal (average).
  Daily wastewater: 50,000L/day (medium facility).
  Annual disposal cost: 50,000L × 365 × $0.03
                      = $547,500/year.

  Processing on-site with P1S-B:
    Dry mix cost: 50,000L × $0.10/L = $5,000/day
    (slightly higher than sugar, lower than fish,
     because nitrogen and fat content is moderate).

    Product value: 50,000L × 2% solids = 1,000kg harvest.
    At $6/kg (poultry waste-based product, specialty
    horticulture): $6,000/day.
    Annual: $2,190,000.

    Less dry mix cost: $1,825,000.
    Net: $365,000/year.

  FACILITY SPECIFICS:

    US has ~150-200 significant poultry processing
    facilities.
    Average value per facility: $300,000-$500,000/year.
    Total addressable market: ~$50-75M/year.

  PRODUCT CHARACTERISTICS:

    Poultry processing wastewater → P1S-B harvest:
      NPK: ~2-3% N, 0.3-0.5% P, 0.5-1% K.
      Plus: amino acids, minerals, trace elements.
      Market: organic fertilizer, specialist applications.
      Price: $5-8/kg wholesale.
      Competitors: meat meal products, blood meal.

  VERDICT:

    Poultry processing wastewater is a MODERATE opportunity.
    Volume and facility count are substantial.
    Product value is adequate.
    Net profit per facility: $300K-$500K/year.

    BUT: not as strong as slaughterhouse blood
    (which is more concentrated, higher N value) or
    sugar processing (which has lower dry mix cost
    relative to value).

    This is a SECONDARY opportunity, viable but not
    primary target.

## PART II: COMPARATIVE RANKING OF BYPRODUCTS

### 2.1 — Opportunity Scoring Matrix

```
SCORING CRITERIA:

  A. Concentration (solids % in liquid).
     Score 1-10: higher concentration = higher score.
     (Matters because lower concentration = higher
      SAP/dry mix cost.)

  B. Nutrient density (N + P + K mg/L).
     Score 1-10: higher nutrient = higher score.
     (Matters because higher nutrient = higher
      product value.)

  C. Facility scale (volume/day).
     Score 1-10: larger volume = higher score.
     (Matters because larger volume = more
      absolute profit.)

  D. Disposal cost ($/L facility is paying now).
     Score 1-10: higher current cost = higher score.
     (Matters because cost avoided is profit gained.)

  E. Current market value of product ($/ kg finished).
     Score 1-10: higher price = higher score.
     (Matters because product value is ultimate ROI.)

  F. Facility readiness (how many facilities exist,
                        existing infrastructure).
     Score 1-10: more facilities = higher score.
     (Matters because deployment is easier.)

  G. Seasonality (impact on cash flow).
     Score 1-10: year-round = 10, seasonal = lower.
     (Matters because consistent cash flow is preferred.)

RANKING TABLE:

  Byproduct              A    B    C    D    E    F    G   TOTAL
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Slaughterhouse blood   9    9    8    9    8    9    10   62
  Fish hydrolysate*      6    9    7    7    9    5    10   53
  Sugar processing       7    7    8    7    7    2    4    42
  Poultry processing     5    6    8    7    6    8    10   50
  Cheese whey            4    5    9    6    5    9    10   48
  Brewery grain water    3    4    3    5    4    8    10   37
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  * = assumes pre-concentration via existing evaporators

WINNER: SLAUGHTERHOUSE BLOOD WATER RUNOFF
Score: 62/70 (88.6%)
- Highest nutrient density.
- Highest facility disposal cost being paid.
- Largest facility scale (in terms of daily volume).
- Excellent facility readiness (600+ slaughterhouses).
- Year-round operation (consistent cash flow).
- High product market value.
- No pre-concentration needed (can apply P1S-B directly).
