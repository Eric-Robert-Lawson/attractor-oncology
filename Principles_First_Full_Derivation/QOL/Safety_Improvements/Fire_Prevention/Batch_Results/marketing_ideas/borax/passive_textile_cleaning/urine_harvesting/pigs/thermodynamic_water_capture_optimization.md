## PART I: THE THERMODYNAMICS OF EVAPORATION

### 1.1 — The Energy of Phase Change

```
When water evaporates, it undergoes a
phase change from liquid to gas.

This phase change is ENDOTHERMIC.

It absorbs heat.

THE LATENT HEAT OF VAPORIZATION:

Water absorbing heat to evaporate:
Latent heat of vaporization = 2,257 kJ/kg.

What this means in real terms:

To evaporate 1 kg of water, you need to
supply 2,257 kilojoules of energy.

At 1 liter per kg:

To evaporate 1 liter of water requires
2,257 kJ.

In thermal units:
= 539 kilocalories.
= 2.14 MWh per 1,000 liters.
= 2,140 kWh per 1 million liters.

At a pig facility with 2-5 million liters
of water evaporating per year (from the
drying system):

Annual energy from evaporation:
= 4,280-10,700 MWh.

This is MASSIVE energy.

This energy has to come from SOMEWHERE.

In a solar drying shed:
It comes from the SUN.

The sun is HEATING the water.

The water absorbs that heat and
evaporates.

The COOLING EFFECT of that evaporation
can be used for THERMAL MANAGEMENT.

THE MECHANISM:

When water evaporates in an enclosed or
semi-enclosed space:

(1) The evaporating water absorbs heat
    from the surrounding air.

(2) The air temperature drops.

(3) The air becomes more humid (the
    evaporated water becomes vapor in
    the air).

(4) The combination of cooler air +
    higher humidity creates a
    CONDITIONED MICROCLIMATE.

This is the principle behind:
- Swamp coolers (evaporative cooling).
- Evaporative humidification.
- Natural cooling in desert climates.

THE ENERGY BALANCE:

Solar input (heating the shed): 50-100 kW.
Evaporative cooling effect (from water
leaving the system): 30-50 kW.
Net heating of the shed: 20-50 kW.

This means the shed maintains a LOWER
temperature than it would without
evaporation.

The temperature difference:
Full sun, no evaporation: 45-55°C inside.
Full sun, with evaporation: 35-42°C inside.

A 10-15°C DROP in temperature just from
the evaporation effect.

That cooling is AVAILABLE for use.

Either:
(a) To cool the drying shed itself.
(b) To cool adjacent spaces (like parts
    of the pig facility).
(c) To provide humidified air to the
    facility (conditioning the air
    quality).
```

```
STRATEGY 1: PASSIVE THERMAL BUFFERING
(Drying shed as thermal battery)

Concept:
The drying shed is a large thermal mass.

During the day:
- The shed absorbs solar heat.
- Water evaporates (endothermic process,
  absorbs heat).
- Net effect: shed stays cooler than
  it would otherwise.

At night:
- The shed releases accumulated heat
  (radiative cooling).
- The thermal mass moderates temperature
  swings.

If the drying shed is ADJACENT to or
INTEGRATED with the pig facility:

The thermal moderating effect of the
drying shed reduces the temperature
swing the facility experiences.

Less swing = Less HVAC needed to maintain
set point.

EXAMPLE SCENARIO:

Day temperature cycle without drying shed:
- 6 AM: 18°C.
- 12 PM: 32°C.
- 6 PM: 25°C.
- Midnight: 20°C.

Delta (swing): 14°C.

Facility needs to actively cool during
peak (12 PM - 4 PM) to keep facility at
20°C when ambient is 32°C.

Day temperature cycle with adjacent
drying shed (evaporative cooling reduces
ambient swing):
- 6 AM: 18°C.
- 12 PM: 28°C (reduced due to evaporative
  cooling from shed).
- 6 PM: 24°C.
- Midnight: 20°C.

Delta (swing): 10°C.

Facility needs less aggressive cooling
(maintaining 20°C when ambient is 28°C
is easier than maintaining 20°C when
ambient is 32°C).

HVAC cooling load reduction:
Rough estimate: 10-20% reduction during
drying peak hours.

Energy balance:
- Solar heat absorbed by drying shed:
  50 kW (mid-day).
- Evaporative cooling effect: 30 kW

- Net heating: 20 kW.

If the facility intake receives this
pre-cooled air:

Reduction in HVAC load: ~20-30 kW during
drying peak (mid-day, when cooling is
most needed).

EXAMPLE:

Summer day:
- Ambient: 32°C.
- Facility target: 20°C.
- Normal HVAC cooling load: 50 kW (to
  cool 32°C air to 20°C).

With evaporative pre-cooling:
- Air entering drying shed: 32°C.

STRATEGY 4: THERMAL MASS INTEGRATION
(Drying shed as heat storage)

Concept:
The gel being dried is a THERMAL MASS.

At temperatures above facility target

At temperatures below facility target

- Drying shed cools to 18-20°C.

  facility (raising facility temperature).

- Shed cools to 5-10°C.
- Thermal exchange stops (facility
  loses heat, facility heating system
  engages).

The thermal mass of the drying shed acts
as a thermal buffer, reducing the
temperature swing the facility experiences.

This reduces both cooling (summer) and
heating (winter) loads.

HVAC load reduction:
- Summer cooling: 15-25%.
- Winter heating: 10-20%.
```
