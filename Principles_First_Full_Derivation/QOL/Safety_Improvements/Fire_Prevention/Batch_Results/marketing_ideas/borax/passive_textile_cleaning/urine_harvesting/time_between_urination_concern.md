```

Hours pass before the next event.

Does this waiting period:
  Degrade the borax inhibition?

## PART I: WHAT IS HAPPENING IN THE
## GEL DURING THE WAITING PERIOD

### 1.1 — The Gel State After One Event

```
IMMEDIATELY AFTER URINATION:

  The 250mL stream has contacted
  the dry mix bed.
  SAP has absorbed the liquid.
  The free liquid is gone within
  2–5 minutes of the event ending.
  What remains:
    A semi-solid to solid gel mass.
    SAP polymer network swollen
    with absorbed urine solution.
    Borax dissolved and distributed
    throughout the water phase
    of the gel.
    Baking soda dissolved and
    buffering the pH to 7.5–8.
    Bentonite hydrated and
    distributed through the matrix.
    Borate ions (B(OH)4⁻) present
    throughout at inhibitory concentration.

THE GEL IS NOT AN OPEN LIQUID.
IT IS A MATRIX.
This is the first and most important
fact about the waiting period.

The urine is no longer a liquid
sitting in a bucket exposed to air.
It is immobilised inside a polymer
network. The relevant chemistry
happens inside that network,
not at an open liquid surface.
```

### 1.2 — What Urease Needs to Work

```
UREASE DECOMPOSITION REQUIRES:

  1. Urease enzyme — present in
     environmental bacteria.
  2. Urea substrate — present in gel.
  3. Water — present in gel.
  4. Access — bacteria must reach
     the urea in the water phase.

THE BORAX FACTOR:

  Borate ions are competitive
  inhibitors of urease.
  They bind to the enzyme's
  active site and prevent
  urea from binding.
  The inhibition is:
    Continuous — not time-limited.
    Not consumed by the reaction
    (competitive inhibition —
    the borate ion is not used up).
    Concentration-dependent —
    as long as borate is present
    above MIC, inhibition is active.

  BORATE CONCENTRATION IN GEL
  DURING WAITING PERIOD:

    21g borax distributed in
    ~250mL absorbed urine water.
    Borax concentration: 21/0.250 = 84g/L.
    This is the initial concentration
    at event 1.
    Borate ions: 84/381.4 × 4 × 1000mM
    = 882mM borate.
    MIC: 5mM.
    Multiple of MIC in gel: 176×.

    Even as water slowly evaporates
    between events, the borax
    concentration in the remaining
    water phase INCREASES —
    the borax does not evaporate
    with the water.
    As water leaves, borax becomes
    more concentrated.
    The inhibition does not weaken
    during the waiting period.
    It strengthens.

THE WAITING PERIOD IS NOT A
VULNERABILITY WINDOW.
IT IS A PERIOD DURING WHICH
INHIBITION IS ACTIVE AND CONCENTRATING.
```

### 1.3 — What Happens to the Urea During the Wait

```
THREE FATES FOR UREA IN THE GEL:

  FATE 1 — STAYS AS UREA (dominant):
    Borax inhibition prevents urease
    from converting it.
    Urea is a stable molecule.
    It does not spontaneously decompose
    at room temperature without
    enzymatic catalysis.
    In the absence of active urease:
    urea simply sits in solution.
    It does not react. It does not
    evaporate (non-volatile). It waits.
    It will still be there hours,
    days, or weeks later.
    OUTCOME: Nitrogen retained.

  FATE 2 — ADSORBED BY BENTONITE (minor):
    Some urea adsorbs onto
    bentonite clay surfaces.
    This is actually a positive outcome:
    adsorbed urea is held in the
    clay matrix and released slowly
    when the product is applied to soil.
    This is the slow-release mechanism
    that gives the product its
    agronomic value.
    OUTCOME: Nitrogen retained and
    converted to slow-release form.

  FATE 3 — PARTIAL DECOMPOSITION
  (very minor, at gel edges):
    At the surface of the gel where
    air contacts the gel matrix,
    environmental bacteria may
    carry urease that partially
    overcomes the inhibition at
    the very surface layer.
    This produces trace ammonia
    at the gel surface — this is
    the faint odor that may be
    detectable close to the bucket
    after a long wait.
    The amount is small:
      The gel surface area is small
      relative to total volume.
      The borate concentration is
      very high even at the surface.
      The SAP matrix physically
      restricts bacterial access
      to the liquid phase.
    OUTCOME: Negligible nitrogen loss.
    Trace odor possible but minor.
```

## PART II: THE EVAPORATION FACTOR

### 2.1 — Water Leaving the Gel During the Wait

```
EVAPORATION DURING THE WAIT IS
NOT A PROBLEM. IT IS THE MECHANISM.

The entire system is designed around
evaporation removing the water fraction
and leaving the dissolved solids behind.
Evaporation happening between events
is the same process that happens in
the drying phase — just slower.

WHAT EVAPORATES:
  Water (H₂O) — leaves as vapour.
  Volatile organic compounds —
  trace amounts, suppressed by borax
  and bentonite adsorption.
  Ammonia — suppressed by borax
  inhibition. Trace amounts only
  at gel surface.

WHAT DOES NOT EVAPORATE:
  Urea — non-volatile. Stays.
  Uric acid — non-volatile. Stays.
  Potassium salts — non-volatile. Stays.
  Sodium chloride — non-volatile. Stays.
  Phosphate salts — non-volatile. Stays.
  Creatinine — non-volatile. Stays.
  Borax — non-volatile. Stays and
  concentrates as water leaves.
  Bentonite — solid mineral. Stays.
  SAP polymer — solid. Stays.

EVERY VALUABLE COMPONENT STAYS.
ONLY WATER LEAVES.
EVAPORATION BETWEEN EVENTS IS
DOING USEFUL WORK.

AFTER A LONG WAIT (8–12 hours):

  Some water will have evaporated
  from the gel surface.
  The gel may appear drier or have
  a surface crust.
  This is fine.
  When the next urination event
  arrives, the stream rehydrates
  the surface and penetrates through
  to the remaining SAP capacity
  beneath.
  The system reactivates.
  The new urine adds to the
  accumulated dissolved solids
  from prior events.
  Nothing was lost during the wait.
  The system was simply sitting,
  slowly concentrating.
```

### 2.2 — Overnight Wait — Specific Case

```
SCENARIO: Urine deposited at 10pm.
Next event at 7am. 9-hour gap.

WHAT HAPPENS OVERNIGHT:

  Borax inhibition: fully active
  throughout the 9 hours.
  Urea: stable. Not decomposing.
  Water: slowly evaporating from
  gel surface into room air.
  At room temperature (20°C),
  low indoor humidity (40–60%):
  Evaporation rate from gel surface
  approximately 1–3mm depth per hour
  from exposed surface.
  Standard bucket opening (~0.07m²):
  Overnight water loss: modest.
  Perhaps 10–30mL from the gel
  surface over 9 hours.
  (Gel matrix slows evaporation vs
  open water surface.)

  ODOR OVERNIGHT:
  The borax-inhibited gel produces
  minimal odor.
  A light earthy-mineral smell
  at most from the bentonite.
  The ammonia that would be
  responsible for the characteristic
  urine odor is suppressed at source.
  A loose cover on the bucket
  (not sealed — ventilation needed
  for the water vapour to exit)
  eliminates even this.
  Practical recommendation:
  a board, plate, or mesh cover
  resting on the bucket rim.
  Not airtight. Just a cover.

  MORNING EVENT:
  Stream contacts the partially
  dried gel surface.
  Stream rehydrates surface.
  Penetrates to SAP capacity below.
  Borax distributes into new liquid.
  System continues as designed.
  No reset required.
  No action required.
  Urinate and walk away.

CONCLUSION: 9-HOUR OVERNIGHT WAIT
HAS NO NEGATIVE EFFECT ON HARVEST
QUALITY, NITROGEN RETENTION, ODOR
CONTROL, OR SYSTEM FUNCTION.
```

## PART III: LONG WAIT PERIODS —
## DAYS BETWEEN EVENTS

### 3.1 — What If the Bucket Sits for Several Days

```
SCENARIO: Single-person household.
Bucket charged with dry mix.
Person travels for 3 days.
Bucket sits with first urination
from before departure.
Returns and resumes use.

WHAT HAPPENS OVER 3 DAYS:

  Borax: still present. Still active.
  Not consumed. Not degraded.
  The gel is now drier than before
  as 3 days of ambient evaporation
  have removed water.
  The gel may appear partially
  or fully dried — a consolidated
  mass in the bottom of the bucket.

  NITROGEN STATUS:
  Urea is still present.
  Non-volatile. Stable without urease.
  Borax was concentrating as water
  left — the remaining water is
  MORE inhibitory than the initial
  state.
  Even if urease from environmental
  bacteria reached the outer surface:
  the borate concentration increases
  as water evaporates, making the
  inhibition stronger, not weaker,
  over time.

  ON RETURN:
  The person urinates into what
  may look like a partially dried
  or even nearly dry gel mass.
  New stream rehydrates and the
  cycle continues.
  Nothing was lost.
  In fact: the 3-day partial drying
  is useful — it is partial progress
  toward the final drying stage.

EXTENDED WAIT LIMIT:

  Is there a point where waiting
  too long damages the system?

  The only realistic concern:
  If the bucket is left in conditions
  of high humidity AND warmth
  (above 30°C, >80% humidity)
  for extended periods, mould or
  bacterial growth could establish
  on the gel surface.
  The borax antimicrobial suppresses
  this but is not a sterilant.
  At high temperature and humidity
  over weeks: surface growth is possible.

  PRACTICAL GUIDANCE:
  In normal indoor or outdoor
  shade conditions: no concern
  for waiting periods up to 2 weeks.
  In hot humid environments
  (tropical, summer): keep bucket
  in a cooler location. A loose cover
  helps by reducing humidity at the
  gel surface.

  FOR TYPICAL USE:
  Hours to days between events
  is completely normal and expected.
  The system handles it without
  any adjustment.
  The chemistry is designed for
  exactly this — a slowly accumulating,
  slowly concentrating gel matrix
  that receives events at irregular
  intervals and is harvested when full.
```

## PART IV: DOES WAITING AFFECT
## THE HARVEST PRODUCT?

```
SUMMARY OF WHAT WAITING DOES
AND DOES NOT DO:

WAITING DOES:
  Allow some water to pre-evaporate.
  → Reduces total drying time needed
    in the drying phase.
  → A bucket that has been sitting for
    days is partially pre-dried.
    It reaches harvest-dry state
    faster than a fresh full bucket.
    This is a minor benefit, not a cost.

  Concentrate the borax in remaining
  water phase.
  → Inhibition becomes stronger
    over time as water leaves.
  → Not weaker. Stronger.

  Allow bentonite to continue
  adsorbing the dissolved compounds
  in the water phase.
  → More contact time = more complete
    adsorption = better P and NH4+
    retention in the clay matrix.
  → This improves the slow-release
    character of the harvest product.
    More time = better product.

WAITING DOES NOT:
  Allow ammonia to form.
    (Borax inhibits the enzyme
    that would produce it.)
  Allow nitrogen to escape.
    (Urea is non-volatile.
    It cannot leave as a gas.
    It stays until the product
    is harvested.)
  Damage the SAP polymer.
    (SAP degrades from ionic loading
    over repeated absorb/dry cycles —
    not from sitting wet.
    Sitting wet between events
    does not accelerate SAP degradation.)
  Change the harvest composition.
    (The solids present at event 1
    are still present at event 15
    regardless of how much time
    elapsed between events.)
  Create a pathogen hazard.
    (Borax antimicrobial is active
    throughout. Drying to zero moisture
    at harvest eliminates any residual
    pathogen concern.)
  Require any user action.
    (Walk away. Come back.
    Urinate again.
    The system does not need
    to be managed between events.)

THE HARVEST PRODUCT FROM A BUCKET
WITH LONG INTER-EVENT GAPS IS
IDENTICAL IN COMPOSITION AND QUALITY
TO A BUCKET USED ON A TIGHT SCHEDULE.

TIME BETWEEN EVENTS IS IRRELEVANT
TO PRODUCT QUALITY.
THE CHEMISTRY DOES NOT CARE
ABOUT THE CLOCK.
IT CARES ABOUT THE CONCENTRATION
OF BORATE IONS RELATIVE TO UREASE.
THAT RATIO IS MAINTAINED REGARDLESS
OF ELAPSED TIME.
```

## SUMMARY

```
QUESTION: Does waiting between
urination events affect harvest?

ANSWER: No. Positively confirmed.

REASONS:

  1. Borax inhibition is not
     time-limited. It does not
     expire between events.
     It concentrates as water
     evaporates — getting stronger,
     not weaker, over time.

  2. Urea is non-volatile.
     It cannot escape as a gas.
     It is stable without urease.
     It will be present in the
     harvest regardless of elapsed
     time between events.

     exposed to uncontrolled

     distributed throughout.

  4. Partial evaporation between
     events is useful — it pre-dries
     the gel, reduces final drying
     time, and concentrates the
     dissolved solids.

  5. Bentonite adsorption continues
     during the wait — longer contact
     time slightly improves nutrient
     retention in the clay matrix.

  6. No user action is required
     between events. The system
     is passive and self-managing
     between urination events.

OPERATIONAL IMPLICATION:
  The system is designed for
  exactly the usage pattern of
  a normal human being —
  irregular events, long gaps
  overnight and during work hours,
  variable event volumes.
  None of this variability affects
  the outcome.
  The chemistry handles all of it.
