## 1.1 — The Four Active Mechanisms in P1S Gel

```
MECHANISM 1 — SUSTAINED OCCLUSION:
  The SAP polymer matrix and bentonite clay particles
  form a conformal film on any surface they contact.
  This film:
  - Prevents transepidermal water loss (TEWL) from skin
  - Prevents moisture evaporation from hair cortex
  - Prevents dehydration of nail plate during treatment
  - Creates a sealed microenvironment at the skin-gel interface
  
  Comparative: Vaseline (petrolatum) is the gold standard
  occlusive in dermatology — rated 98% TEWL reduction.
  P1S provides meaningful occlusion without petrolatum's
  greasy, non-breathable, non-rinsable properties.
  P1S rinses cleanly with water.

MECHANISM 2 — ACTIVE MOISTURE RESERVOIR:
  The SAP polymer network holds water in a state of
  reduced vapor pressure — releasing it slowly and
  continuously to the adjacent surface throughout
  the treatment period.
  
  CRITICAL DISTINCTION FROM CONDITIONER/CREAM:
    Standard moisturizer: deposits a moisture-trapping film,
    relies on skin's own water, depletes quickly.
    P1S gel: actively DELIVERS water to the surface from
    the gel reservoir for the entire contact period.
    The skin is simultaneously not losing its own water
    AND receiving additional water from the gel.
    Two-directional moisture benefit.

MECHANISM 3 — CLAY MINERAL DELIVERY:
  Bentonite clay dispersed throughout the gel delivers:
  - Calcium ions (keratin interaction in hair and nails)
  - Magnesium ions (skin barrier enzyme cofactor)
  - Iron (trace mineral in skin metabolism)
  - Potassium (cellular hydration regulation)
  - Adsorption of surface toxins/excess sebum via
    negative charge attracting positively charged debris
  
  The SAP matrix keeps the clay in sustained contact
  with the surface — unlike clay masks that dry rapidly
  and pull away. P1S keeps clay in contact while wet.

MECHANISM 4 — SILICA SURFACE INTERACTION:
  Hydrophilic fumed silica at 2.2% in the gel provides:
  - Surface smoothing (silica particles fill micro-gaps
    in skin texture — the "blurring" effect used in primers)
  - Sebum absorption (silica adsorbs surface oils)
  - Texture modulation (silica gives the gel a silky,
    non-sticky skin feel on application)
  - Pore appearance minimization after treatment
  - Potential mild physical exfoliation at the
    nano-particle surface interaction level
    (too gentle to cause irritation, sufficient to
    interact with stratum corneum surface cells)
```
