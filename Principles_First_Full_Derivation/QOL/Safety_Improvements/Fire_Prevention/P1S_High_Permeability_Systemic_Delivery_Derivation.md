## The Pharmacology of Rectal Administration

```
RECTAL DRUG DELIVERY IS ESTABLISHED MEDICINE.
This is not a fringe concept.

Rectal administration is used clinically for:
  Diazepam (seizure emergency — rectal gel)
  Acetaminophen (suppositories — standard pediatric care)
  Aspirin (suppositories)
  Mesalamine (rectal foam/enema for ulcerative colitis)
  Corticosteroids (rectal foam for IBD)
  Ondansetron (anti-emetic suppository)
  Misoprostol (various obstetric indications)
  Cannabis (rectal suppositories — growing clinical use)
  
The route is extensively characterized pharmacologically.

THE VASCULAR ANATOMY — THE CRITICAL VARIABLE:

  THE RECTUM HAS TWO DRAINAGE PATHWAYS:

  PATHWAY 1 — LOWER RECTUM (below peritoneal reflection):
    Inferior hemorrhoidal veins →
    Internal pudendal vein →
    Internal iliac vein →
    Inferior vena cava →
    SYSTEMIC CIRCULATION
    NO FIRST-PASS HEPATIC METABOLISM
    
  PATHWAY 2 — UPPER RECTUM (above peritoneal reflection):
    Superior hemorrhoidal vein →
    Inferior mesenteric vein →
    Portal vein →
    LIVER → first-pass metabolism →
    Systemic circulation
    SAME AS ORAL ROUTE — FIRST PASS APPLIES

  THE PRACTICAL CONSEQUENCE:

  Retention in the LOWER RECTUM (approximately 0-15cm
  from anal verge, below the peritoneal reflection):
  → Systemic delivery WITHOUT first-pass metabolism
  → Bioavailability can be HIGH (50-75%+ for suitable agents)
  → Drug concentration in systemic circulation faster
     onset than oral (no GI transit wait)
  
  Retention HIGHER in rectum:
  → Upper drainage pathway → portal → liver
  → First-pass metabolism applies
  → Behaves more like oral delivery
  
  A LOW-VOLUME ENEMA RETAINED IN THE LOWER RECTUM
  ACHIEVES NEAR-INJECTION-EQUIVALENT SYSTEMIC DELIVERY
  FOR SUITABLE WATER-SOLUBLE AGENTS — WITHOUT INJECTION.
  
  This is not speculative.
  This is established clinical pharmacology.
  It is the mechanism used in palliative care when
  patients cannot swallow or tolerate IV access.
  The rectal route IS the backup systemic delivery
  route in clinical medicine.

RECTAL MUCOSAL PROPERTIES:

  The rectal mucosa is a MUCOSAL MEMBRANE —
  fundamentally different from skin.
  
  Skin: lipid barrier, stratum corneum, designed as barrier
  Rectal mucosa: single cell layer epithelium, designed
  for absorption (water and electrolytes from stool),
  extremely high blood flow, no lipid barrier of significance
  
  WATER-SOLUBLE AGENT ABSORPTION AT RECTAL MUCOSA:
  The rectal mucosa does NOT have the lipid matrix
  barrier that makes skin difficult for water-soluble agents.
  
  Water-soluble molecules face minimal barrier at rectal
  mucosa compared to skin.
  The LogP limitations that apply to skin DO NOT apply
  in the same way to rectal mucosal absorption.
  
  Small water-soluble molecules cross rectal mucosa
  readily — the same molecules that struggle through
  the skin lipid matrix cross mucosal epithelium
  relatively easily.
  
  THIS RESOLVES THE PRIMARY LIMITATION OF THE SKIN ROUTE.
  
  The rectal route is the appropriate route for the
  agents that were classified as "MARGINAL" or "POOR"
  transdermal crossers in the prior document — including
  B12, folate, and larger water-soluble molecules.
```
