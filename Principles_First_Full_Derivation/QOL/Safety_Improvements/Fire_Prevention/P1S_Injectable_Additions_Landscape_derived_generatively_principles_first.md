## THE WATER SOLUBILITY BOUNDARY — STATED PRECISELY

```
WHAT "WATER SOLUBLE" MEANS AS A DELIVERY CRITERION:

  An agent is usable in P1S if it dissolves, disperses,
  or suspends in the aqueous phase of the gel without:
  a. Collapsing the SAP network (ionic strength limit)
  b. Separating into a visible oil phase
  c. Denaturing at the temperature of gel preparation
  d. Chemically reacting with SAP, silica, or bentonite

THE CONTRAST WITH FAT/OIL SOLUBLE DELIVERY:

  Fat-soluble agents (retinol, vitamin D3, vitamin K2, CoQ10,
  omega-3 fatty acids, most steroids, most cannabinoids):
  CANNOT enter the P1S water phase directly.
  They require emulsification, encapsulation, or cyclodextrin
  complexation to become water-dispersible.
  These are NOT primary candidates for P1S.
  Their oil-soluble analogs or encapsulated forms may be.

  Water-soluble agents (most vitamins B and C, amino acids,
  peptides, proteins, polysaccharides, most ionic drugs,
  most mineral salts within ionic limits, most alkaloids,
  most organic acids, most enzymes):
  CAN enter the P1S water phase directly.
  These ARE the candidates. This document maps them.

THE IONIC STRENGTH CEILING (restated as precision boundary):
  SAP begins to collapse at approximately 0.5% NaCl equivalent.
  Divalent ion salts (Ca2+, Mg2+, Fe3+) collapse at lower levels.
  Organic molecules regardless of molecular weight:
  low ionic contribution — compatible up to physically meaningful
  concentrations.
  This makes ORGANIC water-soluble agents the primary class.
  Inorganic ionic agents require care and low concentration.
```

## The Geometric Significance of Small Molecule + Occlusion

```
Lipinski's Rule of Five describes ideal oral drug absorption.
For topical delivery, the analogous rule is simpler:
  MW < 500 Da + LogP 1-3 = transdermal penetration candidate.

Water-soluble small molecules in this range are unique:
  They dissolve in water (P1S compatible).
  They are small enough to diffuse through skin barrier.
  The occlusion from P1S gel hydrates the stratum corneum,
  expanding the aqueous channels in the skin barrier.
  
  OCCLUSION EFFECT ON BARRIER PERMEABILITY:
    Hydrated stratum corneum: aqueous channel diameter increases
    Water-soluble small molecules pass through aqueous channels
    Occlusion → more hydration → wider channels → more transport
    
  The combination of water-soluble small molecule +
  P1S occlusion = enhanced transdermal delivery of that
  molecule compared to unaided topical application.
  
  This is not a claim. This is physical chemistry of
  aqueous transport through a hydrated membrane.
  The mechanism is textbook dermatopharmacology.
```

# PART V: GASES AND DISSOLVED GAS AGENTS
## The Most Novel Addition Class in This Document

---

```
THIS CLASS HAS NOT BEEN PREVIOUSLY DERIVED FOR P1S.
It emerges here from geometric reasoning about what the
water phase of P1S can carry that other delivery formats
cannot retain.

THE GEOMETRIC INSIGHT:
Gases dissolve in water. Henry's Law: dissolved gas
concentration = (solubility constant) × (partial pressure).
Cooler water holds more dissolved gas.
P1S gel is aqueous. It holds dissolved gases.
As the gel warms on skin contact: dissolved gas slowly
evolves from solution at the gel-skin interface.
The gel surface is not a barrier to gas evolution —
it occurs throughout the gel and at the skin contact surface.

This is a PASSIVE DELIVERY mechanism for gaseous agents.
No pumping. No pressurization.
Cool gel applied to skin → warms → gas evolves at skin surface.

AGENT: CARBON DIOXIDE (CO2)

  How to achieve CO2 saturation in P1S:
  Prepare P1S gel water phase under mild CO2 pressure
  (carbonation). The acidic CO2 lowers pH — compensate
  with slightly more bentonite or add sodium bicarbonate
  buffer to maintain near-neutral pH in the gel.
  
  OR: Add sodium bicarbonate + citric acid to P1S.
  The acid-base reaction generates CO2 IN SITU as the
  gel is applied to skin and warms up.
  Fizzing gel.
  
  What CO2 at skin surface does:
  CO2 dissolves in skin surface moisture and is absorbed
  through the skin (CO2 penetrates far more readily than
  water). It causes local vasodilation (known as the Bohr
  effect) — hemoglobin releases oxygen preferentially
  in the presence of CO2.
  
  Increased local skin oxygenation from CO2 vasodilation.
  This is the mechanism of CO2-based "carboxytherapy"
  injections and CO2 gel treatments used in some medical
  spas in Europe and Asia.
  
  CO2 gel treatments (German/Japanese CO2 pack technology):
  These exist as medical devices and professional treatments.
  A CO2-releasing P1S gel would democratize this mechanism
  into a home treatment format.
  
  CARBONATION APPROACH (simpler):
  Mix P1S base in carbonated water (soda water) instead of
  distilled water. The dissolved CO2 is trapped in the SAP
  matrix during gelation. On skin contact and warming,
  CO2 slowly evolves at the skin interface.
  
  This is trivially testable: make P1S with sparkling water.
  The gel retains carbonation better than liquid water
  because the SAP matrix slows gas diffusion.
  
  P1S + CO2 (carbonated water base):
  "Sparkling mineral mask" — simultaneously delivers:
  CO2 vasodilation at skin surface
  Clay mineral adsorption
  SAP moisture
  Silica smoothing
  
  The tingling from CO2 evolution is a strong sensory
  signal — consumers experience it as "active."
  Same principle as CO2 facial in professional setting.
  Home format.

AGENT: DISSOLVED OXYGEN (O2)

  Oxygen-enriched water: prepared by dissolving O2 under
  pressure in the water phase before adding SAP.
  
  STABILITY CONCERN: O2 in water is less stable than CO2.
  Oxidizes other components (including SAP over time).
  Not a long shelf-life formulation.
  
  SINGLE-USE FORMAT: O2-enriched water prepared immediately
  before gel preparation (or using sachet format where
  O2-releasing compound is activated at time of use).
  
  O2-releasing compounds (hydrogen peroxide at very low level,
  or sodium percarbonate — these are oxidizing agents that
  must be at trace level to avoid SAP degradation):
  
  FRAGILE CHEMISTRY. Not recommended as primary path.
  CO2 approach is more structurally robust.

AGENT: NITRIC OXIDE (NO) — via NO-releasing precursors

  NO is a potent vasodilator. It is the mechanism of
  nitroglycerin patches (glyceryl trinitrate releases NO
  transdermally — one of the most effective transdermal
  drug delivery systems in medicine).
  
  Water-soluble NO precursors:
  - L-arginine (amino acid, NO synthase substrate)
    P1S + L-arginine: provides NO precursor amino acid
    to skin. Skin contains NOS enzymes that convert
    arginine to NO locally.
    INDIRECT NO delivery. Not direct gas delivery.
  
  - Sodium nitrite (NaNO2) at trace level: controversial,
    some skin applications in research.
    IONIC — must stay below SAP tolerance.
    Very low concentration only.
  
  THE L-ARGININE PATH:
  L-arginine is an amino acid — water-soluble, non-ionic,
  compatible with P1S at cosmetic concentrations.
  
  P1S + 1-3% L-arginine:
  L-arginine is delivered to skin surface.
  Skin NOS (nitric oxide synthase) converts arginine to NO.
  NO vasodilates local capillaries.
  Enhanced local blood flow during treatment.
  
  WHAT THIS MEANS PRACTICALLY:
  More oxygen and nutrient delivery to skin cells during
  the treatment period (increased perfusion).
  Skin appears more flushed and "alive" after treatment.
  
  This is a genuine mechanism with a significant cosmetic effect.
  The visible flush from NOS activation after arginine contact
  is a strong visual feedback signal to the consumer.
  "Glow" treatments in high-end facial services often
  use NO-related mechanisms.
  
  P1S + L-arginine: home "glow" treatment.
  Mechanism: conformal amino acid delivery → local NO synthesis
  → vasodilation → enhanced perfusion → visible glow.
  
  STRUCTURAL CERTAINTY: High.
  L-arginine is an established cosmetic ingredient.
  NOS activity in skin is documented.
  The delivery advantage of P1S (sustained arginine surface
  concentration) vs standard serum is clear.
```
