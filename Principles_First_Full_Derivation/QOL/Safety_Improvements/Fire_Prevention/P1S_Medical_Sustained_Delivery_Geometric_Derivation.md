# PART V: THE ZERO-ORDER RELEASE ARCHITECTURE
## The Formal Derivation of Why This Is Structurally Different

---

```
THE MATHEMATICAL STRUCTURE:

FIRST-ORDER RELEASE (standard topical):
  dC/dt = -k × C(t)
  
  Where:
    C(t) = concentration at skin surface at time t
    k = first-order rate constant (related to absorption,
        evaporation, migration)
  
  Solution: C(t) = C₀ × e^(-kt)
  
  This is exponential decay. Concentration drops fastest
  when concentration is highest (right after application).
  This is the opposite of what is clinically desired.
  
  Time above MEC: limited to early period after application.
  Total therapeutic exposure: integral of C(t) above MEC.
  This integral is small relative to total treatment time.

ZERO-ORDER RELEASE (P1S approximate):
  dC/dt = -k₀ (constant)
  
  Where:
    k₀ = rate constant determined by:
         gel concentration, thickness, temperature, occlusion
  
  Solution: C(t) = C₀ - k₀ × t
  
  This is linear decline. Concentration falls at constant rate.
  The full concentration is maintained far longer.
  
  Time above MEC: extends to time = (C₀ - MEC) / k₀
  This can be engineered to equal the desired treatment duration
  by adjusting C₀ (initial concentration) and gel thickness.
  
  Total therapeutic exposure: dramatically larger integral
  than first-order format for same dose applied.

WHAT THIS MEANS IN PRACTICE:
  The same amount of drug applied in P1S format delivers
  more therapeutic effect than the same drug in cream format.
  
  OR equivalently:
  A smaller dose in P1S format delivers equivalent
  therapeutic effect to a larger dose in cream format.
  
  DOSE EFFICIENCY = f(delivery format)
  
  For agents with significant systemic side effect risk
  from topical absorption (corticosteroids, for example):
  ACHIEVING THE SAME LOCAL EFFECT WITH LOWER DOSE
  IS A GENUINE SAFETY IMPROVEMENT.
  
  Lower total dose applied → lower systemic absorption
  → reduced systemic side effects
  while maintaining local therapeutic concentration.
  
  This is the safety case for P1S delivery of potent
  topical drugs like corticosteroids and calcineurin inhibitors.
  The safety improvement is mechanistically derivable,
  not speculative.

THE ENGINEERABLE VARIABLES:
  The release profile can be tuned:
  
  FASTER RELEASE (higher k₀):
  - Thin gel layer
  - Higher temperature (occlusive wrap)
  - Lower viscosity (more dilute gel)
  
  SLOWER RELEASE (lower k₀):
  - Thicker gel layer
  - Lower temperature (uncovered gel, cooler ambient)
  - Higher SAP concentration (tighter polymer network)
  - Higher agent loading (steeper gradient maintained longer)
  
  This tunability means P1S is not a single delivery
  device with fixed parameters. It is an adjustable
  delivery platform where the clinician can engineer
  the release profile to match the therapeutic requirement.
  
  No transdermal patch offers this adjustment in the
  field. Patches are manufactured to a fixed release rate.
  P1S is formulated to the desired release rate at
  point of care.
```
