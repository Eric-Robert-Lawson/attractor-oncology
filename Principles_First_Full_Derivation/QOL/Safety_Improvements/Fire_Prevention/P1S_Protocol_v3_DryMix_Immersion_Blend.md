# P1S Synthesis Protocol — Version 3
## Dry Mix → Immersion Blend Method
## Supersedes: Staged Compound Mixing Protocol (v1, v2)
## Applies To: Full P1S (SAP + Silica + Bentonite) and No-Silica Variant
## Date: 2026-05-12
## Author: Eric Robert Lawson / Copilot Synthesis Agent

---

## WHY THIS PROTOCOL SUPERSEDES THE STAGED METHOD

The staged compound mixing protocol (v1 and v2) required sequential
preparation of three separate component dispersions with independent
rest periods, timed parallel preparation windows, and a compounding
sequence totalling 2–3 hours of active management.

The primary failure mode of that protocol in practice was the
**fish-eye problem** in SAP hydration — gel shells forming around
dry SAP granule interiors before water could penetrate, producing
hard lumps that could not be fully broken up after formation.

The immersion blending method resolves the fish-eye problem
mechanically and completely:

```
FISH-EYE ROOT CAUSE:
  Water contacts SAP powder surface → gel shell forms instantly
  → dry interior trapped → lump cannot dissolve

IMMERSION BLENDER SOLUTION:
  High shear continuously ruptures forming gel shells
  faster than they can establish → powder disperses
  into water uniformly before any lump can set
  → no fish-eyes possible at sufficient shear rate

CONFIRMED BY BATCH OBSERVATION (2026-05-12):
  Staged hand-mixing batch:    significant fish-eyes, clumping,
                                gel falling off skin on application
  Immersion blender batch:     significant improvement, clumping
                                issue resolved
```

The dry mix → immersion blend protocol also reduces total active
session time from 2–3 hours to approximately 70–80 minutes including
the post-blend rest period.

This document covers two variants:

```
VARIANT A — NO-SILICA (SAP + Bentonite only):
  For: Beauty applications, skin testing, hair, nails,
       any application where fumed silica is unavailable
       or intentionally excluded (eye area, intimate area)
  Safety: Standard — no respirator required

  For: Fire protection, full beauty formula, forming medium,
```

Both variants use the same dry mix → blend → rest protocol.
The only differences are the ingredients included in the dry mix
and the safety requirements during powder handling.

## 1.3 — Water Quality

```
Distilled water:        REQUIRED. Not optional.

WHY TAP WATER FAILS:
  Calcium and magnesium ions in tap water:
  1. Compete with sodium ions in SAP — compress swelling capacity
     by ion-exchange. SAP absorbs less water. Gel is weaker.
  2. Intercalate into bentonite clay layers — reduce swelling
     by 20–40%. Thixotropy is degraded.
  3. These effects are NOT recoverable by any mixing adjustment.
     Wrong water = permanently degraded batch regardless of technique.

  Hard well water produces 85–92% quality.
  Standard tap water produces 90–95% quality.
  Distilled water produces 100% quality.

  At 200mL batch scale the difference is small but present.
  At 1750mL batch scale for fire protection testing the difference
  is measurable in fire protection ratio.
  Always use distilled water.
```

## 2.2 — Variant B (Full P1S) Safety — NON-NEGOTIABLE

```
⚠ FUMED SILICA INHALATION HAZARD ⚠

Fumed silica particles are 7–40 nanometers in diameter.
They are invisible to the naked eye.
They remain airborne for minutes when disturbed.
Inhalation causes silicosis — permanent, irreversible lung
scarring. There is no treatment. There is no reversal.
Prevention is the only option.

N95 RESPIRATOR RULES:
  PUT ON before opening the fumed silica container.
  KEEP ON until all silica powder is in water and
  the container is sealed.
  This means: weigh with N95 on, add to dry mix with N95 on,
  add dry mix to water (silica contacts water) — then N95 off.
  Once silica is fully wetted it cannot become airborne.

THE IMMERSION BLENDER VORTEX CREATES A SPECIFIC RISK:
  A spinning vortex pulls air downward into the liquid
  and throws it outward at the top of the vessel.
  Adding dry silica powder near a running blender means
  some powder may be caught in upward air currents
  before it contacts water.

  THEREFORE:
  Add dry powder SLOWLY at the LIQUID SURFACE EDGE —
  not into the center of the vortex where air moves fastest.
  The powder is drawn into the water at the edge and pulled
  down into the vortex immediately.
  Keep your face back from the vessel during powder addition.
  N95 eliminates the inhalation risk regardless.

FIRE TEST SAFETY:
  All torch tests outdoors or fully ventilated space.
  Non-combustible surface under test materials.
  Water bucket within arm's reach before torch is lit.
```

# PART III: DRY MIX PREPARATION

## 3.1 — Why Dry Mix First

The dry mix step pre-distributes all three components relative
to each other before any contact with water. When this pre-mixed
powder hits the water simultaneously under high shear, all three
components begin hydrating in the same environment at the same
moment — rather than the SAP encountering water alone and gelling
before the other components can distribute.

```
STAGED METHOD: SAP contacts water → SAP gels → bentonite and
               silica must distribute into already-forming network
               → fish-eyes possible at every addition step

DRY MIX METHOD: SAP, bentonite, silica already distributed
                among each other → all contact water simultaneously
                → blender shear prevents any one component from
                gelling ahead of the others
                → uniform hydration across all components
```

## 3.2 — Dry Mix Procedure

**Step 3-1.** Place the small dry mixing bowl on the scale. Tare to zero.

**Step 3-2 (Variant B only — N95 ON before this step):**
Weigh fumed silica first, into the dry bowl:

```
Variant B: Add 40g fumed silica to the dry bowl.
           Seal the silica container immediately.
           The silica goes in first so it is at the bottom —
           the heavier bentonite and SAP on top hold it down
           during the brief stirring step, reducing airborne risk.
```

**Step 3-3.** Add bentonite to the dry bowl on top of the silica
(or directly if Variant A):

```
Variant A small batch:   3g bentonite
Variant A standard:     30g bentonite
Variant B standard:     30g bentonite (on top of silica)
```

**Step 3-4.** Add SAP powder on top of the bentonite:

```
Variant A small batch:   1g SAP
Variant A standard:     10g SAP
Variant B standard:     10g SAP
```

The layering order (silica bottom, bentonite middle, SAP top)
is intentional:
- Silica is lightest and most airborne-prone — kept at bottom
- Bentonite acts as a physical barrier holding silica down
- SAP on top is visible and easy to verify by weight

**Step 3-5.** Using a dry teaspoon, stir the dry blend gently for
**60–90 seconds**. The goal is uniform distribution of all components
through the blend. Stir slowly and deliberately — do not create a
dust cloud by stirring too vigorously.

```
WHAT UNIFORM DRY MIX LOOKS LIKE:
  Variant A: uniform grey-tan powder, no white or cream
             zones of pure SAP visible
  Variant B: uniform light grey powder, no bright white
             zones of pure silica visible, no dark zones
             of pure bentonite visible
```

**Step 3-6.** Set the dry mix bowl aside. Do not cover with water yet.
Prepare the water vessel first (Step 4-1 below).

**Step 3-7 (Variant B only):**
N95 may be removed after the dry mix is confirmed uniform and the
silica container is sealed — the powder is still dry and airborne
at this point but the mixing is complete. Keep N95 on until the
dry mix is added to water in Step 4.

## 4.1 — pH Adjustment (Optional — for Sensitive Applications)

For fire protection and standard body/hair applications: skip to 4.2.

# PART V: IMMERSION BLEND PROTOCOL

This is the core of the new method. Read completely before starting.

## 5.1 — Blender Setup

**Step 5-1.** Insert immersion blender into the water vessel.
Position the blender head at the bottom third of the water column —
not at the surface, not resting on the vessel bottom.

**Step 5-2.** Start the blender at medium speed first, then increase
to high speed. A clear vortex should form — a depression in the
water surface above the blender head with a spinning water column.

```
CORRECT VORTEX:
  Clear central depression reaching at least 1/3 down
  the water column from the surface.
  Spinning water visible throughout the vessel.
  No splashing over the vessel edge.

IF NO VORTEX FORMS:
  Vessel is too wide — use a narrower vessel.
  Blender speed is too low — increase to high.
  Water volume is too low for the vessel width — add more water
  or use a smaller vessel.

IF EXCESSIVE SPLASHING:
  Reduce blender speed slightly.
  Tilt vessel at a 10–15° angle — this deepens the vortex
  without increasing speed.
```

## 5.2 — Powder Addition

**Step 5-3 (Variant B only — N95 ON before this step):**
The dry mix contains fumed silica. N95 must be on before
the dry mix container is opened for addition.

**Step 5-4.** With the blender running at high speed, begin adding
the dry mix to the water. Use a teaspoon to add the powder
**slowly at the liquid surface edge** — at the outer ring of the
vortex, not into the center depression.

```
ADDITION TECHNIQUE:
  Hold the dry mix bowl over the vessel.
  Tilt and tap the bowl gently to allow powder to slide
  off the bowl edge in a slow, thin stream at the liquid surface.
  Alternatively: use a teaspoon to spoon powder
  in 3–5 small portions at the vessel edge.

  The powder should be pulled INTO the water within
  2–3 seconds of touching the liquid surface edge.
  If powder sits on the surface for more than 5 seconds:
  increase blender speed or move addition point closer
  to the active vortex edge.

DO NOT:
  Pour powder into the center of the vortex depression
  (the air column in the center carries powder upward,
  not into the water — especially critical for silica)
  Add all powder at once
  Stop the blender during addition
```

**Step 5-5.** Add all dry mix over approximately **60–90 seconds**
of continuous slow addition. Distribute the additions around
the vessel edge — do not add all from one point.

**Step 5-6 (Variant B):** Once all powder is visibly in the water
and no dry powder remains in the addition bowl, the silica is
fully wetted. N95 may now be removed. Seal the blending vessel
with your free hand if possible while continuing to blend
(to prevent any residual aerosol).

## 5.3 — Extended Blending

**Step 5-7.** After all powder is added, continue blending at
high speed for **8–10 minutes continuous**.

```
WHAT IS HAPPENING DURING THE 8–10 MINUTE BLEND:

  Minutes 0–2:  Powder fully wetting. Visible clumps still present.
                Suspension heterogeneous. Do not stop.

  Minutes 2–4:  Fish-eye shells being ruptured continuously.
                SAP granule interiors accessing water through
                broken shells. Suspension becoming more uniform.
                Color transitioning from streaky to more uniform.

  Minutes 4–7:  SAP hydrating more uniformly. Bentonite platelets
                separating and distributing. Silica (if present)
                distributing into forming SAP-bentonite matrix.
                Suspension visibly thickening.

  Minutes 7–10: Network approaching equilibrium under shear.
                Consistency becoming uniform. Blender resistance
                increasing slightly as gel thickens.
                Final dispersion quality being established.

DO NOT STOP EARLY.
The 8–10 minute blend replaces the three separate rest periods
of the staged protocol. Stopping at 5 minutes is equivalent
to the old protocol's incomplete rest periods.
```

**Step 5-8.** Monitor vessel temperature during extended blending.
The blender motor transfers heat to the liquid through friction.
At small batch sizes (200mL) with extended blending, the gel
may warm slightly. This is acceptable — the blender heat does
not damage the components at normal operating temperatures.
At large batch sizes (1750mL) this is not a concern.

**Step 5-9.** During blending, check for these indicators every
2 minutes:

```
PROGRESS INDICATORS — WHAT TO WATCH FOR:

  GOOD SIGNS:
  □ Suspension becoming uniformly opaque rather than streaky
  □ Gel visibly thickening — blender requires more motor effort
  □ No visible dry powder clumps floating or sitting on surface
  □ Color becoming uniform throughout

  CONCERN SIGNS — RESPOND AS NOTED:
  □ Large lumps still present at 5 minutes:
    Move blender head to contact lumps directly.
    Press blender against vessel side to break lumps
    against the vessel wall. Continue blending.
  □ Powder floating on surface and not being pulled in:
    Reduce speed briefly, use spoon to push floating
    powder into the water, resume high speed.
  □ Gel so thick blender is stalling (at standard batch sizes):
    Add 50mL additional distilled water. Continue blending.
    Record water addition for batch record.
```

## 5.4 — Post-Blend Inspection

**Step 5-10.** After 8–10 minutes of blending, stop the blender.
Remove it from the vessel. Inspect the gel immediately:

```
EXPECTED APPEARANCE IMMEDIATELY AFTER BLENDING:

  Variant A (no silica):
    Color:       Light grey-tan or cream. Slightly translucent.
    Consistency: Light to medium gel. Thinner than expected —
                 thickening continues during rest.
    Uniformity:  No visible streaks or lumps. May look slightly
                 bubbly from blender aeration — this settles.

  Variant B (full P1S):
    Color:       White to milky grey-white. More opaque.
    Consistency: Medium gel, thickening. More substantial than
                 Variant A due to silica contribution.
    Uniformity:  Uniform white opacity throughout. No clear zones.
                 No visible lumps. May be slightly bubbly.

CONCERN — IF LUMPS STILL VISIBLE AFTER 10 MIN BLEND:
  The SAP granules were too large or added too fast.
  Continue blending for 3 more minutes targeting the lumps.
  If lumps persist: push gel through a coarse strainer,
  discard trapped lumps, record observation for next batch
  (add powder more slowly, or use finer SAP grade).
```

# PART VI: POST-BLEND REST

## 6.1 — Why the Rest Period is Still Required

The immersion blender creates uniform dispersion under shear —
but the final network structure of the gel requires undisturbed
time to establish. Under continuous shear, the SAP polymer
network and the bentonite thixotropic network are continuously
being disrupted as fast as they form.

When the blender stops, the networks establish simultaneously
and undisturbed. The rest period is when the gel goes from
a uniform dispersion to a unified composite gel with full
thixotropic, cohesive, and adhesive properties.

```
WITHOUT THE REST:
  Gel is uniform but has lower viscosity, reduced thixotropy,
  and weaker surface adhesion. It spreads easily but does
  not hold position on vertical surfaces or skin.

WITH THE 1-HOUR REST:
  Full thixotropic network established. Bentonite edge-face
  platelet connections complete. SAP network fully relaxed
  into thermodynamic equilibrium. Gel at maximum performance.

THE REST PERIOD CANNOT BE SHORTENED.
It is not dead time — it is the time during which the gel
becomes itself rather than just a mixture of components.
```

## 6.2 — Rest Procedure

**Step 6-1.** Cover the vessel immediately after blending stops.
Use plastic wrap pressed directly onto the gel surface
(not just over the top of the vessel) — this prevents surface
drying and minimizes air exposure.

**Step 6-2.** Set timer for **60 minutes**. Do not stir. Do not
disturb. Do not move the vessel.

**Step 6-3.** The gel will visibly thicken during the rest period.
The aeration bubbles from blending will rise and dissipate.
The color may deepen very slightly as the network tightens.

**Step 6-4.** After 60 minutes, perform the quality assessment
checks in Part VII before use or storage.

# PART VII: QUALITY ASSESSMENT

## 7.1 — Visual Assessment

```
LIFT TEST:
  Dip a spoon into the gel and lift slowly.
  The gel should coat the spoon and:
  - Hold on the spoon for 3–5 seconds before sliding
  - Leave a visible coating on the spoon surface
  - Not pour off immediately like water
  
  Variant A: holds 2–4 seconds (lighter gel, expected)
  Variant B: holds 4–8 seconds (silica adds body)

CHANNEL TEST:
  Draw a spoon through the gel surface, leaving a channel.
  Time how long before the channel closes.
  
  Variant A: 2–4 seconds (correct for light gel)
  Variant B: 3–6 seconds (correct for full P1S)
  
  Closes in <1 second: too thin — see troubleshooting
  Does not close at all: too thick — see troubleshooting
```

## 7.2 — Thixotropy Check

```
PROCEDURE:
  Stir the gel vigorously for 30 seconds.
  Remove spoon. Set timer. Watch the surface.

PASS:
  Within 2 minutes, the surface stills and the gel
  firms up noticeably. It offers more resistance to
  prodding than water. The channel you made during
  stirring holds its shape as the gel re-gels.

FAIL (remains liquid, no recovery):
  Variant A: Insufficient bentonite or SAP.
             Add 0.5g more bentonite, blend 2 min, rest 20 min.
  Variant B: Same. Additionally check silica was included.
```

## 7.3 — Uniformity Check

```
Hold the vessel up to a light source and look through the gel
(or hold a small amount between gloved fingers).

PASS:
  Uniform opacity throughout. No clear zones.
  No visible lumps or gritty particles.
  For Variant B: uniform white, not patchy white.

FAIL (lumps present):
  Run blender for 3 more minutes targeting lumps.
  If lumps persist: strain through coarse strainer.
  Record for next batch — powder was added too fast.
```

# PART VIII: APPLICATION METHODS

## 8.1 — Skin Applications (Variant A or B)

```
PATCH TEST — MANDATORY BEFORE FIRST USE:
  Apply a small amount to inner forearm.
  Wait 24 hours.
  Check for redness, itching, irritation.
  Proceed to target area only if patch test is clear.

GENERAL SKIN APPLICATION:
  Apply with ring finger (lightest pressure) or fingertip.
  Spread to 1–3mm thickness depending on application.
  Leave 10–20 minutes.
  Rinse with warm then cool water.
  Pat dry.

EYE AREA (Variant A, no silica ONLY):
  Below orbital bone only — never on eyelid or lash base.
  Apply with ring finger, 1–2mm thickness.
  Leave 10–15 minutes.
  Rinse gently with cool water.
  If gel contacts eye: rinse immediately with water
  for 30 seconds. All components water-soluble.

BEARD / HAIR:
  Apply to damp hair from ends toward roots.
  Work through with fingers.
  Leave 10–20 minutes.
  Rinse warm then cool.

NAILS / CUTICLES:
  Dispense into a small bowl.
  Submerge fingertips 15–20 minutes.
  Remove, push back cuticles.
  Rinse warm then cool.

BODY:
  Apply 3–4mm layer to target area.
  Optional: wrap with cling film for enhanced effect.
  Leave 20–30 minutes.
  Shower off warm then cool.
```

## 8.2 — Fire Protection Applications (Variant B only)

```
VERTICAL SURFACE COATING:
  Apply 3–5mm layer using spatula, brush, or
  zip-lock bag with corner cut.
  The thixotropic gel holds on vertical surfaces —
  apply in one pass, do not over-work.
  Allow 5 minutes before flame exposure.

ZIP-LOCK BAG DEPLOYMENT:
  Fill bag. Seal. Remove air.
  Cut 5mm corner.
  Squeeze firmly to direct gel stream.
  Works on surfaces at any angle including overhead
  (thixotropy holds in place once flow stops).

APPLICATION THICKNESS FOR FIRE TESTS:
  Minimum effective: 3mm
  Standard test: 3–5mm
  Extended protection: 5–8mm (diminishing returns above 8mm)
```

# PART IX: STORAGE AND PRESERVATION

## 9.1 — The Four-Factor Preservation System

```
Without chemical preservatives, the four factors below
combine to achieve 2–4 week refrigerated shelf life.
All four must be maintained together — each factor alone
is insufficient.

FACTOR 1 — REFRIGERATION (2–5°C):
  Store at back of refrigerator shelf (most stable temperature).
  Not the door. Not the freezer.

FACTOR 2 — AIR REMOVAL:
  Remove all air from storage container before sealing.
  For zip-lock bag: press from bottom to seal, squeezing
  all air out before the final seal closure.
  Repeat this after every dispensing event.

FACTOR 3 — SEALED CONTAINER:
  Bag or container must be fully sealed between uses.
  The seal is re-established after every use, not just
  before storage.

FACTOR 4 — CLEAN DISPENSING:
  This is the most critical factor in practice.
  Every opening is a contamination event.
  
  DISPENSING PROTOCOL:
  - Wash hands before handling
  - Cut smallest possible opening for dispensing
  - Squeeze directly onto application surface
  - Do not touch cut opening with fingers or tools
  - Fold and clip opening after dispensing
  - Never re-insert a used tool or finger into container
```

## 9.2 — Shelf Life by Protocol Adherence

```
All four factors, strictly maintained:    3–4 weeks
Three factors maintained:                 2–3 weeks
Two factors maintained:                   1–2 weeks
No protocol (open, room temperature):     24–48 hours

NOTE ON FUMED SILICA AND SHELF LIFE:
  Silica has mild incidental antimicrobial properties
  at the nano-particle level. This effect is not strong
  or reliable enough to meaningfully extend shelf life
  and is not used as a preservative in any cosmetic
  formulation. The four-factor protocol determines
  shelf life — not silica presence or absence.
```

## 9.3 — Spoilage Indicators

```
DISCARD IMMEDIATELY IF ANY OF THESE APPEAR:
  □ Sour, musty, or off odor — earliest indicator
  □ Unexpected color change or spotting
  □ Sudden texture change — unexpected thinning
  □ Any visible growth or surface film

NORMAL AND ACCEPTABLE:
  □ Slight water layer at top of bag (syneresis)
    → Re-knead bag 30 seconds before use
  □ Gel stiffer from cold
    → Rest at room temperature 5–10 min before applying
  □ Slight settling visible through bag
    → Re-knead before dispensing
```

## 9.4 — Extending Beyond 4 Weeks

```
If 4+ week shelf life is required, the formulation needs:
  Phenoxyethanol: 0.8% of total formula weight
  Ethylhexylglycerin: 0.2% of total formula weight
  (Combined: 1% of total)

Both are cosmetic-grade, widely available.
This is the standard broad-spectrum preservative system
for water-based cosmetics.
Without it, 4 weeks is the practical ceiling regardless
of handling discipline.

NOTE: A preserved formulation requires full cosmetic labeling
including the preservative in the INCI list.
```

```
  ✗ Three separate component dispersions
  ✗ Sequential timed rest periods (30 min SAP, 45 min bent,
    10 min silica — running in parallel)
  ✗ Thixotropy check before compounding
  ✗ Bentonite-first compounding sequence with staged silica addition
  ✗ 6-portion silica addition with 2-minute whisking between portions
  ✗ Syneresis monitoring during SAP rest
  ✗ Silica re-agglomeration clock management

ADDED TO PROTOCOL:
  ✓ Single dry mix step (all components)
  ✓ Single blending vessel (all components)
  ✓ 8–10 minute continuous high-shear blend
  ✓ 1-hour unified post-blend rest (replaces all staged rests)
```
