# P1S — Water-Soluble Additive Reference
## Comprehensive Catalog: Effects, Interactions, and Emergent Use Cases
## OrganismCore Technical Record
## Date: 2026-05-09

---

## FRAMING

This document catalogs water-soluble additives that can be dissolved into the
water phase of P1S before or during synthesis. The approach is property-first:
each additive is assessed for what it physically and chemically does to the
P1S system — to the SAP network, the bentonite platelet network, and the
fumed silica suspension — and then what use cases emerge from those effects.

Use cases are not pre-selected. They are derived from what each additive
structurally does to the system.

**P1S base system reminder:**
- Sodium polyacrylate (SAP): ~0.6% by mass — the primary water-retention
  network. Anionic polymer. Highly sensitive to divalent cations and ionic
  strength.
- Fumed silica: ~2.2% by mass — aerogel precursor. Negatively charged
  colloidal particles. Forms the thermal insulation layer under heat.
- Sodium bentonite: ~1.7% by mass — thixotropic structural network. Clay
  platelets with high cation exchange capacity.
- Water: ~95.5% by mass — the continuous phase. ALL additives dissolved
  here interact with all three solid components.

**Critical interaction rule:** Because the SAP is an anionic polyelectrolyte,
it is highly sensitive to the ionic composition of the water phase. Divalent
cations (Ca²⁺, Mg²⁺, Cu²⁺, etc.) dramatically collapse the SAP network by
charge-shielding at concentrations as low as 0.1%. Monovalent cations (Na⁺,
K⁺) reduce swelling more gradually. Any additive that introduces significant
ionic content must be evaluated for this effect first.

**Addition point:** All additives in this document are dissolved in the water
phase before SAP hydration begins, unless noted otherwise. Adding to a
fully formed gel is possible for some additives but produces less uniform
distribution.

**Hazard rating system used in this document:**
- ✅ BENIGN: Food-grade or cosmetic-grade. No special precautions beyond gloves.
- ⚠ MODERATE: Handle with gloves and ventilation. Irritant or mild hazard.
- 🔴 SIGNIFICANT: Requires full PPE, careful handling, and disposal awareness.
- ☠ TOXIC: Not recommended for bench-scale open use. Included for completeness.

# CATEGORY 1: FIRE PERFORMANCE ENHANCERS

*Additives that amplify or extend the fire-protective mechanisms of P1S.*

---

## 1.1 — Borax (Sodium Tetraborate Decahydrate, Na₂B₄O₇·10H₂O)

**Concentration range:** 2–8% by mass of water phase
**Water solubility:** ~5g/100mL at 20°C, highly soluble at elevated temperature
**Hazard:** ✅ BENIGN at low concentration. Food additive status in many jurisdictions.
Reproductive toxin classification at high repeated exposure — wear gloves.

**Effect on P1S system:**
Borax dissolves in water releasing borate ions (B(OH)₄⁻). These do NOT
significantly compress the SAP network (monovalent/weak interaction). They
DO interact with bentonite platelet edges, slightly stiffening the
thixotropic network. Primary functional effect is chemical, not rheological.

At fire exposure temperatures (>150°C), boron compounds release boric acid
which coats burning surfaces, catalyzing char formation and forming a glassy
boron oxide (B₂O₃) layer. This layer excludes oxygen at temperatures where
the P1S aerogel residue alone is beginning to sinter.

**Mechanism in context of P1S fire protection:**
- Phase A (evaporative cooling): No significant change
- Phase C (aerogel transition): Borate ions in the water phase concentrate
  alongside silica as water evaporates. The resulting residue is a
  borosilicate composite rather than pure silica — higher temperature
  stability, lower melting point (good for conformal flow-and-seal at
  fire surface), and better oxygen barrier
- Phase D (ceramic residue): Borosilicate ceramic. More durable than silica
  alone. Better resistance to thermal cracking.

**Gel property changes:** Slight increase in yield stress (stiffer on vertical
surfaces). Minimal effect on flow through nozzle.

**P1S + Borax interaction with SAP:** Benign. Borate ions do not significantly
collapse the SAP swelling network at the concentrations described.

**Emergent use cases:**
- Enhanced fire protection of wood surfaces — boron penetrates wood and
  provides permanent char-forming chemistry independent of the gel's water
- Combined P1S/borax as a fire retardant treatment for textiles (borax is
  a historically validated textile fire retardant)
- ICKS shell additive — borosilicate residue improves aerogel layer persistence
  at post-flash temperatures
- Insect and decay resistance in wood applications (borax disrupts insect
  metabolic function at residual concentrations)

---

## 1.2 — Boric Acid (H₃BO₃)

**Concentration range:** 1–5% by mass of water phase
**Water solubility:** ~5g/100mL at 20°C (slightly less soluble than borax)
**Hazard:** ⚠ MODERATE. Same reproductive toxin classification as borax.
Mild eye/skin irritant. Gloves and eye protection required.

**Effect on P1S system:**
Boric acid is a weak acid (pKa ~9.2). At P1S working pH (approximately
neutral to mildly alkaline from bentonite), it exists primarily as undissociated
H₃BO₃. At elevated pH it becomes borate (same as borax endpoint). Slightly
more acidic effect on the gel matrix than borax.

Boric acid is a crosslinker for polyvinyl alcohol (PVA) and similar polymers.
It does NOT significantly crosslink sodium polyacrylate (different chemistry).
No meaningful SAP network disruption at suggested concentrations.

**Fire mechanism:** Identical to borax at fire temperatures — boric acid
decomposes to B₂O₃ (glassy oxygen barrier) and catalyzes char formation.
Often combined with borax to extend the temperature range of boron fire action.

**Synergy with P1S silica:** Borosilicate glass formation in the aerogel
residue. The silica + boric acid combination at high temperature produces
a denser glass layer than either alone.

**Emergent use cases:**
- Wood preservative P1S — both fire and fungal resistance
- P1S as a carrier for boric acid treatment in termite control (boron is
  a primary termiticide — the gel delivers it conformally into wood surface)
- Insect bait station medium (P1S holds moisture and borax/boric acid in
  accessible form for insects)

---

## 1.3 — Diammonium Phosphate / Monoammonium Phosphate (DAP / MAP)

**Concentration range:** 3–12% by mass of water phase
**Water solubility:** DAP: ~57g/100mL. MAP: ~36g/100mL. Both highly soluble.
**Hazard:** ✅ BENIGN. Both are common fertilizers and food additives.
Ammonium release at fire temperatures — use in ventilated space.

**Effect on P1S system:**
Phosphate salts introduce NH₄⁺ and phosphate anions. NH₄⁺ is monovalent —
moderate reduction in SAP swelling (~15–25% at 5% DAP). This must be
compensated by slightly increasing SAP mass (add 1–2g extra SAP per 5%
DAP added to the water). The bentonite network is minimally affected.

The phosphate anion (PO₄³⁻) competes with silanol groups on fumed silica
surface, potentially disrupting dispersion stability. Prepare the silica
suspension separately in plain water, add DAP solution to SAP+bentonite
only, then add silica last during compounding.

**Fire mechanism:** Among the most well-validated fire retardant additives
for cellulosic materials. At temperatures >150°C, phosphate decomposes to
phosphoric acid, which dehydrates cellulose to form a protective char rather
than flammable volatile pyrolysis products. The char physically blocks oxygen
from reaching unburned wood beneath. This works in conjunction with P1S
evaporative cooling — the phosphate chemistry takes over where the water
phase has depleted.

**What this creates:** A P1S-DAP gel provides TWO sequential fire protection
mechanisms — first water evaporation, then phosphate char chemistry. The
protection window is substantially extended beyond plain P1S.

**Emergent use cases:**
- Forest fire management deployment — P1S-DAP gel is structurally similar
  to aerial fire retardant slurry (Phos-Chek and equivalents) but deployable
  through any nozzle system
- Permanent wood fire retardant treatment (DAP penetrates wood in the
  P1S closed-system steam process described in v3 documents)
- Agricultural dual-use: fire retardant AND fertilizer. P1S-DAP applied to
  dry grass or fields provides fire suppression while delivering nitrogen
  and phosphorus to soil
- Paper and cardboard fire protection coating

## 1.4 — Sodium Silicate (Water Glass, Na₂SiO₃)

**Concentration range:** 5–15% of water phase (as 40% solution — diluted in)
**Water solubility:** Fully water-soluble as solution
**Hazard:** ⚠ MODERATE. Strongly alkaline (pH ~12). Skin/eye irritant.
Gloves, eye protection. Can disrupt SAP network at high concentration.

**Effect on P1S system:**
Critical interaction: sodium silicate is strongly alkaline AND introduces
significant sodium ion load. Both effects reduce SAP swelling capacity.
At 5% sodium silicate (40% solution, by water phase mass), expect 20–30%
reduction in SAP network swelling. Compensate: increase SAP by 2–3g per
batch. Alternatively, use the silicate in a thinner gel formulation and
accept lower viscosity (thinner gel penetrates porous substrates better).

Effect on fumed silica: sodium silicate is itself a silica compound. At
fire temperatures, dissolved sodium silicate in the water phase adds to the
silica concentration available for aerogel/glass layer formation. This
significantly increases Phase D ceramic layer density and quality.

Research confirms: polyacrylate-silicate hybrid hydrogels extend fire
insulation time dramatically compared to plain polyacrylate gels. The
silicate contributes to water binding at temperatures above 100°C —
water evaporation onset is delayed (observed up to 120–180°C vs 100°C
for plain water), extending the evaporative cooling phase.

**Emergent use cases:**
- P1S-Softener/Hardener process (v3 document) — primary agent
- Fire protection glass interlayer — sodium silicate-polyacrylate gel
  between glass panes is commercial technology for fire-rated glazing;
  P1S provides a manufacturable variant
- Rock and masonry consolidation — silicate penetrates porous stone and
  polymerizes in situ, consolidating crumbling surfaces; P1S delivers it
  conformally to irregular stone surfaces
- Soil stabilization — silicate in P1S gel applied to loose sand forms
  a soil-binding silica residue on drying
- Enhanced ICKS shell hardener (from the ICKS system documentation)

**Formulation note:** Do NOT add concentrated sodium silicate directly to
the P1S gel. Dissolve it in the water phase before SAP hydration, at no
more than 20% final concentration of silicate in water. Stir vigorously.
The resulting gel will be thinner but functional.

## 1.5 — Potassium Bicarbonate (KHCO₃) / Sodium Bicarbonate (NaHCO₃)

**Concentration range:** 2–10% by mass of water phase
**Water solubility:** KHCO₃: 22g/100mL. NaHCO₃: 9g/100mL. Both soluble.
**Hazard:** ✅ BENIGN. Food-grade compounds. No safety concerns at these concentrations.

**Effect on P1S system:**
Monovalent cation load (K⁺ or Na⁺). Mild reduction in SAP swelling at
higher concentrations. The bicarbonate anion is mildly alkaline — slightly
increases pH of the water phase, which actually increases the anionic
character of the SAP and can slightly improve swelling at low salt concentration.
Net effect at 5%: minimal gel property change.

**Fire mechanism:** Potassium bicarbonate is the "Purple-K" fire suppressant.
At fire temperatures, it decomposes releasing CO₂ (inert gas displacing
oxygen) and K⁺ ions which terminate flame chain reactions by interrupting
radical propagation. Purple-K is more effective than sodium bicarbonate
because K⁺ is a more effective radical terminator than Na⁺.

**What this creates in P1S:** A gel that releases CO₂ and flame-terminating
potassium radicals at fire surface temperature. Unlike the wax-encapsulated
Phase 2 CO₂ foaming system (which requires the wax melting temperature to
trigger), dissolved bicarbonate releases gradually as temperature rises through
the evaporative phase. Continuous mild CO₂ and K⁺ release throughout Phase A
and Phase C.

**Emergent use cases:**
- Augmented fire suppression gel — bicarbonate adds gas-phase suppression
  to the condensed-phase mechanisms of plain P1S
- Kitchen fire suppressant gel — food-safe, immediately available, purple-K
  chemistry in an easy-apply format
- Electrical fire application — CO₂ is non-conductive; P1S-bicarbonate would
  not short circuits

# CATEGORY 2: STRUCTURAL MODIFIERS

*Additives that change the mechanical, rheological, or adhesive properties of P1S.*

---

## 2.1 — Polyvinyl Alcohol (PVA, 87–89% hydrolyzed)

**Concentration range:** 1–5% by mass of water phase
**Water solubility:** Fully soluble when dissolved in hot water (70–90°C),
  then cooled. Add to water before SAP hydration.
**Hazard:** ✅ BENIGN. Non-toxic, food-contact approved in many applications.

**Effect on P1S system:**
PVA is a linear polymer that hydrogen-bonds extensively with water and with
other hydrogen-bond donors (SAP carboxylate groups, silica silanol groups,
bentonite hydroxyl groups). It does not significantly reduce SAP swelling at
the concentrations listed — unlike ionic compounds, it competes for water
molecules rather than collapsing the SAP charge network.

PVA increases the gel's elongation at break (less brittle when partially
dried) and significantly improves adhesion to smooth non-porous surfaces
(glass, metal, painted surfaces) where plain P1S has weaker adhesion due
to the bentonite network being insufficient to anchor to very smooth surfaces.

At 2–5%, PVA stiffens the partially-dried gel (during Phase C of fire
exposure) — the PVA chains form additional physical crosslinks with the
SAP and silica network as water evaporates, producing a denser, more
mechanically coherent aerogel layer. Research confirms PVA increases ice
fracture toughness 3–5× — same mechanism applies to frozen P1S for ICKS shells.

**Emergent use cases:**
- ICKS shell reinforcement (primary structural dopant described in ICKS documentation)
- Improved P1S adhesion to glass and smooth metal surfaces
- P1S film formation — higher PVA concentration produces a flexible film
  on drying that can be peeled, used as a temporary protective sheet
- Window fire protection — P1S-PVA gel applies to glass, provides
  evaporative cooling AND the dried PVA-silica film delays glass fracture
- Fire retardant textile treatment — PVA improves the adhesion of the gel
  coating to fabric fibers

---

## 2.2 — Glycerol (Glycerin, C₃H₈O₃)

**Concentration range:** 3–10% by mass of water phase
**Water solubility:** Fully miscible in all proportions
**Hazard:** ✅ BENIGN. Food-grade humectant. No precautions required.

**Effect on P1S system:**
Glycerol is a small polyol (3 hydroxyl groups). It does not significantly
disrupt the SAP ionic network but does compete for water molecules. Net
effect: slightly reduced SAP swelling at high concentration (>8%), but
significantly slowed drying rate.

Glycerol's primary effect: it is a humectant — it absorbs water from the
atmosphere and slows evaporation from the gel surface. This extends the
Phase A duration (evaporative cooling window) by slowing the rate at which
the water phase depletes.

Glycerol also acts as a plasticizer for the partially-dried gel. As the
SAP/silica network dries under heat, glycerol distributes between the
polymer chains and silica particles, keeping the partially-dried layer
flexible rather than cracking. This prevents the aerogel layer from
developing gaps from thermal stress cracking during Phase C.

At 5–8% glycerol, the gel retains noticeable plasticity even after 30%
water loss — the P1S-Softener formulation (v3 document) exploits exactly
this property for wood plasticization.

**Emergent use cases:**
- Wood plasticizer medium (P1S-Softener) — confirmed primary use
- Extended protection duration fire gel — glycerol slows depletion in
  high-heat-flux environments
- Cold-temperature adhesion — glycerol depresses the freezing point of
  the water phase, preventing gel from freezing in cold environments
  (deployment in winter conditions)
- Wound dressing humectant — glycerol is standard in wound care hydrogels
  for this function (reformulated without bentonite for skin contact)
- Agricultural water retention — glycerol in P1S seed coating slows
  moisture loss from seed contact zone

## 2.3 — Urea (CO(NH₂)₂)

**Concentration range:** 5–20% by mass of water phase
**Water solubility:** ~108g/100mL. Extremely soluble.
**Hazard:** ✅ BENIGN. Fertilizer and cosmetic ingredient. No PPE required.
Note: decomposes above ~133°C releasing ammonia — ensure ventilation
when gel is heated above this temperature.

**Effect on P1S system:**
Urea disrupts hydrogen bonds throughout the water phase. This has a
complex effect on the SAP network: at low concentration (2–5%), urea
slightly reduces SAP swelling by competing for water. At moderate concentration
(8–15%), urea penetrates the SAP polymer chains and disrupts hydrogen
bonding between chains — this SOFTENS the crosslinked network and reduces
effective crosslink density, producing a more fluid gel at rest but one
that loses viscosity more rapidly under shear. The gel becomes more
injectable but less stable on vertical surfaces.

Critically for wood applications: urea is one of the most validated wood
cell wall plasticizers in the literature. Dissolved in the water phase of
P1S, it travels with condensing steam into wood cell walls during the
steam-pressure process, disrupting hydrogen bonding between cellulose
chains and reducing the lignin glass transition temperature (Tg) from
~80–100°C down to 60–70°C in wet conditions. This is the mechanism behind
the P1S-Softener formulation.

**Emergent use cases:**
- P1S-Softener primary agent (confirmed — see v3 document)
- Enhanced evaporative cooling duration — urea/water mixtures have higher
  latent heat of evaporation than pure water in some concentration ranges
- De-icing gel — urea is a road deicer; P1S-urea applied to surfaces
  in cold conditions provides slow-release deicing while adhering to
  vertical surfaces (stairs, ramps, walkways)
- Nitrogen fertilizer delivery medium — P1S as a slow-release carrier
  for urea in agricultural applications

## 2.4 — Sodium Carboxymethylcellulose (CMC, NaCMC)

**Concentration range:** 0.5–2% by mass of water phase
**Water solubility:** Fully soluble. Dissolve before SAP hydration.
**Hazard:** ✅ BENIGN. Food additive (E466). No safety concerns.

**Effect on P1S system:**
CMC is an anionic water-soluble polymer. At 0.5–2%, it significantly
increases the viscosity of the water phase before any other components
are added. When SAP hydrates into a CMC-containing water phase, the
combined network is stiffer and more elastic than either alone. CMC
chains form hydrogen bonds with both SAP chains and bentonite platelet
surfaces, creating additional inter-component bridges.

CMC is documented to increase bentonite dispersion viscosity and stability —
this is actively used in drilling fluids. In P1S context, this means
better retention of bentonite within the gel matrix (less bentonite
platelet settling over time) and a more stable long-term gel.

CMC's interaction with fumed silica: CMC adsorbs onto fumed silica particles,
coating them and increasing their colloidal stability. This directly addresses
the silica re-agglomeration problem described in the timing document — CMC
in the silica suspension phase dramatically extends the usable window of the
silica dispersion before re-agglomeration occurs.

**Emergent use cases:**
- Gel stability extender — P1S-CMC has a longer usable window after mixing
  (silica stays dispersed longer; gel maintains viscosity longer)
- Clear/non-staining P1S variant — CMC can replace bentonite for applications
  where clay staining is unacceptable (art/archival applications, pale fabrics)
- Improved vertical adhesion on very smooth surfaces
- Printable or extrudable P1S — CMC increases the consistency suitable
  for extrusion-based deposition or 3D printing applications

## 2.5 — Xanthan Gum

**Concentration range:** 0.2–1% by mass of water phase
**Water solubility:** Fully dispersible in cold water.
**Hazard:** ✅ BENIGN. Food-grade biopolymer. No safety concerns.

**Effect on P1S system:**
Xanthan gum is one of the most powerful thixotropic rheology modifiers
available. At 0.3–0.5%, it dramatically increases yield stress (resistance
to flow under gravity) while maintaining shear-thinning behavior (flows
under applied pressure). In P1S context, xanthan reinforces the bentonite
thixotropic network multiplicatively — not additively.

Xanthan gum also acts as an "ice-shaping agent" — it modifies ice crystal
growth, producing smaller, more uniform crystals. This is directly relevant
to the ICKS shell formulation where ice crystal uniformity affects fracture
behavior.

At fire temperatures, xanthan gum contributes to char formation (polysaccharide
char chemistry). The resulting char is integrated into the silica aerogel
layer, potentially improving its mechanical coherence.

**Emergent use cases:**
- High-adhesion P1S for overhead surfaces, underside coating, very steep angles
- ICKS shell ice-shaping additive (reduces defect density)
- Extended shelf-life gel (xanthan prevents bentonite settling during storage)
- Spray-applied P1S — xanthan + P1S can be thinned to spray viscosity while
  retaining sufficient yield stress to hold on vertical surfaces after deposition

---

## 2.6 — Polyethylene Glycol (PEG, MW 400–4000)

**Concentration range:** 2–8% by mass of water phase
**Water solubility:** Fully miscible for PEG <8000 MW
**Hazard:** ✅ BENIGN. Food and pharmaceutical excipient. No safety concerns.

**Effect on P1S system:**
PEG is a neutral, non-ionic polymer. It does not disrupt SAP ionic swelling
and has minimal effect on bentonite platelet interactions. Primary effect:
PEG acts as a pore-forming agent and plasticizer within the gel matrix.
As the gel dries under heat, PEG chains remain between the SAP and silica
particles, preventing dense packing and maintaining a more open pore
structure in the aerogel residue. This increases the aerogel's thermal
insulation property by preserving porosity.

Lower MW PEG (400–600): primarily a humectant, delays drying
Higher MW PEG (2000–4000): contributes mechanical flexibility to the
partially-dried gel; reduces cracking during Phase C

**Emergent use cases:**
- High-insulation aerogel residue P1S — PEG-modified formulation produces
  more porous, better-insulating Phase D ceramic
- P1S as passive building insulation coating (application 8.1 from v2 document)
- Controlled release vehicle — PEG-modified P1S allows water-soluble
  active agents to remain mobile within the gel network longer before the
  gel fully dries, extending release duration
- Phase change material analog — PEG itself has a latent heat of melting
  that contributes to the thermal buffer capacity of the gel

# CATEGORY 3: IONIC / INORGANIC SALTS

*Additives that modify through ionic interactions. Handle SAP sensitivity carefully.*

---

## 3.1 — Potassium Chloride (KCl)

**Concentration range:** 0.5–3% by mass of water phase
**Water solubility:** ~34g/100mL. Highly soluble.
**Hazard:** ✅ BENIGN. Table salt substitute. No safety concerns.

**Effect on P1S system:**
Introduces K⁺ and Cl⁻ ions. K⁺ moderately reduces SAP swelling (less
than Na⁺ relative to charge, but still meaningful). At 1%, expect ~20%
reduction in swelling capacity. At 3%, ~40% reduction. Adjust SAP upward
accordingly.

More significantly: KCl at low concentration reduces bentonite swelling.
At 3% KCl, sodium bentonite clay platelets undergo rapid ion exchange
(K⁺ replaces Na⁺ in the interlayer space) and collapse. This DRAMATICALLY
reduces bentonite swelling and thixotropic network formation. At 3% KCl,
bentonite may not function adequately in P1S.

**Use window:** ≤1% KCl. Above this, bentonite network is compromised.

**Emergent use cases:**
- Electrolyte P1S for ionic conductivity testing (see Application 14.1 from v2)
- Modified osmotic pressure gel for controlled water release applications
- Cold-temperature gel — KCl depresses the freezing point

---

## 3.2 — Magnesium Sulfate (Epsom Salt, MgSO₄)

**Concentration range:** 0.1–0.5% by mass of water phase
**Water solubility:** ~26g/100mL. Highly soluble.
**Hazard:** ✅ BENIGN. Food and pharmaceutical grade. No concerns.

**Effect on P1S system:**
⚠ DIVALENT CATION ALERT. Mg²⁺ collapses SAP swelling severely even at very
low concentrations. At 0.5% MgSO₄ (~0.04M Mg²⁺), SAP swelling capacity
is reduced by 60–80%. This is not correctable by adding more SAP — the
network physically contracts around the divalent ions.

At trace concentrations (0.05–0.1%), the effect is less severe but still
measurable. If MgSO₄ is intentionally used, the gel will be thinner and
more liquid than standard P1S — this may be useful for some applications
requiring low viscosity with some residual SAP structure.

**Primary utility:** Very low concentration MgSO₄ can slightly stiffen
bentonite platelet networks (magnesium cross-links clay platelets). At 0.1%,
the net effect might be: slightly thinner SAP network, slightly stiffer
bentonite network. The combination produces a gel with faster initial flow
(lower apparent viscosity under stress) but better static yield stress.

**Emergent use cases:**
- Spray-optimized P1S formulation where reduced viscosity allows finer
  nozzle atomization while yield stress is maintained by the bentonite
- Agricultural mineral amendment delivery (magnesium as plant nutrient)

---

## 3.3 — Sodium Bicarbonate (Baking Soda, NaHCO₃)

*Already covered in Category 1.5 for fire performance. Additional structural note:*

At concentrations below 5%, sodium bicarbonate has minimal effect on SAP
or bentonite. The pH increase (to ~8–8.5) actually slightly improves SAP
swelling by increasing anionic character of the polyacrylate chains.

**Additional emergent use case:**
- pH buffer for applications requiring neutral to mildly alkaline P1S
- Odor neutralization in P1S applied to malodorous surfaces (bicarbonate
  absorbs acidic odor compounds)

---

## 3.4 — Copper Sulfate (CuSO₄)

**Concentration range:** 0.01–0.1% by mass of water phase
**Water solubility:** ~20g/100mL. Highly soluble.
**Hazard:** ⚠ MODERATE. Cu²⁺ is a divalent cation — severe SAP network
collapse above 0.05%. Also an algicide/fungicide — ecological concern for
discharge. Wear gloves. Do not discharge to waterways.

**Effect on P1S system:**
Cu²⁺ at even trace concentration severely collapses SAP swelling. At 0.01%
CuSO₄ (~0.0006M Cu²⁺), SAP swelling is reduced ~40%. At 0.1%, gel is
effectively destroyed as a swollen network. Copper sulfate cannot be used
as a bulk additive for standard P1S without reformulation.

However: the gel's heavy metal sequestration capacity (Application 11.1
from v2) is demonstrated using copper sulfate as the test analyte. At very
low concentration (0.01%), copper ions are adsorbed onto the silica and
bentonite surfaces. The gel matrix retains the copper while the SAP network
is still partially functional.

**Emergent use cases:**
- Algicide/fungicide surface treatment — P1S with trace CuSO₄ applied to
  surfaces prevents algal and fungal growth (copper is a validated biocide)
- Heavy metal sequestration demonstration (as described in v2 document)
- Antifouling marine coating analog — copper biocide in a gel matrix
  applied to underwater surfaces

**Formulation note:** For fungicide applications, use CuSO₄ at 0.01–0.05%
and increase SAP by 3–4g to partially compensate for network collapse.
The resulting gel will be thinner but functional for surface application.

---

## 3.5 — Potassium Permanganate (KMnO₄)

**Concentration range:** 0.01–0.05% by mass of water phase
**Water solubility:** ~6g/100mL. Moderately soluble.
**Hazard:** 🔴 SIGNIFICANT. Strong oxidizer. Contact with organic materials
can initiate combustion. Reacts with SAP polymer chains (oxidation of
carboxylate groups). Stains skin and surfaces persistently. Full PPE required.

**Effect on P1S system:**
⚠ REACTIVE WITH SAP. Permanganate is a strong oxidizer that will partially
degrade the SAP polymer network over time through oxidative chain scission.
At 0.05%, the SAP network degrades noticeably within 1–2 hours. The
resulting gel is significantly thinner. This limits shelf life.

However, at very low concentration (0.01%), the oxidation rate is slow enough
that the gel remains functional for several hours. The permanganate is partially
reduced to manganese dioxide (MnO₂) which deposits as a brown precipitate
within the gel matrix — this is visible as a tan coloration.

**Emergent use cases:**
- Antimicrobial surface treatment (permanganate is a potent broad-spectrum
  biocide) — applied and consumed within hours, leaving MnO₂ residue
  that continues mild antimicrobial activity
- Odor elimination coating — permanganate oxidizes sulfur-containing and
  amine odor compounds on contact; P1S delivers it conformally to odorous
  surfaces
- Ethylene gas scrubbing (fruit/produce storage) — permanganate oxidizes
  ethylene; P1S-permanganate placed in produce storage absorbs ethylene
  and delays ripening

**Important caveat:** Do not add KMnO₄ to a P1S batch that contains
significant organic matter (lignosulfonate, CMC, xanthan gum, etc.).
The oxidizer will react preferentially with these organics, depleting the
active permanganate and potentially generating heat.

# CATEGORY 4: BIOLOGICAL / ANTIMICROBIAL AGENTS

*Additives that confer biological activity on the P1S surface or volume.*

---

## 4.1 — Hydrogen Peroxide (H₂O₂, 3% solution)

**Concentration range:** 0.5–3% by mass of water phase (using 3% H₂O₂ solution)
**Water solubility:** Fully miscible
**Hazard:** ⚠ MODERATE at 3%. Higher concentration is 🔴 SIGNIFICANT.
Oxidizer — do not mix with organic additives (CMC, xanthan, lignosulfonate).
Wear gloves. Good ventilation.

**Effect on P1S system:**
At 3% H₂O₂ in the water phase (i.e., adding 3% H₂O₂ solution directly),
the effective concentration in the gel is approximately 0.15–0.3% H₂O₂
(diluted by the large water volume). At this concentration:
- SAP network: minimal degradation over hours (meaningful degradation at
  >1% H₂O₂ concentration in the gel)
- Bentonite: unaffected (clay is inorganic)
- Fumed silica: unaffected

Primary effect: H₂O₂ decomposes slowly in the gel, releasing oxygen. The
released O₂ creates fine bubbles within the gel matrix, slightly reducing
apparent density and viscosity. This also means the gel has a finite useful
life — H₂O₂ depletes. Prepare and use within 4–6 hours.

Antimicrobial effect: H₂O₂ at 0.1–0.3% in the gel provides broad-spectrum
antimicrobial activity. Kills bacteria, fungi, viruses on contact. This is
validated in wound dressing hydrogels at these concentration ranges.

**Emergent use cases:**
- Disinfectant surface coating (conformally applied, self-sterilizing surface)
- Wound surface irrigation via hydrogel medium (reformulated without bentonite)
- Ethylene peroxide bleaching medium — P1S-H₂O₂ applied to stained porous
  surfaces (concrete, stone, fabric) provides slow oxidative bleaching
- Mold remediation coating — applied to mold-contaminated surfaces, provides
  biocidal action while the gel mechanically encapsulates spores

---

## 4.2 — Ethanol (at low concentration)

**Concentration range:** 5–15% by mass of water phase
**Water solubility:** Fully miscible
**Hazard:** ⚠ MODERATE. Flammable — reduces the flash point of the gel
significantly. The flame-retardant properties of P1S are REDUCED at
high ethanol content. Do not use >10% ethanol in fire protection applications.
Ventilation required during preparation and application.

**Effect on P1S system:**
Ethanol reduces the surface tension of the water phase. This has one
significant positive effect: it dramatically improves wetting of hydrophobic
surfaces (wood with waxy extractives, plastics, painted surfaces). Plain P1S
beads on waxy or hydrophobic surfaces; P1S-ethanol wets and adheres.

Ethanol at 5–15% reduces SAP swelling capacity by 15–30% (reduces osmotic
gradient). Bentonite hydration is also reduced. The gel is thinner and
less thixotropic. This may actually be useful for spray application —
the lower viscosity allows finer atomization.

At these concentrations, ethanol provides meaningful antimicrobial effect
(bactericidal above ~60%, bacteriostatic at lower concentrations — so the
5–15% range is below effective antimicrobial threshold for short contact
times but may function for extended surface contact).

**CRITICAL WARNING:** Ethanol is flammable. P1S-ethanol gel in any fire-
adjacent application must account for the contribution of ethanol vapor to
ignition risk. Do not use for fire protection above 5% ethanol.

**Emergent use cases:**
- Improved surface wetting on hydrophobic substrates (plastic, sealed wood,
  metals with oxide coatings)
- Spray-optimized P1S with better atomization and faster initial wetting
- Surface disinfection gel (ethanol + H₂O₂ combination at appropriate
  concentrations)
- Adhesive preparation — wetting + subsequent gel adhesion on difficult surfaces

---

## 4.3 — Citric Acid (C₆H₈O₇)

**Concentration range:** 1–5% by mass of water phase
**Water solubility:** ~147g/100mL. Highly soluble.
**Hazard:** ✅ BENIGN. Food acid. No safety concerns at these concentrations.

**Effect on P1S system:**
Citric acid reduces the pH of the water phase. This has a direct and
significant negative effect on SAP swelling: sodium polyacrylate is most
swollen at neutral to alkaline pH. At pH <5 (achievable with 3–5% citric
acid), the carboxylate groups on the SAP are protonated, losing their ionic
charge and their ability to expand through electrostatic repulsion. SAP
swelling drops 40–70% depending on concentration.

Important interaction: citric acid + sodium bicarbonate in the water phase
produces CO₂ gas in situ (the same chemistry as the Phase 2 CO₂ foaming
system, but dissolved in the water phase). If both are present, this creates
a lightly effervescent gel. The CO₂ bubbles are released gradually and
provide mild agitation during gel formation. The net pH depends on the ratio.

At 1% citric acid alone (pH ~4.5): gel is noticeably thinner, bentonite
network partially disrupted, silica remains dispersed.

**Emergent use cases:**
- P1S-bicarbonate + P1S-citrate mixed at application: in-situ CO₂ foaming
  gel (the Phase 2 concept without wax encapsulation, at the cost of less
  controlled release)
- pH-triggered gel thinning — a P1S gel with dissolved bicarbonate applied
  to a surface, then citric acid spray applied over it, generates local
  foaming and penetration
- Food preservation pH-modifying coating (citric acid inhibits oxidative
  browning and microbial growth)
- Metal cleaning preparation — citric acid in P1S conformally applies
  to corroded metal surfaces, chelates iron oxides over extended contact time

---

## 4.4 — Lignosulfonate (Sodium Lignosulfonate)

**Concentration range:** 2–8% by mass of water phase
**Water solubility:** Fully soluble
**Hazard:** ✅ BENIGN. Agricultural dust suppressant and dispersant.
Dark brown coloration — significantly discolors the gel.

**Effect on P1S system:**
Lignosulfonate is a water-soluble lignin derivative. It is an anionic
polyelectrolyte — it does not collapse the SAP network and actually
competes for surface sites on bentonite platelets, modifying platelet
interactions. At 2–4%, lignosulfonate slightly reduces bentonite thixotropy
(platelets are coated by lignosulfonate, reducing edge-face electrostatic
attraction). This makes the gel more uniform (lower thixotropic recovery
after shearing) but slightly less adherent to vertical surfaces.

Primary chemical effect: lignosulfonate is a humectant and dispersant. It
improves dispersion stability of the fumed silica (adsorbs onto particles,
stabilizing them against re-agglomeration). This extends the silica
suspension usable window significantly beyond the baseline 45 minutes.

Fire interaction: lignosulfonate decomposes at fire temperatures to produce
a carbonaceous char. This char integrates into the silica aerogel layer,
potentially improving its mechanical coherence.

**Emergent use cases:**
- P1S-Softener wood plasticization (lignosulfonate softens wood lignin by
  disrupting inter-chain hydrogen bonding — same mechanism as urea but
  through a different molecular interaction)
- Silica dispersion stabilizer — small addition (1%) dramatically extends
  silica suspension quality window, easing the timing constraints in the
  optimized protocol
- Dust suppression coating — lignosulfonate is the primary agent in
  commercial road dust suppressants; P1S-lignosulfonate provides this
  function with the additional benefit of bentonite crust formation on drying

# CATEGORY 5: THERMAL MODIFIERS

*Additives that alter the thermal behavior, transition temperatures, or phase-change properties of P1S.*

---

## 5.1 — Propylene Glycol (PG, C₃H₈O₂)

**Concentration range:** 5–15% by mass of water phase
**Water solubility:** Fully miscible
**Hazard:** ✅ BENIGN. Food-grade, pharmaceutical excipient. No concerns.

**Effect on P1S system:**
Propylene glycol is a polyol humectant similar to glycerol. It slows
evaporation and acts as a cell-wall plasticizer in wood. Slightly reduces
SAP swelling (5–10% at 10% PG concentration). Minimal bentonite effect.

More significantly: propylene glycol lowers the freezing point of the gel
(cryoprotectant). A P1S gel with 10–15% PG remains unfrozen at temperatures
as low as -8 to -12°C. This is directly relevant to ICKS shell formulation
where the shell layers must have different thermal stability profiles —
a PG-containing inner layer (Layer 1) could be designed to remain liquid
at temperatures where the LN2-cooled outer shell is fully frozen.

**Emergent use cases:**
- ICKS Layer 1 formulation — PG maintains the inner melt zone in liquid
  state at temperatures above its suppressed freezing point
- Winter-deployment P1S — gel remains usable in freezing conditions
- P1S-Softener wood plasticization (similar mechanism to glycerol, slightly
  less viscous)
- Anti-freeze surface treatment — applied to locks, door seals, mechanisms
  vulnerable to ice formation

---

## 5.2 — Dissolved Sugars (Sucrose, Glucose, Sorbitol)

**Concentration range:** 5–20% by mass of water phase
**Water solubility:** Very high for all three. Sucrose: 200g/100mL.
**Hazard:** ✅ BENIGN. Food compounds. No concerns.

**Effect on P1S system:**
Sugars introduce osmotic competition that moderately reduces SAP swelling
(10–30% depending on concentration and molecular weight). They act as
humectants (slowing evaporation). They are cryoprotectants — they suppress
the freezing point and modify ice crystal formation.

At fire temperatures, sugars (especially glucose and sucrose) undergo
caramelization and then carbonization, forming a sticky, black carbonaceous
char. This char has some fire-retardant properties (physical barrier). More
interestingly, the molten sugar phase before carbonization fills pores and
surface irregularities in the silica aerogel layer, producing a denser
composite at Phase C temperatures.

Sorbitol is specifically documented as an ice-shaping agent — it modifies
ice crystal growth toward smaller, more uniform crystals. This is the ICKS
shell property relevant to uniform fracture behavior.

**Emergent use cases:**
- ICKS shell ice-shaping additive (sorbitol)
- Cryoprotective P1S for cold-storage applications
- Char-forming additive for enhanced fire protection at moderate temperatures
- Food preservation coating — sucrose/glucose in P1S creates an oxygen-
  excluding, moisture-retaining coating for cut produce (Application 7.4 from v2)

---

## 5.3 — Calcium Chloride (CaCl₂)

**Concentration range:** 0.01–0.1% by mass of water phase (EXTREME care)
**Water solubility:** ~74g/100mL. Very highly soluble.
**Hazard:** ⚠ MODERATE. Divalent cation — extreme SAP network collapse.
Skin irritant at concentration. Handle with gloves.

**Effect on P1S system:**
Ca²⁺ is the most damaging common divalent cation for the SAP network.
At 0.1% CaCl₂ (~0.009M Ca²⁺), SAP swelling drops >80%. The gel is
essentially destroyed as a water-retaining medium at meaningful concentration.

The ONE useful property of controlled CaCl₂ addition at trace levels (0.01%):
calcium ions bridge adjacent bentonite platelet faces through divalent
cation exchange, causing clay aggregation and rapid settling. Normally a
problem, but if controlled low-viscosity bentonite is desired for a specific
penetration application, CaCl₂ can be used to collapse the thixotropic
network intentionally.

CaCl₂ also generates significant heat when dissolved (exothermic dissolution).
This is not practically useful in P1S context but should be noted.

**Emergent use cases (limited):**
- Controlled bentonite collapse — if a thin, low-viscosity liquid that
  still contains dispersed silica is required for deep penetration into
  porous materials, CaCl₂-modified P1S (heavily reformulated, minimal SAP)
  may allow this
- De-icing agent — CaCl₂ is the most effective deicing salt at low
  temperature; P1S-CaCl₂ provides slow-release deicer with surface adhesion.
  Do NOT use for any fire application — the gel has no functional P1S
  fire-protection properties at useful CaCl₂ concentrations.

# CATEGORY 6: WATER-SOLUBLE POLYMERS AND BIOPOLYMERS

*Additives that introduce additional polymer networks into P1S.*

---

## 6.1 — Gelatin (Type A or B)

**Concentration range:** 1–5% by mass of water phase (dissolved hot, cooled before SAP)
**Water solubility:** Soluble in hot water (above 40°C), gels on cooling
**Hazard:** ✅ BENIGN. Food ingredient. No concerns.

**Effect on P1S system:**
Gelatin is a thermoreversible polymer — it forms a gel below ~30°C and
melts above ~35°C. Added to P1S, it creates a secondary temperature-responsive
network within the SAP-bentonite-silica composite. Below room temperature,
the gel is stiffer (gelatin contributes to network). Above 35°C, the gelatin
network melts and the gel becomes more fluid.

At fire temperatures: gelatin denatures and carbonizes, contributing to char
formation similar to other polysaccharides/proteins.

The thermoreversible behavior creates an interesting application geometry:
a P1S-gelatin gel stiffens in cold conditions (better vertical adhesion in
cold environments) and becomes more fluid in warm conditions (self-leveling
on warm substrates).

**Emergent use cases:**
- Temperature-responsive adhesion — gel self-levels on warm surfaces,
  then firms as it cools to ambient temperature
- Controlled-release matrix — gelatin melts at body temperature (~37°C),
  releasing encapsulated agents when the gel is applied to warm surfaces
- ICKS shell thermal interface — gelatin-containing Layer 2 might provide
  a more controlled thermal transition zone between frozen outer shell and
  liquid inner zone

---

## 6.2 — Polyvinylpyrrolidone (PVP, K-30 or K-90)

**Concentration range:** 1–4% by mass of water phase
**Water solubility:** Fully soluble
**Hazard:** ✅ BENIGN. Pharmaceutical excipient. No concerns.

**Effect on P1S system:**
PVP is a neutral water-soluble polymer. Similar to PVA in many respects but
does not form crosslinks with borate. PVP forms a secondary network through
Van der Waals interactions and hydrogen bonding. It improves film-forming
properties of the gel on drying — a P1S-PVP gel dries to a more continuous,
crack-resistant film than plain P1S.

PVP is a well-known dispersion stabilizer — it adsorbs onto particle
surfaces (silica, bentonite) and sterically prevents aggregation. Combined
with CMC, PVP significantly extends colloidal stability of the silica
suspension.

PVP also provides some antimicrobial activity (used in iodine-PVP antiseptic
complexes — Betadine). While not antimicrobial alone, PVP-iodine complexes
dissolved in P1S water phase create an antiseptic gel.

**Emergent use cases:**
- Stable long-shelf-life P1S — PVP dispersion stabilization
- PVP-iodine (Betadine) carrier in P1S — antiseptic surface treatment
- Flexible dried film P1S — less cracking in dried-coat applications
- Reduced silica re-agglomeration rate, extending the silica timing window

# CATEGORY 7: GAS-GENERATING / FOAMING AGENTS

*Additives that generate gas in response to heat or pH change, extending P1S protective mechanisms.*

---

## 7.1 — Sodium Bicarbonate + Citric Acid (Pre-dissolved separately, mixed at use)

**Concentration range:** Each at 3–8% in separate water aliquots
**Hazard:** ✅ BENIGN individually. CO₂ generation — ensure ventilation.

**Effect on P1S system:**
This is the dissolved equivalent of the Phase 2 wax-encapsulated foaming
system, but without thermal triggering. When the bicarbonate and citric
acid solutions are mixed at compounding, CO₂ is generated immediately. The
result is a foamed gel — the CO₂ bubbles become trapped in the SAP-bentonite
matrix, reducing gel density and creating a foam structure.

The foam has significantly higher surface area per unit volume than plain gel,
improving evaporative cooling efficiency (more water-air interface). It also
has better thermal insulation in its dry state (air pockets in the aerogel
residue).

**Emergent use cases:**
- Foamed fire suppression gel — higher water surface area = faster initial
  cooling with less material
- Foam insulation medium — expanded P1S-foam on drying produces a low-density
  insulating solid
- Lightweight P1S application for weight-sensitive scenarios

---

## 7.2 — Hydrogen Peroxide (higher concentration, 30% solution)

**Concentration range:** 0.1–0.5% of water phase using 30% solution
**Hazard:** 🔴 SIGNIFICANT. 30% H₂O₂ is a strong oxidizer. Full PPE required.
Do not mix with organic additives or SAP in dry form.

**Effect on P1S system:**
At higher effective concentration (0.3–0.5% H₂O₂ in gel), decomposition
to O₂ is faster, producing a more actively foaming gel. This creates a
gas-evolving gel that expands as it is applied. The O₂ release also creates
an oxidizing microenvironment at the gel surface — antimicrobial.

Interaction with SAP: at these concentrations, H₂O₂ begins oxidizing SAP
chains within hours. Shelf life is very limited. Prepare and deploy within
2 hours maximum.

**Emergent use cases:**
- Expanding wound-filling gel (medical context only, reformulated)
- Active antimicrobial foaming surface treatment
- Oxygenating soil amendment carrier

# CATEGORY 9: SPECULATIVE / HIGH-INTEREST ADDITIVES

*Additives with unusual or strongly emergent properties worthy of exploration.*

---

## 9.1 — Sodium Alginate

**Concentration range:** 0.5–2% by mass of water phase
**Water solubility:** Fully soluble (dissolve before SAP hydration)
**Hazard:** ✅ BENIGN. Food-grade gelling agent (seaweed derivative).

**Effect on P1S system:**
Sodium alginate is an anionic polysaccharide. It forms a gel in the presence
of divalent cations (Ca²⁺, specifically). In the P1S water phase (distilled
water, no Ca²⁺), alginate remains dissolved and contributes to gel viscosity.
If Ca²⁺ is subsequently encountered (hard water, contact with calcium-containing
substrate), the alginate instantly gels through ionic crosslinking with Ca²⁺.

This creates a triggered-stiffening property: P1S-alginate applied to a
concrete or limestone surface (both calcium-containing) will undergo a
secondary stiffening event at the gel-substrate interface as Ca²⁺ ions
diffuse from the substrate into the gel and crosslink the alginate chains.
The gel "locks" itself to calcium-containing substrates.

**Emergent use cases:**
- Self-anchoring P1S on concrete, masonry, limestone — the gel stiffens
  at the interface, dramatically increasing adhesion to calcium-rich surfaces
- Encapsulation medium — alginate gels are widely used as encapsulation shells;
  the P1S-alginate system allows droplet encapsulation on contact with Ca²⁺
- Wound dressing (reformulated) — alginate + calcium is the basis of commercial
  calcium alginate wound dressings

---

## 9.2 — Tannic Acid

**Concentration range:** 0.5–3% by mass of water phase
**Water solubility:** ~280g/100mL. Very soluble.
**Hazard:** ✅ BENIGN. Food-grade tannin (from tea, bark, fruit). No concerns.
Significant brown staining of any contact surface.

**Effect on P1S system:**
Tannic acid is a polyphenol that forms hydrogen bonds and metal-coordination
complexes with extraordinary promiscuity. It binds to and physically crosslinks:
SAP polymer chains, silica silanol groups, bentonite platelet surfaces, and
cellulose/lignin in wood. At 1–3%, it creates additional physical crosslinks
throughout the P1S network without significantly disrupting the ionic swelling
of SAP.

Net rheological effect: moderately increased gel stiffness and yield stress.
Improved adhesion to organic substrates (wood, fabric, skin) through tannic
acid's universal adhesion chemistry.

Fire effect: tannic acid produces a dense, coherent char on heating. Research
shows it integrates into aerogel matrices to produce mechanically superior
residue (same mechanism observed in borax-PVA-tannic acid aerogels in the
literature). The char formed is more resistant to mechanical disturbance than
plain silica aerogel.

**Emergent use cases:**
- Improved aerogel layer mechanical resistance — the weakest property of
  plain P1S residue is brittleness; tannic acid dramatically addresses this
- Wood tannin treatment — P1S-tannic acid applied to wood conformally
  delivers tannin into wood surface for improved UV resistance, weather
  resistance, and appearance
- Antioxidant coating — tannic acid is a potent antioxidant; P1S-tannic
  acid applied to cut metal surfaces reduces surface oxidation rate
- Leather-substitute surface finishing (tannins tan hides — applied to
  collagen-containing materials, produces a leather-like surface modification)

---

## 9.3 — Sodium Hydroxide (NaOH, dilute)

**Concentration range:** 0.05–0.3% by mass of water phase (very low)
**Water solubility:** Fully soluble
**Hazard:** 🔴 SIGNIFICANT even at low concentration — strongly alkaline,
corrosive to skin and eyes. Gloves and eye protection mandatory. Full PPE for
concentrations above 1%.

**Effect on P1S system:**
Sodium hydroxide increases pH of the water phase into the alkaline range.
Effect on SAP: alkaline pH increases anionic character of polyacrylate
chains, IMPROVING swelling capacity slightly (opposite of acidic conditions).
Effect on bentonite: sodium hydroxide promotes sodium saturation of the
clay interlayer — can improve swelling of partially calcium-contaminated
bentonite.

Significant effect on fumed silica: alkaline conditions increase the
negative charge on silica particles, improving electrostatic stabilization
and DRAMATICALLY extending the silica dispersion stability window. Research
confirms silica suspensions at alkaline pH resist re-agglomeration far
longer than at neutral pH.

At 0.1% NaOH in the water phase (pH ~11): silica suspension stability
extends from ~45 minutes to potentially several hours. SAP swelling is
maintained or slightly improved. Bentonite is improved if calcium-contaminated.

**Trade-off:** Alkaline gel may attack some surfaces (aluminum, zinc, some
paints). Always check substrate compatibility.

**Emergent use cases:**
- Extended silica stability window — addresses the most critical timing
  constraint in the synthesis protocol
- Improved P1S for hard water situations where calcium contamination
  is partially reverting the bentonite to calcium form
- Concrete/masonry P1S formulation — alkaline environment matches the
  substrate chemistry, improves adhesion and compatibility

---

## 9.4 — Activated Charcoal Powder (Water-suspended, not truly dissolved)

**Concentration range:** 1–4% by mass as fine powder suspension
**Water solubility:** NOT water-soluble — aqueous suspension only
**Hazard:** ✅ BENIGN. Food-grade activated charcoal available. Respiratory
precaution during dry handling. Once wet, no airborne concern.

**Note on inclusion in this document:** Activated charcoal is not water-soluble
but it suspends stably in the P1S gel matrix. Included here because it
can be added to the water phase as a pre-made suspension before SAP hydration,
distributing uniformly through the final gel.

**Effect on P1S system:**
Activated charcoal particles are neutrally charged and do not significantly
interact with the SAP network or bentonite platelets. They distribute as an
additional particulate phase within the gel matrix. The gel turns grey-black.

Activated charcoal has extremely high surface area (500–3000 m²/g) and
adsorbs virtually any organic molecule and many gases on contact. This means
the P1S-charcoal gel is an adsorption medium for any toxic or odorous compound
it contacts.

At fire temperatures: charcoal contributes to char layer formation and
maintains structural porosity in the aerogel residue. It is also a reducing
agent that can buffer against oxidation of the SAP polymer chains under heat.

**Emergent use cases:**
- Smoke toxicity reduction (Application 4.1 variant from v1 document) —
  activated charcoal adsorbs CO and volatile toxic organics released during
  combustion, directly at the fire surface where the gel is deployed
- Odor elimination surface treatment — P1S-charcoal applied to odor-emitting
  surfaces (pet areas, industrial surfaces) provides sustained odor adsorption
- Heavy metal sequestration gel (Application 11.1 from v2) — charcoal adds
  a fourth sequestration mechanism alongside SAP carboxylates, silica silanol
  groups, and bentonite interlayer sites
- Soil remediation for organic pollutants — charcoal sequesters hydrophobic
  organic contaminants that other components of P1S do not address

## INTERACTION RULES — ADDITIVE COMBINATIONS

Some combinations amplify effects; others conflict:

**SYNERGISTIC (beneficial combinations):**
- Borax + DAP: dual-mechanism fire retardant (boron char + phosphorus char)
- CMC + PVP: extended silica stability window
- Glycerol + urea: wood plasticization (P1S-Softener) — both active, different mechanisms
- Borax + sodium silicate: borosilicate ceramic residue (superior Phase D)
- Tannic acid + CMC: improved adhesion + stability
- NaOH (trace) + fumed silica suspension: extends silica quality window

**CONFLICTING (avoid together):**
- Citric acid + sodium bicarbonate: immediate CO₂ release — useful ONLY if
  foaming is the intent; destroys structured gel if unintended
- Potassium permanganate + any organic additive (CMC, xanthan, lignosulfonate,
  PVA, gelatin): permanganate preferentially oxidizes organics, depleting itself
- Ethanol + fire protection application: ethanol contributes to flammability,
  directly opposing fire protection intent
- Any divalent cation (Ca²⁺, Mg²⁺, Cu²⁺) + standard P1S: SAP network collapse
- Sodium silicate + calcium-containing water: silicate precipitates as calcium
  silicate before it can be incorporated into the gel
