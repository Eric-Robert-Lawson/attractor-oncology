## WHAT THIS DOCUMENT IS

This is the complete, standalone Phase 1 protocol for the EHAB
(Evaporative Hydrogel Aerogel Barrier) substance. It supersedes
the Phase 1 sections of the original protocol document.

Phase 1 produces a milky-white, thixotropic gel composed of three
components: sodium polyacrylate (SAP), fumed silica, and sodium
bentonite clay. When applied to a surface and exposed to heat,
this gel provides fire protection through four sequential mechanisms:

```
MECHANISM 1 — EVAPORATIVE COOLING (Phase A, 0-100°C):
  Water in the gel absorbs heat via evaporation (2,260 J/g).
  The surface it coats cannot exceed ~100°C while liquid water
  is present. This is a physical ceiling, not a rate — it holds
  regardless of how intense the heat source is, until the water
  is gone.

MECHANISM 2 — OXYGEN EXCLUSION (continuous, all phases):
  The gel layer is a physical barrier between the coated surface
  and atmospheric oxygen. No oxygen, no combustion, regardless
  of temperature. This is the mechanism that closes the geometry
  of every application beyond fire suppression — forming, moisture
  modification, wood aging, controlled heat processing.

MECHANISM 3 — AEROGEL TRANSITION (Phase C, gel depleting):
  As water evaporates under heat, fumed silica particles
  concentrate and self-assemble into a silica aerogel layer
  at the surface. This aerogel is a persistent thermal insulator
  that remains after all water is gone. Bentonite clay in the
  residue reinforces this aerogel mechanically — this is an
  emergent positive property not explicitly designed for.

MECHANISM 4 — CERAMIC RESIDUE (Phase D, fully dry):
  The final dried state is a silica-clay composite ceramic
  bonded to the surface. This residue continues to insulate
  and exclude oxygen as a solid layer.
```

This same gel, with the same formulation, also serves as:
- A forming medium for hot-press processing of flammable solids
- A moisture gradient control medium for material conditioning
- An oxygen exclusion barrier for controlled thermal chemistry
- A deployable surface protection agent via rapid-mix packet systems

All of these applications are validated by testing the same gel
batch produced in this protocol.

## CRITICAL SAFETY — READ BEFORE OPENING ANYTHING

### The Non-Negotiable Rule

Fumed silica is the only genuinely hazardous material in this protocol.
Everything else is food-grade or cosmetic-grade.

Fumed silica particles are nanometer-scale — invisible to the naked eye,
lighter than air, and they remain suspended for several minutes when
disturbed. Inhalation causes silicosis — permanent, irreversible lung
scarring. There is no treatment. Prevention is absolute.

**The N95 respirator goes ON before the fumed silica container is opened.
It stays on until all silica is in water and the container is sealed.
No exceptions. No shortcuts.**

### Fire Test Safety

All torch tests occur outdoors or in a space with door and windows
fully open. The metal sample holder is on a non-combustible surface
(concrete, brick, metal workbench — not a wooden table). A bucket
of water large enough to submerge a burning panel is placed within
arm's reach before the torch is lit.

### Workspace Preparation

1. Lay down newspaper or a garbage bag opened flat across your work area.
2. Place all tools and containers within arm's reach before opening anything.
3. Put nitrile gloves on now. Wear them for the entire session.
4. Have distilled water jug accessible — do not use tap water for any
   mixing step. Calcium and magnesium in tap water reduce both SAP
   swelling and bentonite gelation efficiency.

# PART A: STANDARD LABORATORY METHOD
## The Full-Quality Reference Batch

This method produces Phase 1 gel at approximately 95-98% of maximum
theoretical quality. It requires 2.5-3 hours including rest periods.
This is the formulation to use for all fire protection testing,
forming tests, and moisture modification tests.

**Total ingredients:**
- SAP: 10g
- Fumed silica: 40g (increased from original 25g — see note below)
- Bentonite: 30g
- Distilled water: approximately 1750mL

```
NOTE ON SILICA QUANTITY — WHY 40g INSTEAD OF 25g:

The geometric analysis of the aerogel transition identified
silica concentration as the dominant variable controlling
Phase C and Phase D protection quality. At 25g silica in
~1750g total gel (1.4% by mass), the silica may be below the
percolation threshold needed for a CONTINUOUS aerogel layer.

Below the percolation threshold: isolated islands of aerogel form.
Above the percolation threshold: a connected aerogel network forms.

A connected network provides dramatically better protection than
isolated islands. The difference is the difference between a
continuous barrier and a barrier with gaps.

At 40g silica in ~1775g total gel (2.25% by mass), the
formulation is more reliably above the percolation threshold
across the range of application conditions. If aerogel formation
is strong at 40g, subsequent batches can explore the lower bound
to find the threshold empirically.

This is the single highest-value change from the original protocol.
```

## A1 — SAP HYDRATION
**Time: 15 minutes active + 30 minutes rest**

### What this step does

10g of SAP powder is hydrated into approximately 1 liter of clear,
viscous gel. The SAP polymer chains absorb water and swell, creating
the sticky, pressure-sensitive network that adheres to surfaces and
holds the other components in place.

### Particle size note

Use SAP in its coarse granule form if you can choose between sizes.
Do NOT grind or pulverize the SAP before use. Larger SAP granules
hydrate more slowly at the particle surface — the outer gel shell
forms before the inner particle is fully hydrated, naturally extending
the window during which silica and bentonite can distribute before
the SAP network fully interconnects. This is counterintuitive but
geometrically important for formulation uniformity.

### Procedure

**A1-1.** Place large mixing bowl on scale. Tare to zero.

**A1-2.** Slowly pour SAP powder into bowl until scale reads 10.0g.
10g looks like approximately one heaping teaspoon — less than you
expect. This is correct. Do not increase the amount.

**A1-3.** Remove bowl from scale. Measure 1000mL distilled water separately.

**A1-4.** Add water to SAP in 10 increments of 100mL each.
After each 100mL addition, stir vigorously for 60 seconds before
adding the next increment. Do not rush this. The slow addition
is what prevents lumps (fish-eyes) that cannot be broken up later.

Total time for all 10 additions: approximately 12 minutes.

**A1-5.** After all 1000mL is added and after the final 60-second stir,
set the bowl aside undisturbed for **30 minutes**.

Do not stir during the rest period. The polymer network needs
undisturbed time to fully interconnect.

### Expected result after 30 minutes

Clear to slightly hazy gel. Consistency like thick aloe vera or
thick hair conditioner. Holds a brief peak when scooped but flows
and levels within 5-10 seconds. If you tilt the bowl, it moves
slowly rather than flowing freely.

### Troubleshooting

Too thin (pours freely like water): Wait additional 15 minutes.
If still thin, add 1g more SAP in 0.5g increments with 15-minute
waits between additions.

Too thick (stiff rubber mass, barely flows): Add 100mL distilled water,
stir thoroughly, wait 10 minutes, reassess.

Lumps visible (hard gel clumps in liquid): The water was added too fast.
Stir vigorously for 5 minutes. If lumps persist, they will not dissolve —
push the mixture through a coarse strainer to remove the largest lumps.
Note this for the next batch (add water more slowly).

## A2 — FUMED SILICA DISPERSION
**Time: 15 minutes active + 10 minutes rest**
**N95 RESPIRATOR ON BEFORE OPENING SILICA CONTAINER**

### What this step does

40g of fumed silica is dispersed into 350mL of water to create
a uniform milky suspension. This suspension carries the silica
particles that will self-assemble into an aerogel layer when the
final gel is exposed to heat. Uniform dispersion here is what
produces a continuous aerogel layer rather than isolated islands.

### Procedure

**A2-1.** Confirm N95 respirator is on. Confirm gloves are on.

**A2-2.** Place small bowl or glass jar on scale. Tare to zero.

**A2-3.** Open fumed silica container slowly and gently. Do not shake it.
Scoop silica slowly with a spoon, keeping the spoon close to the bowl
surface. Do not pour from height. Stop at 40.0g.

**A2-4.** Close and seal the fumed silica container immediately.

**A2-5.** Measure 350mL of distilled water into a separate container
(not the bowl with the silica — you are adding silica TO the water).

**A2-6.** Add the silica to the water in 8 portions of approximately 5g each.
For each portion:
- Slowly sift the silica into the water by gently tapping the bowl
- The silica will float on the water surface initially — this is expected
- Immediately begin whisking vigorously for 90 seconds
- Force the floating powder into the water with the whisk motion
- After 90 seconds, the water should appear uniformly cloudy

Add the next portion only after the previous portion is uniformly incorporated.

**A2-7.** After all 40g has been added, whisk continuously for an additional
4 minutes. Vary the whisk motion — circular, figure-8, back-and-forth —
to ensure uniform dispersion.

If you have an immersion blender, use it at medium speed for 3 minutes
instead of the final 4-minute whisk. The blender produces significantly
better particle dispersion than hand whisking and is recommended if available.

**A2-8.** Allow the suspension to rest for 10 minutes.

**A2-9.** After 10 minutes, inspect the surface. If white powder has
re-floated to the surface (visible as a white film or patches), some
silica was not fully wetted. Whisk for another 2 minutes.

**A2-10.** Once no floating powder is visible, you may carefully remove
your N95 respirator. The silica is now locked in water. The container
is sealed. The airborne risk is eliminated.

### Expected result

Approximately 390mL of uniformly milky white liquid — like skim milk
or very diluted white paint. Uniform cloudiness throughout the volume,
no clear zones, no floating powder on top. Slightly slippery feel
if you rub a drop between gloved fingers.

### Troubleshooting

Clear liquid with white sediment on bottom: Silica has settled —
not adequately dispersed. Return N95 to face (stirring re-suspends
particles), whisk vigorously for 3 minutes, re-inspect.

Thick paste rather than milky liquid: Too little water for the
silica volume. Add 50mL additional distilled water, whisk 2 minutes.

## A3 — BENTONITE DISPERSION
**Time: 10 minutes active + 45 minutes rest**

### What this step does

30g of sodium bentonite clay is dispersed into 400mL of water. Bentonite
clay platelets hydrate and form a loosely interconnected network throughout
the water phase. This network is responsible for the gel's thixotropic
behavior — the property that allows it to flow when squeezed through a
nozzle but hold position on a vertical surface when the pressure stops.

Bentonite also reinforces the aerogel layer in Phase C and Phase D —
an emergent positive not visible until the fully compounded gel is tested.
The clay platelets integrate into the silica aerogel matrix, increasing
its mechanical durability and resistance to flaking under physical
disturbance. This directly addresses the primary weakness of pure silica
aerogel (mechanical fragility) without any additional component.

For applications requiring higher yield stress (forming medium, high-pressure
deployment, coating of smooth vertical surfaces): increase bentonite to 40g
in this step and reduce water by 50mL to compensate. Higher bentonite = more
resistance to slumping under compression or gravity.

### Procedure

**A3-1.** Place medium bowl on scale. Tare to zero.

**A3-2.** Scoop bentonite clay into bowl until scale reads 30.0g.
Bentonite is a grey-tan fine powder. No respirator required for bentonite
at this quantity — it is not hazardous by inhalation at normal working
conditions.

**A3-3.** Add 400mL of distilled water to the bentonite bowl in two additions:
- Pour 200mL, whisk for 90 seconds
- Pour remaining 200mL, whisk for 90 seconds

**A3-4.** After both additions are mixed, whisk continuously for 3 minutes.

The mixture will look like light tan, slightly cloudy water at this point —
not very thick. This is correct. The thickening comes during the rest period.

**A3-5.** Set bowl aside undisturbed for **45 minutes minimum**.
(60 minutes is better if you have time. Longer rest = better clay hydration
= stronger thixotropic network = better vertical adhesion in final gel.)

Do not stir during the rest period.

**A3-6.** After the rest period, perform the thixotropy check:

Stir the bentonite mixture vigorously for 30 seconds.
Remove the spoon. Set a timer.
Watch the surface.

PASS: Within 2 minutes, the surface stops moving and the mixture begins
to hold its shape. When you prod it gently with the spoon, it offers
slight resistance — more than water.

FAIL: The mixture remains completely liquid with no recovery. Stir in
an additional 10g of bentonite, wait 30 more minutes, re-test.

### Expected result

Approximately 430mL of grey-tan, mildly thickened liquid with observable
thixotropy. Moves like thin gravy when stirred, firms up slightly when
left alone. Much less viscous than the SAP gel — the thickening role
of bentonite is primarily felt in the combined gel, not in isolation.

## A4 — COMPOUNDING: ASSEMBLING THE FINAL GEL
**Time: 25 minutes active + 30 minutes rest**

### The sequencing geometry

The order of addition in this step is geometrically critical.
Bentonite integrates with the SAP network first. The combined
SAP-bentonite matrix then receives the silica suspension.

This sequence ensures:
1. The clay network forms within the SAP polymer matrix (correct)
2. Silica particles distribute into an already-structured matrix (correct)

Reversed sequence (silica first, bentonite second) would allow
silica to aggregate before the clay network can separate and
distribute the particles, producing a less uniform result.

### Procedure

**A4-1.** Confirm you currently have:
- Large bowl: SAP gel (~1000g)
- Medium bowl: Bentonite dispersion (~430g)
- Small bowl/jar: Fumed silica suspension (~390g)

**A4-2.** BENTONITE INTO SAP:
Pour the bentonite dispersion slowly into the SAP gel bowl.
Pour in a thin, steady stream while stirring the SAP with your
other hand or with a whisk.

Stir continuously as you pour. The mixture will become slightly
thicker and take on a grey-tan tint as the bentonite integrates.

After all bentonite is added, whisk vigorously for **3 minutes**.
The mixture should become uniform — no visible streaks of tan
running through clearer SAP gel.

**A4-3.** SILICA INTO SAP+BENTONITE:
Divide the silica suspension mentally into 6 equal portions.

Pour the first portion into the SAP+bentonite bowl slowly, in a thin
stream moving around the bowl. Do not pour into one spot.
Whisk for 2 minutes.

Pour the second portion. Whisk 2 minutes. Repeat for all 6 portions.

After all silica is added, perform a final extended mixing session:
- Hand whisk: 6 minutes continuous
- Immersion blender: 3 minutes at medium speed (preferred)

The gel will become visibly whiter, thicker, and more substantial
with each silica addition. By the final addition, it should coat
the whisk heavily and take several seconds to drip off when lifted.

**A4-4.** Set the large bowl aside undisturbed for **30 minutes**.

During this rest, the three components fully integrate into a
unified composite gel. The bentonite-SAP-silica network establishes
its final structure. Do not stir.

### Expected result after rest

Approximately 1820g (~1.8 liters) of white to milky grey-white gel.

Physical properties to confirm:
- **Color:** White or off-white. More opaque than any single component.
- **Consistency:** Like thick yogurt or thick white wood glue.
  Holds a peak when scooped — a spoon dragged through leaves a channel
  that stays open for 3-5 seconds before slowly closing.
- **Flow:** Pours very slowly. Does not flow freely. Requires deliberate
  tilting and several seconds before it begins to move.
- **Recovery (thixotropy):** Stir vigorously 30 seconds, leave alone.
  Within 2 minutes, the gel should firm up noticeably. This is the
  property that makes it flow through a nozzle under pressure and then
  hold on a vertical surface when pressure stops.
- **Smell:** Mild, inoffensive. Slightly earthy from the bentonite.
  No chemical odor. No ammonia. No sulfur. All components are food-grade.

### Troubleshooting

Gel is too thin (does not hold peaks, runs freely):
Option 1: Add 5g more SAP in 1g increments dissolved in 50mL water,
mix thoroughly between each addition, wait 15 minutes after final addition.
Option 2: Add 10g more bentonite, stir thoroughly, wait 30 minutes.
Option 1 addresses water retention. Option 2 addresses structural network.

Gel is too thick (barely flows, does not pass through a 5mm opening
under firm hand pressure):
Add 100mL distilled water, whisk thoroughly, wait 10 minutes, reassess.
Too-thick gel fails the deployability test and is difficult to apply
evenly on surfaces.

White lumps visible in the gel (isolated clumps of silica):
The silica suspension was not uniform before compounding, or was added
too fast. Push the gel through a coarse strainer to remove the largest
aggregates. Note the issue and adjust the silica dispersion step in
the next batch (slower addition, more whisking time, or use immersion blender).

# PART B: RAPID DEPLOYMENT VARIANT
## The Emergency Packet Test

This section tests the rapid deployment architecture derived from
the geometric analysis of deployment requirements. The goal is to
determine how much gel quality is retained when all preparation
steps are eliminated and the protocol reduces to: drop packet in
water, shake, deploy.

This test runs IN PARALLEL with Part A. You do not need to produce
a second separate batch — the same compounding session informs
both the standard protocol and the rapid deployment variant design.
The rapid deployment test uses a pre-mixed dry blend rather than
the sequenced addition of Part A.

**What the rapid deployment variant tests:**

Whether a pre-mixed dry blend of all three Phase 1 components,
sealed in a water-soluble PVA packet, produces acceptable-quality
gel when dropped in water and shaken — without any sequenced
addition, without any laboratory procedure, with no user knowledge
of the formulation.

**Why this matters:**

The standard protocol requires approximately 2.5 hours and
sequential steps. The rapid deployment packet requires 60-90 seconds
and zero expertise. The question is how much quality is sacrificed
for that accessibility. If the quality is adequate (>70% of standard
protocol performance), the packet design is viable for emergency
field deployment.

## B1 — DRY BLEND PREPARATION

**The dry blend formulation (per 1-liter packet):**

```
Component       Mass     Notes
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SAP powder      10g      Coarse granules — do NOT grind
Fumed silica    40g      Same mass as standard protocol
Bentonite       30g      Same mass as standard protocol
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Total dry:      80g      Per 1-liter-of-water deployment unit
```

**N95 respirator on before handling fumed silica powder.**

**B1-1.** Tare a medium bowl on the scale.

**B1-2.** Add each component in sequence, weighing each addition:
- Add bentonite: 30g. Stir briefly with a dry spoon to break any clumps.
- Add SAP: 10g. Stir briefly to distribute through bentonite.
- Add fumed silica: 40g. Stir gently — the silica is very light and
  will float above the other powders initially. Stir slowly and
  deliberately to incorporate without generating a dust cloud.

**B1-3.** Once all three powders are in the bowl, stir the dry blend
gently for 2 minutes. The goal is uniform distribution of all
three components throughout the blend.

**B1-4.** Close the fumed silica container. Remove N95 if not
working with silica further.

**B1-5.** Weigh the combined dry blend. Record this weight — it should
be approximately 80g. This is your packet content.

## B2 — PACKET CONSTRUCTION

**Materials:**
- PVA water-soluble film (purchased from craft store)
  Note: The film sold as "cold water soluble" dissolves in
  approximately 30-60 seconds in cold water. "Warm water soluble"
  dissolves in 10-20 seconds. Both work. Cold-water-soluble
  gives slightly more dispersion time before the SAP gels.
- Heat sealer OR a clothes iron + parchment paper
  (parchment paper protects the iron from the PVA film)

**B2-1.** Cut two pieces of PVA film, each approximately 15cm × 20cm.

**B2-2.** Lay one piece flat on a non-stick surface.
Pour the 80g dry blend onto the center of the film, leaving
at least 3cm clear border around all edges.

**B2-3.** Lay the second piece of PVA film on top.

**B2-4.** Seal three of the four edges:
- Heat sealer method: run the sealer along the edge, 3 seconds per
  section, overlapping seals for continuity
- Iron method: set iron to medium heat (no steam), lay parchment
  paper over the PVA edge, press iron firmly for 5-7 seconds per
  section along the edge

**B2-5.** Before sealing the final edge, check that the dry powder
is settled flat in the packet and not caught in the edge zone
to be sealed. Gently shake to settle if needed.

**B2-6.** Seal the final edge. The packet should now be fully sealed
with powder inside.

**B2-7.** Immediately seal the PVA packet inside a moisture-proof outer
pouch (a standard zip-lock freezer bag works). The PVA film begins
to soften if exposed to ambient humidity over time.

**B2-8.** Label the packet: "EHAB Phase 1 — Add to 1L water, shake 60 sec"

## B3 — RAPID DEPLOYMENT ACTIVATION TEST

**B3-1.** Measure exactly 1000mL of distilled water into a large
bowl or bucket. The same water volume as Step A1.

**B3-2.** Remove the PVA packet from its outer moisture-proof pouch.
Inspect the packet — the PVA film should be intact, no moisture
ingress into the packet.

**B3-3.** Drop the packet into the water. Note the time.

**B3-4.** Begin shaking or stirring immediately.
- If shaking a closed container: vigorous shaking
- If stirring an open bowl: whisk vigorously in continuous motion

The PVA film will begin to dissolve within 15-30 seconds.
You will see the film become translucent, then transparent,
then disappear as the powder contacts the water.

**B3-5.** Continue vigorous stirring for **60 seconds** after the
film has fully dissolved and all powder is in contact with water.

**B3-6.** After 60 seconds of active stirring, allow the mixture
to rest for **5 minutes** without stirring.

**B3-7.** After 5 minutes, observe and assess the gel.

## C1 — VERTICAL ADHESION TEST
**Time: 5 min setup + 30 min observation**

### Purpose
Confirms the gel adheres to vertical surfaces without sliding.
This is the minimum requirement for any surface protection application.

### Materials
- Gel from Part A: approximately 50g per test surface
- 2-3 vertical surfaces to test:
  Surface 1: Raw wood (plywood or dimensional lumber) — most important
  Surface 2: Smooth painted surface or drywall
  Surface 3: Glass or tile (if available — tests behavior on non-porous surface)

### Procedure

**C1-1.** Lean or tape test surfaces to be exactly vertical (90°).

**C1-2.** Mark a 10cm × 15cm rectangle on each surface with a marker.

**C1-3.** Apply gel within the rectangle to approximately 3mm thickness.
3mm is the thickness of two stacked credit cards. Even application
across the entire rectangle is the goal — edges and corners included.

**C1-4.** The moment gel is applied, photograph from directly in front.

## C2 — FIRE PROTECTION DURATION TEST (TORCH TEST)
**Time: 15 min setup + 60 min testing**

### Purpose
Quantifies the fire protection performance by comparing time-to-ignition
of gel-coated wood panels versus bare wood control panels under identical
torch application conditions.

### Setup — do before lighting torch

1. Move outdoors or to fully ventilated space.
2. Position metal sample holder on non-combustible surface.
3. Place water bucket within arm's reach.
4. Prepare 6 matched wood panels (same species, same cut, same approximate
   moisture content):
   - Group A (Control): 3 panels, no treatment
   - Group B (Coated): 3 panels, gel applied to top face at 3mm thickness
5. Apply gel to Group B panels. Allow 5 minutes before testing.
6. Measure and fix your torch distance at **25mm** from panel surface.
   This is approximately 1 inch — the distance from the tip of your
   outstretched thumb to the first knuckle.
7. Start video recording on phone before lighting torch for each test.

### Procedure — Group A (Control)

Test each of 3 bare panels. For each:

**C2-A1.** Place bare panel on metal holder, wood surface facing up.

**C2-A2.** Position torch at 25mm, aimed straight down. Start timer.
Light torch.

**C2-A3.** Hold position. Do not move the torch. Record timestamps for:
- **T-smoke:** First visible smoke rising from wood surface
- **T-char:** First visible blackening of wood surface
- **T-ignite:** First independent flame from wood surface (separate from torch)

**C2-A4.** At T-ignite + 2 minutes, briefly remove torch for 5 seconds.
Observe: does wood continue burning without torch (self-sustained)?
Record: Y / N

**C2-A5.** Maximum test duration: 5 minutes per panel.
If no ignition in 5 minutes, record ">300s."
Extinguish burning panel immediately by submerging in water bucket.

**C2-A6.** After cooling 5 minutes, measure maximum char depth with ruler.

### Procedure — Group B (Gel-Coated)

Test each of 3 gel-coated panels. For each:

**C2-B1.** Place coated panel on metal holder, gel side facing up.

**C2-B2.** Same 25mm distance, same straight-down angle. Start timer. Light torch.

**C2-B3.** Record timestamps for:
- **T-steam:** First visible steam rising from gel surface
  (not smoke — steam is white, clear, quickly dissipating)
  This is the gel's thermal buffering mechanism in operation.
- **T-gel-change:** First visible change in gel appearance in flame zone
  (yellowing, browning, surface drying, loss of shiny wet appearance)
- **T-white:** First visible white residue forming where gel has dried
  This is the silica aerogel transition — the Phase C mechanism.
  Describe what it looks like: ____
- **T-char:** First visible charring or blackening at gel surface
- **T-ignite:** First independent flame from wood surface (if it occurs)

**C2-B4.** Self-sustained burn test: same as Group A.

**C2-B5.** Maximum test duration: **10 minutes per panel**.
(Extended from control panels because the gel provides sustained protection.)
If no ignition in 10 minutes, record ">600s" — this is an excellent result.

**C2-B6.** After cooling, photograph charring pattern. Describe:
- Area of visible aerogel residue (rough estimate: ___cm²)
- Color and texture of aerogel residue: ____
- Whether aerogel appears continuous or patchy: ____

## C3 — AEROGEL TRANSITION ISOLATION TEST
**Time: 5 min setup + 30 min drying observation**

### Purpose
Tests the aerogel transition in isolation — using controlled,
sustained heat rather than a direct torch flame — so the transition
can be observed without the confounding variable of combustion.
This also tests Phase D residue quality as a standalone ceramic barrier.

### Procedure

**C3-1.** Apply approximately 50g of gel to each of 3 flat metal plates.
Spread to approximately 2mm thickness. Weigh each plate+gel, record weight.

**C3-2.** Apply heat using ONE of the following methods:
- Oven at 200°C for 25 minutes (preferred — most uniform heat application)
- Heat gun at 15-20cm distance, slowly swept across surface, 10-15 minutes
- Torch at 20cm distance (not 25mm — far enough for sustained heat, not flame impingement)

**C3-3.** Photograph every 5 minutes.

**C3-4.** When gel appears fully dry (no more steam, surface is not shiny,
gel has hardened or cracked), remove from heat. Allow to cool 5 minutes.

**C3-5.** Weigh the dried plate (you know the empty plate weight from before).
The difference is the dried residue mass.
Dried residue mass = (plate + residue weight) − plate weight = ____g

**C3-6.** With gloved finger, gently press on the dried residue.

**C3-7.** Using a spatula, gently try to lift a corner of the residue.

### What to look for

```
EXCELLENT AEROGEL RESULT:
  - White or off-white layer covering the entire plate
  - Layer has structural integrity — does not crumble immediately
    when touched, offers slight resistance when pressed
  - Lifts as a thin flexible or semi-rigid sheet
  - When rubbed: leaves white powder on finger — like chalk —
    indicating silica-clay composite material
  - Layer is visibly porous or textured, not glassy

ACCEPTABLE RESULT:
  - White or off-white layer present
  - Some fragility — partially crumbles when touched
  - Covers most of the plate area (>70%)

INSUFFICIENT SILICA RESULT:
  - Minimal white residue, mostly clear or transparent dried gel
  - Black or brown residue (charred polymer — no aerogel formed)
  - Bare metal plate visible in most areas after drying
  Action: Increase silica to 50g in next batch

EXCESS SILICA RESULT:
  - Very thick, glassy-looking white layer
  - Cracks aggressively during drying into large rigid islands
  - Provides mechanical barrier but may flake off in use
  Action: Reduce silica to 35g in next batch
```

```
Plate 1: Dried residue mass ____g  Color ____  Continuous? Y/N
         Lifts as sheet? Y/N  Texture description: ____
Plate 2: [same fields]
Plate 3: [same fields]

Aerogel formation assessment: Excellent / Acceptable / Insufficient / Excess
Next batch silica adjustment? Keep 40g / Increase to 50g / Reduce to 35g
Reason: ____
```

## D1 — OXYGEN EXCLUSION DEMONSTRATION (APPLE TEST)
**Time: 5 min setup + 4 hours observation**

### Purpose
Demonstrates the oxygen exclusion property of the gel in a context
completely unrelated to fire. Apple browning is driven by polyphenol
oxidase reacting with oxygen at the cut surface. Excluding oxygen
prevents browning. This is the same property that prevents combustion
in fire applications — the gel does not know the difference.

This test is simultaneously a demonstration of the platform breadth
and a validation of oxygen exclusion as a distinct property.

### Procedure

**D1-1.** Cut an apple in half across the middle.

**D1-2.** Apply gel to the cut surface of ONE half only (test half).
Spread gel to approximately 2mm thickness covering the entire cut surface.
Leave the other half completely bare (control half).

**D1-3.** Place both halves cut-surface-up on a flat surface.
Do not refrigerate — room temperature is required.

**D1-4.** Photograph both halves immediately (T=0), then at:
T=30min, T=1hr, T=2hr, T=4hr.

**D1-5.** At the 4-hour mark, remove the gel from the test half
by wiping gently with a damp cloth. Photograph the underlying
apple surface and compare to the control half.

**Expected result:**
Control half: Significant browning (oxidation) of cut surface by 2 hours.
Test half (under gel): Minimal to no browning. The cut surface should
appear nearly fresh when the gel is removed.

This result directly validates the oxygen exclusion mechanism that also
prevents combustion in the fire protection application.

### Record

```
T=0:    Control half appearance: ____   Test half appearance: ____
T=30min: Control: ____                  Test (gel still on): ____
T=1hr:  Control: ____                  Test: ____
T=2hr:  Control: ____                  Test: ____
T=4hr:  Control: ____                  Test (gel removed): ____

Browning comparison (scale 0-10, 0=no brown, 10=fully brown):
  Control at 4hr: ____/10
  Test at 4hr (after gel removal): ____/10

Oxygen exclusion validated? Y / N
```

## D3 — FORMING MEDIUM TEST (THERMAL COMPRESSION)
**Time: 30 minutes active + 60 minutes oven time**

### Purpose
Tests whether the gel functions as a forming medium during heat
and compression application — the application class derived from
the geometric identity between oxygen exclusion + thermal buffering
and controlled thermal processing of flammable solids.

This test directly evaluates the most immediately testable forming
application: hot-press forming with a controlled thermal ceiling.

### Procedure

**D3-1.** Select two matched wood samples — same species, same dimensions,
approximately 15cm × 5cm × 2cm. Ideally from the same piece of wood.

**D3-2.** Soak both samples in distilled water for 30 minutes.
Remove, shake off excess water. Both samples should be at elevated
moisture content (heavy, slightly darker than dry). This simulates
optimal starting moisture for forming and reduces crack risk.

**D3-3.** Apply gel to all surfaces of SAMPLE B only, at 5mm thickness.
Fully coat all six faces. Sample A remains uncoated (control).

**D3-4.** Apply compression to both samples simultaneously using
C-clamps or by stacking heavy books:
- Compress to approximately 50-60% of original thickness
  (if original is 2cm, compress to approximately 1-1.2cm)
- Apply uniform pressure across the face using flat metal plates
  between the clamp and the wood surface (prevents point loading)

**D3-5.** With compression applied, place both compressed samples
in oven at 180°C for 40 minutes.

**D3-6.** After 40 minutes, remove from oven WITHOUT releasing compression.
Allow to cool to room temperature (approximately 30-45 minutes) while
maintaining compression.

**D3-7.** Release compression. Remove gel from Sample B by wiping
with damp cloth.

**D3-8.** Compare the two samples:

```
MEASUREMENTS:
  Thickness after forming (no springback) — Sample A: ____cm  Sample B: ____cm
  Surface hardness comparison (scratch test — draw the same object
    across both samples with equal pressure): which is harder? A / B / same
  Color: Sample A surface: ____  Sample B surface: ____
  Charring on Sample A surface? Y / N
  Charring on Sample B surface? Y / N
  Gel residue on Sample B surface? Y / N — removes with water? Y / N

FORMING ASSESSMENT:
  Did Sample B compress more uniformly than Sample A? Y / N
  Did Sample B show less surface damage (charring, cracking)? Y / N
  Is the forming medium concept validated at this scale? Y / N
```

# APPENDIX 2: FAILURE MODE CORRECTIONS

```
FAILURE MODE 1: GEL RUNS OFF VERTICAL SURFACE (<80% retention)
  Root cause: Insufficient bentonite concentration.
              Bentonite is the structural network that holds
              the gel against gravity.
  Correction: Increase bentonite from 30g to 40g in next batch.
              Use same water volume (1750mL).
              Extend bentonite rest period to 60 minutes.
  Secondary check: Was the bentonite thixotropy test passed in Step A3?
              If bentonite showed no thixotropy in isolation, the
              bentonite product may be low-quality or contaminated.

FAILURE MODE 2: GEL WILL NOT FLOW THROUGH 5mm OPENING
  Root cause: Gel is too thick — over-concentrated in SAP or bentonite.
  Correction: Add 100-150mL distilled water to existing batch.
              Stir thoroughly. Wait 10 minutes. Retest.
              For next batch: reduce bentonite to 25g.

FAILURE MODE 3: FIRE PROTECTION RATIO <2x (MARGINAL OR FAIL)
  Root cause A: Silica aerogel layer insufficient — patchy or absent.
  Correction A: Increase silica from 40g to 50g in next batch.
                Ensure silica dispersion is fully uniform before
                compounding (no floating powder after 10-min rest).
  
  Root cause B: Gel too thin — water depletes too quickly at the
                test torch distance (25mm is intense heat flux).
  Correction B: Increase SAP from 10g to 12g to hold more water
                per unit volume of gel.
  
  Root cause C: Gel was applied too thin (<2mm) on test panels.
  Correction C: Increase application thickness to 4-5mm and retest
                at the same formulation.

FAILURE MODE 4: NO WHITE AEROGEL RESIDUE AFTER HEAT DRYING
  Root cause A: Silica concentration insufficient (most likely).
  Correction A: Increase silica to 50g in next batch.
  
  Root cause B: Silica not uniformly dispersed — settled to bottom
                of the gel during rest period.
  Correction B: Use immersion blender for silica dispersion step.
                Stir gel before applying to any surface.
  
  Root cause C: Heat application was too brief — gel not fully dried.
  Correction C: Extend oven drying to 30 minutes at 200°C.
                Ensure no visible wet zones remain before assessment.

FAILURE MODE 5: FISH-EYES (HARD LUMPS) IN FINAL GEL
  Root cause: SAP gelled before silica and bentonite distributed.
  Correction: In next batch, add bentonite first and stir for
              5 minutes before any silica addition.
              Divide silica addition into 8 portions instead of 6.
              If persistent: add the silica suspension to water first,
              then add bentonite, then add SAP last in very small
              increments (2g at a time) with thorough mixing between.

FAILURE MODE 6: RAPID DEPLOYMENT PACKET — POOR QUALITY GEL
  Root cause: SAP gelled before silica/bentonite distributed.
  Correction A: Switch to two-packet sequential system (B5).
  Correction B: Use larger SAP granules (slower hydration rate).
  Correction C: Reduce SAP mass in dry blend from 10g to 8g
                (lower concentration = slower network formation).
```
