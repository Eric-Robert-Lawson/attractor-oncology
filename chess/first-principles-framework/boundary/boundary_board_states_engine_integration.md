# Engine Integration: d-depth Certification as a Modular Add-on

**Status.** A record of the direction the designer articulated between 16 and 19 November 2026 for integrating the program with a search engine. It states how the d-depth metric joins heuristic search and exhaustive coverage, how the engine's bias coverage and engine self-play compose with the part-wise structure, and what is left open. It is stated in general terms: no piece, material, position or engine is part of it. It adds no primitive and does not touch the hard core (`boundary_board_states.md` §14 items 7 to 11). Statements marked **the designer's** are the designer's. Statements marked **this file's reading** are readings of the designer's words, offered for correction. No measurement is recorded in this file.

Companion documents: `boundary_board_states_d_depth_applications.md` (the applications, including the depth limit and play analysis), `boundary_board_states_calibration.md` (the stored band and what absence means), `boundary_board_states_cost_model.md` (the priors as a storage cost), `boundary_board_states_start_selection.md` (§2g depth-scoped certification and dependency across segments), `boundary_board_states_batch_composition.md` (the modes and the fetchable batches).

## 1. The designer's statements (16 to 19 November 2026)

1. **One metric between two approaches.** The d-depth metric bridges the exhaustive coverage approach and the heuristic depth approach of engines. In this use the program is a modular addition to an engine, as the tablebase modules that engines already carry are.
2. **Heuristic search becomes partial certification.** A search engine that is calibrated by d-depth and integrated with the program converts heuristic search into partial d-depth certificates. This can be achieved easily relative to the exhaustive coverage front, and a hybrid approach is possible.
3. **The engine's reach and the human's.** An engine has a point at which it understands d-depth as unguided search without a table. The d-depth that a human can reach with no prior context to inform optimality is a parameter that can be derived. The engine can use the difference between its own certification requirement and that parameter to make decisions, or to make moves that are more human, because the decision is made within the same scope of human reachability.
4. **The anchoring point is the d-depth architecture.** The approach does not depend on a tablebase foundation. The priors are a variable. The approach works with no priors and with priors, and priors optimize it at the cost of the storage they occupy. They can be optimized cumulatively with the program's progress within the d-depth scope. The region of interest can be enumerated constructively, adding to the engine what is wanted and excluding what is not.
5. **The limit is the space to resolve.** What measures human reachability is not d alone but the space that the certification has to resolve, which d bounds. A human can look ahead with certainty very deep where the search space is not combinatorially complex. The limitation is the requirement to resolve the space, which is the same requirement that the certification in the program has. The d-depth limit for study is therefore not a hard-coded limit. It is a quantity derived from the proof-size limitation, which calculates it.
6. **The engine is a black box, and segmentation belongs to the tables.** An engine does not consider the irreversible segmented boundaries in its choices. They matter when the residual tables are constructed on the back end to accumulate progress. The black box helps enumerate the tables, and the tables inform the black box, as a feedback loop. This is a deliberate architectural advantage, acceptable even if it is not provably better than the certainty that d-depth alone gives. The engine can keep bad lines that are exploitable at a certain depth requirement, and they can be identified when they are arrived at. When the engine consults the program's tables a different route can emerge, because segmented dependencies can resolve a better route that depended on priors beyond the d-depth scope.
7. **The human parameter is open.** The human limitation that follows from proof size has an answer that can be sourced eventually. It informs the human limitation, and it does not exclude chess knowledge and priors that do not depend on proof size. It can help to detect cheating and is not an absolute qualifier.
8. **Which engines are compatible.** The calibration of d-depth certification that the program's architecture outlines tells which engines are compatible with the approach. An engine that can be calibrated by the search depth required to make moves under certainty is important to integrate. The program can then resolve tables on the back end as the engine progresses and plays more games. The designer states that the loop is resolved, and that it depends on finding the proof size for the human limitation and the engine that fits the compatibility requirements best.
9. **Bias coverage is an asset.** What an engine's priors cause it to examine is a bias coverage, and it is a deliberate part of the approach. The d-depth limit makes the result not exact, because resolving a line can depend on plies deeper than d. When a line that follows the bias meets a counter, play at a depth beyond human capacity from the point where it is losing either shows that the line is beaten or enumerates the better branch that the bias did not take. That branch needed awareness at a depth the bias coverage did not have. The program can then direct the engine to resolve by another route. A catalog of the answers that differ by bias records these, and excluding branches as options gives variance where it is wanted. The d-depth that was not available from the initial position can be deployed on later lines to make up the difference.
10. **The engine faces itself.** The calibration can differ between the human and the engine, and the engine can face itself. The opposition is then strong and beyond human capacity. The choices enumerate the refutations most often, which is where the lines that are not wanted are enumerated out. The engine uses the progress that the program has enumerated to try to find ways to refute its own bias.
11. **The part-wise composition carries over to self-play.** Any number of cataloged bias coverages can be placed in the schema, and each can be optimized relative to the others. Engine self-play makes the program accumulate certainty, bias coverage differentiates the part-wise action, and each part has an explicit method for its optimization, integrated into the engine.
12. **The ranking is compositional.** Bias coverages are not ranked against one another by a separate measure. Composed properly, they converge towards ground truth, and the outcome of the composition has more fidelity and resolution according to the dependencies that are composed. The approach is to treat them as interdependent coverage certificates.
13. **Joint optimization chains the certainty beyond one window.** Composed together, the certificates give certainty from an initial position to a depth beyond what one d-depth gives, for example 50 plies from the initial position against 80 or more when the anchoring point downstream differs and gives more resolution. The longest gap still has to fit, and the gaps are filled with resolved positions to complete the chain within the depth requirement. The d-depth that lies downstream is learned only by resolving and understanding through the process. It can cause a deviation at the initial move when the opportunity cost of a refutation makes a different branch more interesting. Removal always costs, either the storage that is kept or the work that another prior does in its place. What is truly not difficult to derive under certainty is removed, and what is difficult is kept.
14. **The gaps are deliberate.** The certified tree is not stored whole. The gaps are chosen to manage storage and to be the easiest to resolve, and storing the whole tree would carry the storage limit that Syzygy has. The self-play enumerates the points of opposition that are likely to be refutation points, the holes in the tree are enumerated again within the gaps of certainty that are provided, and the self-play builds iteratively on the progress. The exactness holds within the scope that is bounded, and that scope is deep enough to gain something useful, at the limit of the paradigm window. The bias coverage also enumerates the branches and openings that are cared about, and the self-play can be calibrated to the goals that are set.
15. **Exhaustive depends on what is covered.** Exhaustive in general means every position of the material is enumerated. Exhaustive certification within d means every reply within d is resolved for the region that is scoped. The table base structure can span exhaustive certification within d without enumerating all the positions. What the program requires is the second. What is stored is a separate matter, and the approach details it as deliberately gapped (item 14 of this file, and `boundary_board_states_calibration.md` §1, items 12, 19 and 25). The generalized approach of the program to exhaustive coverage, without d-depth consideration, holds the framing of the problem. The d-depth application and the engine integration are where the approach differentiates it, and the main documents do not commit to that path before its details are worked out in the d-depth and engine specifications.
16. **The aim is a weak solution in the d-depth sense.** The goal for study was never exhaustive coverage in general. The designer states that exhaustive coverage in general past Syzygy is unobtainable, Syzygy being its most optimized form. It remains an open potential if the architecture allows the limitation to be surpassed with exhaustive coverage, and the designer states that it would not resolve the gap entirely. The aim is the reachability weak solution in the d-depth sense: a solution scoped by the d-depth limitation and the proof size that the program provides, and capped with exactness and soundness within that scope.
17. **The zero-priors case starts from any irreversible segmented boundary.** The case with no priors can begin at any irreversible segmented boundary, including the initial game position. Syzygy is a basis for priors only where it is chosen as one. It is not a scalable basis beyond its own limits, and it may not be required as a basis at all. Working in less complexity gives more certainty, fewer viable deviations that need to be worked out, and less storage to manage as the resolution continues. The part-wise compositional front means the program does not need to fit everything into storage at once, because the gapped approach optimizes for the storage concern. The storage of the part-wise bias coverage informs the gapped storage approach, as compositions of the part-wise dependencies.
18. **The architecture allows a choice of engine.** The solution is architected so that which engine is used is a choice. Any engine that meets the compatibility requirement can serve as the black box, and engines of different roots give different bias coverages. The divergence between them is valued for the uniqueness it brings to the part-wise composition. Self-play can be run in several ways: with no priors, adversarially to find different unique branches, and to stress test existing bias coverage.

## 2. This file's readings, offered for correction

1. **The roles.** The engine plays. The program certifies lines that the engine proposes, and builds tables from what the engine and the study enumerate. The segmented dependencies matter where a line is verified and where tables are built, for example for the value of an exit into a lower segment. They do not enter the engine's own choices (§1, item 6).
2. **Two kinds of statement.** An integrated engine gives certified statements, a result proved within d by exact verification against every reply, and heuristic statements. A verified win is permanent. A line that is not verified proves nothing, and what is not proved stays conditional (calibration §2, item 11). The interface marks which kind a statement is.
3. **Priors and the certified set.** With no priors a line is certified where it ends in a terminal, or in an exit whose value the budget resolves. Priors enlarge the set of certifiable lines and shorten the proofs (§1, item 4). Where the tables exist they also serve as the test oracle for the verifier.
4. **The feedback loop and coverage.** Tables built from an engine's play and enumeration record what was examined. What was not examined stays distinct from what was pruned (batch composition §1.9, item 10). Each certified claim stays sound, and the coverage follows what the play reaches.
5. **Two quantities for the human limit.** Reading §1, item 5, d is the scope of what is certified, and the size of the proof is the cost of certifying it. The d-depth limit for study is derived from the proof-size limit and is not hard-coded. The documents that state the student's limit carry the designer's refinement of 19 November 2026: `boundary_board_states_d_depth_applications.md` §1, item 8, `boundary_board_states.md` (the calibration bullet), `boundary_board_states_calibration.md` §1, item 14, and `boundary_board_states_batch_composition.md` §1.15, item 10.
6. **The compatibility requirement.** Reading §1, item 8, an engine is compatible when the depth or effort at which it finds a move can be reported, and its line can be checked exactly. Which engines meet this is not determined.
7. **Bias is a selection policy.** The priors decide which positions are examined. They do not decide whether an examined result is correct. Certification within d is exact against every reply, so a certified win stays permanent and a conditional draw stays exact within its scope (calibration §2, item 11). The bias determines coverage and never soundness. Reading §1, item 9, the catalog records the selection policy beside d, as the provenance of what was certified.
8. **The limits of what bias failure gives.** Within d the replies of the opposition are exhaustive. Beyond d, the counters that are met depend on the play that is reached, so the refutations enumerated are as broad as the play reached. Where the mistake lies more than d plies before the loss, the loss shows where to look, and not what is better. An alternative at the earlier position is a lead until it is certified within its own d. The designer confirmed these limits on 19 November 2026.
9. **The adversarial configuration.** Reading §1, item 10, one engine plays the biased side and another plays the refuter, with the calibration of each set separately, for example the human-calibrated limit for one and the engine's reach for the other. The verification is the program's, so the refuter's strength decides which refutations are found and does not decide whether one is valid. How often a line is refuted is a signal of which lines to examine and exclude (the work selection of the program). A line is excluded on proof when a specific refutation is verified within its d. A line that is not refuted is not certified beyond d by that fact. Two instances that share an engine and priors share their blind spots, so the refuter differs from the biased side in engine, priors or calibration.
10. **Composition of coverage certificates.** Reading §1, item 12, a coverage certificate holds its scope (d), which positions were examined and which were not, the policy that selected them, and the certificates it depends on (an exit into a lower segment, or a refutation of a line in another coverage). Composition is a merge that is monotone: the union of the wins, and the larger scope for a conditional draw. Two sound certificates cannot contradict each other, so a contradiction between them marks a fault in a verification. The composition therefore guarantees soundness and a certified set that does not decrease. It does not guarantee completeness, and whether the composition for a segment approaches the ground truth is measured against an exhaustive table where one exists, and is not asserted. The dependency among certificates is the one-way dependency from lower to upper segments (`boundary_board_states.md` P-003), so this adds no primitive.
11. **Chaining.** Reading §1, item 13, and §2, item 3, a line is certified where it ends in a terminal or in an anchor whose own value is certified. The depth certified from a root along a line is the depth searched to reach the anchor plus the depth for which the anchor is itself certified. The root's statement is certified when every reply of the opposition has been resolved, either stored or re-derived from the anchors around it. The stored tree can have holes. The self-play places the stored replies at the points where refutations are likely, and the other holes are enumerated again within the gaps that the anchors bound, at the work of the re-derivation instead of the storage. A reply that is not examined stays unexamined and is not assumed safe. The depth is a property of the resolved tree, and individual lines can be shorter or longer. Adding resolved positions in a gap adds anchors and completes the chain, at the storage that each anchor occupies. The depth certified at a root is therefore derived from the chain beneath it, and it is not set at the root.
12. **Information flows backward through the chain, and the keep-or-remove decision follows it.** Reading §1, item 13, what is resolved downstream changes the value of the branches at the root, so a refutation there can change which initial move is chosen. Reading §1, item 9 records the forward direction, in which the depth that was not available at the start is used on later lines. The decision to keep or remove an entry is made after composition, with what the integration has shown. An entry that is costly to resolve again and cheap to store is kept, and an entry that is cheap to derive again is removed. Because the anchors form the chain, the decisions interact, and they are made together and not entry by entry. The storage and search exchange is the cost model's (`boundary_board_states_cost_model.md` §3).
13. **Three words kept apart.** Enumerated means a position is listed. Exhaustively certified within d means every reply within d is resolved for the scoped region (§1, item 15). Stored means the pattern of kept rows holds it (calibration §1.3 and §2, items 9 to 12). In this file, exhaustive and every reply mean exhaustive within d over the scoped region, which the program verifies, and not exhaustive in general and not stored whole. Where another document says "exhaustive coverage" without more, it is read by its context: the Syzygy tables, the exhaustive verification of the rule set, or the scoped region.
14. **Engine choice and exactness.** Reading §1, item 18, the exactness within d is the program's check of a line and does not depend on the engine that produced it, so any compatible engine can be exchanged for another. An engine's own result at a depth is not a certificate where its search uses forward pruning, which can overlook a line (reading 6). Engines of different training lineage can share blind spots, so the divergence that §1, item 18 values is between engines of different roots. It is measured by the distinct refutations that a pair finds, and it is not assumed. The catalog records the engine, its version, its settings and its network as part of the selection policy of a certificate (reading 10). An engine that can be reached only through a remote service cannot be driven locally and is not compatible.

## 3. What is open

- **The human parameter** (§1, items 3 and 7): how the proof size that a human can resolve is sourced, and how it is recorded for a player.
- **The measure of the space to resolve** (§1, item 5): what is counted, and how it combines with d.
- **Which engines are compatible** (§1, item 8) and what the interface between engine and program is.
- **How the tables are built from the engine's play** (§1, item 6): what is recorded, in which mode, and how it is marked as examined.
- **What the engine reads from the tables** when it consults them (§1, item 6).
- **How a statement is marked as certified or heuristic** (§2, item 2).
- **What an interdependent coverage certificate records** (§2, item 10): the fields, how the dependencies among certificates are recorded, and how a contradiction is reported.
- **How a bias coverage is recorded in the catalog** (§1, item 9): the selection policy as a quantity beside d, and how excluded branches are listed.
- **The adversarial configuration** (§1, item 10): which side is calibrated how, and how the refutation frequency is recorded and used to select lines, apart from exclusion on proof.
- **How the depth certified at a root is computed** (§2, item 11): what is counted along the chain, how it is reported for the stored tree, with its holes, and for a line, and how it is recorded.
- **How anchors are placed** (§1, items 13 and 14; §2, item 12): which resolved positions are added to complete a chain, and how that is weighed against the storage they occupy.
- **How the divergence between engine roots is measured and recorded** (§2, item 14): what is counted for a pair, and how it is recorded beside the selection policy.
