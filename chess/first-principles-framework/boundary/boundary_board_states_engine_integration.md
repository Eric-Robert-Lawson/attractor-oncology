# Engine Integration: d-depth Certification as a Modular Add-on

**Status.** A record of the direction the designer articulated between 16 and 20 November 2026 for integrating the program with a search engine. It states how the d-depth metric joins heuristic search and exhaustive coverage, how the engine's bias coverage and engine self-play compose with the part-wise structure, how a population of agents builds priors on a boundary, the two fronts that it serves, and what is left open. It is stated in general terms: no piece, material, position or engine is part of it. It adds no primitive to the primitive catalog (`boundary_board_state_primitive_catalog.md`, whose admission rule is §1) and does not touch the hard core (`boundary_board_states.md` §14 items 7 to 11). The methods, goals and procedures recorded in it belong to the protective belt, and none of them is a primitive. Statements marked **the designer's** are the designer's. Statements marked **this file's reading** are readings of the designer's words, offered for correction. No measurement is recorded in this file.

Companion documents: `boundary_board_states_d_depth_applications.md` (the applications, including the depth limit and play analysis), `boundary_board_states_calibration.md` (the stored band and what absence means), `boundary_board_states_cost_model.md` (the priors as a storage cost), `boundary_board_states_start_selection.md` (§2g depth-scoped certification and dependency across segments), `boundary_board_states_batch_composition.md` (the modes and the fetchable batches).

## 1. The designer's statements (16 to 20 November 2026)

1. **One metric between two approaches.** The d-depth metric bridges the exhaustive coverage approach and the heuristic depth approach of engines. In this use the program is a modular addition to an engine, as the tablebase modules that engines already carry are.
2. **Heuristic search becomes partial certification.** A search engine that is calibrated by d-depth and integrated with the program converts heuristic search into partial d-depth certificates. This can be achieved easily relative to the exhaustive coverage front, and a hybrid approach is possible.
3. **The engine's reach and the human's.** An engine has a point at which it understands d-depth as unguided search without a table. The d-depth that a human can reach with no prior context to inform optimality is a parameter that can be derived. The engine can use the difference between its own certification requirement and that parameter to make decisions, or to make moves that are more human, because the decision is made within the same scope of human reachability.
4. **The anchoring point is the d-depth architecture.** The approach does not depend on a tablebase foundation. The priors are a variable. The approach works with no priors and with priors, and priors optimize it at the cost of the storage they occupy. They can be optimized cumulatively with the program's progress within the d-depth scope. The region of interest can be enumerated constructively, adding to the engine what is wanted and excluding what is not.
5. **The limit is the space to resolve.** What measures human reachability is not d alone but the space that the certification has to resolve, which d bounds. A human can look ahead with certainty very deep where the search space is not combinatorially complex. The limitation is the requirement to resolve the space, which is the same requirement that the certification in the program has. The d-depth limit for study is therefore not a hard-coded limit. It is a quantity derived from the proof-size limitation, which calculates it.
6. **The engine is a black box, and segmentation belongs to the tables.** An engine does not consider the irreversible segmented boundaries in its choices. They matter when the residual tables are constructed on the back end to accumulate progress. The black box helps enumerate the tables, and the tables inform the black box, as a feedback loop. This is a deliberate architectural advantage, acceptable even if it is not provably better than the certainty that d-depth alone gives. The engine can keep bad lines that are exploitable at a certain depth requirement, and they can be identified when they are arrived at. When the engine consults the program's tables a different route can emerge, because segmented dependencies can resolve a better route that depended on priors beyond the d-depth scope.
7. **The human parameter is open.** The human limitation that follows from proof size has an answer that can be sourced eventually. It informs the human limitation, and it does not exclude chess knowledge and priors that do not depend on proof size. It can help to detect cheating and is not an absolute qualifier.
8. **Which engines are compatible.** The calibration of d-depth certification that the program's architecture outlines tells which engines are compatible with the approach. An engine that can be calibrated by the search depth required to make moves under certainty is important to integrate. The program can then resolve tables on the back end as the engine progresses and plays more games. The designer states that the loop is resolved, and that it depends on finding the proof size for the human limitation and the engine that fits the compatibility requirements best.
9. **Bias coverage is an asset.** What an engine's priors cause it to examine is a bias coverage, and it is a deliberate part of the approach. The d-depth limit makes the result not exact, because resolving a line can depend on plies deeper than d. When a line that follows the bias meets a counter, play at a depth beyond human capacity from the point where it is losing either shows that the line is beaten or enumerates the better branch that the bias did not take. That branch needed awareness at a depth the bias coverage did not have. The program can then direct the engine to resolve by another route. A catalog of the answers that differ by bias records these, and excluding branches as options gives variance where it is wanted. The d-depth that was not available from the initial position can be deployed on later lines to make up the difference.
10. **The engine faces itself.** The calibration can differ between the human and the engine, and the engine can face itself. The opposition is then strong and beyond human capacity. The choices enumerate the refutations most often, which is where the lines that are not wanted are enumerated out. The engine uses the progress that the program has enumerated to try to find ways to refute its own bias.
11. **The part-wise composition carries over to self-play.** Any number of cataloged bias coverages can be placed in the schema, and each can be optimized relative to the others. Engine self-play makes the program accumulate certainty, bias coverage differentiates the part-wise action, and each part has an explicit method for its optimization, integrated into the engine.
12. **The ranking is compositional.** Bias coverages are not ranked against one another by a separate measure. Composed properly, they converge towards ground truth, and the outcome of the composition has more fidelity and resolution according to the dependencies that are composed. The approach is to treat them as interdependent coverage certificates.
13. **Joint optimization chains the certainty beyond one window.** Composed together, the certificates give certainty from an initial position to a depth beyond what one d-depth gives, for example 50 plies from the initial position against 80 or more when the anchoring point downstream differs and gives more resolution. The longest gap still has to fit, and the gaps are filled with resolved positions to complete the chain within the depth requirement. The d-depth that lies downstream is learned only by resolving and understanding through the process. It can cause a deviation at the initial move when the opportunity cost of a refutation makes a different branch more interesting. Removal always costs, either the storage that is kept or the work that another prior does in its place. What is truly not difficult to derive under certainty is removed, and what is difficult is kept.
14. **The gaps are deliberate.** The certified tree is not stored whole. The gaps are chosen to manage storage and to be the easiest to resolve, and storing the whole tree would carry the storage limit that Syzygy has. The self-play enumerates the points of opposition that are likely to be refutation points, the holes in the tree are enumerated again within the gaps of certainty that are provided, and the self-play builds iteratively on the progress. The exactness holds within the scope that is bounded, and that scope is deep enough to gain something useful, at the limit of the paradigm window. The bias coverage also enumerates the branches and openings that are cared about, and the self-play can be calibrated to the goals that are set.
15. **Exhaustive depends on what is covered.** Exhaustive in general means every position of the material is enumerated. Exhaustive certification within d means every reply within d is resolved for the region that is scoped. The table base structure can span exhaustive certification within d without enumerating all the positions. What the program requires is the second. What is stored is a separate matter, and the approach details it as deliberately gapped (item 14 of this file, and `boundary_board_states_calibration.md` §1, items 12, 19 and 25). The generalized approach of the program to exhaustive coverage, without d-depth consideration, holds the framing of the problem. The d-depth application and the engine integration are where the approach differentiates it, and the main documents do not commit to that path before its details are worked out in the d-depth and engine specifications.
16. **The aim is a weak solution in the d-depth sense.** The goal for study was never exhaustive coverage in general. The designer states that exhaustive coverage in general past Syzygy is unobtainable, Syzygy being its most optimized form. It remains an open potential if the architecture allows the limitation to be surpassed with exhaustive coverage, and the designer states that it would not resolve the gap entirely. The aim is the reachability weak solution in the d-depth sense: a solution scoped by the d-depth limitation and the proof size that the program provides, and capped with exactness and soundness within that scope.
17. **The zero-priors case starts from any irreversible segmented boundary.** The case with no priors can begin at any irreversible segmented boundary, including the initial game position. Syzygy is a basis for priors only where it is chosen as one. It is not a scalable basis beyond its own limits, and it may not be required as a basis at all. Working in less complexity gives more certainty, fewer viable deviations that need to be worked out, and less storage to manage as the resolution continues. The part-wise compositional front means the program does not need to fit everything into storage at once, because the gapped approach optimizes for the storage concern. The storage of the part-wise bias coverage informs the gapped storage approach, as compositions of the part-wise dependencies.
18. **The architecture allows a choice of engine.** The solution is architected so that which engine is used is a choice. Any engine that meets the compatibility requirement can serve as the black box, and engines of different roots give different bias coverages. The divergence between them is valued for the uniqueness it brings to the part-wise composition. Self-play can be run in several ways: with no priors, adversarially to find different unique branches, and to stress test existing bias coverage.
19. **Self-play is goal-oriented, and the loop changes with it.** Each iteration of the loop has a goal for its self-play, and the self-play allows variance. The loop as stated in §2, reading 15, is the base form, and it changes with goal-oriented self-play. The designer's examples of goals: specifying and enumerating the refutation branches; continuing to enumerate an existing branch to further resolution; and mining the space, systematically, for better branches that resolve only at a greater depth. The designer's non-trivial example is the part-wise convolution merging of existing spaces, used as a generalized approach to self-play and as a class of emergent possibilities to test. These examples are not a unique or exhaustive list, and the door is open to other, emergent ways of leveraging goal-oriented, iterative self-play. A new goal or method for self-play is added to the protective belt. It is recorded as a statement with its status (the designer's or this file's reading), tested by the loop, and revised or dropped as the results require. It does not touch the hard core (`boundary_board_states.md` §14 items 7 to 11). A primitive is admitted only through the admission rule of the primitive catalog (§1 of `boundary_board_state_primitive_catalog.md`), and a goal or method is not a primitive and does not pass through it. This is an integrated specification, and it is not fully fleshed out.
20. **The loop compresses to a function with the self-play goal as a parameter, and the composition of that function is the progression of the program.** The designer states that the loop of §2, reading 15 compresses into a function in which the goal of the self-play is a parameter. This is for following the program as an iterative, progressive, step-wise process, and for seeing the loop as something that scales with progression and is not a fixed sequence bounded by its goal. The outcome of the function is not exactly reproducible each time. Reproducibility depends on how the bias coverage is constructed and on how the program has progressed. The accumulation of certainty through coverage makes the results tend towards reproducible within the d-depth scope. The function is therefore first about understanding the compositional nature of the workflow, and only then about reproducibility. The designer's schematic, derived live and not offered as a formal specification: `F(batches, engine_w, engine_b, priors_w, priors_b, goal_w, goal_b) = batch_of_games`. Engine_w plays White with priors_w for goal_w, and engine_b plays Black with priors_b for goal_b. The goal can be null. A null goal does not change the goal of the game, which is optimality for winning. It means only that the engine follows its priors. The batch is not run blindly. It can be tethered to the goal, so that goal_w or goal_b dictates the decisions made. Each side can be made aware of both sets of priors or only its own. This is a choice of parameterization that directly affects coverage and how the goals influence move choices for the accumulation of the program. A second function changes the priors from the outcome of the first: `P(priors_w, priors_b, batch_of_games) = (new_priors_w, new_priors_b)`. P involves merging, and also decisions about what to remove, to give gaps where possible. The composition is `F(batches, engine_w, engine_b, P(priors_w, priors_b, F(...))[0], P(priors_w, priors_b, F(...))[1], goal_w, goal_b) = batch_of_games`. It is a cumulative, progressive, recursive loop that the architecture of the program outlines. The priors are the segmented dependency units and the move chain understanding, and they are modular additions to any compatible engine for any irreversible segmented boundary. The routing of the parameterization affects the trend towards resolution, and the limiting factor is the storage limitation. The designer states that an optimal routing exists and can be discovered, that finding it can take many attempts, and that resolution within the window can require a specific setup, because the attempts vary from their initial point. The engine, the goal, the visibility of the priors, the d-depth limitation, what P keeps and omits, and d-depth partial certification are parameters or choices. The d-depth limitation is a calibration knob that also affects the viability of storage. The schematic can be calibrated to follow exhaustive coverage, certifying exhaustively within d with the deliberate gaps of item 14, which sidesteps storing the whole solution. The designer states that the schematic specifies the architecture and does not bound the parameters or how they interact. How goals work within F, and how P prunes and integrates batches into priors, are not specified. The best emergent process is to be discovered empirically on this foundation, following the structure that the program specifies. This is an integrated specification, and it is not fully fleshed out.
21. **A boundary is an entity that a population builds priors on, and the priors are emergent to a process outside the optimization of the program.** The designer states that irreversible segmented boundaries exist regardless of context, and that optimality can be built progressively upon each of them, with explicit consideration of d-depth. A boundary and its priors are different things. Each boundary can be built upon as an object, or a repository of objects, of priors, and it is an entity that the community can progressively contribute to: a master irreversible segmented boundary entity. The designer chooses "master" and not "global" so that it is not confused with the global and local senses of boundary board states. A population of agents, machine or human, engages with the program at any moment, interacts with the priors, and emerges and iterates from them. An agent can produce a result that is not within the master priors. That result is understood relative to the master prior. It can be a deliberately omitted position, a deterministic piece of information that affects the understanding of the master prior, or an optimization of the efficiency of storage and of the speed to resolve that covers all that the previous priors covered. It can then be fed into the master priors object, or into a catalog of priors objects. The designer states that the priors are emergent to a process outside the optimization that the program aims to achieve, which is the coverage of the local boundary board states, and that chess study is the core impetus for building towards it. The designer states that this harvests the data for the core purpose, a weak solution, from a scalable position to which machines and humans can contribute. If the main goal is not reached, the limit is found, the scope of what can be understood with chess is expanded, and the process is documented explicitly up to that point. This makes reproducible progress beyond current limitations, with scoped certainty. It benefits chess study decoupled from the progress of the program, and the progress of the program is made decoupled from the chess study front. The designer states that this progress does not help certify the boundary board states, and that it helps certify forced sequences and constant points of draw under optimality, and sets this aside as a digression. The designer states that this follows from the architecture, and that leaving it out would leave a gap in the understanding of how to scale and properly use the program. The designer's concern with hosting very large files of different compositional structure, and with analysing them for optimizations in an organized way, is a bias of the designer's and does not decide the matter. This is a direction that the designer is still working out, and it is not fully specified.
22. **Two fronts, with different incentives, and credit that must not cost the mission.** The designer states that there are two fronts: chess study, and the program's own front for the weak solution. The certification of the boundary board state constraints is not exactly achieved through the process of study. The frays of the actual solutions under optimality, with the d-depth limitation, are resolved by chess study up to the point where that is useful for chess study. Beyond that limit, progress is made outside the purposes of chess study, to expand the weak solution front. More contributions will come from chess study in volume, because contribution is not the core reason for using the program. The benefit to study is the reason, and the progress that is fed into the master priors is a side effect of it. The contributions to the explicit weak solution front will be quantifiably fewer, because individuals are less incentivized to make them beyond the progression of that front. The designer is sure that many will contribute if they are made aware and given access. The designer's reasoning about adoption is that if the program is a useful chess study tool, people will use it more than they use the program to progress the weak solution. If the chess study front is weak, the weak solution front will likely not be strong, because it requires much more work than chess study, and study does work that the weak solution needs anyway. There is also the actual weak solution of chess, and the weak solution within the d-depth limitation, and the designer thinks that the second is valuable too. If study progresses far enough to come closer to a weak solution of chess, that affects over-the-board chess at the highest level and brings attention to the program. The designer states that the cost of not using the program is a consequence of the strength of its chess study front. If the program gives a measurable benefit to play relative to what is available, there is a cost to not using it, in the game-theory sense that an opponent studied with it and one did not. This does not apply if the chess study front is not effective at providing that benefit. The designer states that the structure of the chess study front suggests that the highest level of play benefits most, and that if the program delivers the benefit, the cost is an inevitable consequence once high-level players discover it, and may change their play so much that using the program is required to compete. The designer states that credit for contributions must not be paid for by the mission. The designer does not object to contributors getting credit, and does object to credit that costs the program its storage or its goal. These are the designer's reasoning and predictions about adoption. No result supports or refutes them in this file. This is a direction that the designer is still working out, and it is not fully specified.
23. **The first iteration, the sources of priors, and the freedom of goals.** The designer states that the first iteration can be as simple as one engine facing another from zero priors, at a start that is left to choice: the start of the game, a material advantage, or a specific board state. A batch of games is taken, it is ensured that the games diverge from existing branches and are not repeats, and the games are merged into a network that forms the first prior. For simplicity, everything can be kept and merged. The goal can be null, which is normal engine play. The engine's search depth is also a consideration, together with d. In the next iteration, the new prior can be given to both sides. A goal can then be chosen from the emergent priors and from what is wanted: to focus on a specific line, to set up a bias coverage basis for a specific trajectory, to survey a point in the game, to look for refutations, to seek to deviate from the priors, or to attack with a targeted deviation from the priors that the opponent is known to take. This is where variance enters, and it is left to the agent to construct whatever it wants to do, including scripting it. Presets that are commonly used can be provided. The designer states that there should be no hard-coded general rules for the goals, for the priors or for the merging, other than to optimize towards progressing the program, for storage and for coverage, and that it would be a mistake to bound this explicitly. It is left open as a front that can be improved, and it belongs to the protective belt. The designer states that the priors can also be formed from databases of games, and that self-play is not needed to form them. The designer states that the specification of the program is sound as it stands and that the integrated specification is not going to be resolved exhaustively. The designer states that the first step is to work in terms of merging games and working with priors under the architecture of the program, and that the program is then integrated with compatible engines. The designer states that starting within the scope of Syzygy, or linking the approach to it, would be a poor architectural choice, because it limits the application to the scope that Syzygy covers, and that the decoupling from it is why the d-depth approach allows engine integration at all. This is an integrated specification, and it is not fully fleshed out.
24. **What matters to a student, and the competition on depth.** The designer states that what does not matter to a chess student is the exact perfect solution where it lies outside the d-depth scope. Such a solution is noise that is not reproducible over the board and is specialized to one position. It matters relative to ground truth, because the line exists and could have been used if it were known. The cost of studying a line that deep does not help the student. It matters only if the opponent is trained in the same approach at a deeper d-depth that matters, where a forced sequence is deterministic and is realized and remembered over the board. The designer states that pruning this noise, and showing only what lies within the proof size bounds that matter, improves the student's performance, and that current approaches leave the noise in, which distracts from what is important. The designer states that once the program is integrated into the community, the cost of not using it is an inevitable consequence if it is effective on the chess study front. The better options that d-depth pruning removes begin to matter only when players compete on the basis of deeper study with the program, where the advantage lies within the difference in d-depth. The designer states that this is the end state of the optimization loop that is already inherent in chess. The designer's view is that the program delivering on the chess study front looks inevitable. These are the designer's reasoning and predictions. No result supports or refutes them in this file. This is an integrated specification, and it is not fully fleshed out.
25. **A modular layer that takes the priors and the goals as inputs, with the compatible-engine architecture as an anchor.** The designer states that integrating the priors and fully customizable goal inputs with existing compatible engines may reshape what compatible engines look like, and that the approach should stay generalizable and compatible with many engines, with bare minimum requirements. The designer states that building a component that allows an engine to be added modularly, and that does precisely what the designer wants with the priors as an input, is not opposed. The compatible architecture of the engines can be built around as an anchor point, together with the priors, and this provides the programmable front for the goal inputs. In this arrangement the designer reads the engine as an evaluator: the layer takes the priors and the goal, and the engine evaluates. The designer notes that this resembles AlphaZero-style search, that the designer derived it without knowing of it, and regards the convergence as a good sign for the direction. This is a direction that the designer is still working out, and it is not fully specified.

## 2. This file's readings, offered for correction

1. **The roles.** The engine plays. The program certifies lines that the engine proposes, and builds tables from what the engine and the study enumerate. The segmented dependencies matter where a line is verified and where tables are built, for example for the value of an exit into a lower segment. They do not enter the engine's own choices (§1, item 6).
2. **Two kinds of statement.** An integrated engine gives certified statements, a result proved within d by exact verification against every reply, and heuristic statements. A verified win is permanent. A line that is not verified proves nothing, and what is not proved stays conditional (calibration §2, item 11). The interface marks which kind a statement is.
3. **Priors and the certified set.** With no priors a line is certified where it ends in a terminal, or in an exit whose value the budget resolves. Priors enlarge the set of certifiable lines and shorten the proofs (§1, item 4). Where the tables exist they also serve as the test oracle for the verifier.
4. **The feedback loop and coverage.** Tables built from an engine's play and enumeration record what was examined. What was not examined stays distinct from what was pruned (batch composition §1.9, item 10). Each certified claim stays sound, and the coverage follows what the play reaches.
5. **Two quantities for the human limit.** Reading §1, item 5, d is the scope of what is certified, and the size of the proof is the cost of certifying it. The d-depth limit for study is derived from the proof-size limit and is not hard-coded. The documents that state the student's limit carry the designer's refinement of 19 November 2026: `boundary_board_states_d_depth_applications.md` §1, item 8, `boundary_board_states.md` (the calibration bullet), `boundary_board_states_calibration.md` §1, item 14, and `boundary_board_states_batch_composition.md` §1.15, item 10.
6. **The compatibility requirement.** Reading §1, item 8, an engine is compatible when the depth or effort at which it finds a move can be reported, and its line can be checked exactly. Which engines meet this is not determined.
7. **Bias is a selection policy.** The priors decide which positions are examined. They do not decide whether an examined result is correct. Certification within d is exact against every reply, so a certified win stays permanent and a conditional draw stays exact within its scope (calibration §2, item 11). The bias determines coverage and never soundness. Reading §1, item 9, the catalog records the selection policy beside d, as the provenance of what was certified.
8. **The limits of what bias failure gives.** Within d the replies of the opposition are exhaustive. Beyond d, the counters that are met depend on the play that is reached, so the refutations enumerated are as broad as the play reached. Where the mistake lies more than d plies before the loss, the loss shows where to look, and not what is better. An alternative at the earlier position is a lead until it is certified within its own d. The designer confirmed these limits on 19 November 2026.
9. **The adversarial configuration.** Reading §1, item 10, one engine plays the biased side and another plays the refuter, with the calibration of each set separately, for example the human-calibrated limit for one and the engine's reach for the other. The verification is the program's, so the refuter's strength decides which refutations are found and does not decide whether one is valid. How often a line is refuted is a signal of which lines to examine and exclude (the work selection of the program). A line is excluded on proof when a specific refutation is verified within its d. A line that is not refuted is not certified beyond d by that fact. Two instances that share an engine and priors share their blind spots, so the refuter differs from the biased side in engine, priors or calibration.
10. **Composition of coverage certificates.** Reading §1, item 12, a coverage certificate holds its scope (d), which positions were examined and which were not, the policy that selected them, and the certificates it depends on (an exit into a lower segment, or a refutation of a line in another coverage). Composition is a merge that is monotone: the union of the wins, and the larger scope for a conditional draw. Two sound certificates cannot contradict each other, so a contradiction between them marks a fault in a verification. The composition therefore guarantees soundness and a certified set that does not decrease. It does not guarantee completeness, and whether the composition for a segment approaches the ground truth is measured against an exhaustive table where one exists, and is not asserted. The dependency among certificates is the one-way dependency from lower to upper segments (`boundary_board_states.md` P-003), so this adds no primitive to the primitive catalog.
11. **Chaining.** Reading §1, item 13, and §2, item 3, a line is certified where it ends in a terminal or in an anchor whose own value is certified. The depth certified from a root along a line is the depth searched to reach the anchor plus the depth for which the anchor is itself certified. The root's statement is certified when every reply of the opposition has been resolved, either stored or re-derived from the anchors around it. The stored tree can have holes. The self-play places the stored replies at the points where refutations are likely, and the other holes are enumerated again within the gaps that the anchors bound, at the work of the re-derivation instead of the storage. A reply that is not examined stays unexamined and is not assumed safe. The depth is a property of the resolved tree, and individual lines can be shorter or longer. Adding resolved positions in a gap adds anchors and completes the chain, at the storage that each anchor occupies. The depth certified at a root is therefore derived from the chain beneath it, and it is not set at the root.
12. **Information flows backward through the chain, and the keep-or-remove decision follows it.** Reading §1, item 13, what is resolved downstream changes the value of the branches at the root, so a refutation there can change which initial move is chosen. Reading §1, item 9 records the forward direction, in which the depth that was not available at the start is used on later lines. The decision to keep or remove an entry is made after composition, with what the integration has shown. An entry that is costly to resolve again and cheap to store is kept, and an entry that is cheap to derive again is removed. Because the anchors form the chain, the decisions interact, and they are made together and not entry by entry. The storage and search exchange is the cost model's (`boundary_board_states_cost_model.md` §3).
13. **Three words kept apart.** Enumerated means a position is listed. Exhaustively certified within d means every reply within d is resolved for the scoped region (§1, item 15). Stored means the pattern of kept rows holds it (calibration §1.3 and §2, items 9 to 12). In this file, exhaustive and every reply mean exhaustive within d over the scoped region, which the program verifies, and not exhaustive in general and not stored whole. Where another document says "exhaustive coverage" without more, it is read by its context: the Syzygy tables, the exhaustive verification of the rule set, or the scoped region.
14. **Engine choice and exactness.** Reading §1, item 18, the exactness within d is the program's check of a line and does not depend on the engine that produced it, so any compatible engine can be exchanged for another. An engine's own result at a depth is not a certificate where its search uses forward pruning, which can overlook a line (reading 6). Engines of different training lineage can share blind spots, so the divergence that §1, item 18 values is between engines of different roots. It is measured by the distinct refutations that a pair finds, and it is not assumed. The catalog records the engine, its version, its settings and its network as part of the selection policy of a certificate (reading 10). An engine that can be reached only through a remote service cannot be driven locally and is not compatible.
15. **The loop.** This is the base form of the cumulative, iterative process, with the self-play step open to the goals of §1, item 19 and to goals that have not yet been named. Reading 17 gives the same loop in the function form of §1, item 20.
    1. The start is any irreversible segmented boundary, including the initial game position, with zero priors allowed (§1, item 17).
    2. An engine plays as a black box and proposes lines (§1, items 6 and 18).
    3. The program checks each line exactly within d: every reply ends in a terminal or in an exit whose value is certified, and what is not examined stays unexamined (reading 11).
    4. The result is stored as a certificate with its d, what was examined, the selection policy, and the engine and settings that were used (readings 10 and 14).
    5. Self-play runs with a goal for the iteration. The refuter differs from the biased side in root, priors or calibration. Verified refutations exclude lines on proof, and a failed bias gives certified entries (readings 7 and 9). The goal decides who plays which side, what is enumerated, what is recorded and when the iteration ends.
    6. The keep-or-remove decision follows composition. What is costly to resolve again and cheap to store is kept, and what is cheap to derive again is left as a gap. The anchors chain the certified depth beyond one window (readings 11 and 12).
    7. The certified results and the selection policy become the priors of the next iteration (§1, items 4 and 6).
16. **Goals and the merging of spaces.** Reading §1, item 19, the goal is a parameter of step 5 of the loop, and of goal_w and goal_b in the function F of §1, item 20. The merge of certificates in reading 10 is monotone, and it is a different thing from the part-wise convolution merging of spaces that the designer names as a goal for self-play. The rules of that merging are not specified, and this file reads nothing more into it.
17. **The loop as the composition of F and P.** This reading takes §1, item 20, and is offered for correction. It is recorded as a statement of the protective belt. It does not touch the hard core and it is not a primitive of the primitive catalog.
    - **Iteration form.** The nested composition of item 20 unrolls to `priors[n+1] = P(priors[n], F(batches, engine_w, engine_b, priors[n], goal_w, goal_b))`, starting from the priors of the first iteration, which may be zero (§1, item 17).
    - **The seven steps in function form.** Steps 2 and 5 of reading 15, the engine's play and the goal-oriented self-play, are F. Steps 3, 4, 6 and 7, the exact check, the certificate, the keep-or-remove decision and the passing of results to the next priors, are read as P. Step 1 is the boundary and the first priors.
    - **F proposes and P admits.** F can be any engine, any goal, or a null goal, and it affects coverage and not soundness (reading 7). The exact check within d is read as the gate on what enters the priors as certified, so that soundness is held at P. The gate marks an entry as certified only when every opposing reply within d is resolved from the anchoring point (reading 22). This is a reading, and the designer has not specified where the check sits.
    - **The carried state includes the certificates.** Reading 15, step 7 passes the certified results and the selection policy on as priors. The priors of the function are read to include the certificate record of reading 10, so that the record of d, of what was examined, of the selection policy and of the engine and settings is not lost between iterations.
    - **Reproducibility has parts.** The truth of a certified entry does not depend on the path: a certificate can be checked independently, the merge of reading 10 is monotone, and a contradiction is a verification fault. Which entries exist does depend on the path, through the construction of the bias coverage, and this is the dependence that item 20 states. Nondeterminism of the engine, such as threading, is a separate source of variance, and it is recorded rather than assumed away. That results tend towards reproducible as certainty accumulates is not asserted here. As in reading 10, it is measured against an exhaustive table only where one exists.
    - **Routing.** The routing is the sequence of parameter choices over the iterations. The designer states that an optimal routing exists and can be discovered. The reading adds that "no routing found that fits the window at this d" is an admitted outcome of an attempt, so that the statement remains testable.
    - **What stays unspecified.** The signature in item 20 is a schematic. It does not yet carry the starting boundary, d, or a seed, and P is not yet given the engine and goal metadata that a certificate records. These are listed in §3. The interaction of the parameters is not bounded by this reading.
18. **Boundary entities, priors and a population.** This reading takes §1, item 21, and is offered for correction. It is recorded as a statement of the protective belt. It does not touch the hard core and it is not a primitive of the primitive catalog.
    - **Boundary and priors.** A boundary is an entity that is fixed by the rules and exists regardless of what any agent does. It is the key. Its priors are the content: certificates, the selection policy, gaps and anchors (readings 10 to 12). Priors are not read as becoming boundaries. The one-way dependency of the lower on the upper boundaries is read to hold between entities: the certified results of a lower boundary serve as anchors for the boundary above it.
    - **The population as P at a larger scale.** The merge of reading 10 is the same whether two certificates come from two iterations of one agent or from two agents. The master's policy for admitting and merging contributions is read as P at the level of the population (reading 17).
    - **Admission is by verification.** A certificate can be checked independently, so a contribution is read as admitted as certified only after the program has checked it exactly within its stated d, and the identity of the contributor, human or engine, does not affect soundness. A contribution that is not verified is not admitted as certified. Whether it is kept as a heuristic prior (§2, item 2) is open.
    - **A contribution is read relative to the master.** The cases are: it extends coverage into a region that the master has not examined; it falls in a deliberate gap, where it matches what the master would derive again and may still serve as an anchor; it is the same coverage at a lower storage cost or a faster resolution, and replaces the master's form only if the coverage is preserved; it records what was examined with nothing newly certified, which is selection policy information; or it conflicts with the master. Two sound certificates that conflict are a verification fault (reading 10), and the first thing to rule out is a difference in d or scope. The cases are this file's reading of the designer's examples, and the designer has not specified them.
    - **The size of a repository.** A repository stores gapped certificates and does not store the whole certified tree (§1, items 14 and 15), so its size is set by d and by the storage window and is not read to grow without bound. The cost of verifying contributions and the number of boundaries are listed in §3.
    - **The use outside the program.** The designer states that the priors serve chess study and that the program's progress is decoupled from it (§1, item 21). The statement about forced sequences and constant points of draw is read in reading 19.
19. **The two fronts.** This reading takes §1, items 21 and 22, and is offered for correction. It is recorded as a statement of the protective belt. It does not touch the hard core and it is not a primitive of the primitive catalog.
    - **What study certifies.** The statement about forced sequences and constant points of draw (§1, items 21 and 22) is read as follows. The study front yields certified forced sequences, and the points where optimal play holds the draw, within d, as the fray of optimal play. The coverage of the boundary board states is the program's own front. Both are held as priors in the same master entity (reading 18). The scope of such a certificate, and how it relates to the certificates of the program, are not specified.
    - **The fronts have different selection policies.** Study contributions follow what players care about, and the program's front covers what no one studies. The selection policy is recorded beside d (reading 7), so the origin of a certificate by front is not an extra record. This is a reading.
    - **The architecture does not depend on a population.** One agent that runs F and P works (reading 17). The population is a route to scale. That the weak solution front is weak if the study front is weak is the designer's reasoning about adoption (§1, item 22), and it is not read as a property of the architecture.
    - **The opportunity cost is conditional.** It holds only if the study front gives a measurable benefit relative to what is available, as the designer states. Players at the highest level already prepare with engines, so the benefit rests on what the program adds, which is read to be results certified within d at a proof size that a player can resolve. That depends on the human parameter (§3), which is open. That the highest level benefits most is the designer's prediction, and this file does not test it. A measure that the program can take on itself early is whether it certifies refutations in lines that engine-prepared analysis missed. This is the divergence measure of reading 14, and it is listed in §3.
    - **Credit is outside the priors.** The soundness of the program does not need the identity of a contributor. Admission rests on the exact check (reading 18). Any record of credit is read to sit outside the priors and outside the storage window, the program does not read it, and removing it does not change the coverage. This is separate from the engine and settings in the certificate record of reading 10, which serve the divergence measure and are counted as a cost of that record.
20. **The first iteration and the minimum interface.** This reading takes §1, item 23, and is offered for correction. It is recorded as a statement of the protective belt. It does not touch the hard core and it is not a primitive of the primitive catalog.
    - **A merged network of games is a prior, and it is not yet a certificate.** The games give move chain information and the positions that were examined. An entry is certified only when the program has checked it exactly within d, and until then it is marked as heuristic (§2, item 2). Keeping and merging everything is read as the simplest form of P (reading 17). The same holds for a prior that is formed from a database of games.
    - **The engine's depth and d are different.** The engine's depth and settings are recorded with the batch, since they affect which lines are proposed. They do not certify a line (reading 14). d is the scope of the program's own exact check.
    - **Divergence from existing branches** is read as a property of F, ensured by the program. Which means is used, such as excluding the moves that the priors already hold, several lines from the engine, or randomness, is not specified.
    - **The minimum interface of a compatible engine.** Extending reading 6, this is read as: the engine runs locally and speaks UCI; the program sets a position and runs it to a limit of depth, nodes or time; the engine returns a move, and several lines where available; the engine's settings, such as threads, seed and hash, can be set and are recorded; and the moves that the priors already hold can be excluded where the engine supports it, as UCI's `searchmoves` does. Exactness is never delegated to the engine. Which engines meet this is not determined.
    - **No start is tied to a table.** The start is any irreversible segmented boundary (§1, items 17 and 23). A table such as Syzygy is optional (§1, item 4) and is not the scope of the program's application.
21. **Two senses of what matters, and the competition on depth.** This reading takes §1, item 24, and is offered for correction. It is recorded as a statement of the protective belt. It does not touch the hard core and it is not a primitive of the primitive catalog.
    - **Truth and study.** In the sense of ground truth, a position beyond d is unexamined, and it is not assumed safe (reading 11). In the sense of study, what matters is what a player can resolve and retain, which is set by the human parameter (§1, items 3 and 7). The designer's pruning of noise is in the second sense. It does not change the first, and both hold together.
    - **Proof size, not ply depth alone.** The boundary of what is noise for a student is read as set by proof size, and not by ply depth alone. A line that is deep and narrow can be remembered, and a shallower line with many branches cannot. The d for study is therefore read as varying by player, which is the human parameter as a calibration (§3).
    - **The competition on depth.** The advantage over the board is read to arise where one player's resolvable depth exceeds the other's, and the cost of not using the program follows only if the study front delivers, as in reading 19. That the program delivers, and that this is the end state, are the designer's predictions (§1, item 24), and this file does not test them.
22. **Exactness within d.** This reading takes §1, items 15 and 16, and readings 6, 11 and 14, and is offered for correction. It is recorded as a statement of the protective belt. It does not touch the hard core and it is not a primitive of the primitive catalog.
    - **What is exact.** Certification is against refutation, from an anchoring point. The moves of the certified side are those of the prior, and the check covers every reply of the opposing side within d. Where a reply reaches a stored entry, that entry resolves it. Where a reply reaches a gap, the gap is re-derived within d from certified anchors (reading 11). If any reply cannot be resolved this way within d, the claim is not certified, and what was not examined stays unexamined.
    - **What it is not.** It is not a sweep of every position within d, and it does not require the opposing replies to be stored. "Every reply" means every reply of the opposing side along the lines that the moves of the prior reach from the anchoring point. The wording "every reply within d" can be read as exhaustive coverage of the region, and it does not mean that (§1, item 15).
    - **Bounded by d and proof size.** The cost of one claim is the proof size of that claim within d, and it is a cost of computation. A claim whose proof does not fit within d is left unexamined. The working memory of a check is not stored priors, and what is kept is decided by P (readings 11 and 12).
    - **Three roles.** Exactness makes each certified entry sound. The priors decide which claims are examined (reading 7), and they supply the certified exits that end a check early and extend its reach (readings 11 and 12). The gaps decide what is kept (§1, items 14 and 15). Exactness of each entry therefore does not call for exhaustive coverage or for storing the region.
    - **Refuting and certifying are different.** A reply that breaks a line excludes the line on proof, and the reply can be replayed. That no reply breaks the line requires every opposing reply within d, which an adversary that selects its replies does not show. The gate in P (reading 17) marks an entry as certified only on this. Priors that have not been checked are admitted and marked as heuristic (reading 20).

23. **The layer, the engine as evaluator, and the interface as anchor.** This reading takes §1, item 25, and is offered for correction. It is recorded as a statement of the protective belt. It does not touch the hard core and it is not a primitive of the primitive catalog.
    - **The layer.** The layer is read to own the search over the game tree. It takes the priors and the goal as inputs, and it calls an engine to evaluate positions.
    - **The minimum interface shrinks.** When the layer owns the search, an engine is read to need only to run locally, speak UCI, evaluate a position to a limit, and report the depth that it reached. The fuller interface of reading 20 remains the minimum for a program-side controller that restricts an engine's moves and does not use the layer. Engines that differ as evaluators still give the divergence of reading 14.
    - **The goal front as hooks.** The programmable front for the goals (§1, item 19) is read as hooks in the layer, such as which nodes are expanded, how a leaf is valued, including against a model of the opponent built from the opponent's priors, how the opponent's replies are weighted, and when to stop. This is not a specification, and the hooks are not limited to these (§1, item 19).
    - **The interface is the anchor.** The interface of the layer, which is the lookup of the priors, the goal hooks and the evaluator, is read to be fixed once, while the implementations vary: any compatible engine through the layer, or an engine that supports the interface natively.
    - **The resemblance to AlphaZero-style search and training.** The resemblance is read as structural, at two levels. In the search, priors enter the rule that selects which lines to expand, and an evaluator values the positions. In the loop, self-play proposes and an improvement step updates a stored policy that guides the next round of self-play, which is the loop of F and P (reading 17). The store here is explicit, it carries a certified or heuristic mark (reading 20), and it is not a trained policy. The resemblance was recognized after the loop had been derived, and it was not an input to the design. This file reads the resemblance as structural and does not claim that the two are the same.
    - **Soundness is unchanged.** The priors and the goals change coverage and not soundness (reading 7). What is certified is decided by exactness within d (reading 22).

## 3. What is open

- **The human parameter** (§1, items 3 and 7): how the proof size that a human can resolve is sourced, and how it is recorded for a player.
- **The measure of the space to resolve** (§1, item 5): what is counted, and how it combines with d.
- **Which engines are compatible** (§1, item 8; §2, items 6 and 20): which engines meet the minimum interface of reading 20, and what the interface between engine and program is in detail.
- **How the tables are built from the engine's play** (§1, item 6): what is recorded, in which mode, and how it is marked as examined.
- **What the engine reads from the tables** when it consults them (§1, items 6 and 25; §2, item 23).
- **How a statement is marked as certified or heuristic** (§2, item 2).
- **What an interdependent coverage certificate records** (§2, item 10): the fields, how the dependencies among certificates are recorded, and how a contradiction is reported.
- **How a bias coverage is recorded in the catalog** (§1, item 9): the selection policy as a quantity beside d, and how excluded branches are listed.
- **The adversarial configuration** (§1, item 10): which side is calibrated how, and how the refutation frequency is recorded and used to select lines, apart from exclusion on proof.
- **The priors-aware layer** (§1, item 25; §2, item 23): its interface (the lookup of the priors, the goal hooks, and what it needs from the evaluator), its search method, and its cost relative to an engine's own search.
- **Exactness within d** (§1, items 15 and 16; §2, item 22): what a check from an anchoring point takes (the moves of the prior, d, the certified exits that are available, and the rule set), what it returns (certified with its tree, or unresolved), how a gap is re-derived within it, how it handles repetition and move-count rules, and its cost per claim in relation to d.
- **How the depth certified at a root is computed** (§2, item 11): what is counted along the chain, how it is reported for the stored tree, with its holes, and for a line, and how it is recorded.
- **How anchors are placed** (§1, items 13 and 14; §2, item 12): which resolved positions are added to complete a chain, and how that is weighed against the storage they occupy.
- **How the divergence between engine roots is measured and recorded** (§2, item 14): what is counted for a pair, and how it is recorded beside the selection policy.
- **How the goal of an iteration of self-play is specified** (§1, items 19, 20 and 25; §2, items 15 to 17 and 23): the classes of goal, which are not limited to the examples of item 19, how a goal sets the roles, the enumeration, the record and the end of the iteration, and how the part-wise convolution merging of spaces is carried out.
- **The specification of F and P** (§1, item 20; §2, item 17): what F takes beyond its schematic (the starting boundary, d, a seed), how a goal enters the engine's move choices, whether each side sees both sets of priors or only its own and how that changes coverage, how the batch is tethered to the goal and sized, what P receives, and how P merges, prunes and leaves gaps.
- **The routing of the parameterization** (§1, item 20; §2, item 17): the sequence of choices of engine, goal, priors, visibility, d and P policy over the iterations, how it is searched for within the storage window, and how a routing that does not fit the window is recorded.
- **The master boundary entity** (§1, item 21; §2, item 18): what a repository of priors for a boundary holds, how a catalog of priors objects is organized, how an entity is keyed and versioned, and where any credit for a contribution is kept: outside the priors and outside the storage window (§1, item 22; §2, item 19).
- **Admission of contributions** (§2, item 18): who runs the exact check and at what cost, how a contribution is classified against the master (extending, within a gap, a cheaper form, examined only, conflicting), how a difference in d or scope is told from a conflict, whether an unverified contribution is kept as a heuristic prior, and how a replacement by a cheaper form is checked to preserve the coverage.
- **Hosting at scale** (§1, item 21; §2, item 18): the size of a repository for a given d and storage window, the number of boundary entities, and how repositories of different compositional structure are held and analysed for optimizations. The designer states that this does not decide whether the feature is part of the program.
- **What the priors certify outside the coverage of the boundaries** (§1, items 21 and 22; §2, item 19): read as forced sequences and the points where optimal play holds the draw within d. The scope of such a certificate, and how it relates to the certificates of the program, are not specified.
- **The measure of the benefit to study** (§1, items 22 and 24; §2, items 19 and 21): what counts as a measurable benefit relative to what is available, for which players, how it is measured, and whether the early measure of certified refutations in engine-prepared lines is used. It depends on the human parameter.
