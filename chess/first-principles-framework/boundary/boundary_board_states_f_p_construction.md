# F and P Construction: A Working Record

**Status.** A working record, begun on 21 November 2026, of the designer's understanding of how F is to be constructed within the F/P workflow, and of what that understanding requires of P. It takes `boundary_board_states_engine_integration.md` as its base (§1, items 19, 20, 23 and 25; §2, readings 10, 11, 17, 20, 22 and 23) and does not change it. At the designer's direction it is kept as a separate file, so that it can be revised, folded into the other documents, or dropped without affecting them. It is stated in general terms: no piece, material, position or engine is part of it. It adds no primitive to the primitive catalog (`boundary_board_state_primitive_catalog.md`, whose admission rule is §1) and does not touch the hard core (`boundary_board_states.md` §14 items 7 to 11). The methods, goals and procedures recorded in it belong to the protective belt, and none of them is a primitive. Statements marked **the designer's** are the designer's. Statements marked **this file's reading** are readings of the designer's words, offered for correction. Nothing in this file has been run, and no measurement is recorded in it. Where the understanding of the d-depth metric in this file differs from the earlier statements in the other documents, the difference is a deliberate change to the protective belt, pending empirical results (§1, item 35), and the other documents are not changed.

Companion documents: `boundary_board_states_engine_integration.md` (the base of this file), `boundary_board_states_calibration.md` (the stored band and what absence means), `boundary_board_states_cost_model.md` (the priors as a storage cost), `boundary_board_states_d_depth_applications.md` (the applications, including the depth limit).

3. **The engine is an evaluator, with the widest compatibility.** The engine is meant to be open to as wide a pool of choice as possible. 37. **How the balance is read to play out.** This file's reading of items 34 and 37, offered for correction. The aims are full coverage, the least storage and the most speed. If coverage is kept, speed is given up, down to a ceiling that is the proof size limitation of the machine. If a hard limit is set on speed, the requirement is paid for in storage, since more states have to be stored for the checks between them to meet it. Storage can become a global limitation if the proof size for resolving gaps exhaustively exceeds what storage allows. In each case the distance that counts is the proof size between two points of resolution (reading 36). The enumeration of the whole coverage space of a boundary is read as a separate measurement, which shows that the space is bounded, and not as a step that the loop requires. How a speed limit is set, or left to emerge, is not specified here.
