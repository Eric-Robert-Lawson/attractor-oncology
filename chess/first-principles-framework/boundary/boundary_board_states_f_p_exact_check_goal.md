# F and P: Exact Check Goal

**Status.** A working record, begun on 23 November 2026, of what the designer has stated about the approach to the goal of the exact check, and about the structure that the check works within. It is a modular file. It takes `boundary_board_states_f_p_construction.md` as its base and does not change it, and it changes no other file, so that it can be revised or deleted without affecting the others. It adds no primitive to the primitive catalog, does not touch the hard core (`boundary_board_states.md` §14 items 7 to 11), and belongs to the protective belt. It is stated in general terms: no piece, material, position or engine is part of it. The statements are the designer's, kept as close to the designer's words as they could be, and they are offered for the designer's correction. This file holds no readings. Item numbers cited from the construction file are named as such. Nothing in this file has been run.

## 1. The designer's statements (23 November 2026)

1. **The anchor of a self-play need not be the initial position, and the work runs from the most terminally understood root.** The designer states that the anchor of a self-play need not be the initial position, so that a batch of games in the typical sense is not needed, and that the priors are board states with self-play between board states on the evaluator front. The designer states that the workflow runs from back to front, from the most terminally understood root, which is the edge of the certainty boundary given by the progress of the program and its priors. The designer states that regions of d-depth compose from there until the coverage requirement is met, and that the result is denoted into the priors as a certified boundary board state that is reachably resolvable. The designer states that the rederivation costs and the conditional costs are decided in composition with the existing landscape of the priors (23 November 2026).
2. **The reachability of d across segments is part of the structure and must be considered when scaling.** The designer states that the reachability of the d-depth within an irreversible segmented boundary, and how it is reachable to another unit downstream so as to resolve certainty, is a structure that still exists and must be considered when scaling (23 November 2026).
3. **The first anchoring is in the endgames, and the test is the designer's to specify.** The designer states that the initial anchoring point should reside in the endgames to start. This is constructing from zero priors the equivalent of tablebases, Syzygy for instance, for an irreversible segmented boundary that Syzygy has already solved, as a test case, to see how the approach resolves it into priors in the compatible format. The designer states that it is possible that this is less efficient than the storage efficiency inherent in the Syzygy solution, and that Syzygy will always beat the approach on speed, unless the engine fixes that by having perfect coverage of the endgame to begin with. The designer states that the designer does not want to execute an endgame test that the agent specifies, since the agent may not know what must truly be tested until the thing being tested for by the integrated specification is understood. The test is the designer's to specify (23 November 2026).
4. **The evaluator does a lot of work, and it mitigates the requirement for exhaustive coverage.** The designer states that the evaluator does a lot of work in the approach. The proof size and the enumeration of the proof tree, and the judgement of how well the engine did without the proof tree resolved as a prior, are informative. The designer states that this is not a measurement to keep track of explicitly. If there are deviations from true optimality within the proof tree and the proof size, composing board states with d-depth certainty in that limitation region can inform optimality for the engine beyond the scope of its current capacity to reach that certainty. The designer states that this is where coverage, speed and storage are explicitly measured. The designer states that the engine is integrated as an ability to mitigate the requirements for exhaustive coverage and for certainty as a storage requirement, with the engine's heuristic strengths resolving the gap inherent in the priors, and with measurements already available of the gaps that it can resolve with certainty within a given speed tolerance (23 November 2026).
5. **The d field also denotes the region that it encloses.** The designer states that when the d field denotes 1, the region enclosed by that coverage has been checked within those bounds, relative to the anchor point that it encompasses. The designer states that this is informative about what has been checked without adding a field, because the information is held within storage and serves more than one function, compressed in a smart fashion. The designer states that a board state depends on being within a region already evaluated, denoted by the metric, for it to be understood whether it has been attempted, and that it can be derived more specifically to what degree of certainty it has been attempted. The designer states that this was deliberately architected into the approach (23 November 2026).
6. **A case in general terms: the check against the engine's native play, and the step when nothing is determined.** The designer gives the case of an irreversible segmented boundary in which earlier work, derived from exhaustive coverage, shows that every forced sequence under optimality resolves within a known d-depth, and that beyond it the position is a draw. The designer states that drawn board states are not added to storage at all. The designer states that where the draw status is not yet known with exhaustive certainty, the space can be searched to that depth at each board state. One board state is the anchor, with a proof tree of that depth, and the result is compared with what the engine natively played without priors. If it matches, the space is resolved. If it deviates there is a problem, and a prior can be added somewhere that resolves the uncertainty relative to the proof tree that took the work to enumerate. The designer states that the moves that the evaluator would play can be checked at each board state of the proof tree, to show agreement in optimality across the proof tree, that agreement across the proof tree means resolved and no prior is needed to inform it, and that the resolved states added to the priors can then be used in parity with the evaluator to make move choices. The designer states that where no forced sequence is determined, one can go one ply down from the board state and enumerate another edge on the frontier, and asks whether that iteration resolves the closure requirements. The designer states that whether that step is taken depends on the approach, for instance on whether the storage limit allows it, on measurements of how regions are already resolved, and on the difficulty of rederiving one board state relative to another. It can be an expansion of the d-depth limit, or a variable programmable front, whichever is decided. In the case given, every board state resolves within the known depth, or the forced sequence does not exist. The designer states that where the depth that resolves the draws with certainty is not known, the emergent result is stated relative to full coverage, as the d-depth limit of certification that has been resolved. The designer gave the specific ending and depth for the case, which are not recorded because this file is stated in general terms (23 November 2026).
7. **A perimeter at d of 1 is left only for what the goal decides to leave in the priors, and a list of them is open.** The designer states that each sub-region has a perimeter at d of 1 for the measurement that it decides to leave into the priors, and that not every boundary attempted as the check expands is enumerated, because that places a large number of 1 values in the d-depth field and ruins the purpose of the perimeter being valuable to denote at the d of 1 board state. The designer states that it is the moment chosen to resolve the placement of the prior position onto the board, and that the priors are integrated based on the board state chosen. A proof tree can resonate from that board state to gain the certainty that it provides, and the d-depth limit that the board state prior enables certainty within is then added, calibrated for chess study among other purposes, depending on what is wanted. The designer states that the d of 1 is attached to the anchor point itself, and that it can serve as a decision point. The designer asks whether a list of where the d-depth is one, relative to the index of the anchor point, should be added into storage, and whether it can serve as an indication of the points at which different batches of games should be considered in composition, or whether a hybrid or other approaches not yet considered would serve. The designer states that this is an integrated specification front in itself (23 November 2026).
8. **Partial certification at a chosen d-depth limit is a choice that can be made.** The designer states that the program currently hinges on exhaustive coverage and on certainty based on depth assurance to resolve. As the irreversible segmented boundaries progress, resolving board states under forced sequence for the closure constraint may not be reachable because of limits on certainty. It can then be important to pivot the schematic and the purpose of the d-depth limit, to denote priors for a specific d-depth limit of proof size certification, considering what can be certain within that proof size. The designer states that this turns partial progress into partial certification priors that are still useful, and that the current specifications do not clarify it as a choice that can be made. A d-depth limit that is acceptable to the one who builds can be chosen, and priors built on the partial certification within the denoted limit. The designer states that for chess study the d-depth limit informs the capacity to think at a given depth, which may exceed human capacity, so that the emergent states that are unresolvable under the certainty constraint of the limit are important, and the progression of the priors up to a d-depth limit of certainty is useful. The designer states that pruning what lies beyond the capacity for over the board play is an added benefit, since it scopes the certainty that can be studied and used over the board, and that this was the focus of the documents on chess study and on scoping the limit to resolve moves that would otherwise be unreachable under it (23 November 2026).
9. **Limited scope inherited down a chain is confirmed, and is not the present focus.** The designer confirms the reading that a window that lands in a downstream region certified only to a limited scope inherits that limit, so that the scope of a certificate along a chain is bounded by its weakest downstream link. The designer states that this is why full coverage for the priors is wanted for the segmented units, within a depth of certainty as the constraint, to resolve the forced sequences and to leave holes for those that cannot. The designer states that variability should not exclude limited scope inheritance, that it is not a focus of the understanding being constructed at the moment, and that it is an integrated specification. The designer states that the point about partial certification stands doubly here, and that it is the best part with respect to chess study (23 November 2026).
10. **Apparent conflicts between statements are analyzed as dependencies on the goal approach, and three that were read as tensions are not.** The designer asks that an apparent conflict between statements be analyzed first as a possible dependency on the goal approach. The designer states that a d-depth limit can be chosen for partial certification, and that forced sequences under certainty resolved within the partial certification remain right no matter how deep the check goes, so that a chosen limit and an emergent limit are not a contradiction. The designer states that starting in the endgames is not a contradiction of item 32 of the construction file. It is a statement about engine certainties within trivial regions: where the engine covers a region with certainty, it is near worthless to apply any priors there, since the engine resolves that irreversible segmented boundary with zero priors. The designer states that a constant perimeter is inherent in the priors when the priors are not used to iterate the progression and only inform the engine's moves, and that the perimeter is variable when the priors are changed. The designer wonders whether the documents denote these points poorly (23 November 2026).
11. **The index points to board states, and the legend is superseded.** The designer states that the legend and the symmetry field were superseded by the integrated specification of the newer documents, which drops the symmetry field altogether and has the index point to board states, to save on storage overhead, because a legend would have to be compatible to fetch from the coding front. The designer states that referencing board states self-referentially, as segmented dependency board state locations, is important. The board state that the index points to is the last board state that precedes the terminal condition that moves the irreversible segmented boundary, or is the terminal condition within the irreversible segmented boundary that the prior defines. The designer states that this was stated explicitly earlier in the session (23 November 2026).
12. **A crossing counts as reaching a destination, the downstream must already be certified, and the base is what the engine resolves without priors.** The designer states that a crossing counts as reaching a destination when it changes the irreversible segmented boundary or meets the terminal condition, which is mate if the priors keep forced sequences. The designer states that a crossing from one irreversible segmented boundary to another is trivial to understand, since the boundaries are properly defined, and that it is a pawn move, a capture, or an irreversible metric progression. The designer states that the downstream board state must already be certified, because the work runs back to front from the coverage of certainty, and optimality cannot be determined without knowing the downstream certainty from the priors. The designer states that there is no seed for the bottom of the chain that is separate from this: the engine evaluator evaluates a position and resolves it where it is solvable without priors, and this is checked and assured. Where it is off, the basis for the priors is created, which the seeds depend on to reach certainty with those priors (23 November 2026).
