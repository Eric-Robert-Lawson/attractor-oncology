# F and P: Exact Check Goal

**Status.** A working record, 23 to 24 November 2026, of the designer's statements on the approach to the exact check goal and the structure it works within. It is modular: it takes `boundary_board_states_f_p_construction.md` as its base, changes no other file, and can be deleted without affecting them. It adds no primitive, does not touch the hard core (`boundary_board_states.md` §14 items 7 to 11), and belongs to the protective belt. It is stated in general terms. All statements are the designer's, kept close to his words and offered for his correction. No readings are held. Nothing has been run. Statements are integrated by topic: a later statement replaces the part of an earlier one that it changes. `boundary_board_states_f_p_goals_general.md` holds what applies to goals in general.

## 1. The designer's statements

1. **Anchor, direction, segments and destinations.** The anchor of a self-play need not be the initial position, so a batch of games in the typical sense is not needed. The priors are board states, with self-play between board states on the evaluator front. The work runs back to front from the most terminally understood root, the edge of the certainty boundary given by the progress of the program and its priors. Regions of d-depth compose from there until the coverage requirement is met, and the result is denoted into the priors as a certified boundary board state that is reachably resolvable. Rederivation and conditional costs are decided in composition with the existing landscape of the priors. Each board state resides within an irreversible segmented boundary (a position with castling rights is not reachable to one without them without an irreversible move). That confinement must be understood, with an immediate understanding of how to resolve the downstream positions for certainty: priors for the downstream segmented dependency units, resolved to a d-depth of partial or full certification, whichever is available given the progress on the boundaries. The reachability of d within a boundary, and how it reaches a unit downstream to resolve certainty, must be considered when scaling. A crossing counts as reaching a destination when it changes the boundary (a pawn move, a capture, an irreversible metric progression) or meets the terminal condition, which is mate if the priors keep forced sequences. The downstream board state must already be certified, since the work runs back to front and optimality cannot be determined without the downstream certainty. There is no separate seed for the bottom of the chain: the engine evaluator resolves a position where it is solvable without priors, and this is checked and assured. Where it is off, the basis for the priors is created, which the seeds depend on to reach certainty.
2. **First anchoring and the test.** The initial anchoring point should reside in the endgames: construct from zero priors the equivalent of tablebases, Syzygy for instance, for a boundary already solved, as a test case, to see how the approach resolves it into priors in the compatible format. This may be less efficient than the storage efficiency of the Syzygy solution, which will always beat the approach on speed unless the engine fixes that by having perfect coverage of the endgame to begin with. The designer does not want to execute an endgame test that the agent specifies, since the agent may not know what must be tested until what the integrated specification tests for is understood. The test is the designer's to specify.
3. **The evaluator.** The evaluator does a lot of work and mitigates the requirement for exhaustive coverage. The proof size, the enumeration of the proof tree, and how well the engine did without the proof tree resolved as a prior are informative, but not a measurement to keep track of explicitly. Where there are deviations from true optimality within the proof tree and proof size, composing board states with d-depth certainty in that region can inform optimality for the engine beyond its current capacity to reach that certainty. Coverage, speed and storage are what is explicitly measured. The engine is integrated to mitigate the requirements for exhaustive coverage and for certainty as a storage requirement: its heuristic strengths resolve the gap inherent in the priors, with measurements already available of the gaps it resolves with certainty within a given speed tolerance.
4. **The d field and the perimeter at d of 1.** The d field also denotes the region it encloses: a d of 1 means the region enclosed has been checked within those bounds, relative to the anchor point it encompasses. This informs what has been checked without adding a field, since the information in storage serves more than one function, and it was deliberately architected. A board state's being within a region already evaluated shows whether it was attempted, and to what degree of certainty. Each sub-region has a perimeter at d of 1 only for what the goal decides to leave into the priors. Not every boundary attempted as the check expands is enumerated, because many 1 values in the d field ruin the purpose of the perimeter. It is the moment chosen to resolve the placement of the prior position, and the priors are integrated on the board state chosen. A proof tree can run from that board state to gain the certainty it gives, and the d-depth limit that the board state enables is then added, calibrated for chess study among other purposes. The d of 1 is attached to the anchor point itself and can serve as a decision point. The designer asks whether a list of where the d-depth is one, relative to the index of the anchor point, should be stored, and whether it can indicate where different batches should be composed, or whether a hybrid or another approach would serve. This is an integrated specification, held off for now: there is no need to rush the resolution in multiple disparate problem spaces.
5. **The worked case.** In a boundary where earlier exhaustive work shows every forced sequence under optimality resolves within a known d-depth, and beyond it the position is a draw, drawn board states are not stored. Where the draw status is not yet known with exhaustive certainty, the space can be searched to that depth at each board state. One board state is the anchor, with a proof tree of that depth, and the result is compared with what the engine natively played without priors. If it matches, the space is resolved. If it deviates, the difference is fixed with a prior that resolves it. Working from within the proof tree and the resolution of priors between board states, the check finds where zero knowledge of priors deviates from the proof tree, and priors are added from the proof tree to resolve the difference. The engine can then parse the gaps. The moves the evaluator would play can be checked at each board state of the tree. Agreement across the tree means resolved, with no prior needed, and the resolved states added to the priors can be used in parity with the evaluator to choose moves. In the case given, every board state resolves within the known depth, or the forced sequence does not exist. Where no forced sequence is determined, one can go one ply down and enumerate another edge on the frontier (the designer asks whether that iteration resolves the closure requirements). Whether that step is taken depends on the approach: the storage limit, measurements of how regions are already resolved, the difficulty of rederiving one board state relative to another. It can be an expansion of the d-depth limit, or a variable programmable front. Where the depth that resolves the draws is not known, the emergent result is stated relative to full coverage, as the d-depth limit of certification resolved. The specific ending and depth the designer gave are not recorded here.
6. **Partial certification and limited scope.** The program currently hinges on exhaustive coverage and certainty from depth assurance. As the boundaries progress, resolving board states under forced sequence for closure may not be reachable because of limits on certainty. A d-depth limit acceptable to the builder can then be chosen, denoting priors for a specific proof size of certification, which turns partial progress into useful partial certification priors. The current specifications do not clarify this as a choice. A chosen limit and an emergent limit are not a contradiction: forced sequences certified within the partial certification remain right however deep the check goes. For chess study the limit informs the capacity to think at a given depth, which may exceed human capacity, so emergent states unresolvable under the limit matter, and progression of the priors up to a limit is useful. Pruning what lies beyond over the board play scopes the certainty that can be studied and used, an added benefit and the focus of the documents on chess study. A window landing in a downstream region certified only to a limited scope inherits that limit, so the scope of a certificate along a chain is bounded by its weakest downstream link (confirmed). This is why full coverage is wanted for the segmented units within a depth of certainty, to resolve forced sequences and leave holes for those that cannot. Variability should not exclude limited scope inheritance. It is an integrated specification and not the present focus. The point about partial certification stands doubly here, and is the best part with respect to chess study.
7. **Apparent conflicts are analyzed first as dependencies on the goal approach.** Starting in the endgames does not contradict item 32 of the construction file: where the engine covers a region with certainty, applying priors there is near worthless, since the engine resolves that boundary with zero priors. A constant perimeter is inherent in the priors when they are not used to iterate the progression and only inform the engine's moves, and the perimeter is variable when the priors are changed. The designer wonders whether the documents denote these points poorly.
8. **The index, the board state format and the child field.** The legend and the symmetry field are superseded: the index points to board states, to save storage overhead, because a legend would have to be compatible to fetch from the coding front. Referencing board states self-referentially, as segmented dependency board state locations, is important. The board state the index points to is the last one that precedes the terminal condition that moves the boundary, or is the terminal condition within the boundary that the prior defines. The board state format is a choice: whatever the standard is for storage, considering the architecture. Carrying forward any important structural attribute, and how to store it given the batch, is important. The anchor can be kept from the batch, relative to the initial anchor point or to the nearest upstream neighbor in the chain. That is a single field holding the index of the board state that defines the optimal trajectory of the proof tree, to carry forward its full fidelity. It may be best as a child field rather than a parent: multiple prior board states can transpose into the optimal board states arrived at, there can be multiple viable parents, and the downstream child from viable choices is more informative, if from the pool of choice, than the parent as an upstream relation. The child holds one choice from the viable pool, and the pool can be one board state or more than one. Where there is a choice, choosing the board state that resolves in a different direction and extends coverage is good, and choosing board states that converge works against covering the irreversible segmented boundary. This is how the integrated specification is chosen to be integrated, and it stays variable: the field is variable to the storage front and may be shown redundant or poorly specified by the emergent results of the program's composition. The field carries a scalable storage cost with each index in the proof tree. It is chosen now as an option, put in to be safe, that can be removed from the goal and the field, and alleviated for storage concerns, if found redundant, if emergent results favor another approach, or if the concern arises. It belongs to the protective belt and is variable by goal.
9. **The approach chosen first, and P's input.** The specification remains agnostic for specification reasons, but one approach must be chosen to construct anything for the goal front. The first is the exact check goal on a batch of board states, an integrated specification for the planned empirical route and a defined purpose explicitly specified. The focus is programming a batch of board states as the input to the prior function, to start. The file is named for it because it describes the goal and how it depends on the input being a batch of board states. The goal for P takes a batch of board states and not a batch of games, and P merges nothing, because there should be no trajectory, and the trajectory a merge would carry has no consequence for the exact check. The code for P integrates a specification and excludes architectural variance meant to be open to programming, and requires change immediately. Since P takes board states, the anchoring points for the exact check can be the board states of the batch, and the child field helps denote the anchoring trajectory for the proof tree. The code is not changed yet.
10. **The proof tree.** The trajectory itself is the d-depth certification: the proof tree that proves closure of the board state under optimality, or at least that optimality is forced, leaving open the positions potentially drawn or incalculable from the depth reachable, and the open fronts to be attempted to fit within the window. The batch of board states and the calibration of the reach of the d regions may resolve the certainty of a proof tree that resolves unknowns in the priors, so priors can be added that make the gap reachable and resolvable. This allows a choice in the layout of the board states carried over to be priors, constructed directly from the batch, with consideration to the coverage of the landscape needed to derive certainty, through a direct calibration front from a program that allows this interface. The proof tree need not be stored. Where the full tree does not fit the proof tree size, the addition of a board state can be certifiable part-wise, and the chained certainty already inherent in the priors mitigates the size requirement, so certification can resolve within the proof size window. Instead of carrying the tree into storage, it can be compressed into index additions with a d-depth metric requirement for certifying it, with calibrated positioning of gaps to resolve, for balancing speed, coverage and storage. One index addition takes the burden of deriving the entire proof tree to resolve. The proof tree and the engine's evaluators are taken to see which priors can be resolved between moves with certainty, and this dictates which board states are added from the tree. That determines the number of indices added, and how the d-depth metric is documented with them. The check is a separate piece, modular to the process of writing entries to the priors. The process of the check includes writing entries to compose a proof tree, and the resulting proof tree is used to resolve the priors if certainty is achieved, based on the proof tree and the potential priors in composition.
11. **The calibration case.** If the priors almost resolve a board state, F can be programmed so that the goal of the self-play is to explore the proof tree from the proof size, starting at the points of certainty and going toward what is known to be unresolved, to fill the gap and progress the certainty. If something is found that allows certification, the required trajectory itself, the literal board states that compose the difference, like the full proof tree, can be taken, the d-depth limit for the exact check set to 1 ply, and the proof tree resolved if storage permits. If storage does not permit, this scales in principle to fit storage, if the resolution between engines fits the proof tree well enough. The information found is then given to the goal for P, which converts the old prior into the new one (`boundary_board_states_f_p_goals_general.md`, statement 1).
12. **structure.py is left unused,** as an integrated specification, since it conflicts with what is being done. It may be useful to check whether the loop is compatible in structure during composition, to flag incompatibilities. The designer is not sure it is useful.
