## 1. The designer's statements

1. **Anchor, direction, segments and destinations.** The anchor of a self-play need not be the initial position, so a batch of games in the typical sense is not needed. The priors are board states, with self-play between board states on the evaluator front. The work runs back to front from the most terminally understood root, the edge of the certainty boundary given by the progress of the program and its priors. Regions of d-depth compose from there until the coverage requirement is met, and the result is denoted into the priors as a certified boundary board state that is reachably resolvable. Rederivation and conditional costs are decided in composition with the existing landscape of the priors. Each board state resides within an irreversible segmented boundary (a position with castling rights is not reachable to one without them without an irreversible move). That confinement must be understood, with an immediate understanding of how to resolve the downstream positions for certainty: priors for the downstream segmented dependency units, resolved to a d-depth of partial or full certification, whichever is available given the progress on the boundaries. The reachability of d within a boundary, and how it reaches a unit downstream to resolve certainty, must be considered when scaling. A crossing counts as reaching a destination when it changes the boundary (a pawn move, a capture, an irreversible metric progression) or meets the terminal condition, which is mate if the priors keep forced sequences. The downstream board state must already be certified, since the work runs back to front and optimality cannot be determined without the downstream certainty. There is no separate seed for the bottom of the chain: the engine evaluator resolves a position where it is solvable without priors, and this is checked and assured. Where it is off, the basis for the priors is created, which the seeds depend on to reach certainty.

## 3. What the empirical work established for the exact check

This file's reading of runs of a stored goal for P that enumerates the proof tree from the board states of a batch, offered for correction. It carries what was found and not the runs. The runs were on small endgames, with seven anchors, in one process, with the stand-ins named in the header of the goal (`code/goals/proof_tree_p.py`).

1. **A pruned proof tree can be built inside the process, and it was sound.** After each layer the tree proves what it can, win, loss or draw for the side to move, by backward induction, and a ply is dropped when it leads to a state proven to be a win for the side to move there. No wrong proof appeared in any run. The expansion stops at the proof size limit, which is a variable of the goal.
2. **What pruning saves depends on outcomes being proven inside the tree.** Far from the ends nothing is proven and nothing is saved. At the distance to the end the saving was small, about 1 to 6 percent. One to four plies past it, depending on the anchor, the tree collapsed: once every alternative of the side to move is a proven win, what is kept is the plies of the anchor and nothing below them.
3. **What it keeps and what it loses.** The value class of every kept state and the sets of best plies were unchanged. The distance to the end, the slowest defence and the winning line are not kept. A state that has moves and no kept plies is lost, and has to be told apart from a state that was never expanded.
4. **Pruning after the whole tree exists does not lower the peak. Pruning after each layer can, and what it achieves depends on what is remembered about pruned states.** Without a record of the states proven lost, a state deleted below a pruned part returns with less depth when it is met again by a longer route, and some proofs are not reached: in one case 1,063 states that the whole tree proves were not proven, and the tree stayed at about 350 KB where the pruned whole tree is under 1 KB. With a record of the proven lost keys, which is explicit storage and is counted in the peak, the same case reached under 1 KB and the peak at the deepest depth run was about a quarter of the whole tree's. Where the proofs only complete at the last layer, neither helps.
5. **States merge by position and each takes the most depth any route gives it, so a proof can be longer than the depth allowed.** Proofs of up to 9 plies were found for states 2 plies from the anchor at a depth of 6. A search that counts depth along each path cannot see them, and held about 477 KB in a case where this holds under 1 KB. The merge rule and the order of expansion decide what pruning can see.
6. **The cost of the whole tree.** Size follows the number of states held and time follows the number of plies examined, so states per second fall as the depth grows. Anchors close to each other share states, and the share grows with depth and with closeness. Anchors far apart are nearly additive until the depth is large: for 16 anchors at a depth of 5, 77 percent of the states were shared for close anchors and 25 percent for distant ones.
7. **The proven outcomes came only from games that ended inside the tree.** States at a change of segment were left unproven, so priors proven downstream would add proven outcomes at the ends of the tree. That was not tested, since no priors exist.
