# F and P Goal Work File

**Status.** A work file begun on 22 November 2026, for the goal of the exact check that P gives to F, and for making the F and P workflow executable. It takes `boundary_board_states_f_p_construction.md` as its base and does not change it. The work that carries a risk of failing is kept here, so that the construction record is not affected by attempts (construction file, item 23 and reading 19). It adds no primitive to the primitive catalog, does not touch the hard core (`boundary_board_states.md` §14 items 7 to 11), and belongs to the protective belt. It is stated in general terms: no piece, material, position or engine is part of it. Item and reading numbers refer to the construction file unless another file is named. Statements marked **the designer's** are the designer's, and statements marked **this file's reading** are readings offered for correction. Nothing in this file has been run, and no attempt has been made yet.

    - **The two applications.** In progression the evaluator serves the construction of the priors, and in use it navigates the gaps (items 43 and 60 to 62).

Open in §3: which engine the keep decision is made for, whether the priors are held per engine, how the depth of the evaluator relates to d, and what "provably better than the native line" means.

## 2. The designer's statements for the goal

Statements of 23 and 24 November 2026. They are the specifications chosen for the goal approach, so that anything can be executed at all, and they are integrated specifications. Statements that are exact check or agnostic to goals are recorded in `boundary_board_states_f_p_exact_check_goal.md` and `boundary_board_states_f_p_goals_general.md`, and what is here is neither. This section holds no reading of them.

1. **Directed rederivation and the condition based search are used together.** (23 November 2026) The designer states that the d-depth limitation certification informs the search within d to find the line, and that the condition also scopes the search within the d-depth limit to find that outcome. The designer states that one does not exclude the other, that both can apply at the same time and be used in combination, and that choosing one and excluding the other leaves optimizations on the table without a blocking reason. The designer states that the condition based basis can be omitted when the aim is to ensure that all viable pools of optimally certified options still hold, or to find whether the priors need alterations due to a newly resolved scope of certainty, and that there are many ways to manage that front. The designer states that the condition based basis is excluded in that case so that the viable pool of equally optimal options is considered and the priors hold as the program progresses, and that this is an important aspect of the program not to overlook.
2. **The engine evaluates and is not trusted first where priors exist.** (24 November 2026) The designer states that the engine cannot make decisions based on the priors but acts as an evaluator of board states, and that the priors can be used to understand whether an engine move is optimal relative to the priors that exist. The designer states that where priors exist and a segment is not fully resolved, there are conditions for why those priors exist. The designer states that for a segment of small material an engine may resolve the segment with certainty, and then no priors are needed. If the space cannot be resolved with certainty within the engine's scope, the forced sequence within the segment is understood to be reachable under optimality within 7 plies, which was derived from the tablebase, and without exhaustive coverage it is only a heuristic d-depth limitation set without a bound of certainty, which is useful because it limits the depth required to derive the certainty within the priors that exist. The designer states that the engine can then be used as an evaluator to evaluate the navigation of board states with that understanding in mind, in combination with the parsing of the priors and with when engines arrive on board states that have priors, and that the redirected search is given a conditional search parameter that informs the optimal routing without impacting the evaluator itself. The designer states that where priors exist, calibrated with respect to an existing engine, it is because the engine does not fully resolve the segment. The designer states that the superposition of the board state being evaluated relative to the priors and the certified board states that are reachable gives the front for deploying redirected derivation and conditional search based on priors, while the engine is used to parse the understanding that its instantly derived moves are never to be trusted where priors exist, and that the priors that exist are calibrated to make resolution as reachable as possible. The designer states that the amount of priors that exist informs the degree to which the engine can be trusted, via the superpositional understanding and whether full coverage has been certified. The designer states that full coverage is certified because work on upstream segments began, which requires coverage of the downstream dependencies to resolve, though not exhaustive coverage of depth, and that segments can be chained with full coverage while the resolution of optimality between them is bounded by the depth limitation of the certification.
3. **Engine variation has to be accounted for, and it is open.** (24 November 2026) The designer states that the engine has a bias that does not reproduce the same moves exactly every time, that engine reproducibility is a problem that may also depend on speed, and that this is an open problem of integrated specification. The designer states that it must be accounted for, since the decisions made on the proof tree construction for the placement of the priors may need to take the variation into consideration, to tune the priors so that they resolve the gaps with certainty.
4. **The priors judge the engine's picks afterwards, and putting them into the evaluator is not excluded.** (24 November 2026) The designer confirms that the engine's move choice is not changed by the priors, and that the priors are used afterwards to judge the moves it picks. The designer states that for the priors to change the engine's decisions, the engine would need to integrate the priors into its evaluator state, which has not been discussed, that this is not excluded as an integrated specification, and that he does not plan to go that deep, but that if the solution required it he would eventually have to. The designer states that it depends on whether it is required, or whether the situation can be handled by using the priors afterwards.
5. **The condition chosen from a pool of equals is relative to the scope, and the check of it is left open.** (23 November 2026) The designer states that when certified optimal routing from a board state has equally optimal options within the certified scope, one condition must be chosen to bias the routing, which removes the other viable options from view, and that this one is denoted in the d-depth field. The designer states that if the scope is later increased, the chosen route may turn out to end in a forced sequence that resolves negatively, that the options of the previous pool may be found to be viable, and that the chosen condition may not be optimal. The designer states that nothing in storage then indicates that there was a pool, and that as the irreversible segmented boundary resolves further, the branches of the other options are likely to be more resolved, so that clean up work can be performed. The designer states that this is handled either by additional notation, which adds to the storage burden, or by constant clean up and analysis of the priors as a maintenance process, which adds to the rederivation burden and can be a mechanism of the programmable goal front, and that this does not need to be resolved now. The designer states that item 78 stays true, since equally viable choices do not matter if they are certified as viable within the d-depth limit, and that what is at issue is whether they are equally optimal when the d-depth scope changes. The designer states that when the optimality changes, the condition in the d-depth field changes for the board state, that it is an open point of integrated specification whether other changes to the priors are required as a result, and that downstream dependencies may need more clean up. The designer states that the batch composition clean up work can be this process, or another function of P, or P itself with a goal designed to perform that clean up work, that whatever works will work, and that making the choice now may leave the program ignorant of benefits that emerge from the other options. The designer states that, since certified markers are usually added only when certainty has been derived, the clean up work may be redundant, and that it is considered in order to account for the case where it is not, to be extra safe.
6. **The output of F is any batch compatible with the input of P, and how it is made is left open.** (24 November 2026) The designer states that the goal allows the output of F to be any batch of information compatible with the input of P. The designer states that chosen starts, or board states produced from some operations on the priors, can be programmed into the goal front of F in any variable way that is left deliberately open, and that these are not mutually exclusive.
7. **The resolved anchor is the proof tree surface query, and no engine dependent action has been taken.** (24 November 2026) The designer states that the gap is read in two anchor points. The resolved one is the point of the proof tree surface query that has now been arrived at, and the other is the output of priors that optimize relative to no priors, which is relative to engine calculations. The designer states that the surface query is an integrated specification that depends on the resolution point arrived at. The designer states that how the application of using the engine as evaluator would be integrated into the programmable goal has not been considered, that no engine dependent action has been performed, and that the priors do not feed into the decisions of the evaluator, which remains true. The designer states that everything found so far was found without a dependency on engine evaluation, except for the priors that are kept (statement 10 of this file).
8. **How the evaluator is used is specified before which board states are kept or regenerated.** (25 November 2026) The designer states that how to use the evaluators is to be resolved before considering which board state is kept and which is regenerated, and that the keep or regenerate question is an integrated specification that is pending the specification of the evaluator integration. The designer states that how speed, coverage and storage are measured and balanced will be taken up after this is integrated into the documents.
9. **The priors are tied to the engine and the configuration specifications of the engine.** (25 November 2026) The designer states that, since engine variation presents a problem for using priors from another engine, or another engine on priors constructed by a different engine, the priors can be tied to the engine itself and to the configuration specifications of that engine. The designer states that this is not a loosely decided choice, that he arrived at it through a deeper understanding of what is being done, and that it is a deliberately calculated integrated specification so that the priors are useful for the engines they were constructed from (statement 11 of this file). The designer states that this makes the priors not certainly usable with another engine, that this is a dependency of how it was specified and does not exclude another approach in a general sense, and that where the engine settings are held is not known, is likely individualized for each engine given its compatibility constraints if such settings exist at all, and is an integrated specification.
10. **Keeping a board state as a prior requires the engine evaluator as a dependency.** (24 November 2026) The designer states that the priors require the engine evaluator as a dependency to keep a board state as a prior, because what is kept is measured by how it optimizes the engine or the agent, which in chess study depends on the agent studying. The designer states that this is a dependency of keeping and is not to be framed as a progression step of the program.
11. **What a prior measures, given the engine that constructed it.** (25 November 2026) The designer states that, given what has been composed, a prior built with a specific engine is constructed from the evaluator itself. The designer states that priors constructed on another engine would therefore optimize relative to the anchoring point and the gap, and would emerge from the sub-optimality of the engine they were built upon. The designer states that this is why the approach is beneficial for chess study, because it leaves the scope of what is important to the evaluator variable to the frontier of their limitations.
12. **The priors are the bridge between accumulated certainty and the heuristic variance of the engine.** (25 November 2026) The designer states that the evaluator makes move decisions independent of the priors, with a native navigation path that is heuristic and not entirely reproducible, that this variance has to be accounted for, and that the pruned proof tree provides depth dependent certainty. The designer states that what is important to keep in the priors is important only because it bears on whether the engine's choices are optimal relative to the certainty that can be derived from the priors, and that the priors are the bridge between the certainty that can be accumulated and carried forward and the sub-optimal choices inherent in the engine, bridging the gap of its heuristic variance. The designer states that the criteria for excluding and keeping are programmable and variable and are not described here.
