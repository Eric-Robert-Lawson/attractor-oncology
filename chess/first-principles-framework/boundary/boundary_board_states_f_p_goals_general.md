## 1. The designer's statements (23 November 2026)

1. **The output of F is a batch of information for improving the priors we choose to keep, and the goal front for P converts the old prior into the new one.** The designer states that the output of F is a batch of information that is useful for the improvement of priors that we choose to keep. The input for priors can be the feeding of this information within the priors, and one can then continue forward with a specified goal, based on the exact check constraints required to fulfill a specified requirement that is resolved by the addition from the self play. This could have carried forward the provable constraints from a previous proof tree that is no longer stored but is still verifiably derivable. The designer states that merging new priors into old ones is a misnomer. What is taken is the new proof-dependent information from the operation, whether it is a batch of whatever supplies this information. The goal front for P then takes that information and converts it to take the old prior into the new one. The new prior is the output. The input is the information required to continue the certification, or the proof tree itself to continue the certification (23 November 2026).
2. **The goals in general file holds what applies to the programmable goal front generally, and the exact check goal file is scoped to the approach chosen first.** The designer states that the statements that belong in the goals in general file are the statements that apply to the programmable goal front generally, while the exact check goal being defined is scoped for the integration specification of the approach that is going to be specified first. The designer states that this is the reason for the distinction between the files, and that it may help in understanding what is going on (23 November 2026).
3. **The return type of F loosens.** The designer states that the return type of F loosens, considering what is being changed here (23 November 2026).
