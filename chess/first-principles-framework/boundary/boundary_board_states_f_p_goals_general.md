# F and P: Goals in General

**Status.** A working record, 23 to 24 November 2026, of the designer's statements on goals in general: the goals of F and the goal of P, apart from the exact check, which is in `boundary_board_states_f_p_exact_check_goal.md`. It is modular: it takes `boundary_board_states_f_p_construction.md` as its base, changes no other file, and can be deleted without affecting them. It adds no primitive, does not touch the hard core (`boundary_board_states.md` §14 items 7 to 11), and belongs to the protective belt. It is stated in general terms. All statements are the designer's, kept close to his words and offered for his correction. No readings are held. Nothing has been run. Statements are integrated by topic: a later statement replaces the part of an earlier one that it changes.

## 1. The designer's statements

1. **F and P.** F takes priors as input, not batches of board states. The output of F is a batch of information useful for improving the priors we choose to keep, whether it is board states, games or whatever one wants it to be, as programmed by the programmable goal front. The return type of F loosens accordingly. The distinction between a batch of board states and priors may at times be null and void, since priors and outputs of batches of games can sometimes emerge as the same structure. The emergent result of F is not a prior created deliberately as P's architecture dictates, which is why P and F are distinct: one includes the d-depth metrics, the other carries forward information. F's output is not incompatible with P: it can have the trajectory removed and be composed into a batch of board states, based on discoverable, important trajectory findings that can emerge and impact the calculation and certification of the priors. Merging new priors into old ones is a misnomer. What is taken is the new proof-dependent information from the operation, and the goal front for P takes that information and converts the old prior into the new one. The new prior is the output. The input is the information required to continue the certification, or the proof tree itself. P handles priors, F cannot convert a previous prior into a new one, and the conversion is made with deliberate consideration to the coverage required to resolve what requires being resolved, to resolve more certainty on board states that require large proof sizes. This could carry forward the provable constraints of a previous proof tree that is no longer stored but is still verifiably derivable.
2. **Which file a statement belongs in.** The goals in general file holds the statements that apply to the programmable goal front generally. The exact check goal file is scoped to the integration specification of the approach to be specified first, and holds the statements about the exact check goal, including those that generalize it.
3. **Tractability and magnitude.** Engines already configure priors based on reachability and tractability. Choosing which priors to keep is decided by the ability of the engine to resolve the gap within the limit it is searching for. The priors are established with respect to depth limitations, which mitigates some risks. The magnitude can be measured by sparsity, so that tractability is measured and built into the construction of the priors. If the failure is one of magnitude, it is a matter of the scaling approach and of searching for compression optimizations that scale with the approach, in the way the d-depth metric schematics exploit the architecture of the program. This is the general aspect of scalability of the approach to the front of storage, speed and coverage.
4. **The variance of the programmable goal is in how optimally it navigates, and the composition of the architecture is not variable.** (24 November 2026) The designer states that the variance in the programmable goal does not come down to a choice of what the goal is, but to how optimally one is able to navigate to accomplishing the goal given the constraints one is working under. The designer states that what is not variable is the composition of the architecture that allows for the variance in how to navigate the goal. The designer states that the choice of how to compose that architecture emerges the optimal viability that is emergent from understanding the optimality of the navigation of the composition.
5. **Where the integrated specifications are kept.** The designer states that the specifications chosen as integrated specifications for the approach, to make it possible to execute anything empirically, reside in the modular architecture of the goal approach alone, and not in the construction file.
6. **The goal is not built until the specification is understood.** The designer states that the goal is not to be built until it is specified properly enough to be certain about how to specify, and that everything that can be specified is to be specified, and the idea understood, before anything is done.
7. **Certainty is resolved back to front, and the search is bounded between the priors.** (24 November 2026) The designer states that to resolve certainty from an upstream segment the downstream segments have to be resolved, so the work goes back to front, because the dependency chaining that resolves certainty depends on this downstream relationship. The designer states that when priors resolve a segment, or an engine is understood to resolve the segment with certainty without priors, those priors, or the lack of them, can be relied on to resolve the move chaining for optimality as the program specifies. The designer states that the search work is bounded to the search between the priors that exist, with conditional search and redirected derivation, and that the engine can help.
8. **Certification of a board state can be scoped through a composition of trees, with the proof trees held as specifications that are regenerated.** (24 November 2026) The designer states that it is possible to take an existing pruned proof tree, understand what is required to resolve a board state and the initial requirements to rederive the proof tree, and resolve those constraints from another angle to manage the storage limitation. The designer states that this is done by keeping certification values for specific board states, understanding the configuration needed to reproduce the state, and creating certifications that are provable from the composition and the configurations that are required to certify. The designer states that this scopes the certification of specific board states through a composition of trees, to leverage the coverage requirement, while the storage of those proof trees is denoted as specifications within the architecture of the program, which can be generated and regenerated to resolve a partial proof without reaching the storage limit. The designer states that this may scale the ability to certify specific board states through chaining of this kind.
