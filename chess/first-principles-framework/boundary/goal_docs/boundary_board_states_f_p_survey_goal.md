# F Goal: The Survey

**Status.** A working record, begun on 27 November 2026, of the designer's statements on the survey of irreversible segmented boundaries, as a goal of F. It takes `boundary_board_states_f_p_construction.md` as its base and does not change it. It adds no primitive, does not touch the hard core (`boundary_board_states.md` §14 items 7 to 11), and belongs to the protective belt. It is stated in general terms, apart from words the designer used in his own statements. All statements are the designer's, kept close to his words and offered for his correction. No readings are held. Nothing has been run.

---

## 1. The designer's statements

1. **Survey the engine before considering any prior.** (27 November 2026) The designer states that the irreversible segmented boundaries are first surveyed relative to the evaluator, to find which are already resolved by its native choices, given an acceptable time t per board state. Those regions can then be certified as resolved. A d-depth limit can bound this to what is cared to be carried forward, which is an integrated specification that can be progressively calibrated.
2. **The survey runs back to front.** (27 November 2026) The designer states that resolution depends on what is downstream, and that the engine is progressively less calculable further upstream, so progression between boundaries follows the same gradient of difficulty. He states that this can be proven, and leaves the proof as an integrated specification.
3. **Syzygy can measure the engine where it reaches.** (27 November 2026) The designer states that it can be determined with certainty, relative to Syzygy, whether the engine's moves are optimal, and that equally optimal routes can exist. Syzygy can also handle the proof tree operation until storage becomes a problem, where storage management strategies are then needed. This measures the engine, and the program generalizes beyond the scope of Syzygy, where the pruned proof tree understanding integrates.
4. **Solved up to certainty within x.** (27 November 2026) The designer states that a move departing from Syzygy's certainty is provably sub-optimal, but is acceptable if the certainty it misses cannot be reached within the d-depth limit x, so a deviation at x+1 is acceptable. Where deviation occurs, it is still good to understand at what depth, and relative to what depth of chained dependencies downstream.
5. **The survey is an operation of F.** (27 November 2026) The designer states that surveying an irreversible segmented boundary can be composed as an operation of what F does, programmed as a goal. One possible approach is to put the Syzygy solution of the boundary within the priors and have the engines self-play, relative to whatever goal one wants, checking their moves against optimality to determine the native optimality of the boundary.
6. **What the survey outputs is variable.** (27 November 2026) The designer states that the batch of information can be whatever the integrated specification chooses, so it is not a restriction. What matters is how the process feeds into others, what is kept and what is left to rederive.
7. **A Syzygy table is not an irreversible segmented boundary.** (27 November 2026) The designer states that boundaries are defined by irreversible moves and reachability between segment units, while Syzygy defines its solution by piece combinations, which does not match the architecture 1:1. A pawn's position itself defines the boundary, so kpvk consists of a large set of boundaries, and a pawn move navigates to a new one. After promotion that irreversible nature is gone, and play stays in the same boundary, as in krvk, knvk, kbvk or kqvk.
8. **The priors front waits on the survey.** (27 November 2026) The designer states that the survey, as an operation of F, is an earlier dependency of resolving how P outputs priors with the engine as evaluator, since that integration depends on the downstream native resolution relative to ground truth and the acceptable boundary within which that certainty is understood.
9. **Depth gained through chaining is kept and used.** (27 November 2026) The designer states that the limitation stays the same while certainty at a greater depth can be derived with the limitation still below it, with a fresh window of proof size at each prior arrived at with directionality. This emergent efficiency of the architecture is how certainty is reached with bounded storage, and it is not to be removed. In the survey, the d-depth limit x does not cap the depth of certainty that chaining reaches.
